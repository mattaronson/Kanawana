## Summary of Findings

### Research Questions — Status

**1. Is there any published ecological data about the site?**
- YES, partially. Lac Kanawana participates in RSVL (station 573) with monitoring since at least 2013 (Secchi disk transparency, phosphorus, dissolved organic carbon, chlorophyll a). However, actual measured values remain behind the CRE Laurentides 403 paywall. Physical lake data is well-documented: surface area 0.2388 km2, max depth 12.4 m, mean depth 5.2-5.4 m, volume 1,273,000 m3, altitude 318.4 m, watershed 5.17 km2. The camp beach is monitored for swimming safety via Swim Guide and Great Lakes Guide.
- No formal biodiversity inventories, ecological surveys, or academic ecological studies of the site were found.

**2. Is there a conservation designation?**
- NO evidence found. No provincial, federal, or municipal conservation designation, protected area status, or ecological reserve designation was found for the Camp Kanawana site. The site is privately held by YMCA Quebec. No Nature Conservancy of Canada or provincial conservation registry entries were found.

**3. What species inhabit the lakes?**
- NO specific species data found despite searches in English and French across fishing databases, species registries, and wildlife databases. The lake is private (YMCA property), which likely explains the absence of public angling reports. Papineau-Labelle Wildlife Reserve (where Kanawana runs canoe trips) has documented populations of white-tailed deer, moose, and beaver.

**4. Any environmental programs or certifications?**
- PROGRAMS: Yes, well-documented: C-Vert environmental leadership (ages 14-16, since 2005, 120 teens/summer); EMSB school conservation programs; Nature Awareness program (since 1980, Chris Adam); water hydrology instruction; nature interpretation activities; spring flora/fauna hikes.
- CERTIFICATIONS: NO formal environmental certifications found. Green Camps (greencamps.org) offers a certification for sustainable camps, but no evidence that Kanawana holds it. No ACA Healthy Camp accreditation, Green Key certification, or other environmental certifications were found.
- INFRASTRUCTURE: Clivus Multrum composting system (16 units, 32 dry toilets, 576,000 gal/yr water savings, installed 2006-2008). Artificial wetland (marais artificiel) for wastewater treatment. Heat exchangers. Energy-efficient buildings from 2006 green shift.

### New Facts for KB Consideration

1. Camp beach is listed on Swim Guide (ID 5490) and Great Lakes Guide as "Plage du camp Kanawana YMCA camp Beach" — indicates some form of water quality monitoring for swimming safety.
2. Lake Kanawana is part of the Riviere du Nord watershed system, upstream of Lake Tamaracouta. Associated watershed organization is ABRINORD.
3. CRE Laurentides Atlas des lacs launched February 2014; technical support reports for Saint-Sauveur lakes exist for years 2013-2024.
4. Kanawana programming includes "water hydrology" instruction (per YMCA website).
5. Some Kanawana toilet fixtures may be foam-flush (6 oz per use) rather than fully waterless.
6. Chris Adam was the 10th Pip Award recipient; the award was established in 2007.
7. Green Camps (greencamps.org) exists as a formal certification body for sustainable camps — Kanawana does not appear to hold this certification.
