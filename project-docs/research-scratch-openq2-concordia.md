## Summary of Findings

### The SGW/Concordia — Kanawana Connection: A Multi-Layered Institutional Relationship

The connection between Sir George Williams University (later Concordia University) and Camp Kanawana is deep and multi-layered, rooted in the shared YMCA parentage of both institutions:

#### 1. Shared YMCA Parentage (1851-late 1960s)
- YMCA Montreal (est. 1851) ran both Camp Kanawana (from 1894) and SGW College/University (from 1926).
- SGW held classes in the YMCA building on Drummond Street until the Henry F. Hall Building opened in 1966.
- SGW students used the YMCA's chapel, gym, and pool facilities until the 1970s.
- SGW severed financial ties with the YMCA in the late 1960s, but the institutional culture and personnel connections persisted.

#### 2. Direct Staff/Faculty Who Bridged Both Institutions

**Harold H. Potter (1914-2004):**
- B.A. from SGW College (1935-1939)
- Worked summers as camp counsellor at Kamp Kanawana during his college years
- Appointed lecturer in Sociology at SGW in 1947 (first Canadian-born Black sociologist hired by a Canadian post-secondary institution)
- Maintained YMCA ties throughout his career
- Set up the Department of Sociology and Anthropology at SGW in 1960

**Hedley G. Dimock (b. c.1928):**
- Coordinator of Staff Development and Training for Montreal YMCA
- Lecturer at SGW University
- First Chairman of the Department of Applied Social Science (1963)
- First Director of the Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies (1963)
- Conducted decade-long research program at Kamp Kanawana on counsellor effectiveness (1960-1970)
- Retired from Concordia University in 1980

**A. Ross Seaman:**
- Director of Kamp Kanawana for many years
- Part-time faculty in Applied Social Science at SGW from 1963
- Active collaborator in the Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies
- Teacher and chairperson of CRLT Department at Dawson College (1974-1984)
- Acting chair of Concordia University Leisure Studies (1986-87)
- Memorial scholarship established 1987 at Concordia, Dawson, Kanawana, and YMCA

**Raye Kass:**
- Hired as Assistant Professor of Applied Human Sciences at Concordia in 1972
- Collaborated extensively with Hedley G. Dimock on publications
- Program Director of Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies
- Presented on "Leadership on a hot tin roof: Applications for Camp Professionals" at International Camping Congress

**Stuart McLean (1948-2017):**
- B.A. in Applied Social Science from SGW University, graduated 1971
- Simultaneously served as counsellor at Camp Kanawana (1969-1973)
- Became Assistant Director at Kanawana (1974-1975)
- Once passed up a chance to study journalism at Carleton University to return to Kanawana
- After his death in 2017, family established the Stuart McLean Camp Kanawana Fund (raises $150K+/year)

#### 3. Academic Programs Born of the YMCA-Camp Nexus

**Department of Applied Social Science (1963):** Created at SGW under Principal Robert C. Rae and Hedley G. Dimock. This department, which directly employed camp professionals (Dimock, Seaman), was the institutional ancestor of Concordia's current Department of Applied Human Sciences.

**Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies (1963):** Created simultaneously with the APSS department, with Dimock as director and Seaman as collaborator. This centre was the research arm that formalized the camp-university knowledge transfer.

**Departmental Evolution:**
- Applied Social Science (1963, SGW) + Leisure Studies → amalgamated in November 1997 to form the Department of Applied Human Sciences at Concordia
- Current programs include: Human Relations, Recreation & Leisure Studies, Therapeutic Recreation, Youth Work

#### 4. Formal Research Programs

**YMCA Camping Research Project (1960-1970):**
- Conducted by Hedley G. Dimock at Kamp Kanawana
- Studies included: "A Study of the Change in Attitudes and Temperament of Counselors during a Summer in Camp" (1961), "A Study of the Prediction of Success of Twenty-Seven Camp Counselors" (1961)
- Research methodology included medical exams, camping skills tests, intelligence/values tests, behaviour frequency rating scales, behaviour observation reports, weekly progress reports, and parent assessments
- Later reports: "Selecting and Training Group Leaders" (Project Reports #10, 1967; #13, 1969)
- Camp Directors' Research Seminar (1970)

**YMCA Staff Task Group on Outdoor Education (1974):**
- Active during the SGW-Concordia transition
- Camping and outdoor education planning conducted 1973-1975

#### 5. Modern Concordia-YMCA Partnership (2019-present)

**YMCA Concordia Fine Arts Camp (started 2019):**
- Faculty of Fine Arts signed partnership with YMCA
- Concordia provides students, programming, and spaces; YMCA provides logistics
- Camp counsellors are primarily Concordia students and Stingers athletes

**Expanded Programs:**
- YMCA Concordia Science and Engineering Camp
- STEAMpunks camp (ages 13-15, STEAM focus)

Note: These modern partnerships are day camps at Concordia facilities, not at Kanawana's site in Saint-Sauveur.

#### 6. Alumni/Community Connections

**Rochelle Lazarus:** Concordia Sociology undergraduate who founded the YMCA Kanawana Sam Lazarus Fund, recognized as outstanding Concordian in 2011.

**Sam Lazarus (1979-2004):** Camper and staff member at Kanawana, died in Ghana. Family created campership fund. SAM JAM is annual fundraiser at Kanawana, running 17+ years.

**Grace McMorris (2023):** Concordia History MA thesis entirely about Kanawana, 1894-1967.

#### 7. Archival Heritage

Concordia University Archives hold the YMCA of Montreal fonds (P0145), which contains the primary documentary record of Camp Kanawana's entire history. The Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies fonds is also held at Concordia. This makes Concordia the de facto institutional custodian of Kanawana's historical record.

### Was There a Formal Pipeline?

No explicit formal "pipeline" program between SGW/Concordia and Kanawana has been documented. However, the evidence shows something functionally equivalent:

1. The Department of Applied Social Science was created in 1963 by people who were simultaneously YMCA camp professionals (Dimock as YMCA Staff Development Coordinator; Seaman as Kanawana Director).
2. Students in Applied Social Science could (and did) work at Kanawana — Stuart McLean is the most famous example, earning his B.A. in Applied Social Science (1971) while working summers at Kanawana (1969-1975).
3. The department's research program was literally conducted at Kanawana (Dimock's decade of counsellor research).
4. The YMCA connection meant the same organization was recommending career paths across both institutions — one search result noted that when a person graduated from SGW, Seaman offered him a job at the Cornwall YMCA as a youth worker.

The relationship was less a formal pipeline and more a deeply intertwined institutional ecosystem where the same YMCA professionals built both the camp program and the university department, creating a natural flow of students, knowledge, and careers between the two.
