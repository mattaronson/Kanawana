## The rules that actually govern the work

Not style preferences. Each exists because it was violated and cost something. Ten carried over; three
more were earned on 2026-09-06, all by me.

1. **A negative result from a tool is a fact about the tool, not about the world.** Record it as an
   ACCESS FAILURE with the untried routes named. Never as "does not exist."
2. **"X appears nowhere in this project" is one grep, and must never be written without running it.**
3. **Where a document is known only from an image, "the document does not say X" must be written as
   "the image does not show X."**
4. **Corrections go in place and visibly.** Superseded facts are prefixed `[SUPERSEDED <date>: …]` and
   kept, never deleted. A fact that is *accurate but was misread* gets `[NOTE <date>: …]` instead —
   see `f_1870`, which correctly reports what the 1974–75 annual report says and was misread by two
   articles.
5. **Oral history yields to documents only where they DIRECTLY conflict.**
6. **Read the page image, not the OCR, when one letter carries the claim.**
7. **Search the KB before reading a source, not after.** *And its harder form, learned 2026-09-06:
   searching the KB does not help when the KB does not know the name to search for.* Camp Olier and
   Camp Hiawatha were sitting in the January 1975 CCA Directory, which this project had read word for
   word, and it took a government toponymic dataset to say what to grep for.
8. **When you settle a date, redo the arithmetic that sat beside it.** *And check the other articles:*
   the attendance series corrected 475 campers from 1974 to 1975 on 2026-09-05 and
   `winter-programming.md` still carried the old date a day later.
9. **Locate by structure, not by first string match.**
10. **A string-matching coverage sweep lies in two directions.**
11. **Test a capability before spending a session on what it promises.** `f_2244` said the whole
    Montreal Gazette was searchable and the Phase 2 people questions were "each now one query away."
    Four search routes are closed from here. Half a day would have gone into that claim.
12. **A silent failure that returns HTTP 200 is the worst kind.** The Google News Archive page-image
    endpoint returns a 43-byte transparent GIF, under 200, when the `Referer` header is missing. It
    reads as a served image.
13. **When a priority's own closing sentence turns out to be wrong, correct the priority.** p_423 said
    sweeping the toponymic register's Camp class would map Quebec's camps. It would not — about half
    the class is hunting camps on Anticosti — and the correction is written into p_423 itself.

**The single best find is the 1900 report.** "Seventh Annual Report of the Current Camp Committee",
10 December 1900, is eight pages of cursive manuscript. The Internet Archive scan carries no OCR text
layer because no OCR was going to read handwriting, and this project held a 0-byte file for it for
years. The page images were read by eye and fully transcribed. It gives: two committees above it and a
third beside it; three parties in 1900 at Lac St. Joseph, one of them the board; **a dated camper
survey of 20 October 1900** with about thirty-five replies printed verbatim and the repeated ones
underlined; and the physical camp named feature by feature. **The method works on any image-only
manuscript in that collection, and there are others.**

**Two capabilities established, one destroyed.**
- *Toponymic register (works).* The Commission's search form is an ASP.NET postback that returns
  nothing to a script, but **fiche pages answer a direct GET on `no_seq`**, and the `no_seq` values
  come from the Données Québec CKAN datasets (`toponymes-officiels`, `toponymes-desofficialises`,
  `noms-traditionnels-autochtones`). This settled Round Lake = **Lac Rond**, located **Île Jones**,
  and surfaced two camps three kilometres from Kanawana.
- *Montreal Gazette (mostly closed).* All four search routes fail: books.google.com renders snippets in
  JavaScript, `output=html_text` returns 403, corpus-wide search 429s behind a challenge, and the Books
  API is out of quota. Browse-by-date and page images do work, with the `Referer` trap above, a zoom
  ceiling of 3 (headlines legible, body text not), and **date labels that are sometimes wrong** — read
  the masthead.

**Infrastructure.** `data_integrity.py` now fails on a dangling `source_id` and reports duplicate cache
