# Camp Kanawana Oral History Interview Instrument

*Prepared: 2026-06-11 | For: Matt Aronson (src_oral_aronson) | Status: awaiting operator session*

## How to Use This Document

Answer in any order, in as much or as little detail as you like. Partial answers are valuable — "I don't remember" and "we never said that in my era" are themselves data points (they date traditions). Where possible, note **approximately what years** your memory covers, since traditions change between eras. Everything you provide will be recorded as oral history facts (confidence: `user_knowledge`) with you as the cited source, and clearly distinguished from documentary evidence.

Each section notes what research has already established, so you can react to it ("yes, that's it," "no, we told it differently") rather than starting from scratch.

## Section 1 — The Chopsy Legend *(unblocks p_045; affects myths-and-legends)*

No spelling of "Chopsy" appears in any online source — the story exists only in oral tradition. Research established the broader "Cropsey" camp-legend archetype (documented at US camps since the 1970s, with variants Cropsy, Stumpy, Madman Marz), but nothing connects it to Kanawana.

1. Tell the story as you remember hearing it (or telling it). Who or what is Chopsy? What's the backstory, and how does the telling end?
2. How is the name spelled/pronounced as you knew it? (Chopsy, Choppsy, Chopsie...?)
3. When and where was it told — Council Ring, cabin at lights-out, canoe trips, a specific campsite?
4. Was there a jump-scare, a ritual element, or a counsellor "performance" (e.g., someone bursting out of the woods)?
5. Is Chopsy tied to a specific place — a building, trail, lake, or the haunted house (see Section 5)?
6. When did you first hear it, and from whom (counsellor, older camper)? Any sense of how old the story already was?
7. Did the story resemble the American "Cropsey" pattern (a disfigured man with an axe/hook who haunts the camp)? Or was it something different?

## Section 2 — Songs, Cheers, and Grace *(unblocks p_048; affects camp-songs-cheers)*

Research established: the Kanawana Marching Song/Cheer is set to "Alabama Jubilee" (1915), with lost verses rediscovered at Concordia in 2006; the full 1922 cheer text is documented; Richard Kerr's "On My Way to Kanawana" exists on a CD in the Concordia archives (P145/SR0001) but no lyrics are accessible; song books 1925–1945 are in the archives.

1. Sing/write out the Marching Song as sung in your era — every verse you can recall. (We can compare against the 1922 text and the 2006 rediscovered verses.)
2. What was the dining hall **Grace**? Exact words if possible. Was it sung or spoken? Same at every meal?
3. What were the **section cheers** (L&V game cheers, section-specific chants)? Which sections had their own, and what were the words?
4. Do you know "On My Way to Kanawana" (Richard Kerr)? Can you recall any of it?
5. What was the core **campfire repertoire** of your era — the songs everyone knew? Any songs unique to Kanawana (not general camp standards)?
6. Were there cheers/chants tied to specific events — canoe trip departures/returns, banquet, L&V games, the dam ceremony?
7. Any song parodies or unofficial/irreverent songs that wouldn't appear in official song books?

## Section 3 — Grand Portage, the Longhouse, and Building Names *(unblocks p_051; affects places-and-locations)*

"Grand Portage" and "Longhouse" are known only from your earlier mentions; no written documentation was found. The documented modern buildings are Katimavik, Rose des Vents, the dining hall (300+ capacity), and the Desjardins pavilion (2018).

1. What and where was/is **Grand Portage**? A building, a trail, a campsite, an event?
2. What and where was/is the **Longhouse**? What was it used for, and what did it look like?
3. List every **named building, cabin, or place** you can remember, with location (Centre Camp / Front Camp / Lake Wilson side) and use. Even fragmentary lists help.
4. Which buildings existed in your era that are now gone? Any you watched being built or demolished?
5. Were cabins/tents named or numbered? By section?

## Section 4 — Lake Desjardins *(NEW — conflict c_006; affects j-w-mcconnell, the-kanawana-site)*

You've said J.W. McConnell donated **Lake Desjardins** to the camp. But no lake by that name exists in any documented inventory near the site: the camp's three documented lakes are Kanawana, Wilson, and Round Lake, and "Lac Desjardins" appears in neither the Commission de toponymie records nor the 96-lake inventory of Sainte-Anne-des-Lacs. Separately, Desjardins Group donated $1M in 2018, funding the "Desjardins pavilion."

1. Where is/was Lake Desjardins? Can you place it relative to the three known lakes or camp areas?
2. Could "Lake Desjardins" be an informal camp name for Round Lake (Lac Rond) or another small lake?
3. Where does the McConnell donation story come from — who told it to you, in what era, in what words? Was it "McConnell donated a lake" or specifically "Lake Desjardins"?
4. Is there any chance the name in the story was different (e.g., Lake Wilson) or that the Desjardins name attached later?

## Section 5 — The Haunted House and Ghost Stories *(affects myths-and-legends)*

The 1935 camp history mentions a final-week "eating-out day, when every camper went over to the haunted house and cooked his own supper" — so by 1935 a specific reachable structure was casually called "the haunted house."

1. Was there still a "haunted house" (or a building called that) in your era? Which building, and where?
2. What stories attached to it?
3. What other ghost stories or supernatural narratives circulated — tied to Lake Wilson, the Council Ring, the woods, abandoned structures?
4. What "everyone knows" claims about camp history circulated among campers/alumni (e.g., about the name, the founding, famous alumni)? We want these even if — especially if — they may be exaggerated.

## Section 6 — Directors, Staff, and Eras *(affects directors-index, sean-day, dave-twynam)*

Documented directors near your era: Dave Twynam (~1976/1979–1980 — archival dating ambiguous), Bruce Netherwood (1980s?), Sean Day (c. 2004–2023, your counsellor), Kate Taylor (current). The 1981–2003 period is almost entirely undocumented.

1. Who was camp director in each year you attended? Who were memorable section directors/staff?
2. What years were you at camp (camper/staff)?
3. Anything you know about directors before your time, from camp lore — names, reputations, stories?
4. What do you remember about Sean Day as a counsellor and (later) director — programs he started, changes he made?
5. Do you remember the names Twynam, Netherwood, Nancy Sawyer, or Hanagan being spoken of at camp?

## Section 7 — Traditions and Ceremonies *(affects traditions-and-culture)*

1. The **dam ceremony** (opening the dam between Lakes Kanawana and Wilson, documented since 1935): did it still happen in your era? Describe it.
2. Describe the **Council Ring** ceremonies of your era — opening/closing rituals, fire-lighting, who spoke, any Indigenous-derived elements and how they were handled.
3. Banquet and final-night traditions?
4. Section identity in your era — names, progression, rivalries, initiation customs?
5. Any traditions you suspect were unique to Kanawana?
