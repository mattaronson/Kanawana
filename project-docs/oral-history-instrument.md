# Camp Kanawana Oral History Interview Instrument

*First prepared 2026-06-11. Rebuilt 2026-09-07 against everything found since, under the standing
DRAFT_ORAL_HISTORY_INSTRUMENT priority. For: Matt Aronson (`src_oral_aronson`).*

## How to Use This Document

Answer in any order, in as much or as little detail as you like. Partial answers are worth having, and so
are refusals: "we never said that in my era" and "I have no idea" both date things, and both get recorded.
Where you can, say roughly **which years** a memory covers, because almost everything in this document
changed at least once between 1894 and now.

Everything you give becomes a fact cited to you, marked `user_knowledge`, and kept visibly separate from
documentary evidence. **Where your memory and a document disagree, the document wins on the point of
disagreement and your account is kept beside it, not deleted.** That is the project's standing rule and it
is not a comment on your memory; it is how the two kinds of evidence are weighted.

Each section says what the research has already established, so you can react to it rather than start from
nothing. Three sections are new since June and three have been rewritten because the documentary position
moved under them.

## Section 1 . The Chopsy Legend

**Status: the documentary record is exhausted.** Zero hits for any spelling of Chopsy across all 634
digitised text items this project holds. The story exists only in oral tradition, which makes this section
the single most valuable thing you can give the project.

**Two findings have reframed the question since June.**

First, **"the Cave" in the legend is a real building doing exactly what the story says it does.** The
camp's 1987 report has the Cave as the hike-and-trip equipment store. So at least one concrete element of
the legend is not invented.

Second, **the camp had an earlier ghost, and it stops just before Chopsy is said to start.** A structure
called the Haunted House is documented from 1928 to 1940, with its own legend and its own rule: the
spirits appear only when the wind is from the east and the moon and stars are invisible. Campers walked
over to it and cooked supper there. Chopsy's stated origin is 1945.

**And one methodological finding governs how these questions are asked.** David Hartry, then president of
the Canadian Camping Association, published an account in 1971 of a legend at his own camp that he had
personally invented: staged for the first hunt in 1968 by setting the campers' watches forward, the
creature built by campers let in on the secret the year before, given a false pedigree by a book planted
in the camp library, and expected annually within three years. It tells us nothing about Chopsy. It tells
us **what kind of evidence a legend like this leaves behind**: people remember the staging, nobody
remembers the invention, and what looks like corroboration can be part of the hoax. So several questions
below ask about production rather than plot.

1. Tell the story as you heard it or told it. Who or what is Chopsy, what is the backstory, and how does
   the telling end?
2. How was it spelled and pronounced in your era? Chopsy, Choppsy, Chopsie, something else?
3. Where and when was it told: Council Ring, cabin at lights-out, canoe trips, one particular campsite?
4. **Was anything staged?** A counsellor bursting out of the woods, a sound cue, a prop, an object
   produced as proof, a night when something was "found"?
5. **Were you ever let in on it, and at what point?** Did staff talk about it among themselves as a thing
   they ran, or as a thing that simply existed?
6. Was anyone ever shown documentary "proof": a newspaper clipping, a book in the lodge, a photograph, a
   name carved somewhere?
7. Is Chopsy tied to a specific place? Does the Cave in the story mean the hike-and-trip store?
8. **Is Chopsy the Haunted House ghost under a new name?** Did you ever hear the two connected, or hear of
   a building called the Haunted House at all?
9. A 1933 camp newsletter has campers passing **"the deserted village"** on a trip. Does that phrase mean
   anything to you, and was there a ruined or abandoned place anywhere near camp that stories attached to?
10. When did you first hear the story, from whom, and did it feel old at the time?

## Section 2 . Songs, Cheers, and Grace

**Status changed since June: this section now tests rather than supplies.** A full read of the camp
newspaper printed the **"Yo Triumphy"** cheer again on 9 July 1938, but introduced it as "a **former**
Kanawana yell, which is very effective when shouted rapidly", with a performance instruction the 1922
brochure does not carry: "(sung slowly)" against the opening line. It was still in the repertoire in July
1933, so it went out of use between 1933 and 1938. The Marching Song is set to **"Alabama Jubilee"** (1915). Richard Kerr's "On My Way to
Kanawana" exists on a disc in the Concordia archives with no accessible lyrics. Song books from 1925 to
1945 are in the archives and unread.

1. Sing or write out the Marching Song as sung in your era, every verse you can get to. We can set it
   against the 1922 text and the verses rediscovered in 2006.
2. What was the dining hall **Grace**, in exact words if you can? Sung or spoken? The same at every meal?
3. What were the **section cheers**, and which sections had their own? Words where you have them.
4. Do you know "On My Way to Kanawana"? Any line of it at all?
5. What was the core campfire repertoire of your era? Which songs were Kanawana's own rather than general
   camp standards?
6. Cheers tied to specific events: canoe trip departures and returns, banquet, the L&V games, the dam.
7. Parodies, unofficial and irreverent songs that would never appear in an official song book.
8. Does **"Yo Triumphy"** mean anything to you, or had it gone entirely by your time? A clean "never heard
   of it" is a useful answer, because it confirms a disappearance the documents already date to the
   1930s. If you have heard it, that is a bigger finding: it would mean the camp revived a cheer it had
   already called former in 1938.

## Section 3 . Buildings, Named Places, and the Ones That Are Gone

**Status: partly answered by documents, and one passage of the wiki is now known to be wrong-ish.** The
current facilities list gives 550 acres, three lakes, six overnight campsites, the Katimavik gymnasium, a
dining hall seating over 300, the Rose des Vents and Desjardins pavilions, the Farmhouse and Blockhouse in
Front Camp. Separately, the article on places carries section age bands in two-year steps with cabin and
tent counts, taken from a version of the camp's website that no longer exists and cannot be dated.

1. What and where was or is **Grand Portage**? Building, trail, campsite, event?
2. What and where was or is the **Longhouse**? What was it used for and what did it look like?
3. List every **named building, cabin or place** you can remember, with rough location (Centre Camp, Front
   Camp, Lake Wilson side) and use. Fragmentary lists are fine.
4. Which buildings existed in your era and are gone now? Any you saw built or demolished?
5. Were cabins and tents named or numbered, and by section?
6. In your era, what were the **age bands** for Pioneers, Woodsmen, Pathfinders and Coureurs des Bois? The
   camp now says 7 to 12 and 13 to 16; an older version of its own site said 8 to 9, 10 to 11, 12 to 13
   and 14 to 15. Both may have been true at different times and we cannot date the older one.
7. The camp claims **550 acres** and one third-party source says 455. Did anyone at camp ever quote an
   acreage, and which?

## Section 4 . Lake Desjardins

**Status unchanged, and it is a real conflict.** You have said J. W. McConnell donated **Lake
Desjardins**. No lake of that name is documented anywhere near the site. The camp's three lakes are
Kanawana, Wilson and Round Lake, and "Lac Desjardins" is in neither the provincial toponymy register nor
the 96-lake inventory of Sainte-Anne-des-Lacs. Desjardins Group gave $1M in 2018, which funded the
Desjardins pavilion.

1. Where is or was Lake Desjardins? Can you place it against the three known lakes?
2. Could it be a camp nickname for Round Lake, or for a pond that has no official name?
3. Where did the McConnell story reach you, from whom, and in what words? Was it "McConnell donated a
   lake" or specifically "Lake Desjardins"?
4. Is there a chance the lake in the story had a different name and the Desjardins name attached later,
   after 2018?

## Section 5 . Ghosts, Rumours, and What Everyone Knew

1. Was there still a building called the **Haunted House** in your era, and which one?
2. What other supernatural stories circulated: Lake Wilson, the Council Ring, the woods, an abandoned
   structure?
3. What "everyone knows" claims about camp history went round among campers and staff, about the name, the
   founding, famous alumni, buried things, the war? **We want these especially where they are probably
   wrong**, because a wrong story that many people believed is itself a fact about the camp.
4. Was there ever an "oldest camp in Canada" claim made at camp, and in what form?

## Section 6 . Directors, Staff, and Eras

**Status changed considerably since June.** The 1981 to 2003 hole is now partly filled from outside the
YMCA's own files: **Dave Twynam** is last confirmed in April 1981; **Peter Gilling Goddard** (1953 to
2016) signs a camp newsletter as Director in November 1982 and appears in national camping association
papers as a Quebec representative through 1986; **Jay Netherwood** in 1986 and 1987; **Bruce Netherwood**
from 1988 through the 1994 centennial; **Joanna Hoad** around 1995 to 2000, spelling uncertain;
**Nicolas Garcia** from 2009; **Sean Day** through to 2023; **Marie-Pierre Lacasse** as Executive Camp
Director from 2024; **Kate Taylor** as camp director.

1. Who was director in each year you were there, and which section directors and staff stand out?
2. What years were you at camp, as camper and as staff?
3. Names from lore, before your time: any of Twynam, Goddard, either Netherwood, Hoad, Hanagan, Sawyer?
4. **Joanna Hoad, Hode, Houde or another spelling?**
5. Two people are recorded holding the single on-site Director role for 2013 into early 2014. Do you know
   how that worked, or who was actually running camp?
6. Sean Day: what did he start, what did he change?
7. **Five names surfaced in the last month with nothing attached to them.** Do any mean anything to you,
   in any connection with camp? **Andy Barrie. Dr Bernard Villemaire. Chuck Baronowski. Diana Deakin.
   Colin Kirk.**

## Section 7 . Julien Tassé *(new)*

**Status: he has an article now, and it has holes only a person can fill.** Julien Tassé was caretaker for
more than thirty years, from about 1958 until his death around 1992 to 1995. Documents name him in eight
annual and director's reports, usually with real warmth: in 1970 he "once again did a fantastic job
holding the camp together with chewing gum." The camp's own title for him was **Maintenance Supervisor**.
His son **Yves** was on the payroll with him by 1976. A 1965 camper profile puts his birth at about 1932
and says he wanted the camp to go coeducational, three years before it did. The 1964 report puts **four
Tassé children** on the property.

1. Do you remember him, or remember him being talked about?
2. **The dining hall was renamed Salle Julien Tassé after he died, and the name never stuck.** Was there a
   formal dedication, a board resolution, a plaque? None of the 151 photographed plaques is a dedication.
3. What is his actual death date? The range 1992 to 1995 comes from you and nothing else.
4. Who were the other three children? Only Yves is named on any board.
5. Is any of the family still reachable?

## Section 8 . The 1970s Camp, Which You Did Not See *(new)*

Ask yourself here what you were **told**, not what you saw. Several things the reports describe would have
left traces in camp talk a decade later.

1. **Horses.** Kanawana ran a riding programme, praised in 1969, condemned in 1970, cancelled, run anyway,
   praised again in 1971 and cancelled again on cost. Was there ever any talk of horses at camp, or a
   place still called the stables or the riding ring?
2. **Becsies.** The camp's second property on Lac des Becs-scie was, by 1970, being used by "young people
   on the move" as a place to swim and crash, and the 1971 report recommended selling it. **We do not know
   when or whether it was sold.** Did the camp still have or talk about a second property in your time?
3. **The kitchen.** 1970 was the first year the camp's food was **catered by an outside company** rather
   than cooked by camp staff. Was the kitchen contracted out in your era, and did anyone say when that
   started?
4. **The Rangers.** There was a fifth section called the Rangers, which ran to 1964 and then vanished.
   Did the name survive at all?
5. **A "giant reunion" in 1976** is mentioned once and nowhere else. Any trace of it?

## Section 9 . Traditions and Ceremonies

1. The **dam ceremony**, opening the dam between Lakes Kanawana and Wilson, documented from 1935. Did it
   still happen? Describe it. When did it stop?
2. The **Council Ring** of your era: opening and closing rituals, fire-lighting, who spoke, and the
   Indigenous-derived material, including how it was handled and whether that changed while you were
   there.
3. Banquet and final-night customs.
4. Section identity: progression, rivalries, initiations.
5. **Non Nobis Solum.** When did you first meet the motto, and was there ever an English one before it?
6. Anything you believe was Kanawana's alone.

## Section 10 . The Pip Alumni Award and the Alumni Record *(rewritten, and now urgent)*

**Status: the source has disappeared.** Most of what this project knows about the Pip Alumni Award, the
Stuart McLean fund and the 2014 reunion came from pages of the YMCA Quebec website that **no longer
exist**. The site was rebuilt around late 2025 and the current alumni page is about volunteer recognition.
Twenty-five facts here now rest on a citation that names a domain rather than a document, and the archived
copies cannot be read from this environment.

1. **Who received the Pip Award in 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022 and 2023?** The 2019 recipient is a "Dr." on
   Andrew Caddell's LinkedIn and nothing else is known.
2. The award paddles and your own roster disagree twice: **Oscar or Oskar Newsam** for 2008, and the 2009
   name. Which is right?
3. Can you reach **Andrew Caddell**, who sponsors the award?
4. Do you have anything saved from the old alumni pages: screenshots, a printout, a forwarded newsletter?
5. The **120th reunion at Victoria Hall on 5 September 2014**: were you there, and does anything survive
   from it, a programme, a photo set, a guest list?
