### NEW FACTS DISCOVERED

#### Philip "Pip" Caddell
1. **Full name: Philip Mortimer Caddell**
2. **Born: July 7, 1913, Brantford, Ontario, Canada**
3. **Died: July 24, 2004, Ottawa, Ontario, Canada (age 91)**
4. **Born in Canada but raised in Scotland; returned to Canada as a teenager**
5. **Parents: Captain William Persse Caddell and Florence May Ogden**
6. **Had an older brother: Shirley Lee Forsythe Caddell (born November 1911)**
7. **Married Elga Ramsey in 1945** (Elga born 1917; devout Anglican, associated with St. Philip's in Montreal West)
8. **Employer was a local brewer** (per "The Black Horse" story in "The Goal")
9. **Attended the 1936 Montreal Maroons vs Detroit Red Wings six-overtime playoff game** (settled by Mud Bruneteau)
10. **Witnessed the 1935 Maroons Stanley Cup win**
11. **Played clandestine role helping his employer honor commitment to reward Maroons captain Hooley Smith with a Percheron horse** (the "Black Horse" story)
12. **His spoken story "The Black Horse" was transcribed from memory by Andrew and published posthumously in "The Goal"**
13. **Described as "a wonderful storyteller"**
14. **Never played hockey himself, but loved the game**

#### James Lawrence Caddell
1. **Full name: James Lawrence Caddell**
2. **Born: March 16, 1973, Montreal**
3. **Died: October 16, 2005, Tupiza, Bolivia (age 32)**
4. **Cause of death: Pulmonary edema caused by altitude sickness** (Tupiza is below 3,000m; he had suffered altitude sickness on Mount Everest 9 months earlier above 5,000m; nobody in the hotel or local hospital recognized the symptoms or had oxygen)
5. **Mother: Norma Lewis, journalist and communications lecturer at Dawson College** (covered John Lennon's "bed-in" for peace for the Montreal Gazette in 1969; Andrew was one of her former students)
6. **Siblings (half): Emily Caddell, Jack Caddell** (from Andrew's marriage to Elaine Feldman)
7. **Education timeline:**
   - Cortland High School near Syracuse, NY (graduated 1991)
   - Moved to Montreal; attended Dawson College
   - McGill University: enrolled in North American studies (cross-disciplinary history/economics/political science), switched to political science and history, BA ~1997
   - Summer 1997: Normandy Foundation Scholar (six-week tour of European battlefields: Waterloo, Somme, D-Day Normandy)
   - 1998: Royal Military College, Kingston -- MA in War Studies (as a civilian)
   - At RMC, met Tara Newell (MA in Public Administration at Queen's); neither knew anyone in Kingston, but recognized each other from McGill (James had worked part-time for the Student Society, Tara had signed his paycheques as Student Society president)
8. **Military service:**
   - Joined Royal Canadian Hussars reserve unit while at Dawson/McGill
   - 1995: took year off, trained at CFB Valcartier
   - Deployed to Bosnia: Rotation 5 of Operation Cavalier, May-October 1995
   - Served in CANBAT 2 (Canadian Battle Group 2), part of UNPROFOR
   - Role: Cougar driver in 42 Troop of "D" Squadron of the 12e Regiment blinde du Canada
   - Awarded: Peacekeeping Service Medal and UNPROFOR Medal
   - After moving to Ottawa (~2000), switched to 2nd Intelligence Platoon of Royal Canadian Hussars
   - Received the Queen's Jubilee Medal from his new regiment in Ottawa
9. **Career: Senior Program Manager, Human Resources Development Canada** (on leave for world trip)
10. **World trip (started June 2004):** Left government jobs with Tara Newell. Itinerary: crossed Australia, toured Hanoi and Angkor Wat, humanitarian work in East Timor and Indonesia, 2 months as CMAT volunteer at orphanage in Kannyakumari district (Tamil Nadu, southern India) living with children orphaned by 2004 tsunami ("changed everything for us"), trekked in Nepal, toured Gallipoli and Jerusalem, dined in Provence, learned Spanish in Buenos Aires and Santiago, arrived by bus in Bolivia
11. **Death circumstances:** Arrived in Tupiza by local bus; felt unwell at dinner; returned to hotel; died while wife Tara was beside him; nobody in hotel or local hospital recognized altitude sickness symptoms or had oxygen
12. **Memorial service: Ottawa, November 5, 2005**
13. **Obituaries published in Globe and Mail and Ottawa Citizen**
