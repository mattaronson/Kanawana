## Consolidated Findings

### Full Name and Dates
- **Harold Herbert Potter** (March 27, 1914 -- May 8, 2004)
- Died in Montreal at age 90

### Parents
- **Alfred Moore Westerby Potter** (1885--1947): Emigrated from British Guiana (now Guyana) to Canada in 1907. Union-seeking redcap train station porter at Windsor Station, Montreal. It was not until 1943 that Alfred and other CNR porters received official union recognition and a first collective agreement. Married Gertrude in June 1913.
- **Gertrude Miller Potter** (1890--1979): Emigrated from British Guiana to Canada in 1910. Volunteer fundraiser and part-time worker for Charles Este's Negro Community Centre from its opening in 1927. Rented out rooms of the family's modest apartment to Black American tourists who would have been denied accommodation at local hotels.
- Both parents arrived despite a Canadian immigration system that sought to deny entry to non-Whites.

### Early Life
- Grew up in Little Burgundy, Montreal -- home of Montreal's working-class, English-speaking Black community (also known as "Harlem of the North")
- Engaged in public life at an early age, especially within the YMCA
- **At age 14**, Potter was winning piano awards and speaking contests at the YMCA on Drummond Street
- The politics of overcoming racial prejudice was foundational to Potter's childhood

### Education
1. **B.A., Sir George Williams College** (1935--1939)
   - Editor of *The Georgian*, the SGW student newspaper, in 1938--39
   - Quote about the paper: "It was a dull, straight, serious and earnest newspaper" (said when the paper folded in 1980)
   - Mordecai Richler held the same editorial position approximately a decade later
2. **M.A. in Sociology, McGill University** (enrolled 1947, defended 1949)
   - Thesis: *"The Occupational Adjustments of Montreal Negroes, 1941--48"*
   - Supervised by **Oswald Hall** (one of many Chicago School alumni who dominated McGill's Department of Sociology into the 1950s)
   - Key findings: In the early 1940s, nearly 50% of all Black male wage earners were sleeping car porters; almost 80% of Black women were employed as domestic servants; the remainder in menial occupations or the "entertainment industry"
   - Potter concluded that the "physical visibility of Negro men and women limit[ed] their range of economic opportunity"
   - For decades, this thesis became the standard sociological text cited on limited job opportunities for Black citizens in early 20th-century Montreal
3. **PhD studies (incomplete), University of Chicago** (started 1950)
   - Went south to pursue PhD in Sociology at U of Chicago
   - Returned to teach at SGWC without completing the degree because he had a job waiting

### Camp and YMCA Connection
- **YMCA Kamp Kanawana**: Worked summers as camp counsellor during his undergraduate years (1935--1939 period)
- **YMCA speaking/music**: Active at the YMCA on Drummond Street from at least age 14 (ca. 1928)
- **1949 Ottawa YMCA event**: Argued that "racial superiority causes war"
- **Camp Laquemac**: Co-authored "Camp Laquemac: a bilingual adult education training centre" with Renee Morin (Toronto: Canadian Association for Adult Education, 1953) -- demonstrates continued camp/adult education work beyond Kanawana
- **YMCA connection to hiring**: Potter hired Kurt Jonassohn in 1961, whom he "knew from the Y" -- Jonassohn had also completed an MA at McGill before heading to Chicago
- Maintained YMCA ties throughout his career

### Publications (known)
1. **M.A. Thesis**: "The Occupational Adjustments of Montreal Negroes, 1941--48." McGill University, 1949.
2. **Camp Laquemac**: Potter, Harold H. and Renee Morin. *Camp Laquemac: a bilingual adult education training centre.* Toronto: Canadian Association for Adult Education, 1953.
3. **"Negroes in Canada."** *Race* 3:1 (1961): pp. 39--56.
4. **Republication**: "Negroes in Canada" republished in Richard Laskin (ed.), *Social Problems: A Canadian Profile.* Toronto: McGraw Hill Company of Canada Ltd., 1964.
5. **Royal Commission report**: Potter, Harold H. and Daniel G. Hill. *Negro Settlement in Canada, 1628--1965: A Survey.* Presented to the Royal Commission on Bilingualism and Biculturalism. Ottawa: Government of Canada, 1966.
6. **Book review**: Review of Robert Staples, *The Black Family: Essays and Studies.* *Contemporary Sociology* 2:6 (1973): pp. 633--635.

### Views on Race and Discrimination
- Spoke regularly on topics of race and prejudice
- Optimistically opined that prejudice was on the wane, especially in the wake of World War Two -- a war that, he believed, had shown the necessity of an egalitarian and meritocratic social order
- At 1949 Ottawa YMCA event: "racial superiority causes war"
- Maintained that he had **never experienced discrimination** at Sir George Williams
- In 1969 (sabbatical at Southeastern Massachusetts University): accused by some Black students of not displaying sufficient militancy -- demonstrating his moderate/integrationist stance

### The SGW Computer Centre Affair (1969) -- Context
- Spring 1968: six Caribbean students at SGWU filed complaint that biology professor Perry Anderson practiced racial discrimination
- Committee examined and rejected complaint on Jan 29, 1969
- ~200 demonstrators held sit-in at computer centre on 9th floor of Henry F. Hall Building
- Feb 11, 1969: police forcible intervention; $2M+ damage; 97 arrests; largest student riot in Canadian history
- Potter was **on sabbatical** during the affair (at Southeastern Massachusetts University)
- Potter's position as the sole Black faculty member in a historically white institution while maintaining a moderate stance on discrimination created a complex dynamic
