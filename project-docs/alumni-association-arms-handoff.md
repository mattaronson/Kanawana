## 1. What is being made, and for whom

The **Kamp Kanawana Alumni Association** is being formed. It needs a coat of arms,
usable as a formal achievement on charters and certificates and as a simplified
mark on pins, embroidery and a website header.

**Kamp Kanawana** is a YMCA of Montreal residential summer camp:

- Founded **1894** (as Camp Jubilee, at Lake Saint-Joseph)
- Operating continuously since **1910** at Saint-Sauveur, Quebec, in the Laurentians
- **550-acre site**, two lakes, Lake Kanawana and Lake Wilson
- The **oldest residential summer camp in Quebec** and among the oldest in Canada
- Motto: ***Non Nobis Solum***, "Not For Ourselves Alone"
- Bilingual. Alumni are **Kanawanians** in English and **Kanawanien(ne)s** in French
- The camp spells itself **KAMP** with a K. This is not a typo and must never be
  "corrected" to CAMP, including inside the monogram

The association's subject is not the camp, which has its own identity. Its subject
is what former campers carry away and hold in common. The Pip Alumni Award plaque
at camp puts it in the camp's own words: people who "represent the values of Non
Nobis Solum **in the world beyond camp**." That phrase is the brief.

## 2. Where every element in the design comes from

Nothing in this design is invented. Each charge is a real thing at this camp, and
a new session should not substitute, generalize or "improve" any of them.

### The crossed paddle and double-bitted axe

Quoted from the perpetual trophy of the **Lumbermen and Voyageur Games**, contested
every summer since **1947** in all but one season (2021), and still the emotional
peak of the camp year. The camp is split into two teams for the week. The **axe is
the Lumbermen's emblem** and the **paddle is the Voyageurs'**. Campers have made
model paddles and model axes to wear as team insignia since the 1940s.

The trophy itself is documented in the project wiki at
`wiki/traditions/lv-games.md`, under "The Annual Trophy", from photographs taken at
camp on 2026-08-14. The wiki records it as "a crossed **paddle and axe** over a
**red-painted backing**".

> **CAVEAT, carry this forward.** The wiki records a red *backing*. It does **not**
> record red on the paddle or green on the axe. The red paddle and green axe come
> from the operator, who has seen the object. That is good enough to design from,
> but it should be checked against the trophy before anything is struck in metal or
> cut as a die.

### The green triangle

Kanawana's own historic device. It appears as the title of the camp newsletter
(*The Green Triangle*, 1932 to 1982), as the name of a camper achievement award, in
the coda of the camp's marching cheer, and as a general marker of identity. The
closing banquet of the season is the **Green Triangle Banquet**.

**The point of the triangle is always DOWN.** This is recorded in the wiki at
`wiki/documents/green-triangle.md` under "Which way up", as fact `f_5834`, on the
operator's statement of 2026-09-24. An earlier draft of this emblem drew it
point-up and that was wrong.

The point-down orientation is also the **YMCA's own historic form**, familiar from
the First World War red triangle carrying spirit, mind and body on its three sides.
So Kanawana's green triangle is the parent body's device in the camp's colour.

> **UNCONFIRMED.** The orientation is oral history and has not been checked against
> any photographed badge, masthead or painted device. Open Question 4 in
> `green-triangle.md` names the check: the mastheads of the 38 digitized issues held
> in the project, the felt Kanawana Outing Club badge, the dining-hall boards.

### The torch

Not from the camp's material record. It is the design's own contribution: the thing
carried away and kept lit. It is the emotional centre of the composition and the
reason the association exists.

### The double K monogram

**KK** for Kamp Kanawana, with the **left K mirrored** so the two vertical stems meet
at the centre and the arms flare outward. Two versions were drawn; **Version B, with
the two stems merged into one shared vertical stroke, is the chosen one.** Version A
(two separate stems) reads as a different letter at small size and has been dropped.

### The crest: a maple leaf on a canoe

The **canoe replaces the torse**. In orthodox heraldry a crest sits on a torse, a
twisted roll lying horizontally across the top of the shield. A birchbark canoe seen
in profile has almost exactly the same proportion, so it substitutes without looking
like a gimmick, and it puts the camp's central object in the place reserved for what
the bearer is known by.

The **maple leaf** was the operator's choice and was adopted over a documented
objection (see section 9). It is rendered in **Torch gold, not red and not green**,
which neutralizes the three risks: a second red competing with the paddle blade, a
third green against the triangle and the axe haft, and the national-flag reading.
The gold also rhymes with the flame lower down.

## 4. What is STILL WRONG, and why

Three problems, one root cause. The root cause is that **the torch is doing too much
work vertically**.

### 4.1 The torch shaft splits the monogram

The shaft runs the full height of the shield and passes straight down **between the
two Ks**, so the monogram reads as two separate letters divided by a bar rather than
one joined glyph. It then continues down to divide **18** from **94**.

### 4.2 The maple leaf is lost behind the flame

The flame reaches up past the canoe and lands directly on top of the leaf, **in the
same gold, on the same axis, at the same value**. Two bright objects stacked. Both
are lost.

### 4.3 The canoe is about twice its specified width

Specified at 52 to 55% of shield width. Drawn at roughly full shield width with both
ends overhanging the sides, so it reads as a **crossbar** rather than a wreath. The
paddle blade and the axe head then both collide with it, and the top third of the
mark has four objects fighting in one horizontal strip.

### 4.4 Minor, also outstanding

- The **triangle's apex is buried** behind the monogram, so the point-down form only
  half reads
- The **date still sits inside the shield** instead of on the scroll's rolled ends

### The structural fix

**Let the canoe be the horizon.** Everything gold stays below it except the leaf,
which stays above it. Nothing overlaps. The canoe becomes the dividing line, which
solves 4.2 by construction rather than by nudging.

## 5. THE MASTER SPECIFICATION

This section is the authority. Anything earlier in the project that contradicts it
is superseded.

### 5.1 The shield

- **Shape:** a heater shield. Flat or gently curved top edge, straight vertical sides
  for the upper third, then curving in to a soft point on the central axis
- **Proportion:** height to width about 7:6
- **Outline:** one continuous closed path, one consistent stroke weight all the way
  round. A stroke that stops and restarts shows as a seam
- **Field:** plain. Laurentian night `#141E26`. No division, no ordinaries, no
  pattern, no water, no mountains, no sky, no texture
- **Stroke weight throughout the whole mark:** roughly 1/120 of the shield height

### 5.2 The charges, in draw order back to front

1. The inverted green triangle
2. The torch, behind the crossed implements
3. The paddle
4. The axe, in front of the paddle at the crossing
5. The monogram, in clear field, nothing passing through it

### 5.3 Geometry: the vertical stack

Measure from the **bottom of the shield**. Shield height = 100%.

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| Maple leaf | 105% to 145%, entirely above the canoe |
| Canoe (torse) | resting on the top edge at 100%, **width 55% of shield width** |
| Flame tip | **88% maximum**, clear beneath the canoe with a visible gap |
| Paddle blade tip | 82% maximum, clear of the canoe |
| Axe head top | 82% maximum, clear of the canoe |
| Torch bowl | 74% |
| Triangle's top edge | 72%, spanning about 74% of shield width |
| Saltire crossing | **52%. THE TORCH SHAFT ENDS HERE** |
| Triangle's apex | 34%, fully visible, nothing covering it |
| Monogram | 30% down to 14%, in clear unbroken field |
| Date | **not on the shield.** 18 and 94 on the scroll's rolled ends |

**The vertical axis governs everything.** Reading top to bottom it carries: leaf,
centre of the canoe, flame, torch bowl, saltire crossing, triangle apex, monogram,
shield point, centre of the scroll. All on one true centre line. The crest is the
most visible error if it drifts, because it sits alone against white.

**Overall proportion with the crest:** the full achievement should be roughly 1.6
times as tall as wide. If taller, reduce the crest, not the shield.

### 5.4 The triangle

Equilateral, **point DOWN**, floating. All three corners visible and clear of the
shield's edges. It must not touch the shield's top edge: a triangle that touches the
chief becomes a heraldic pile and reads as a different device. Solid fill, Kanawana
green `#15613C`.

In the **single-colour version** it is rendered as an **outlined** triangle, stroke
only, so it survives with no colour at all.

### 5.5 The paddle

Working in UNITS, where one unit = the shaft width. Total length 60 units. Four parts
on one straight centreline, top to bottom:

- **Blade**, 18 units long, 6 units at its widest. Long flat leaf shape, about 3:1.
  Widest at about 40% down from the tip, tapering to a rounded tip
- **Throat**, 4 units. Narrows from 6 units to 1 unit in a **concave** curve on both
  sides. No step, no corner, no collar. This is what makes it read as a paddle
- **Shaft**, 34 units, constant 1 unit wide, perfectly straight
- **Grip**, 4 units, at the bottom. Flared

**Colour:** blade solid Voyageur red `#B4282E`. Throat, shaft and grip Birchbark
cream `#F2E8D5`. **No red anywhere except the blade.**

### 5.6 The axe

Total length 60 units, matching the paddle. Same shaft width.

- **Haft:** straight, 1 unit wide, full length, Axe green `#4FA06A`
- **The haft passes THROUGH the head and protrudes 2 units above it.** This is what
  shows the head is mounted rather than balanced on the tip
- **Head:** span across both bits 11 units (5 bit, 1 haft, 5 bit). Height along the
  haft 7 units
- **Two bits, exact mirrors.** Each narrow at the eye (about 3 units tall), flaring
  outward and growing taller, ending in a convex curved cutting edge whose chord is
  7 units, roughly parallel to the haft. Fold the head down its centre line and the
  halves must match
- **Butt:** squared off. No pommel, no spike
- **Colour:** head Steel `#C7CBCE`, haft Axe green, continuous cream keyline around
  the whole outline of haft and head together

**The cream keyline is not decorative.** The axe haft is green and the triangle
behind it is green. The keyline plus the value difference between `#4FA06A` and
`#15613C` is what separates them. Test in greyscale.

### 5.7 The saltire

- Both implements the **same total length**, 60 units
- Cross on the central axis at **52% of shield height**
- Each at **35 degrees from vertical**, so the X opens at 70 degrees
- **Paddle** runs lower right to upper left: blade upper left, grip lower right
- **Axe** runs lower left to upper right: head upper right, butt lower left
- **The axe passes in front of the paddle** at the crossing, with a cream keyline
  where it overlaps
- Identical orientation in every variant

> **Note for the record:** this orientation is the mirror of the blazon originally
> written (section 6). The artwork was kept and the blazon follows it.

### 5.8 The torch

- Palewise, centred, behind the crossed implements
- Simple straight handle, flared collar, shallow bowl
- **Shaft ends at the saltire crossing, 52%. Nothing of the torch appears below it**
- Flame: a single confident tongue of fire with two or three inner lobes. Not a
  bonfire, not a cartoon fire, not a realistic fire
- **Flame tip stops at 88%, clearly below the canoe**
- **Colour:** flame Torch gold `#E9B93C`, inner lobes Ember orange `#DE7A21`,
  handle cream. Two flat tones. Fire reads through SHAPE, not blur

### 5.9 The monogram

- Two capital Ks, **left one mirrored**, stems meeting at centre, arms flaring outward
- **Version B:** the two stems **merged into one shared vertical stroke**, making a
  single symmetrical glyph with four arms flaring from a common spine
- Heavy, geometric, flat terminals. Not script, not calligraphic
- Birchbark cream `#F2E8D5`
- **Nothing passes through, in front of, or behind it**

### 5.10 The crest

**The canoe (in place of a torse):**

- Rests directly on the **top edge of the shield**, centred. Touches it, no gap
- **Length 55% of the shield's widest width.** Clearly narrower than the shield,
  with dark shield visible to the left and right of both ends
- Shallow symmetrical crescent, length to depth about 6:1, both ends turning slightly
  upward to soft points
- Detail, minimal: one gunwale line the length of the hull, three short vertical rib
  lines in the lower half. Nothing else. No thwarts, seats, paddles, paddlers, water
- **Colour:** hull Birchbark cream `#F2E8D5`, gunwale and ribs Ink `#14120F`

**The maple leaf:**

- Rises from the centre of the canoe, on the vertical axis, stem meeting the canoe's
  upper edge
- Height about 55% of the canoe's length
- **Naturalistic sugar maple**, five lobes, deeply cut sinuses, visible stem.
  **NOT the eleven-point stylized leaf of the Canadian flag**, which carries a
  national and governmental reading this organization is not entitled to
- **Colour:** Torch gold `#E9B93C`, Ink keyline, three or four simple vein lines
- **Entirely above the canoe.** No part descends past it

### 5.11 The motto scroll

- **Completely below the shield**, never across it
- Gap of about 3% of shield height between the shield's point and the scroll's top
  edge. A visible sliver of white. They must not touch
- Width including rolled ends about **105% of the shield's widest point**
- Shallow arc dipping at centre, two rolled ends turning back and upward, two fold
  shadows only
- **NON NOBIS SOLUM** on the flat centre, one line, small caps, generously
  letterspaced, Ink on Birchbark cream
- **18** on the left rolled end, **94** on the right

### 5.12 Palette, eight colours, exact

| Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Laurentian night | `#141E26` | shield field |
| Kanawana green | `#15613C` | the triangle |
| Axe green | `#4FA06A` | axe haft only |
| Voyageur red | `#B4282E` | paddle blade only |
| Torch gold | `#E9B93C` | flame, maple leaf |
| Ember orange | `#DE7A21` | flame inner lobes only |
| Steel | `#C7CBCE` | axe head |
| Birchbark cream | `#F2E8D5` | paddle shaft, canoe, keylines, monogram, scroll |
| Ink | `#14120F` | type and outlines |

No gradients. No drop shadows. No glows. No bevels. No textures except the two fold
shadows on the scroll.

### 5.13 Typography

- **Organization name:** a transitional or old-style serif with real weight in the
  stems. Caslon, Sabon or Freight Text in spirit. Not a slab serif, not a woodtype
  western face, not a geometric sans, not a script
- **Two lines:**
  ```
  KAMP KANAWANA
  ALUMNI ASSOCIATION | ASSOCIATION DES ANCIENS
  ```
  Thin vertical rule between the two halves of line two, French at the same size as
  the English. Keep an English-only variant as a separate version

### 5.14 The deliverable set

| Variant | Crest? | Scroll? | Date? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full achievement, colour | yes | yes | yes, on scroll |
| Full achievement, black on white | yes | yes | yes, on scroll |
| Shield only, colour | **no** | **no** | **no** |
| Shield only, black on white | **no** | **no** | **no** |
| Horizontal lockup, bilingual | **no** | **no** | **no** |
| Horizontal lockup, English only | **no** | **no** | **no** |

The crest and scroll belong to the full achievement and nothing else. They collapse
into mud at lockup size.

## 6. The blazon

Written to match the artwork as drawn:

> **Sable, upon a triangle inverted vert a double-headed axe and a canoe paddle in
> saltire, the axe hafted vert and headed argent, the paddle argent its blade gules,
> surmounting a torch palewise enflamed or; in base a monogram of two letters K
> addorsed argent. Crest: upon a canoe argent in place of a torse, a maple leaf or.
> Motto: NON NOBIS SOLUM.**

## 7. Hard prohibitions

These are not stylistic preferences.

### 7.1 No Indigenous or Indigenous-coded imagery

No headdresses, no totem poles, no tipis, no dreamcatchers, no "tribal" patterning,
no feathers, no arrowheads, no silhouetted figures in profile.

The camp's name is of **Algonquin origin** (so classed by the Commission de
toponymie du Québec, which does not know what it means), and the camp's own
historical programming included imitative "Indian lore", with an "Indians and
Settlers" vignette printed on its 1928 and 1941 site maps. **The association is not
continuing that.** The paddle and axe are present as fur-trade and forestry working
tools, not as ethnographic references.

Image models pull hard toward this given the camp's name. Expect it and refuse it.

### 7.2 The YMCA trademark

Do not reproduce, imitate or approximate the YMCA's red triangle or any current YMCA
wordmark. A **point-down triangle is close to the YMCA's own historic emblem**, which
makes this more important, not less. Keep it **green, never red**, never with words
along its three sides, never with a horizontal bar across it.

> **ACTION FOR THE OPERATOR:** a conversation with the YMCA about the green triangle
> is worth having before money is spent. The green triangle is genuinely Kanawana's
> own, attested in the newsletter title and the marching cheer, but an alumni
> association that is not the YMCA using a triangle device sits close to a live mark.

### 7.3 Everything else that is banned

No second paddle (it is one paddle and one axe, not crossed paddles). No
single-bitted axe. No canoe inside the shield. No water, waves, lakes, pine trees,
mountains, sun, compass rose, campfire, marshmallows, rope borders, laurel wreaths.
No supporters, no helm, no mantling, no coronet. No people, faces or hands. No mascot
animals, **in particular no owl**. No 3D rendering, no photographic elements, no
watercolour, no distressed or "vintage worn" texture, no halftone. Nothing that
resembles a craft-brewery label or an outdoor-apparel logo.

## 8. Process lessons, learned the expensive way

A new session should read these before sending anything to an image tool.

### 8.1 The image tool draws a picture of a style sheet, not the assets

Asked for a contact sheet of seven variants plus a palette panel, it produced a
palette panel that said "eight colours" and showed four, and labels reading "Small
small (Colour)", "Lawrentian night", "Ember orangs", "one shaft stroke". Those are
not typos in a deliverable. They are evidence the model is painting an impression of
a style guide and spending its attention on layout rather than on the shapes.

**Ask for ONE artwork per generation, large, on plain white, with no labels, no
palette panel and no other variants.** Build the mark one piece at a time.

### 8.2 Crop and magnify before judging

This session wrongly called the axe single-bitted and the paddle "baseball bat like",
**three rounds running**, from thumbnails on a contact sheet. Cropping the three
shields out and viewing them at three times size showed both were correct and had
been for a round. Two rounds of correction were wasted on a misread.

**Before any critique: crop each artwork out individually and upscale it.** The
operator caught this and was right.

### 8.3 Describing a shape in words does not work. Give construction geometry

"A canoe paddle" produced a lollipop and then a baseball bat. What worked was units,
named parts, ratios and explicit landmarks: blade 18 units by 6, throat 4 units
flaring concave from 1 to 6, shaft 34 units. Same for the axe.

### 8.4 Make the model check its own work

Asking it to answer questions about its own drawing before delivering ("where is the
widest point of the blade, as a percentage down from the tip?") catches errors it
otherwise ships.

### 8.5 The end state is vector, not JPEG

The geometry in section 5 is specific enough that a person with Illustrator or
Inkscape open would assemble it in an afternoon. **Once the pieces are right, have
them redrawn as vector paths and assembled by hand**, so the association ends up
with real SVGs rather than a raster of a style guide. Embroidery, die-striking and
vinyl all need vector anyway.
