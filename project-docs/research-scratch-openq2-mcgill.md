## Summary of Findings

### What was the McGill Camping School?

Based on the evidence gathered, two distinct but possibly related "camp school" programs existed at McGill:

1. **The QCA/CCA Camping School at McGill (1937-1940)**: A training program organized by the Quebec Camping Association (a chartered unit of the Canadian Camping Association, both founded 1936-1937). Records are held in Concordia University Archives, P0145/12N03 (YMCA of Montreal fonds, Quebec Section / Canadian Camping Association). This was one of the earliest formal training programs for camp leaders in Canada. The program ran for four years, ending in 1940 (possibly due to WWII). A related "camping evening course" at McGill was held in 1952, suggesting the concept was revived after the war.

2. **McGill School of Physical Education "Camp School"**: A curricular component of McGill's School of Physical Education (founded 1912, directed by Arthur Lamb 1919-1956). Alumni recall a week at Camp Nominingue (founded 1925, private boys' camp in Tremblant area) as part of their PE training, where they learned camping, paddling, portaging, axe use, and "vacation camp life from all perspectives." Alumni Ted Wall is remembered for an axe demonstration accident at "camp school." **The dates for this program are not precisely established** but alumni from the 1940s-1950s reference it. Given that the School of Physical Education's administrative records date from ca. 1930-1948, this camp school component may have existed throughout the 1930s-1950s.

### Connection between the two programs

The two programs may be related or overlapping:
- Both were at McGill in the late 1930s
- The QCA camping school (1937-1940) could have been hosted within or affiliated with the School of Physical Education
- Arthur Lamb's YMCA background (Vancouver YMCA, Springfield College) connects McGill PE to the YMCA camping tradition
- The CCA/QCA was founded at the Central YMCA and had strong YMCA institutional ties
- The YMCA of Montreal fonds at Concordia holds records of both programs

### Who may have been involved?

- **Arthur S. Lamb** (1886-1958): Director of McGill Physical Education 1919-1956. Former Vancouver YMCA PE director. Springfield College graduate (1912). Most likely institutional sponsor of any camping school at McGill.
- **Hay Finlay**: 1922 Kanawana Senior Section director, described as "ex Central YMCA Boys Physical Director, now McGill Phys Ed." Direct documented link between Kanawana staff and McGill PE as early as 1922.
- **Nelson McEwen**: YMCA Boys' Work Secretary, directed Camp Kanawana c. 1927-1947. Co-founded CCA. Correspondence 1941-1945 in Concordia fonds. May have been involved in organizing the McGill camping school through his CCA role.
- **Hedley G. Dimock**: Conducted research at Kanawana in 1960-1961. Dean of George Williams College, ran Summer Camp Institutes 1930-1948. Adjunct at McGill (among 8 universities). Possible connection to the 1937-1940 program but more likely involved later.

### Kanawana connection

The connection to Kanawana is circumstantial but strong:
- The camping school records are in the YMCA of Montreal fonds, alongside extensive Kanawana records
- The QCA was a CCA charter unit with strong YMCA institutional ties
- Nelson McEwen directed Kanawana and co-founded the CCA in the same era
- Hay Finlay was simultaneously at McGill PE and Kanawana staff (1922)
- Kanawana was the primary YMCA camp in Montreal — any YMCA-affiliated camp training would naturally involve Kanawana
- Dimock's later (1960-1961) research was conducted at Kanawana

### New facts discovered

1. **NEW**: The Concordia archives (P0145/12N03) also contain records of a "camping evening course at McGill University" from 1952, indicating the camping school concept was revived after WWII in a different format.
2. **NEW**: Arthur S. Lamb worked at the Vancouver YMCA before McGill and trained at Springfield College (the YMCA training school), establishing a direct YMCA-to-McGill pipeline for physical education and camping traditions.
3. **NEW**: McGill PE alumni recall a "camp school" component at Camp Nominingue as part of their PE training, involving wilderness skills (axe use, camping, paddling, portaging) and "vacation camp life from all perspectives."
4. **CONFIRMED**: McGill School of Physical Education administrative records ca. 1930-1948 (35 cm) exist in McGill Archives, including A.S. Lamb's correspondence and speeches.
5. **CONFIRMED**: Hay Finlay (Kanawana 1922 staff) was at McGill Physical Education, providing the earliest documented Kanawana-McGill PE link.
6. **CONTEXT**: Universities across North America began offering formal camping/PE/recreation curriculum in the 1930s. Dimock's George Williams College institutes (1930-1948) and L.B. Sharp's Columbia Teachers College programs are parallels to whatever McGill offered.
