## 1. The Centennial Voyageur Canoe Pageant -- Core Facts

- **Dates:** May 24 to September 4, 1967 (104 days)
- **Route:** Rocky Mountain House, Alberta to Expo 67, Montreal, Quebec
- **Distance:** 3,283 miles on water + 60 miles portaging
- **Teams:** 10 (8 provinces + Yukon + NWT). PEI and Newfoundland did not enter.
- **Total paddlers:** ~100 men (approximately 10 per team, with 6-man daily shifts)
- **All paddlers were male.** At least 13 were First Nations/Metis/Inuit (over 13%).
- **Organizer:** Reg Fife, under the Centennial Commission (est. 1963, disbanded ~1968)
- **Starter/finisher:** Judy LaMarsh
- **Guinness record:** Certified as the longest canoe race ever held (still holds as of 2012)
- **Prize:** $2,500 per paddler for the winning team; $1,000 base pay per man who completed the trip; $500 per man for 2nd and 3rd place

## 2. Canoe Specifications

This is a significant finding:

- **Original design:** The Chestnut Canoe Company built 12 wood-canvas canoes in 1966 for trials. Dimensions: 25 feet long, 51 inches wide, 19 inches deep, ~235 lbs dry.
- **Problem:** The wood-canvas Chestnut canoes did not hold up to race treatment during 1966 trial runs.
- **Replacement:** The Centennial Commission contracted **Moise Cadorette** of Grand-Mere (north of Trois-Rivieres), Quebec to build fiberglass versions. These were much heavier -- approximately **400 lbs** -- but far more durable.
- **All 10 race canoes in 1967 were Cadorette fiberglass voyageur canoes.**
- The surplus Chestnut wood-canvas prototypes were acquired by Kirk Wipper of Camp Kandalore.
- The modern Clipper Canoes 25-foot Voyageur model is based on the Centennial canoe design.

## 4. Don Starkell

- Only Manitoba team member with no Flin Flon connection (he was from Winnipeg)
- Pierre Berton's book *1967: The Last Good Year* (1997) includes a chapter on the race, relying heavily on Starkell's memories
- Starkell went on to paddle from Winnipeg to the Amazon (1980-1982) with his sons -- 19,603 km, a Guinness World Record for longest canoe journey
- Their canoe "Orellana" is on display at the Canadian Canoe Museum, Peterborough
- He attempted to kayak the Northwest Passage in 1990, losing fingertips and toes to frostbite
- Died in 2012, aged 79
- Inducted individually into MB Sports Hall of Fame in 2006; the full team was inducted in 2010

## 5. Misao Dean's Academic Article

- **Title:** "The Centennial Voyageur Canoe Pageant as Historical Re-enactment"
- **Published:** *Journal of Canadian Studies/Revue d'etudes canadiennes*, Vol. 40, No. 3, Fall 2006, pp. 43-67
- **Available at:** [Project MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/213219/summary) and [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/11558345/)
- **Argument:** The pageant was a state-sponsored spectacle designed to mobilize a nationalist historiography portraying voyageurs as "the founders of Canada," creating an illusion of collective identity while ignoring racial and gender dynamics
- **Book expansion:** Chapter 4 of *Inheriting a Canoe Paddle: The Canoe in Discourses of English-Canadian Nationalism* (University of Toronto Press, 2012)
- **Author:** Misao Dean, Professor of English, University of Victoria

## 6. The CCA Centenary Journey (Separate Event)

This is an important discovery -- a **parallel but distinct** 1967 centennial canoe project:

- **Organizer:** Canadian Camping Association (CCA) and its provincial affiliates
- **Concept:** A series of geographically connected (but not necessarily temporally connected) self-sustained canoe voyages across Canada, covering traditional canoe routes coast to coast
- **Participants:** Youth camps using older campers supervised by canoe-tripping staff. Included YMCA, YWCA, Guides, Scouts, and private camps.
- **Scale:** Reportedly 400,000 youths participated across the country
- **Each camp was assigned a section** of the route to complete during summer 1967
- **Lake Superior gap:** No youth camp volunteered for the dangerous Lake Superior section. **Kirk Wipper** (owner of Camp Kandalore) volunteered his Kandalore staff alumni ("Nor'westers") to cover it. He provided two Chestnut 25-foot wood-canvas canoes (surplus Centennial canoes), truck, trailer, camping gear, and food.
- **Fort Frances to Winnipeg YMCA:** During summer 1967, senior Camp Kandalore boys covered a portion of the Centennial canoe route between Fort Frances and the Winnipeg YMCA -- this is a direct YMCA connection.
- The 50th anniversary celebration was held in Peterborough, Ontario, September 9-10, 2017.
- A related article: Greg Brown, "Memories of the Centenary Journey," *Kanawa* magazine, Spring 2007 -- note that "Kanawa" is the CCA's magazine.

## 7. Camp Kanawana Connection

**No direct link found** between Camp Kanawana (YMCA Quebec) and either the Voyageur Canoe Pageant or the CCA Centenary Journey. However:

- The CCA Centenary Journey explicitly involved YMCA camps across Canada, and sections were assigned to various camps. It is plausible that a Quebec YMCA camp (possibly Kanawana) participated in a Quebec section, but no specific evidence was found online.
- Camp Kanawana's current "Voyageurs" canoe-tripping program (for ages 13-16) shares the voyageur theme but no documented historical connection to the 1967 events.
- The CCA Centenary Journey's section between Fort Frances and the Winnipeg YMCA was completed by Camp Kandalore boys -- "Kandalore" is sometimes confused with "Kanawana" but they are distinct camps (Kandalore is in Haliburton, Ontario).

## 10. Reunions and Legacy

- **1992:** ~1/3 of the 100 paddlers gathered at Rocky Mountain House for the 25th anniversary
- **2017:** 22 surviving voyageurs joined a 50th anniversary celebration at the nation's capital
- **2010:** Manitoba team inducted into Manitoba Sports Hall of Fame
- The Guysborough contingent of the Nova Scotia team was inducted into the local Wall of Fame at the Chedabucto Lifestyle Centre
