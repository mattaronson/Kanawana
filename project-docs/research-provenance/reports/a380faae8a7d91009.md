## Camp Oolahwan -- Research Findings

### Founding and Location

- **Founded:** 1917 by **Mary Susanna Edgar** (1889-1973), on behalf of the YWCA Montreal
- **Location:** Lake Walfred (Lac Walfred), Sainte-Marguerite-du-Lac-Masson, Laurentian Mountains, Quebec -- approximately 45 minutes north of Montreal
- **Operated:** 1917 to 2005 (88 years)
- **Described as:** The longest sustained initiative in YWCA Montreal history; described by the YWCA as "the first summer camp for girls in Canada" (though Camp Northway in Ontario predates it to 1908, Oolahwan may have been the first YWCA girls' camp in Canada)
- **Logo:** An owl

### The Founder -- Mary Susanna Edgar

- Born May 23, 1889 in Sundridge, Ontario; died September 17, 1973
- Daughter of Joseph Edgar and Mary Little
- Educated at Sundridge Public School, Barrie High School, Havergal Ladies College (Toronto), University of Chicago extension courses, Teachers' College at Columbia University
- Graduated from the National Training School of the YWCA, New York City, in 1915
- YWCA Girls' Work Secretary in Montreal, 1915-1919; simultaneously Director of Camp Oolahwan
- In 1920, purchased property on Lake Bernard near Sundridge and founded **Glen Bernard Camp** (opened 1922, which she directed until 1956)
- Author of *Wood-fire and Candlelight* (1945), *Under Open Skies* (1956), *The Christmas Wreath of Verse* (1967), *Once there was a Camper* (1970)
- Wrote the hymn **"God Who Touchest Earth with Beauty"** (published 1925), which won first prize in an American Camping Association contest and has been translated into multiple languages including Cree, appearing in hymnals worldwide
- Archival fonds held at **Trent University Archives** (fonds 82-010)

### Programming and Mission

- English-speaking residential summer camp exclusively for girls and young women
- Mission: developing self-confidence, self-esteem, healthy minds and bodies, leadership skills, and lasting friendships
- Activities included: sports, nature/environmental education, campfire programs, singing, skill-building activities
- Designed to provide affordable summer vacations for female workers and girls
- The YWCA's philosophy (from 1915): "for a young woman to be successful at her job, she must gain self-confidence and have the right to laugh and have fun"

### Notable Staff

- **Mary Susanna Edgar** -- Founder and first Director (1917-1919)
- **Ferna G. Halliday** -- Responsible for young girls at Camp Oolahwan (1918-1921); YWCA Montreal secretary. Later co-founded **Camp Ouareau** (1922) and directed **Camp Oconto** (1924)
- **Gail Small** -- Director of Camp Oolahwan (dates unspecified); started as Director of Health and Physical Education at the YWCA, later held multiple community leadership positions

### Closure (2005)

- Camp Oolahwan closed in 2005. The specific reason for closure was not explicitly stated in any source found, but the YWCA replaced it with **Summer Space Day Camp**, an urban day camp for teenagers aged 10-13, "sharing the same objectives of Camp Oolahwan, but in an urban day camp environment."
- No explicit financial or logistical reason for closure was found in public sources.

### Property After Closure

- **In 2006**, the former camp property on Lac Walfred became the site of a resort development by **Habitations Germat** and **Timber Block Homes**
- The development became **Club Lac Walfred**, a luxury cottage resort with 12 cottages, which opened in late 2015
- The **Association du Lac Walfred** (property owners' association) adopted an owl for its logo "in memory of Camp Oolahwan, whose logo was also an owl"
- Some physical traces of the camp remain on the grounds: a hiking trail, bird feeder, volleyball court, and a main building with cafeteria

### Centennial Reunion (2017)

- Originally planned for **August 25-27, 2017**; appears to have shifted to **September 8-10, 2017**
- Held at **Camp Ouareau** in St-Donat, Quebec (a fitting venue given the Oolahwan-Ouareau historical connection through Ferna Halliday)
- Organized by the **Camp Oolahwan Centennial Committee**, composed of former campers and counselors
- Had a **Twitter account** (@campoolahwan) and Facebook event page
- Covered by **CBC Radio** (audio segment) and **Quebec Chronicle Telegraph**
- Former campers and counselors shared memories and photos

### Connections to Other Camps

- **Camp Ouareau** (est. 1922): Direct lineage -- Ferna Halliday worked at Oolahwan 1918-1921, then co-founded Ouareau in 1922 with Mabel Jamieson. Ouareau hosted the Oolahwan centennial reunion. Both were girls-only camps with roots in Montreal's YWCA community.
- **Camp Oconto** (est. 1924, Tichborne, Ontario): Ferna Halliday left Ouareau to direct this camp.
- **Glen Bernard Camp** (est. 1922, Lake Bernard, Ontario): Founded by Mary Edgar herself after her Oolahwan years. Operated as a private girls' camp.
- **Camp Kanawana** (est. 1894): No direct institutional connection found. Kanawana was YMCA (boys/co-ed), Oolahwan was YWCA (girls). Both were Montreal Y-movement camps in the Laurentians operating in overlapping decades, but no shared staff or joint programming was found in the sources.

### What Remains Unknown

- **Capacity:** No source specified the number of campers per session or total site capacity
- **Specific reason for 2005 closure:** Financial pressures, infrastructure costs, declining enrollment, or strategic YWCA decision -- none of these was confirmed
- **Name origin:** "Oolahwan" likely derives from an Indigenous word; the closest match found is the Lakota Sioux word *olowan* meaning "song" or "to sing," but this is not confirmed
- **Detailed programming specifics:** No source provided a schedule, section system, or activity catalog
- **Buildings and infrastructure:** Only mentions of a main building with cafeteria, hiking trail, volleyball court, and bird feeder (from the post-closure property description)
- **Connection to Camp Weredale:** Not found in any source
