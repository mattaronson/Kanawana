## Summary of Cultural Elements Research

### Songs

**Richard Kerr and "On My Way to Kanawana":** Complete dead end online. The only Richard Kerr who appears in web results is the English pop songwriter (1944-2023) who co-wrote "Mandy" for Barry Manilow -- no connection to camps or YMCAs. The Kanawana-specific Richard "Itch/Itche" Kerr has zero web presence. The song title "On My Way to Kanawana" returns nothing; the Concordia Archives CD (P145/SR0001) remains the sole known copy of this recording. Every search instead surfaces the gospel standard "I'm On My Way to Canaan Land" or the theme song from *Salute Your Shorts* ("Camp Anawanna").

**The Kanawana Marching Song:** The Spirit of Kanawana blog remains the only online source documenting this. The blog confirms the song uses the "Hail, hail, the gang's all here" refrain, which derives from the 1915 American song "Alabama Jubilee" (music by George L. Cobb, words by Jack Yellen), itself set to a melody from Sullivan's *Pirates of Penzance*. Lost verses were rediscovered in the Concordia archives in 2006. No other web source independently documents this song.

**YMCA Song Tradition Context:** The broader YMCA singing tradition is well documented. The YMCA published a formal Association Hymn Book as early as 1904 (the only YMCA collection on IMSLP), a Service Song Book for WWI soldiers, and standardized Camp Songs Books by 1938 (a lot of seven from that year appeared on eBay). The Library of Congress holds the Cooperative Recreation Service collection at the American Folklife Center, which includes camp songbooks. None of these collections contain Kanawana-specific material.

### Cheers and Section Names

**Section cheers** are confirmed as a standard YMCA practice. Camp Sloane YMCA publishes a village cheers and chants document organized by section (Pioneer Girls, Junior Girls, Senior Girls, etc.), which parallels what we know about Kanawana's section cheer tradition. No Kanawana-specific cheer texts were found online.

**Current section names** confirmed from the YMCA Quebec website: Pioneers (girls/non-binary, 7-12), Woodsmen (boys/non-binary, 7-12), Coureurs des Bois (boys/non-binary, 13-16), Pathfinders (girls/non-binary, 13-16), plus Rangers, Voyageurs Ultimate, Trailblazers, Foresters, Adventurers, Explorers, Senior Kanawanians, and the Mountaineer program (an all-gender tent for transgender, non-binary, and gender non-conforming campers). The specific names "Scouts," "Colonists," and "Rovers" that were searched for did not appear at any YMCA camp.

### The Chopsy Legend

**Total blank.** Six targeted searches using "Chopsy," "Chopsey," and "Chopsie" combined with camp, ghost, Quebec, Kanawana, legend, and axe murderer returned zero relevant results. The name does not appear in any folklore archive, academic database, Reddit thread, blog post, or camp ghost story collection. The hypothesis that Chopsy is a Kanawana-local adaptation of the Cropsey/maniac-in-the-woods archetype remains plausible but entirely unconfirmed. Oral history from alumni is the only viable path forward.

### Traditions and Cultural Context

**The L&V Games** at Kanawana are confirmed as a 3-day event (versus 2 days at Pine Crest, where the tradition originated in 1940). A former staff member's blog describes the Capitaine role and the intensity of the competition. Pine Crest's games are well documented: Voyageurs carry a paddle, Lumbermen carry an axe, random team assignment each summer, secret winner-determination rules sworn since the 1970s. The book *Lumbermen & Voyageurs: The YMCA Pine Crest Story* covers 100 years of that camp but is not digitized.

**Stuart McLean** is the most quotable voice on Kanawana's campfire culture. He described Kanawana as "a microcosm of Canada, where English, French, rich, poor, recent immigrants and locals sing together around the camp fire," and credited camp with transforming him from "an utter academic, athletic and social failure" into someone with confidence.

**Concordia Archives holdings** confirmed via the finding aid: Green Triangle newsletters (1932-1940), KA-News and later Green Triangle issues (1976, 1978-1982), plus brochures, photographs, promotional materials, and t-shirt artwork from the 1920s through the 1990s. This is the richest untapped source for cultural elements.

**Canadian camping academic context** expanded: the Canadian Summer Camp Research Project (CCA/University of Waterloo, 2006-2011) interviewed 50+ directors and identified five developmental areas. Taylor Statten's Camp Ahmek (1921) traces directly to his 1905 role as YMCA Boys' Work Secretary. YMCA Camp Waabanaki changed its name in 2023 for Indigenous reconciliation.
