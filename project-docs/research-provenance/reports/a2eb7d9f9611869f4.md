## YMCA Montreal: Founding and Early History

- **Founded: November 25, 1851** -- the first YMCA in North America, predating Boston (December 29, 1851) by about one month.
- The founding meeting was held at **St. Helen Street Baptist Church** in Old Montreal.
- Inspired by pamphlets distributed at the 1851 London World's Fair to visiting Montrealers.
- **J.H. Winn** was elected first president a few months after the founding meeting.
- In **1853**, the association rented its first space at **Odd Fellows' Hall on St. James Street**, offering a library, reading room, lectures, employment services, and charitable relief.
- The YMCA's Canadian national administrative headquarters were in Montreal until the formation of the **National Council of YMCAs of Canada in 1912**, when headquarters moved to Toronto.
- North American YMCAs formed a confederation in **1854**; the World Alliance of YMCAs formed in **1855**.

## Physical Locations Timeline

1. **1851**: Founding meeting at St. Helen Street Baptist Church
2. **1853**: First rented space, Odd Fellows' Hall, St. James Street
3. **1873**: First purpose-built YMCA building erected on **Victoria Square** -- also the year evening educational courses began (French and shorthand)
4. **1891**: Moved to a new building facing **Dominion Square** (site of present Sun Life Building); reportedly the tallest building in the British Empire at the time. Facilities included meeting rooms, library, reading room, gymnasium, swimming pool, bowling alley, dining room.
5. **1912**: Moved to **Drummond Street** -- new facility built at a cost of $540,206. Housed library, bowling alley, billiard room, dining room. This is where the educational programs grew dramatically.
6. **1931**: Drummond Street building remodeled; a **500-room residential annex** added for low-cost accommodation during the Great Depression.
7. **1966**: Sir George Williams University's **Henry F. Hall Building** constructed, moving classes out of the YMCA building.
8. **Current**: The Downtown YMCA building is being put up for sale, with plans to create a new community hub in the same neighbourhood.

## Relationship to Sir George Williams University / Concordia

- **1873**: YMCA inaugurated evening courses in vocational and general education for working people.
- **1926**: Education program reorganized as **Sir George Williams College**, named after YMCA founder George Williams.
- **1934**: First undergraduate credit course in adult education in Canada offered.
- **1948**: Received university charter from Quebec provincial government, becoming **Sir George Williams University** (still the education arm of the Montreal YMCA).
- **1956**: Expanded into first standalone building (Norris Building).
- **Late 1960s**: SGWU severed financial and organizational ties with the YMCA.
- **August 24, 1974**: Merged with **Loyola College** to create **Concordia University** (name from Montreal's motto, *Concordia salus*).
- The YMCA is credited with founding three Canadian universities: **Concordia** (Montreal), **Carleton** (Ottawa, 1942), and **York** (Toronto, 1959).

## Organizational Evolution: YMCA of Montreal to YMCAs du Quebec

The name transition was gradual rather than a single merger event:

- The organization operated as the **YMCA of Montreal** from 1851 through most of the 20th century.
- By the **1970s**, a shift in Quebec government policy meant increased emphasis on community recreational programs; the YMCA began providing input and management services for these.
- **1970**: The YMCA opened all services to all Quebeckers regardless of religion or language, transforming from a Protestant English-speaking organization to a fully bilingual one.
- **1979**: Archival records reference a "Quebec City YMCA-Montreal YMCA management agreement" and a "Management Service Agreement between the YMCAs of Quebec and Montreal" (1979-1981), suggesting organizational integration between Montreal and Quebec City YMCAs.
- **1990s**: Canadian YMCAs nationally dropped "Young Men's Christian Association" from their branding.
- **~2009**: The Concordia archives note the Montreal YMCA was renamed **"Le Y du Quebec"** as of 2009, confirming the formal name change.
- The organization now operates as **Les YMCA du Quebec / The YMCAs of Quebec** (ymcaquebec.org).

## Current Organizational Structure (YMCAs du Quebec)

**Governance:**
- Managed by a **Board of Directors** of 13 members, elected at the Annual General Meeting for 2-year terms (max 10 consecutive years).
- Board committees: Audit, Human Resources, Governance.
- **Local Advisory Councils (LACs)** provide advice to each centre director.
- Partner Members (active volunteers or donors of $100+ in 2 of last 3 years) have voting rights at the AGM.
- Separate **YMCA of Quebec Foundation** with its own Board of Directors.

**Current Leadership (as of early 2025):**
- **President & CEO**: Pascale Audette (appointed November 2024; first woman in this role)
- **Board Chair**: Francois Tremblay
- **VP Operations**: Eric Trudel
- **VP Finance and Administration**: Michel Rheault
- **VP Human Resources, Strategy and Performance**: Christine Lapointe-Lagueux
- **VP Real Estate Management and IT**: Michael Johnston
- **VP Philanthropic Development**: Alexandre Lebel

**Current Facilities (as of 2025):**
- **9 YMCA centres** across Quebec (including Downtown Montreal, Cartierville, du Parc, Westmount, Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, Saint-Laurent, Pointe-Saint-Charles, Saint-Roch/Quebec City, West Island/Pointe-Claire)
- **YMCA Camp Kanawana** (Saint-Sauveur)
- **International Language School** (closing December 2025 after 60 years due to financial pressures)
- **Residence** (the Drummond Street annex)
- **36 service points** across the province
- Serves 120,000+ people annually

**2019 Transformation:**
- Major strategic restructuring announced November 2019
- Shift toward community programs and away from large fitness facilities
- Guy-Favreau, Hochelaga-Maisonneuve centres closed December 2019
- New partnership model with municipalities (Cartierville, du Parc, Saint-Roch)
- Saint-Roch YMCA in Quebec City opened 2020
- West Island YMCA sold to City of Pointe-Claire in 2021
- Downtown YMCA building being put up for sale
- Day camps discontinued as of fall 2025 (Kanawana summer camp continues)

## Camps Within the YMCA System

- **1894**: "Billy" Ball of the YMCA of Montreal took boys camping on an island in Lake St. Joseph -- Quebec's first residential summer camp (Camp Jubilee, 20 campers).
- The YMCA purchased the island plus two others for leadership training.
- The YMCA later acquired the Saint-Sauveur site from the Page family, establishing **Camp Kanawana**.
- Historically operated multiple camps: **Kamp Kanawana**, **Camp Perrot** (compared in a ~1945-46 study), **Camp Otoreke**, and **Camp Weredale** (proposed two-site operation with Kanawana in 1977).
- Today only **Camp YMCA Kanawana** remains operational (550 acres, 3 lakes, Saint-Sauveur).
- Camp motto: "Non Nobis Solum -- Not for ourselves alone."
- Kanawana celebrated 131 years in 2025.

## Key Early Figures

- **J.H. Winn**: First president of the Montreal YMCA (elected shortly after founding in 1851).
- **T. James Claxton**: His father was directly involved in the 1851 founding; T. James chronicled the founding history (letter to G.W. Birks, 1944).
- **D.A. Budge**: Important early figure; authored "The Early Beginning of the YMCA in Montreal, Canada."
- **"Billy" Ball**: YMCA of Montreal staff who launched the camping program in 1894.
- **Harry Ballantyne**: Authored "A Short History of the Canadian Y.M.C.A. 1851-1944" (available on Internet Archive).
- **Ralph Dawson**: Authored "History of Kamp Kanawana" (1933).

## Key Archival Sources

- **Concordia University Archives**: Holds the YMCA of Montreal fonds (P0145), including Metropolitan Board of Directors records, directors/presidents sub-series, camping records.
- **Kautz Family YMCA Archives** at the University of Minnesota Libraries.
