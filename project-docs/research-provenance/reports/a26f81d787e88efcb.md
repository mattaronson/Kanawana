## 2. LONGHOUSE

**Status: No online information found.**

No web sources specifically reference a building called "Longhouse" at Camp Kanawana. However, the McMorris thesis (2023) discusses "playing Indian" as one of three key thematic phases of Kanawana programming, alongside "active Christian citizenship" and "the myth of the voyageur." Given that the thesis discusses the camp's adoption of Indigenous imagery (totem pole, "Indian Grave" on maps, Indian characters on maps, Council Ring ceremonies), it is highly likely that a longhouse structure existed as part of this programming, but this could not be confirmed from publicly available online sources.

**Sources:** [McMorris thesis abstract](https://spectrum.library.concordia.ca/id/eprint/992763/) | [Concordia archives - Kamp Kanawana](https://concordia.accesstomemory.org/kamp-kanawana)

## 10. COUNCIL RING

**Status: CONFIRMED -- Historical structure, rich documented history. Current status unclear.**

This is one of the best-documented structures, primarily from the McMorris thesis (src_mcmorris_thesis) and the 1923 Gas Bag (src_gas_bag_1923):

- **Built 1922:** The Council Ring was built by senior campers in 1922 (KB fact f_0231).
- **Location:** Located between the cabins and the chapel (KB fact f_0232).
- **1923 ceremony:** The 1923 season closing featured a Council Ring ceremony where a record of activities was buried, followed by a Leaders Grand Feed (KB fact f_0323).
- **1927 additions:** Under Harold Cross, a totem pole was added to the Council Ring in 1927 (KB fact f_0233). A teepee was built from old canvas at the Council Ring in 1927 (KB fact f_0234).
- **1929 rebuild:** The Council Ring was rebuilt in 1929 to seat 325 people (KB fact f_0235).
- **Origins:** Ernest Thompson Seton created the "Indian Council Ring" concept, along with "traditional" dances, sweat lodges, and Indian crafts programs (KB fact f_0264). This was part of the broader camping movement's "playing Indian" tradition.
- **Current:** The camp currently has "fire bowls for campfires and cookouts" listed in its facilities, but the specific term "Council Ring" is not used on the current YMCA website. Whether the historical Council Ring structure still exists in its original or rebuilt form is unclear from online sources.

**Sources:** McMorris thesis (Concordia) | 1923 Gas Bag | [YMCA Quebec - Facilities](https://www.ymcaquebec.org/en/summer-camp-kanawana/facilities)

## 11. DINING HALL (Cafeteria)

**Status: CONFIRMED -- Still standing, active use.**

- The current YMCA website refers to it interchangeably as "dining hall" and "cafeteria."
- Capacity of over 300 people (KB fact f_0471).
- Contains an "industrial kitchen" that is off-limits to rental groups.
- **1922:** A "Dining Pavilion" was listed among new facilities in 1922 (KB fact f_0303, src_brochure_1922). This is the earliest documented dining structure.
- **1935:** The 1935 history mentions "2 pavilions" among camp facilities.
- **1950:** By 1950, a Lumberman-Voyageur mural was in the Kanawana dining hall (KB fact f_0196).
- **1923:** A song parodying the dining hall ice cream policy was published in the Gas Bag (KB fact f_0509).

Whether the current dining hall is the original 1922 structure, a replacement, or a renovation is not documented in online sources.

**Sources:** [YMCA Quebec - Facilities](https://www.ymcaquebec.org/en/summer-camp-kanawana/facilities) | 1922 Brochure (Concordia archives) | [YMCA Quebec - Lodging](https://www.ymcaquebec.org/en/summer-camp-kanawana/lodging-equipment-rental)

## 12. CHAPEL (Outdoor Chapel)

**Status: CONFIRMED historically. Current status unclear from online sources.**

- **1922:** The 1922 brochure describes "Sunday services in the open-air chapel and around the fire" as part of the religious program (KB fact f_0528).
- **1935:** The open-air chapel held its first service of the 1935 season on June 30 (KB fact f_0130).
- **1938:** Camp Kanawana had an outdoor chapel with an organized choir in 1938 (KB fact f_0142). Benny Leshley (organist of Christ Church Cathedral) organized the choir to lead singing (KB fact f_0517).
- **Location:** The Council Ring was located "between the cabins and the chapel" (KB fact f_0232).
- **Current:** The chapel is not mentioned on the current YMCA website's facilities pages. This may indicate it is no longer a named or active feature, or it may simply not be listed for rental/marketing purposes.

**Sources:** 1922 Brochure | 1935 History | 1938 Green Triangle | McMorris thesis

### Superintendent's House (destroyed by fire, 2023)

- On **May 16, 2023**, a fire completely destroyed the "maison du surintendant" (superintendent's house) at Camp Kanawana.
- The building was isolated from the rest of the campers' area, located on Montee Sainte-Elmire in the Spring Valley sector.
- A passerby called firefighters at 6 AM. When firefighters arrived, the blaze was generalized and part of the building had collapsed.
- 25 firefighters from Saint-Sauveur, Morin-Heights, and Sainte-Anne-des-Lacs responded.
- No injuries (camp was unoccupied). Cause undetermined. Total loss.
- The YMCA stated only one building was affected, and the camp would open as planned that summer.

**Sources:** [Journal Acces - Un batiment du Camp YMCA Kanawana ravage par les flammes](https://www.journalacces.ca/actualite/un-batiment-du-camp-ymca-kanawana-ravage-par-les-flammes/) | [CIME FM - Le camp YMCA Kanawana ravage par les flammes](https://laurentides.cime.fm/nouvelles/558295/le-camp-ymca-kanawana-ravage-par-les-flammes)
