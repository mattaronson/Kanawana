### 1. CCA Founding (May 20, 1936)

The Canadian Camping Association / Association des camps du Canada was founded on **May 20, 1936 at the Central YMCA, Toronto**. A constitution was adopted and officers named:
- **Taylor Statten** — President (also director of Camp Ahmek, Algonquin Park; later ACA president in 1942)
- **Mary Edgar** — Vice-President (1889-1973; founded Camp Oolahwan for YWCA in Laurentians 1915-1919, then Glen Bernard Camp 1922; OCA founding member 1933)
- **Dr. George S. Patterson** — Secretary
- **Mary C. Donaldson** — Treasurer

The CCA Constitution was formally incorporated by Letters Patent on November 4, 1949. The CCA is a national federation of provincial camping associations (now eight: BC, SK, MB, ON, QC, NL, NB, NS & PEI), representing 800+ camps.

### 2. OCA Archives at Trent University — Kanawana File Confirmed

The **OCA fonds (72-007)** at Trent University Archives contains a **"Kanawana (1940)"** file in Series 1 (Camp Brochures and Photographs, 1922-1967), alongside Kandalore, Kawagama, Kawabi, and Kawartha files.

Additionally, the **OCA 1978 additions (78-006)** list Kanawana among "Y" camps in the Agency Liaison series, alongside Wanakita, Wa-sa-ah-bun, Tapatoo, Tapawingo, Pinecrest, and Camp Stephens.

In the **CCA fonds (78-004)**, "Kamp Kanawana" is associated with **Derek Walsh** and the YMCA in Montreal, Quebec. Derek Walsh's identity and role remain unresolved — a follow-up priority has been registered.

### 3. Quebec Camping Association (QCA)

The **QCA was formed in 1937** — just one year after the CCA — as a chartered unit of the CCA. Concordia University archives hold founding correspondence (1936-37), the 1937 constitution, and executive meeting records (1937-1942). A **camping school was held at McGill University from 1937 to 1940**. An April 17, 1937 report of the QCA nominating committee is preserved in the CCA fonds at Trent.

The QCA fonds (85-013) at Trent holds correspondence, minutes, camp visitation reports, and standards committee papers. Most records are in French.

Today, Camp YMCA Kanawana is an **accredited member of the Association des camps du Quebec (ACQ)**, the modern successor to the QCA.

### 5. Canadian Camping Magazine (1949-1988)

The CCA published *Canadian Camping* magazine from 1949 to 1988. Digitized issues are available on archive.org through efforts of Trent University and Joe Richards. A sample issue (Vol 11 No 2, Feb 1959) is confirmed online. These issues have **not yet been searched for Kanawana content** — registered as priority p_074.

### 6. Centenary Journey (1967)

The CCA organized the **Centenary Journey** for Canada's Centennial in 1967 — campers in every province paddled sections of a coast-to-coast canoe route. Records are in the OCA fonds at Trent (trip logs, photos, area histories, correspondence 1965-1968). Key figures: Bruce Hodgins (Trent professor, Camp Wanapitei), Doug McEven (CCA president), John Latimer (OCA president), Ernie Schmidt (ACA executive director). A Saskatchewan group included Regina YMCA Camp participants.

### 7. Kirk Wipper and the "Kanawa" Name

**Kirk Wipper** (1923-2011) founded Camp Kandalore (1957) and established the **Kanawa International Museum of Canoes, Kayaks and Rowing Craft** there — which became the Canadian Canoe Museum in Peterborough (1997). The "Kanawa" museum name is etymologically interesting given its similarity to "Kanawana." Wipper also developed OCA canoeing standards adopted across Canadian camps. Received Order of Canada in 2002.

### 8. Reconciliation in Canadian Camps

Following the TRC 2015 final report, Canadian camps began reflecting on traditions influenced by Indigenous culture. A CCA-affiliated survey found directors were aware of cultural appropriation harms but had no consensus on how to address them. YMCA Camp Waabanaki (Huntsville, ON, YMCA of Three Rivers) changed its name spelling and committed to reconciliation, acknowledging Haudenosaunee, Anishinaabe, and Chonnonton traditional territory.

### 9. Mary Edgar — Additional Details

Born May 23, 1889 in Sundridge, Ontario. Educated at Havergal Ladies College, University of Chicago, Columbia Teachers' College, and YWCA National Training School (NYC, graduated 1915). YWCA Girls' Work Secretary in Montreal 1915-1919 while directing Camp Oolahwan in the Laurentians. Spent 1920 in Japan for YWCA. Founded Glen Bernard Camp 1922 (38 campers first year). Author of *Wood-fire and Candlelight* (1945). Wrote the hymn "God Who Touchest Earth with Beauty" (translated into Cree and other languages). A 109-page university paper exists on her camping contributions. Her personal papers (Mary S. Edgar fonds) are at Trent University Archives.
