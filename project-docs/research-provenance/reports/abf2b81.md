### ANALYZE PHASE - Key Facts Extracted

**1. FOUNDING & NAME HISTORY**
- **1892**: Lemuel Cushing of the Montreal YMCA inaugurated the camping program by bringing a group of boys to Lake Saint-Joseph in Saint-Adolphe-d'Howard
- **1893-1894**: Camp Jubilee officially opened as Quebec's first residential summer camp outside Montreal, with initial registration of 20 campers
- **Named after**: The YMCA's fiftieth anniversary (jubilee)
- **1909**: Renamed to Camp Otoreke (Iroquoian word meaning "north")
- **1982**: Camp closed after nearly 90 years of operation

**2. LEADERSHIP & FOUNDER**
- **"Billy" Ball**: YMCA official who conceived the initial camping concept
- His vision: Take youth away from urban pollution, teach group dynamics, teambuilding, and leadership
- Lemuel Cushing Jr. was Montreal YMCA president 1869-1870, suggesting family continuity

**3. PHYSICAL LOCATION & FACILITIES**
- **Location**: Lake Saint-Joseph islands, Saint-Adolphe-d'Howard, Quebec (Township of Howard, near Sainte-Agathe)
- **Layout**: Multiple islands plus mainland landing area
- **Geography of lake**: 3 km long × 1 km max width, navigable
- **Islands**: The YMCA purchased three islands for the camp
- **Distance from Kanawana**: Saint-Sauveur to Saint-Adolphe-d'Howard is approximately 20 km by road (20 minutes driving)
- **Postcard evidence**: eBay listing shows a postcard titled "A View Of The YMCA Camp Landing, Otoreke" (Real Photo PostCard, unused, good condition) - indicates professional landing dock facilities

**4. CAMP PURPOSE & EVOLUTION**
- **Original** (1893-1910): Boys' camp providing wholesome outdoor environment
- **Post-1910**: After Kanawana became the main YMCA program location, Otoreke operated as:
  - Camp for men 18+
  - Camp for women and men 18+
  - Camp for married couples
  - Camp for low-income families
  - Various adult and family demographics

**5. RELATIONSHIP TO KANAWANA**
- **1910**: Main YMCA camping program moved to Kamp Kanawana (newly established in Saint-Sauveur on Page family land with 85 campers first season)
- **1923**: Camp Otoreke served as an outpost/secondary camp for Kanawana (mentioned in 1923 Gas Bag publication)
- **1935**: Four-day trip to Otoreke was organized from Kanawana (documented in "A History of Kamp Kanawana 1935")
- **Modern connection**: Kanawana's canoe-camping program (Adventurers/Coureurs des Bois) continues the tradition

**6. THE "PAGES" HIKING DESTINATION QUESTION**
- **Finding**: The "Pages" reference from 1923 Gas Bag refers to the **Page family**, who sold/leased land to the YMCA for Kanawana establishment in Saint-Sauveur
- **Not directly related to Camp Otoreke**, but both camps were YMCA properties in the same Laurentian region during this period
- This is a significant fact for your Kanawana KB, not a separate hiking destination

**7. CLOSURE & POST-1982 HISTORY**
