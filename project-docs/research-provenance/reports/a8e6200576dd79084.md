## Research Findings: Who Is "Wilson" in Lake Wilson?

**Answer: Not definitively established.** No documentary source found across 20+ web searches, the project's full KB (1200+ facts), and all cached sources explicitly names the person for whom Lake Wilson was named. The question remains open, with three hypotheses ranked by plausibility.

### What the evidence shows

**1. The 1935 camp history mentions Lake Wilson only in passing** (line 84-85 of `/home/user/Kanawana/sources/cache/green-triangle/1935-history.txt`): "the dam which had kept the water in Lakes Kanawana and Wilson at a desirable height was let out." No naming explanation is given. The word "Wilson" does not appear anywhere else in this document.

**2. The 1923 Gas Bag** lists "Lac Wilson" as a hike destination alongside external locations (Val Morin, Lac Hughes, etc.) — suggesting the name may have **predated** the YMCA's ownership of that lake, possibly as a pre-existing geographic name.

**3. Oral history** (src_oral_aronson) states Lac Desjardins was the original name, renamed after McConnell's donation. The Pagé-Desjardins genealogical connection (Magloire Pagé fils married Olivine Desjardins, 1875) explains the original name.

### Three Hypotheses (ranked)

**Hypothesis A — J.W. McConnell's middle name/family name "Wilson"** (from his mother Margaret Anne Wilson): If McConnell donated the lake/land and the lake was renamed in his honor, "Wilson" could reference his own family name rather than his surname. This is the strongest hypothesis because: McConnell's deep YMCA ties are documented; the oral history connects the renaming to his donation; and "The John McConnell and Margaret Ann Wilson McConnell Memorial Foundation" shows the family treated "Wilson" as a significant family identity.

**Hypothesis B — Wilson Griffith McConnell (1908-1966)**, J.W. McConnell's eldest son: Plausible but problematic — Wilson McConnell was born in 1908 and would have been approximately two years old when the YMCA acquired the Saint-Sauveur property circa 1910. However, the lake could have been renamed later.

**Hypothesis C — A pre-existing local "Wilson" landowner**: The Commission de toponymie documents that the Lac Wilson in nearby Sainte-Lucie-des-Laurentides is named "for the owner of the lands where the lake is located, a person named Wilson" (fact f_1166). This pattern (lake named for landowner) is common in Quebec. The 1923 Gas Bag listing of "Lac Wilson" alongside external destinations supports this — the name may have already existed before the YMCA owned it.

### No Wilson found on YMCA Montreal board

Web searches for "Wilson" among YMCA Montreal board members, presidents, and directors from 1894-1920 returned no matches. Known YMCA Montreal leadership from this period includes John W. Ross (president 1909-1917), G.W. Birks, and George Drummond — no Wilson.
