### Origin of the Phrase

"Non nobis solum" derives from Cicero's *De Officiis* (On Duties), Book 1, Section 22, written 44 BC. The full passage: *"non nobis solum nati sumus ortusque nostri partem patria vindicat, partem amici"* ("Not for us alone are we born; our country, our friends, have a share in us"). Cicero himself acknowledges this as a translation of a sentiment from Plato's *Epistle to Archytas*. The phrase experienced renewed popularity during the Renaissance as humanists integrated classical ideals with Christian ethics. Common variations include "non nobis, sed omnibus" and "non nobis solum, sed omnibus."

### The LCC-Kanawana Connection

**Key facts established by prior research (already in KB):**

1. **LCC adopted the motto in 1909** when founder Charles Sanderson Fosbery based the school crest and motto on his own family heraldic crest. Fosbery held a BA in classics from Trinity College Dublin and arrived in Montreal in 1900.

2. **Kanawana originally used an English motto:** "Each for all and all for each" is documented in the 1922 and 1927 camp brochures. This English motto was a shared YMCA camping tradition also used by Camp Becket (MA, est. 1903) and Camp Hayo-Went-Ha (MI, est. 1904).

3. **The Latin motto "Non Nobis Solum" is first attested at Kanawana in 1993**, in the documentary *Kamp Kanawana: The Experience that Lasts a Lifetime* (dir. Cathy Reeves). The transition date from the English to Latin motto remains unknown (window: 1927-1993).

4. **Stuart McLean is the only documented personal bridge** between the two institutions. He attended LCC for high school, then became a Kanawana counsellor/assistant director (1969-1975). Both institutions used the same motto.

5. **No institutional link** between LCC and YMCA Montreal has been documented. Both served anglophone Montreal, creating demographic overlap, but no formal connection is evidenced.

6. **The LCC school song "Non Nobis Solum"** was first sung at the 1936 return-to-school assembly, with lyrics by early alumni and melody from a Brahms symphony. This would have made the Latin phrase widely recognizable in anglophone Montreal school communities.

7. **Kanawana is unique within the YMCA system** in using this motto. No other YMCA camp or organization worldwide uses "Non Nobis Solum."

**Working hypothesis (unverified):** The switch from a generic YMCA English motto to the distinctive Latin motto was a deliberate act of identity differentiation. The LCC connection -- through cultural overlap in anglophone Montreal, possibly through LCC alumni who served as counsellors or committee members -- is the most plausible pathway, but no documentary evidence confirms this. The Penton book *Non Nobis Solum: The History of Lower Canada College* (1972, Internet Archive) has been identified but not yet accessed.
