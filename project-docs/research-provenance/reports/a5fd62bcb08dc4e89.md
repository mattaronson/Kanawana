## Research Results: Camp Oolahwan Closure (2005)

### Summary (under 200 words)

Camp Oolahwan was the YWCA Montreal's residential girls' camp, founded in 1917 by Mary Susanna Edgar (1889-1973) on Lake Walfred in Sainte-Marguerite-du-Lac-Masson, Laurentians. It operated from 1917 to 2005 and was described as "the longest sustained initiative in YWCA history." The YWCA has never publicly stated a specific reason for the closure. The property was sold and redeveloped: a vacation cottage development called "Club Lac Walfred" (by Habitations Germat / Timber Block Homes) opened on the site around 2015, and the camp's main building at 54 Chemin d'Entrelacs became "Erabliere Ste-Marguerite" (sugar shack) and "Aventure Oolahwan" (outdoor activities). The Association du Lac Walfred adopted an owl logo in homage to Camp Oolahwan's own owl logo. Some camp traces remain: hiking trail, volleyball court, main building with cafeteria. After closure, YWCA Montreal replaced the residential camp with "Summer Space Day Camp," an urban day camp for girls 10-13. The centennial (1917-2017) was celebrated at Camp Ouareau in St-Donat in September 2017. The closure reason is not documented publicly but likely involved financial/operational pressures of maintaining a remote residential property.
