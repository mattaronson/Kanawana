### 3. Date and Name Discrepancies

**DISCREPANCY 1: Source attribution for the 1968 date**
- The article (line 34) states: "The YMCA Quebec website's official history timeline states '1968' under the heading 'Kanawana starts to welcome girl campers.'"
- KB fact f_0201 attributes the 1968 date to **src_oral_aronson** (an oral history source), not the YMCA website.
- KB fact f_0368 (from src_ymca_website) gives the website quote as welcoming girls "for the first time" but with **no date_ref** (null).
- The YMCA website may indeed say 1968, but the KB records the 1968 claim as coming from the Aronson oral history, not the website. This should be verified: either the KB fact f_0201's source attribution is wrong, the article's source attribution is wrong, or both sources say 1968 and the KB simply hasn't recorded the website as also saying 1968.

**DISCREPANCY 2: "Doers and Darers" as section names**
- The article (line 42) refers to "boys' sections (Doers, Darers, etc.)" as the current naming pattern.
- There are **no KB facts** containing "Doers" or "Darers" as section names. The KB records the current boys' sections as **Coureurs des Bois** and **Woodsmen** (f_0453), and the 1959 names as Pioneers, Woodsmen, Coureurs de Bois, and Pathfinders (f_0216).
- "Doers and Darers" may be historical names from a different era or may be erroneous. This claim has no KB backing.

**DISCREPANCY 3: Otoreke and Weredale as girls' camps**
- The article (line 14) states: "Camp Otoreke and Camp Weredale likely served girls (this gender assignment is inferred from the fonds structure and contextual evidence)."
- KB fact f_0426 states Otoreke served "men 18+, women and men 18+, married couples, and low-income families" after 1910 -- it was an adults/family camp, not a girls' camp.
- KB fact f_0470 states Weredale "was founded in 1934 to serve orphaned or at-risk boys" -- it was specifically a boys' camp.
- The article's inference that these camps "likely served girls" is contradicted by the KB. The article does hedge this with "inferred" and a caveat, but the KB has firmer evidence that undermines the inference.

**DISCREPANCY 4: Pathfinders as a gendered section name**
- The article (lines 18, 22) treats "Pathfinders" as inherently gendered ("the name of the senior girls section") and interprets the 1965 Pathfinder program summary as potentially evidence of girls at Kanawana before official coeducation.
- KB fact f_0216 shows that "Pathfinders (14-15)" was introduced in 1959 as one of four section names at an all-boys camp. The name was not originally gendered.
- The article's interpretive leap -- that a 1965 "Pathfinder program Summer Summary" implies girls -- is weakened by this fact. The Pathfinder name existed at Kanawana for boys from 1959 to at least 1967.

**DISCREPANCY 5: Article says "first 73 or 74 years" (line 8)**
- The camp was founded in 1894. If the boys-only era ended in 1967 (McMorris endpoint), that's 73 years. If it ended in 1968 (YMCA website date), that's 74 years. This calculation is internally consistent with the article's own discussion, so no error, but worth noting that the "73 or 74" hedging correctly reflects the disputed coeducation date.
