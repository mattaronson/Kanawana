## New Facts Discovered

### High Confidence (multiple reliable sources)

1. **Taylor Statten died in 1956.** Full life dates: 1882-1956. Full name: Emerson Taylor Statten. (Sources: Taylor Statten Camps website, Burry thesis abstract, ACA presidents list)

2. **His wife was Ethel Page, known as "Tonakela."** She co-ran the camps and was an OCA founding member. Their son Taylor Statten II ("Dr. Tay," 1915-2016) took over as Camp Director in 1954.

3. **Statten mortgaged his home to fund Camp Ahmek's opening in 1921.** The camp opened for six weeks with 60 boys living in tents. "Ahmek" means "Great Beaver" in Ojibwa.

4. **The CSET program was formally launched in October 1918** with two tiers: Trail Rangers (ages 12-14) and TUXIS (ages 15-17). A parallel girls' program, Canadian Girls in Training (CGIT), was also created. The program was based on a "fourfold" development philosophy: intellectual, physical, spiritual, and social.

5. **Statten organized the first Ontario TUXIS and Older Boys' Parliament in 1917.** He believed boys themselves should have leadership roles in decision-making about the CSET program.

6. **In 1916, Statten embarked on a "Coast to Coast Tour" to promote the CSET program.**

7. **The CSET/TUXIS program was co-managed with Protestant churches via the National Boys' Work Board**, later dominated by the United Church of Canada. The YMCA eventually stepped back, and the program declined after WWII.

8. **Dimock and Hendry conducted their pioneering research at Camp Ahmek starting in 1923**, published as *Camping and Character* (1929/1931). Both were affiliated with Columbia University. William H. Kilpatrick (progressive educator, colleague of John Dewey) wrote the foreword. Dimock and Statten later co-authored *Talks to Counselors* (1939) -- a direct 16-year collaboration.

9. **Statten was elected ACA president in 1941 but resigned due to illness**, with VP Wes Klusmann assuming responsibilities. (The existing KB states "ACA president in 1942" which is imprecise.)

10. **Statten also founded the Ontario Camping Association in 1933** as its first chairman, alongside Mary Edgar, Ethel Statten, A.L. Cochrane, Ferna Halliday, and Mary Hamilton. This predates the CCA founding (1936) by three years.

11. **The Taylor Statten Camps commissioned 42-foot war canoes from the Peterborough Canoe Company in 1926**, still reportedly in use today.

12. **Wallace Forgie, another Toronto YMCA Secretary, co-developed the CSET "Efficiency Tests" concept with Statten**, contributing the fourfold framework tied to nature study and outdoor learning as citizenship training.

### Medium Confidence (needs clarification)

13. **YMCA role dating discrepancy:** The existing article says "Boys' Work Secretary for the national YMCA from 1906." Evidence suggests he began Toronto YMCA boys' work around 1905-1906, then became Boys' Work Secretary on the national YMCA executive by 1912. The TSC website conflates these two distinct roles.

## Answers to Open Questions

**1. Death date:** 1956. Exact month/day not found in web sources. The Edwards biography (1960) or Burry thesis (1985) would contain the precise date.

**2. Did Statten ever visit Camp Kanawana?** No evidence found of a direct visit. The connection is indirect: through the CSET program adopted by YMCA camps nationally, the CCA organizational framework, and the Dimock/Hendry research collaboration. The 1915 weekly letters in Concordia Archives remain the best lead for a direct Montreal YMCA connection.

**3. CSET program details:** Substantially expanded (see facts 4-7 and 12 above).

**4. Relationship with Hedley Dimock:** A long professional collaboration. Dimock researched at Ahmek from 1923, published *Camping and Character* in 1929, and co-authored *Talks to Counselors* with Statten in 1939. Both were pioneers of the "character education through camping" philosophy.
