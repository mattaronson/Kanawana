## Research Findings: Stuart McLean and Camp Kanawana

### Stuart McLean -- Full Biography

**Full name:** Andrew Stuart McLean, OC
**Born:** April 19, 1948, in Montreal West, Quebec
**Died:** February 15, 2017, in Toronto, Ontario (age 68), of melanoma
**Parents:** Andrew Thompson McLean and Patricia/Margaret (nee Godkin) McLean -- Australian immigrants who settled in Canada after WWII
**Siblings:** Brother Alistair, sister Stephanie (eldest of three children)
**Grew up:** Brock Avenue, Montreal West -- described it as "a community fenced in on three sides by railroad tracks"

**Education:**
- Elizabeth Ballantyne School (elementary)
- Lower Canada College (high school) -- felt like an "outsider" among "the jocks and the brains"
- BA in Applied Social Science, Sir George Williams University (graduated 1971; SGW became part of Concordia University in 1974)
- Honorary Doctor of Laws, Concordia University (2014)

**Career highlights:**
- After graduating, worked in student services at Dawson College
- Campaign manager for Nick Auf der Maur's first Montreal city council bid
- Researcher on CBC Radio's *Cross Country Checkup*
- Producer of documentaries for CBC Radio's *Sunday Morning* (from 1978)
- Won 1979 ACTRA Award for Best Radio Documentary (Jonestown coverage)
- Professor of broadcast journalism at Ryerson University (1984-2004), then professor emeritus
- *The Vinyl Cafe* debuted 1994 as summer replacement; became weekly CBC Radio staple by 1997
- Three Stephen Leacock Medals for Humour: *Home from the Vinyl Cafe* (1999), *Vinyl Cafe Unplugged* (2001), *Secrets from the Vinyl Cafe* (2007)
- Officer of the Order of Canada (2011)
- Honorary Colonel, 8th Air Maintenance Squadron, 8 Wing Trenton (2005-2008)

**Death:** Diagnosed with melanoma in November 2015 while on Vinyl Cafe Christmas tour; cancelled remaining dates. Announced second round of immunotherapy December 13, 2016. Repeats stopped airing on CBC Radio One January 2017. Died February 15, 2017.

### McLean's Camp Kanawana Years

**Duration:** Five summers, starting 1969
**Roles:** Began as counsellor in the senior boys' section (age 20 in 1969); worked his way up to assistant director
**Assistant director years:** Summers of 1974 and 1975 (confirmed by a former colleague who was camp director those years: "I was the camp director and Stuart was the assistant camp director (summers of 1974 and 1975)")
**Timeline reconstruction:** Counsellor 1969-1973 (five summers as counsellor), then assistant director 1974-1975. However, the sources consistently say "five summers" total, and that he "worked his way up to assistant director during his five summers." This creates ambiguity -- either he was there five summers total (1969-1973, with assistant director being a late role), or five as counsellor plus additional time as assistant director. The most internally consistent reading given the 1974-1975 confirmation is likely: counsellor ~1969-1973, assistant director 1974-1975 (seven summers total), OR counsellor 1971-1973 after a first visit in 1969, then assistant director 1974-1975 (five summers). The "five summers starting in 1969" language from CBC is somewhat ambiguous against the 1974-1975 assistant director confirmation.

**How he arrived:** In summer 1969, visited a friend who worked at the camp, got hired on the spot as a counsellor in the senior boys' section.

**Passed up Carleton:** One summer he turned down a chance to study journalism at Carleton University to return to Kanawana as assistant director.

**Key quotes from McLean:**
- "When I got to camp, I found a place where I was accepted by others and I was able to contribute to the greater good. It worked its magic on me. Camp is where I found my confidence."
- "It was the first place, as a young person, that I felt I could contribute to the greater good."
- "It was the basis, the ground that I stood on for many years and I think it had a lot to do with the success that I was lucky enough to experience with the rest of my life."
- "I loved working with kids, being around kids, and I was good at it. I was a valued part of something."
- Camp Kanawana was "the first place I ever felt I truly belonged."

**2005 return visit:** McLean returned to Camp Kanawana in 2005 for a week and hosted/broadcast a Vinyl Cafe show there. Sean Day, then director of Camp Kanawana (later regional director of camps for YMCA Quebec), hosted him during that week.

**Pip Alumni Award:** McLean was a recipient of the Camp YMCA Kanawana "Pip" Alumni Award (established 2007, named for Philip "Pip" Caddell (1913-2004) and grandson James Caddell (1973-2005)). Exact year of McLean's award not confirmed but likely around 2009-2010.

### The Stuart McLean Camp YMCA Kanawana Fund

**Establishment:** The fund was established by Stuart McLean and his family during his lifetime (referenced in 2017 CBC article as "a new fund established by the CBC host and his family"). Upon his death on February 15, 2017, his family requested donations to the fund in lieu of flowers. The Vinyl Cafe website and Facebook page also directed supporters to the fund.

**Purpose:** Helps children and teens without the financial means to attend Camp YMCA Kanawana. The camp endeavors to ensure at least one in five campers is from a financially disadvantaged background.

**Administration:** Through the YMCAs of Quebec. Donations can be made via the YMCA Quebec website.

**Financial figures found:**
- In 2016, the YMCAs of Quebec provided over $150,000 in financial support for children and teens to attend Camp Kanawana (this predates the McLean fund and represents overall YMCA financial assistance for Kanawana)
- The broader YMCA Quebec financial assistance program totaled $1.196 million in 2016
- No specific annual totals for the Stuart McLean Fund itself were found in publicly available sources for 2018-2023

**Camp pandemic note:** Camp Kanawana was closed for two years during COVID-19 and reopened in 2022.
