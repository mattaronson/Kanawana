### Key Findings

#### 1. "On My Way to Kanawana" by Richard "Itch" Kerr
- **Confirmed**: The camp song is archived at Concordia as **P145/SR0001** on CD, duration 4 minutes 30 seconds
- The song accompanies the 1993/1996 Cathy Reeves film
- In 1967, a busload of Kanawana campers sang this song to greet the CCA Centenary Journey paddlers in Ottawa
- **No lyrics, sheet music, or digital recording found anywhere online.** The Concordia CD is the sole known copy.

#### 2. The Kamp Kanawana Marching Song / Kanawana Cheer
- The most widely known camp cheer, a call-and-response format
- Includes elements: "Kamp Kanawana, are you ready?!", "K-A-N-A-W-A-N-A, Kanawana!", and the refrain "Hail, hail, the gang's all here"
- **Not original to Kanawana**: The refrain derives from the 1915 American song "Alabama Jubilee" (music by George L. Cobb, words by Jack Yellen/D.A. Esrom), itself set to a tune by Arthur Sullivan from *The Pirates of Penzance*
- **Lost verses rediscovered in 2006** at the Concordia Archives -- originally a three-verse song, only the first verse survives in modern oral tradition
- Research documented by Matt Aronson on the Spirit of Kanawana blog (kanawana.blogspot.com)
- **Full lyrics of the rediscovered verses are not published online**

#### 3. The 1922 Camp Cheer: "Yo Triumphy!"
Full text recovered from the 1922 brochure (`/home/user/Kanawana/sources/cache/brochures/1922-brochure.txt`):

> "Yo Triumphy! Yo Triumphy! / Hoben Sloben Rebecca Leeammour, / De hoop de hoop de Shalla de Veer, / De boom de rye de hye de pop, / Whooneka Heeneka Waac de Waac, / Hob Dob de Bowl de Bara, / Con Slob de Hob Dob Rah, / Kanawana---Rah!"

This is entirely nonsensical, ending only with the camp name. No parallel found in any other camp's repertoire. The phrase "Yo Triumphy" returns zero results in any web search.

#### 4. Original Camp Songs (1923)
Three songs with lyrics survive in the 1923 *Gas Bag* (`/home/user/Kanawana/sources/cache/green-triangle/gas-bag-1923.txt`):
- **"Sunny, How Long"** -- comic song about the cook, with chorus structure and verses ribbing individual staff
- **"She Told Me So"** -- multi-verse comic song with recurring chorus
- **Ice cream parody** set to the tune of "Yes, We Have No Bananas" (a 1923 hit), mocking the no-seconds policy

#### 5. Sing-Songs and Campfire Tradition
- "Sing-songs" documented as central evening activity by 1923
- A "Sing-Sing Service" held around the Senior raft (1923) -- a vesper-style singing event on the water
- Senior "Dumbell" Revue with performance elements documented 1923
- Contemporary YMCA Quebec website confirms "sitting around a camp fire, singing call-and-response songs in the forest" remains a current tradition

#### 6. Formal Musical Events (1930s)
- **1935 musicale**: "without precedent in the annals of Kanawana" -- earliest documented formal musical event
- **1938**: Benny Leshley (organist of Christ Church Cathedral, Montreal) organized a choir at the outdoor chapel to lead singing; Brock Brace (a Juvenile camper who composed) performed in a musicale; Sammy Shashley gave regular piano recitals

#### 7. Grace at Meals
- "A short morning devotional period at the breakfast table" documented in the 1922 brochure -- the earliest reference to a meal-time prayer/grace
- McMorris thesis confirms "Grace" as a continuing camp feature
- **The specific words of Grace as said at Kanawana are not documented in any available written source**

#### 8. Archival Song Collections (Concordia P145)
The following music-related materials exist in the Concordia Archives but are not accessible online:
- **Song sheets and skits** (1925, 1927) -- P0145/12B07
- **Kanawana song books** (1941-1945) -- P0145/12B07
- **Canadian YMCA song book** (1941-1945) -- P0145/12B07
- **"On My Way to Kanawana" CD** -- P145/SR0001
- **CFCF radio broadcast scripts** (1936-1941) -- P0145/12B04

#### 9. Musical Instruments at Camp
Both the 1921 and 1922 brochures list "musical instruments" and "music" among recommended items for campers to bring, indicating camper-supplied instruments were part of the camp music culture from at least 1921.

#### 10. Section Cheers / L&V Cheers
**No specific Kanawana section cheers documented** in any available source. However, the Color War / L&V Games tradition at YMCA camps across North America typically features team cheers, fight songs, and an "Alma Mater" during a culminating "Sing" event, providing the likely framework. Oral history is the only viable path for documenting these.
