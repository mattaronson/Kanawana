## Summary: "Kanawa" and "Kanawana" Etymology Research (p_078)

**"Kanawa"** is definitively Proto-Arawak for "dugout canoe" -- a South American/Caribbean word that entered English as "canoe" via Taino -> Spanish *canoa* (Columbus, 1493) -> French -> English. Kirk Wipper chose this name for his Kanawa International Museum of Canoes deliberately, as the ancient root of the word "canoe."

**"Kanawana"** has no confirmed published etymology. The camp sits in traditional Algonquin territory in the Laurentians, and the name is likely Algonquian in origin. A Lac Kanawana exists nearby, suggesting the camp took its name from a pre-existing geographic feature. The most promising lead is an unpublished 1933 history by Ralph Dawson held in the Concordia University archives.

**The two words are almost certainly from unrelated language families** -- Arawakan (Caribbean) vs. probable Algonquian (northeastern North America). The phonetic resemblance appears to be coincidental.

**No connection found between Kirk Wipper and Camp Kanawana.** Wipper's YMCA involvement was with Camp Pinecrest in Muskoka, Ontario -- not the Montreal YMCA system. He went on to found Camp Kandalore in Haliburton in 1957.

**Best follow-up lead**: The Ralph Dawson "History of Kamp Kanawana" (1933) and R.L. Charlton's notes (1943) in the YMCA of Montreal fonds at Concordia University likely contain the definitive answer on the name's origin.
