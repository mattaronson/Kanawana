### NEW Findings from This Research

**1. Harold Beveridge Cushing -- Major biographical details confirmed**

Searches confirmed that Harold Beveridge Cushing was not just a physician but a **founding figure of pediatrics in Canada**:
- Appointed to McGill faculty in **1902**
- Pediatrics first appeared in his academic title in **1920**
- **Founded McGill's Department of Pediatrics in 1937** (same year the Canadian specialty board was created)
- **Greatly assisted in founding the Montreal Children's Hospital (later Children's Memorial Hospital)**, alongside A.D. Blackader, with encouragement from Sir William Osler. Hospital opened January 30, 1904 at 500 Guy Street.
- The **Montreal Children's Hospital Cushing Memorial Prize** ($300, awarded for highest standing in Pediatrics) is almost certainly named for him.

This strongly confirms the article's hypothesis that Dr. Cushing (1946 polio advisor) = Harold Beveridge Cushing. He was THE leading pediatrician in Montreal -- the founder of the entire department at McGill. His advice to keep Camp Kanawana open during the 1946 polio outbreak (when 1,612 children were affected in Quebec, 625 in Montreal) came from the most authoritative source in Canadian pediatrics.

Sources: McGill architecture PDF (search snippet), Hektoen International (search snippet), existing KB fact f_1162.

**2. Thomas Hubert Cushing ("Hubert Cushing") -- Montreal Royals baseball**

A SABR (Society for American Baseball Research) article reveals that **Hubert Cushing**, a Montreal Brewing Company executive, became a **minority owner of the Montreal Royals baseball team** in January 1908. The deal was brokered with majority owner Sam Lichtenhein and fellow minority owner E.R. Carrington.

This is almost certainly **Thomas Hubert Cushing** (b. December 10, 1877), Thomas Cushing's second son listed in the 1905 genealogy (entry 881). This adds a new dimension to the Cushing-Montreal business story: the family's influence extended beyond brewing into sports ownership.

Source: https://sabr.org/bioproj/topic/montreal-royals-team-ownership-history/

**3. A different Walter Edgar Cushing -- disambiguation**

A Dartmouth Alumni Magazine obituary (February 1964) reveals a **separate Walter Edgar Cushing** (b. August 2, 1885, Ashburnham, Massachusetts; Dartmouth Class of 1909), who spent his career in New England YMCA work (NY, Newark NJ, Boston). This is definitively **NOT** the Montreal Walter Edgar Cushing (b. 1870). The name coincidence is notable but the men are from different Cushing branches. The Montreal W.E. Cushing who wrote the 1943 Lake Saint-Joseph sketches remains identified as Lemuel Jr.'s eldest son.

**4. Cushing Genealogy 1905-1969 supplement exists**

A third edition of the Cushing genealogy, covering 1905-1969, was compiled by **Allston T. Cushing** (not the Montreal branch). It is available on FamilySearch (item 235744) and SeekingMyRoots.com, but both are 403-blocked. This supplement could contain death dates for Walter Edgar, Harold Beveridge, and Thomas, as well as updated information about their later lives. This is a significant **archival lead**.

Source: https://www.familysearch.org/library/books/records/item/235744

**5. Thomas Cushing Aylwin -- not related**

One search disambiguated **Thomas Cushing Aylwin** (1806-1871), a Quebec judge whose grandmother was Lucy Cushing. He is from a different Cushing branch and is NOT related to the Chatham/Argenteuil Cushings.

**6. John Crosbie Cushing -- possible descendant**

A **John Crosbie Cushing** served as Mayor of Westmount (~1957-1960s) and presented Queen Elizabeth II with a silver maple syrup jug in 1959. His relationship to the Chatham/Argenteuil Cushing family is unconfirmed but plausible given the Montreal milieu.

### NEW Findings from This Research

**1. Mickey Stein biographical details expanded**

Web search confirmed: **Marvin "Mickey" Stein** married **Sheila Joan Vineberg** on June 28, 1955. They moved into the old Page farmhouse in Saint-Sauveur-des-Monts. With Sheila's father **Stanley Vineberg** and his son **Stephen Vineberg**, they surveyed the site and hired ski designer **Sel Hannah**. Mont-Habitant Development Ltd. was incorporated July 27, 1959. Stein innovated night skiing on lit slopes starting 1961, and artificial snowmaking in 1964. He died May 16, 1985.

Source: https://museeduski.com/membres-honorifiques/marvin-mickey-stein/ (403 on fetch, details from search snippet)

**2. Cote Saint-Elmire first settlers confirmed**

The SHGPH confirms the first settlers of cote Saint-Elmire included: **Jean-Baptiste Desjardins**, **Magloire Page (pere)**, **Augustin Goyer dit Belisle**, **Louis Alary**, and **Pierre Filiatrault**. They came around 1837, "at the time of the troubles of Saint-Eustache" (Lower Canada Rebellion), "fleeing the rebellion to seek calm and security in the mountains." The presence of Jean-Baptiste Desjardins among the original settlers further strengthens the Desjardins-Lac Desjardins toponymic connection.

Source: https://www.shgph.org/post/premiers-colons-de-saint-sauveur (from search snippet)

**3. A second Telesphore Page identified**

The Ville de Saint-Sauveur pioneer families page describes a different **Telesphore** who was born in April 1908, attended ecole du Grand-Ruisseau, worked in logging camps, then was hired by Auguste Dionne (of lac Millette) for his Montreal business. He married **Marguerite Amyot** in 1935 and returned to Saint-Sauveur to open a grocery store on rue de la Gare. This is likely a different generation from the Telesphore who married in 1925 -- possibly a son or nephew. This could clarify the 1960 "Page farm" sale (if the 1908-born Telesphore was the seller).

Source: https://www.vss.ca/loisirs-et-culture/culture-et-communaute/les-familles-pionnieres-de-saint-sauveur (search snippet)
