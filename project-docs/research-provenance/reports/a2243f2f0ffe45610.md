## Blog Post #1: Introductory / Welcome Post

### Content Reconstructed from Search Engine Snippets

Matt Aronson explains the blog's origin: He had the pleasure of having dinner with a couple of "old kampers," and the discussion quickly turned to talk of days gone by and their mutual home-away-from-home. There are few things he loves as much as talking about Kamp, and he always winds up saying the same thing after a lengthy diatribe on some esoteric point about the origins of a tradition, a finer point of Kanawana's history, or the new development plan: **"I really should write some of this down."**

And so, "The Spirit of Kanawana" was born. His goal is simple: **share what he knows about Kamp's fascinating history and traditions and learn more from others.**

He invites readers to **bookmark the page and return often** as he tries to have a fresh story or thought about the past, present, or future of Kamp. He strongly encourages fellow Kanawanians to contribute with a thought or suggestion, question, comment, or story of their own, because **"like Kamp, this blog belongs to all of those who have been moved by the Spirit of Kanawana."**

Signed: **Non Nobis Solum, Matt**

## Blog Post #2: The Kamp Kanawana Marching Song / Kanawana Cheer

This appears to be the primary substantive post on the blog, and the one that generated the most indexed content.

### The Current Cheer (As Performed at Camp)

The post opens with: **"Anyone who has been to Kanawana knows at least one cheer -- The Kamp Kanawana Marching Song, otherwise known simply as the Kanawana Cheer."**

The blog includes what appears to be the full text of the current version of the cheer. Based on search engine snippets, the cheer includes:

- It begins with a distinctive hushing call-and-response: **"Shhhh, shhh, shhh, shhh, shhh, shhh, shhh!"**
- Call: **"Kamp Kanawana, are you ready?!"** (Response implied)
- **"Then let's go!"**
- **"We're gonna sing our marching sooong-a!"**
- **"And through the woods we're gonna yell! We're gonna yell!"**
- The refrain: **"Hail, hail, the gang's all here"**
- **"So let's sing, sing, sing, siiiinng!"**
- **"Kanawana, Kanawana, Rah-rah-rah!"**
- Spelling segment: **"K-A-N-A-W-A-N-A, Kanawana!"**
- Ending: **"It's true!"**

### The Lost Verses -- Rediscovered at Concordia Archives (2006)

Matt writes that the lost verses were **"Rediscovered in the Kanawana Archives at Concordia in 2006."** They were part of an **original three-verse song, the first verse of which is now part of the cheer we all know.** The other two verses had been lost to time.

- The document containing the lost verses was **undated**, and as such, it is difficult to track down the original author of the Kanawana Marching Song.
- The lost verses contained references to **"our camping songs"** and included the line **"We're gonna tramp, tramp, tramp on through the rain."**

### Research into the Song's Origins

Matt writes that **"a quick internet search revealed something very interesting... the song is not original to Kanawana!"**

Key findings presented in the blog:

1. **"Hail, hail, the gang's all here"** is the popular refrain from the **1915 American song "Alabama Jubilee,"** made famous by **Fred Astaire**.

2. The lyrics were written by **D. A. Esrom** (a **pseudonym of Dolly Morse**) to a tune originally written by **Arthur Sullivan** for the comic opera **The Pirates of Penzance**.

3. According to the **Wikipedia entry on the song's refrain** ("Hail, Hail..."), it is also the origin of the **"fight song" sung by the fans of Glasgow Celtic** in Scotland.

### The Green Triangle Theory

Matt teases a future post: **"Now, as for how it got to Kamp in the first place, well... I have a theory involving the tradition of Green Triangle... but that's for another day."**

(The "Green Triangle" was a newsletter for Kamp Kanawana. According to Concordia University archives, it was the Kamp Kanawana Kampers' newsletter, dating from **1932-1940**. A later iteration, "KA-News, Green Triangle," ran from 1976, 1978-1982. It was also the name of a coveted camper award -- the Green Triangle was awarded to a camper who accomplished a series of feats.)

### Historical Note on Claim #7-8 (Potential Error in Blog)

Matt appears to conflate two related but distinct songs. Based on independent research:
- **"Alabama Jubilee"** (1915) was written by **George L. Cobb** (music) and **Jack Yellen** (words), not D. A. Esrom/Dolly Morse.
- **"Hail, Hail, the Gang's All Here"** is a **separate song** that uses the melody from Sullivan's "With Cat-Like Tread" from *The Pirates of Penzance*, with lyrics by D. A. Esrom (Dolly Morse).
- The two songs share the "Hail, Hail" refrain but are distinct compositions. The blog may have inadvertently merged their histories.
