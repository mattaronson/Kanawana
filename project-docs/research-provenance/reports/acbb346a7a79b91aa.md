### Key Findings

**1. "Chopsy" is completely undocumented online.** No variant spelling (Chopsy, Chopsey, Chopsie) appears anywhere on the internet in connection with a camp legend, ghost story, or axe-related folklore figure.

**2. The Cropsey legend is geographically confined to New York State.** Folklorist Bill Ellis characterized "Cropsey" as "the generic term for maniac in New York State camps." The legend's documented range extends from Staten Island through the Hudson Valley, Catskills, and Long Island -- but no further. No Canadian variants have been documented.

**3. Canada has its own camp ghost story archetype: Anson Minor.** Documented by folklorist Edith Fowke in 1981, this is a farmer who lost his leg to farm machinery, went mad, hanged himself, and now haunts the camp built on his land. It is structurally parallel to Cropsey but distinctly Canadian, told at camps across Ontario and Nova Scotia.

**4. The naming pattern is consistent across the genre.** Camp "maniac in the woods" legends consistently use injury-derived or action-derived names: Cropsey/Cropsy (from "crop" = to chop), Stumpy (from his severed stump, who screams "I'm going to chop you!"), Madman Marz. "Chopsy" (from "chop" + "-sy" suffix) fits this pattern exactly.

**5. Working hypothesis (unchanged from Phase 1).** "Chopsy" is most likely a Kanawana-specific localization of the widespread "maniac in the woods" camp ghost story archetype. Each camp gives its local version a unique name and backstory. The name's etymology and the reported axe-murderer theme align perfectly with the Cropsey/Stumpy naming convention. However, whether Chopsy derives from Cropsey specifically, from the Anson Minor tradition, or is an independent creation cannot be determined without oral testimony.

- Chopsy is **not** a common variant spelling of Cropsey
