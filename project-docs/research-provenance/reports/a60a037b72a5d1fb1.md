### KEY FINDINGS

**1. Mabel C. Jamieson -- the YMCA Montreal link (f_0882)**
- National Student Secretary of the YWCA of Canada in 1915
- Completed studies at Pupils College in Richmond, 1918
- **Secretary general of the Y.M.C.A. of Montreal, at least 1915-1920** (source: Wikipedia citing Camp Ouareau history page)
- Member of the Committee of the Sixteen in 1919 (a Montreal anti-vice committee)
- This is the direct personnel link between Camp Ouareau and the YMCA Montreal that ran Camp Kanawana. Jamieson held a senior YMCA Montreal administrative role during the same era as early Kanawana operations.

**IMPORTANT CAVEAT**: The claim that Jamieson was "secretary general of the YMCA" could not be independently verified. No hits appeared in any search outside of the Camp Ouareau history page and its Wikipedia mirror. This is unusual -- a woman holding a senior YMCA role in 1915 would be noteworthy. Possible explanations: (a) the role was within the YWCA, not YMCA, and was misattributed; (b) the role existed but is not in digitized records; (c) the Camp Ouareau sources conflated YWCA and YMCA. Concordia Archives (YMCA Montreal fonds P0145) should be checked.

**2. Ferna G. Halliday -- the YWCA link (f_0881, f_0883, f_0884)**
- Queen's University graduate
- Secretary of YWCA Montreal, 1918-1921
- Responsible for girls at Camp Oolahwan (YWCA camp in the Laurentians)
- Co-directed Camp Ouareau 1922-1923
- Founded Camp Oconto in Tichborne, Ontario, 1924
- Ran Camp Oconto until 1949
- **Founding member of the Ontario Camping Association, 1933** (alongside Mary Edgar, Taylor Statten, Ethel Statten)
- Halliday Hall at Camp Oconto named in her honour

**3. Camp Ouareau founding and property (f_0880, f_0890)**
- Land acquired 1921 from Mr. Leguerrier, under Quebec law requiring fathers' permission for women to buy property
- Camp opened 1922; co-directed by both founders in 1922-1923
- Jamieson sole director from 1924
- Lodge built 1922 -- one of the oldest log buildings still in use in Canada
- 14.5 hectares on Leguerrier Bay, Lac Ouareau, including a peninsula and two islands
- Address: 2464 Route 125, Notre-Dame-de-la-Merci

**4. Camp Ouareau director succession (f_0886-f_0889)**

**5. Camp Oolahwan (YWCA Montreal) (f_0894-f_0896)**
- Founded 1917 by Mary Susanna Edgar on Lake Walfred, Sainte-Marguerite-du-Lac-Masson
- English-speaking, exclusively for young girls
- Longest sustained YWCA Montreal initiative
- Operated 1917-2005
- Centennial reunion held at Camp Ouareau in 2017 (date conflict: YWCA says Sept 8-10; West Island Blog says Aug 25-27)
- Direct Kanawana connection: Camp Oolahwan visited Camp Kanawana (f_0220); in 1946, Kanawana campers covered themselves in cocoa powder to greet Oolahwan visitors as "Indians" (f_0718)

**6. Camp Ouareau parallels to Kanawana**
- Both founded in the early 20th century by people with YMCA/YWCA Montreal connections
- Both originally English-language camps that later became bilingual
- Both used Indigenous-themed team/naming systems that were later changed (Ouareau changed in 2014; Kanawana's evolution is documented elsewhere)
- Camp Ouareau, like Kanawana, is one of the oldest continuously operating camps in Canada

**7. "First girls camp in Canada" dispute (f_0900)**
- YWCA sources claim Camp Oolahwan (1917) was "the first summer camp for girls in Canada"
- Camp Northway in Ontario was founded in 1906 and is acknowledged as oldest
- The YWCA claim likely means "first YWCA girls camp" -- flagged in KB as disputed
