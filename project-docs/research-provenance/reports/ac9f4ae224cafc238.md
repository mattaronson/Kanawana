## Additional Searches and Findings

Beyond the 10 requested searches, I conducted 14 follow-up searches that yielded significant additional details:

### Key New/Confirmed Findings:

**1. Two Generations of Magloire Pagé confirmed:**
- **Magloire Pagé père (1826-1901):** Pioneer settler, married **Adéline Beauchamp (1830-1898)** on July 28, 1851 at Saint-Jérôme. Owned **lot 43, concession of côte Saint-Elmire** per the 1885 census. 10th-generation descendant of the original Pagé family in New France.
- **Magloire Pagé fils (b. 1853, Saint-Sauveur):** Acquired paternal land in 1874. Married **Olivine Desjardins** on August 3, 1875 at Saint-Sauveur. Olivine died June 20, 1904, age 54, buried in Saint-Sauveur cemetery.

**2. Olivine Desjardins confirmed as daughter-in-law (wife of Magloire fils):**
- Source: SHGPH "Hommage à nos pionniers (2 de 2)"
- Marriage record: "Olivine mariée à Saint-Sauveur, le 3 août 1875, avec Magloire Pagé, fils de Magloire Pagé et d'Adeline Beauchamp"
- Magloire fils "acquired the paternal land in 1874, a year before his marriage"
- URL: https://www.shgph.org/post/hommage-%C3%A0-nos-pionniers-2-de-2

**3. Children of Magloire fils and Olivine Desjardins:**
- **Léonie Pagé (1882-1941):** Married Joseph-Vital Léonard in 1902. Joseph-Vital was born 1868 in Saint-Sauveur; his father Vital left him land near Lac Morin at the top of côte 70.
- **Édouard Pagé (b. March 30, 1893):** Baker, apprenticed 1910, acquired bakery 1914. Had 4 sons and 4 daughters. Sold bakery to sons Bernard and Philippe in 1963. École Saint-Édouard named after him.
- **Ovila Pagé:** Already operating a bakery in L'Annonciation in 1912; ceded his establishment; rented Saint-Sauveur bakery to Édouard.
- **Télesphore Pagé (youngest son):** Married May 27, 1925 to **Clarilda Dagenais (1901-1995)**. Inherited the family land. The old Pagé farmhouse "faced the mountains" near Saint-Sauveur.

**4. Côte Saint-Elmire pioneer settlers confirmed:**
- The first settlers of côte Saint-Elmire included: **Jean-Baptiste Desjardins**, **Magloire Pagé (père)**, **Augustin Goyer dit Bélisle**, **Louis Alary**, and **Pierre Filiatrault**.
- These families came to Saint-Sauveur around 1837, "at the time of the troubles of Saint-Eustache" (the Lower Canada Rebellion), "fleeing the rebellion to seek calm and security in the mountains."
- Source: SHGPH "Les premiers colons de Saint-Sauveur" (https://www.shgph.org/post/premiers-colons-de-saint-sauveur)

**5. Côte Saint-Elmire lot details from "Hommage à nos pionniers":**
- **Noël and Lucie Chalifoux:** settled on lot #28 in the northwest section of Côte Sainte-Elmire
- **Roch (surname not captured):** settled on land #238 in Côte Sainte-Elmire, married Domithilde Maillé, then left for the US
- Other settler: **Patrick** (b. Dec 2, 1849, Saint-Janvier), married Vitaline Godin, died 1934, buried Saint-Sauveur

**6. Bakery founding clarification:**
- The SHGPH article and Les Affaires confirm Boulangerie Pagé has been at the center of Saint-Sauveur since 1903. The bakery at 7 avenue de l'Église is part of Les Moulins La Fayette since 1999. The Pagé family conserved the bakery until 2001.
