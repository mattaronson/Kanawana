### Additional Searches -- Key Findings

#### The Weskarini Algonquin First Nation and Their Territory

**Sources:**
- [Morin Heights Historical Association: The Indigenous People of the Laurentians](https://morinheightshistory.org/the-indigenous-people-of-the-laurentians/) -- 403 on fetch but search snippets very detailed
- [Weskarini Algonquin First Nation - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weskarini_Algonquin_First_Nation)
- [Les Weskarinis - Journal des Citoyens](https://www.jdc.quebec/2021/07/16/les-weskarinis/)
- [La Petite Nation Seignory](http://web.ncf.ca/es568/petitenation.htm)
- [QAHN: The Laurentians, A Very Concise History, Part 1](https://qahn.org/article/laurentians-very-concise-history-part-1) -- 403 on fetch

**Critical facts extracted:**

1. **The Weskarini occupied four river systems**: the Lievre, the Petite Nation, the Rouge, **and the North (Riviere du Nord)**. This directly places Saint-Sauveur within Weskarini traditional territory, as the Riviere du Nord flows through Saint-Sauveur.

2. **The Weskarini name** means "Gens du chevreuil" / "People of the Deer" (wawashkesh irini), reflecting their association with deer hunting.

3. **They were also called the "Petite Nation"** because of their small numbers. The Petite Nation River is named after them.

4. **Their principal summer encampment** was at the mouth of the Petite Nation River at Montebello, likely a permanent camp.

5. **They were cousins of the Kichespirinis** (both branches of the Lower Algonquin with different dialects).

6. **Semi-permanent settlements existed along the major rivers** -- the North and the Rouge -- but populations were generally small and seasonal due to climate.

7. **The 1652-1653 Iroquois offensive**: The Weskarini fled north from the Ottawa River. Many were massacred near Petit Lac Nominingue (northwest of Arundel) during an ambush upon their return from a trading journey in 1653. This massacre occurred deep inside their ancestral territory, between the upper valleys of the Petite Nation and the Rouge Rivers.

8. **Mont Tremblant was sacred to the Weskarini**: Called "Manitou Ewitchi Saga" (the mountain of the great god) or "Manitonga Soutana" (the mountain of the spirits).

9. **Post-1837 dispossession**: After the 1837 Rebellion, colonial authorities distributed much of the Weskarinis' lands as rewards to British immigrants who had participated in the repression against the Patriotes.

10. **When Samuel de Champlain made his first voyage up the Ottawa River in 1613**, the chief of the Weskarini was named Iroquet. The Weskarini had good trade relations with the Wyandot (Huron).

**Sources:**
- [Francis Lamothe, tDAR: Human Presence and Intersocietal Interactions in the Laurentians](https://core.tdar.org/document/442880/human-presence-and-intersocietal-interactions-in-the-laurentians-quebec-canada)

- [A babord: 6000 ans d'occupation laurentienne](https://www.ababord.org/6000-ans-d-occupation-laurentienne)

**Facts extracted:**

1. **Archaeological fieldwork conducted since 2015** (by Francis Lamothe, Karine Tache, and Roland Tremblay) demonstrates integration of the Laurentian landscape within interaction networks encompassing several regions of the greater Northeast at various time periods.

2. **Ceramic remains reveal close links** between Algonquins of the Laurentians and both Hurons to the west and Saint Lawrence Iroquoians to the south.

3. **Small nomadic groups inscribed their presence** on the Laurentian landscape, interacted, and created social memories **for over 5,000 years**.

4. **Artifacts associated with the fur trade** illustrate commercial relationships between Algonquin and their French allies at the beginning of the 17th century.

5. **At Nominingue (Grand Lac Nominingue)**: Pottery fragments, bone points, scrapers, and quartzite tools from Mistassini found, representing occupations spanning the archaic period to the period of European contact.

6. **Exotic materials found**: Onondaga chert from southern Ontario and New York, demonstrating long-distance trade networks.

7. **Les Gardiens du Patrimoine Archeologique des Hautes-Laurentides** (non-profit since 2005) preserves and studies archaeological sites at Nominingue, collaborating with Universite Laval.

8. **Archaeological evidence confirms Algonquin continuous occupation** of the broader region for at least 8,000 years prior to European contact (per QAHN source).

#### The Saint Lawrence Iroquoians

**Sources:**
- [St. Lawrence Iroquoians - Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/st-lawrence-iroquoians)
- [Parks Canada: Dispersion of the St. Lawrence Iroquoians](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/qc/cartierbrebeuf/culture/autochtone-indigenous/natcul7)

**Facts extracted:**

1. The St. Lawrence Iroquoians occupied territory along the St. Lawrence River and **disappeared from the valley sometime prior to 1580**.
2. Their disappearance is attributed to intertribal warfare (possibly Mohawk), epidemics, and climate deterioration.
3. After their disappearance, the St. Lawrence Valley became a kind of **contested zone used by Haudenosaunee as a hunting ground and avenue for war parties**.
4. Most villages were located a few km inland from the river, surrounded by palisades by the late 1400s.
5. Champlain found no evidence of native habitation in the valley when he arrived.
