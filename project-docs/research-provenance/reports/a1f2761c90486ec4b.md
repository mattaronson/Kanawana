## Research Summary: The "Chopsy" Ghost Story

### Key Finding: "Chopsy" Is Completely Undocumented Online

Over 30 web searches using every plausible spelling ("Chopsy," "Chopsey," "Chopsie," "Chops") combined with camp, ghost, story, axe, murder, Kanawana, YMCA, Quebec, Canada, and campfire keywords returned **zero relevant results**. The name does not appear in any:
- Folklore archive (USC Digital Folklore Archives, American Folklore Society)
- Academic database or published paper
- Reddit discussion or forum thread
- Camp ghost story collection or website
- Facebook group or social media post
- Newspaper or magazine article

This confirms that "Chopsy" is an **oral tradition** that has never been written down in any publicly accessible source.

### The Cropsey Connection (Strongest Hypothesis)

The most likely explanation is that "Chopsy" is a **Kanawana-specific local adaptation** of the widespread "Cropsey Maniac" campfire story archetype. The evidence supporting this:

1. **Name pattern**: "Chopsy" evokes "chopping" with an axe, directly paralleling documented variants Cropsey/Cropsy, Stumpy, and Madman Marz -- all named after their weapon or wound.

2. **Documented localization pattern**: Academic research by Haring and Breslerman (1977, *New York Folklore*) established that the Cropsey legend was "always localized to where the informant first heard it" -- each camp creates its own version with its own name, setting, and details.

3. **Known variants**: George Cropsey, Jack Cropsey, Judge Cropsey, Old Man Cropsey, Cropsy, Madman Marz, and Stumpy are all documented names for the same archetype.

4. **Narrative match**: The reported "ax murderer" story at Kanawana matches the core Cropsey pattern (a figure with an axe who threatens campers).

5. **Social function**: These stories serve to keep campers in their cabins at night and reinforce counselor authority -- a universal camp need.

### What We Know About the Archetype

The "maniac in the woods" campfire story has been studied by folklorists since the 1960s:

- **Core narrative**: A respected community member goes insane (usually after campers cause a fire that kills his family) and returns to stalk/murder campers with an axe. Alternatively, an escaped mental patient or disfigured caretaker.
- **Documented since**: At least 1966 (Breslerman's survey of NYC informants)
- **Geographic spread**: Primarily New York State (Hudson Valley, Catskills, Staten Island), New Jersey, and surrounding regions. The Cropsey name is specifically New York; the archetype is continental.
- **Films based on it**: *The Burning* (1981, Cropsy), *Madman* (1982, Madman Marz), *Cropsey* (2009 documentary)
- **Real-life intersection**: Andre Rand, convicted child kidnapper on Staten Island (1987), became the "real Cropsey"
