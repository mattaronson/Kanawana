### Analytical Notes

The most important structural finding is the explicit YMCA-to-university pipeline: in the 1940s, SGW (itself a YMCA institution) offered practical courses in counselling and group work. Dimock formalized these into a degree-granting department in 1963 with backing from Principal Robert C. Rae. A. Ross Seaman was part-time faculty that same year (Source 3). The Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies ran alongside the academic department as a research arm. Richard McDonald succeeded Dimock as Centre Director upon Dimock's retirement in 1980. In 1997, Applied Social Science merged with Leisure Studies to become today's Department of Applied Human Sciences at Concordia.

This gives the project a complete institutional chronology:

- **1940s**: YMCA/SGW offers informal B.A.-track courses in group work, counselling, supervision
- **1960-1961**: Dimock conducts camp counsellor research at Kanawana (published as two studies)
- **1962**: Mistassini-Chibougamau Cree Community Development Study
- **1963**: Dimock becomes first Chairman of Applied Social Science; Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies founded; A. Ross Seaman joins as part-time faculty
- **1965**: Richard D. McDonald joins the department
- **1972**: Dr. Raye Kass hired as Assistant Professor
- **1974-1984**: Seaman chairs CRLT at Dawson College
- **1980**: Dimock retires from Concordia; McDonald becomes Centre Director
- **1986-87**: Seaman serves as acting chair of Leisure Studies at Concordia
- **1987**: Seaman dies; memorial fund established
- **November 1997**: Applied Social Science + Leisure Studies merge into Applied Human Sciences

The publication list for Hedley G. Dimock (12 titles confirmed) extends well beyond what the KB had. The title "Administration of the Modern Camp" is directly relevant to the camping article. The Captus Press imprint and the Japanese translations indicate international reach.

The distinction between the two Hedley Dimocks remains important: Hedley Seldon Dimock (1891-1958) was the pioneer at Camp Ahmek and George Williams College who died before the Kanawana research began; Hedley G. Dimock (born c.1928) was the SGW/Concordia figure who actually conducted the Kanawana studies and built the department. The search confirmed new details about the elder Dimock (parents' names, wife's name, USO directorship, named collaborators in post-war camping philosophy) while adding substantially to the younger Dimock's post-retirement career.
