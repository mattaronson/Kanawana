### Key Findings

#### 1. McConnell Family Tree (HIGH CONFIDENCE -- multiple corroborating sources)

- **J.W. McConnell's full name:** John Wilson McConnell (1877-1963)
- **"Wilson" is his mother's maiden name:** Born to John McConnell and Margaret Anne (nee Wilson) McConnell, both Ulster-Scots immigrants. (Source: McGill Archives MG4240, FamilySearch, American Aristocracy genealogy database, multiple biographical sources)
- **Wife:** Lily May Griffith (married 1905), daughter of a Methodist preacher
- **Children (all with middle name "Griffith" after mother's maiden name):**
  - **Wilson Griffith McConnell (1908-1966)** -- eldest son, managed St. Lawrence Sugar, married Marjorie Wallace, one daughter Jill, jazz enthusiast/record collector
  - John Griffith McConnell (1911-1974) -- publisher of Montreal Herald, later ran family businesses
  - Kathleen Griffith McConnell Laing (1918-2012)
  - David Griffith McConnell (1923-1963)

#### 2. McConnell-YMCA Connection (HIGH CONFIDENCE)

- McConnell moved into a YMCA room in Montreal in 1901, nurturing a lifelong commitment
- Led a massive YMCA fundraising campaign in 1909, raising $300,000+
- Established McConnell Foundation in 1937; YMCA was among earliest recipients
- McConnell Foundation has a documented "long relationship" with Camp Kanawana
- McConnell Foundation recently funded major renovations at Kanawana
- Fong biography (2008) includes a chapter on McConnell's YMCA fundraising (pp. 123+)

#### 3. McConnell and Camp Kanawana Land (MIXED CONFIDENCE)

- The YMCA acquired the Saint-Sauveur site from the **Page family** around 1910 (multiple sources)
- McConnell's 1909 YMCA fundraising campaign raised $300,000+ -- this **could** have funded the land purchase, but no source explicitly links the two
- KB fact f_0036 states "J.W. McConnell donated Lake Desjardins to Camp Kanawana (date unknown)" from oral history source (src_oral_aronson, user_knowledge confidence)
- **No independent source found** to corroborate the Lake Desjardins donation claim

#### 4. Lake Wilson Naming -- Critical Timeline Analysis

- The YMCA acquired the Saint-Sauveur property from the Page family around **1910**
- Wilson Griffith McConnell was born in **1908** -- he would have been approximately 2 years old when the property was acquired
- The 1922 brochure refers to "Lake Kanawana" as the camp site and "nearby lakes" for hiking, but does NOT name Lake Wilson as a camp lake
- The 1923 Gas Bag lists "Lac Wilson" as a **hiking destination** alongside Val Morin, Ste. Agathe, Sixteen Island Lake, and "Page's" -- all locations OUTSIDE camp boundaries, suggesting the lake may have already been a named geographic feature in the area before the YMCA associated it with the camp
- The 1935 History refers to "Lakes Kanawana and Wilson" with a dam between them, clearly as camp lakes by that date
- **No source found anywhere** that explicitly states Lake Wilson was named for McConnell's son, nor for any specific person named Wilson
- The Quebec Commission de toponymie has entries for multiple "Lac Wilson" lakes in Quebec, noting "Wilson" is an English-origin family surname common in Quebec toponymy

#### 5. Alternative Naming Theories

The name "Lake Wilson" could plausibly derive from:
- **A pre-existing geographic name** -- the lake may have been named before the YMCA acquired the property, possibly after a local settler family named Wilson. The 1923 reference treats it as a hiking destination alongside other non-camp locations.
- **J.W. McConnell's mother's maiden name or his own middle name** -- if McConnell was involved in the land acquisition, the lake could have been named (or renamed) in honor of his family's Wilson surname.
- **J.W. McConnell's eldest son Wilson** -- born 1908, so alive when the property was acquired circa 1910, but only 2 years old. This is plausible if naming happened later, but no documentation supports it.
- **An unrelated Wilson family** -- numerous Wilson families lived in the Laurentians and broader Quebec.

**Verdict: CANNOT BE VERIFIED or REFUTED from available sources. The claim is plausible but lacks documentary evidence.**

- The 1923 Gas Bag treats "Lac Wilson" as an external hiking destination alongside other non-camp locations, suggesting it may have been a pre-existing geographic name
- No document found (brochures, histories, newsletters, web sources, archives metadata) explicitly links the naming to any McConnell family member
- Multiple "Lac Wilson" lakes exist across Quebec, suggesting the name may derive from local settlers or other Wilsons
- The Quebec Commission de toponymie notes "Wilson" as a common English-origin family surname in Quebec toponymy
