## BIOGRAPHICAL BASICS

- **Full name:** Christopher Terry Mosher, OC
- **Born:** 11 November 1942, Ottawa, Ontario ("within sight of the Parliament Buildings on Remembrance Day 1942")
- **Pen name:** Aislin — a rendition of the name of his eldest daughter Aislinn (without the second "n")
- **Family:** Two daughters, Aislinn and Jessica, both McGill University graduates

---

## EDUCATION

- Attended fourteen different schools in Montreal, Toronto, and Quebec City
- Studied art at Toronto's **Central Technical School** and the **Ontario College of Art**
- Quit high school to hitchhike across North America for two years, supporting himself by sketching people in bars and taverns
- Graduated from the **Ecole des Beaux-arts** in 1967 (now part of UQAM). He famously forged his high-school graduation certificate to gain entrance — he called it "his most successful work"
- **Note:** There is a discrepancy in sources about whether the Ecole des Beaux-arts campus was in Montreal or Quebec City. The Canadian Encyclopedia says Quebec City; most other sources simply say "Ecole des Beaux-arts" without specifying.

---

## CAREER TIMELINE

- **1963-1967:** Drew portraits and caricatures of tourists on the streets of Quebec City. Co-founded **Artists' Alley**, an open-air gallery on Rue du Tresor (still a popular attraction)
- **September 1967:** First published cartoon — a drawing of Charles de Gaulle in *Saturday Night* magazine
- **10 December 1967:** First cartoon published in a newspaper (*The Montreal Star*) — about a police raid on an African entertainment group
- **1969:** Appointed official cartoonist of *The Montreal Star*
- **1972:** Moved to *The Montreal Gazette*, where he has remained ever since
- **Still active** as of March 2026, posting cartoons to the Gazette (over 54 years at the paper)
- Has produced more than **14,000 editorial cartoons** over his career
- Freelanced for: *Punch*, *The Atlantic Monthly*, *Harper's*, *National Lampoon*, *Time*, *The Washington Star*, *The New York Times*, *Reader's Digest* (Canadian edition)
- Illustrated multiple covers for *TIME* magazine

## PUBLICATIONS

- Author of approximately **51-52 books** (sources vary slightly on the exact count; described as "52 books" in some places)
- Notable titles include:
  - *The Hecklers: A History of Canadian Political Cartooning* (1978/79, with Peter Desbarats) — a National Film Board film based on this book was released in 1980
  - *Did the Earth Move* (McClelland & Stewart, 1980)
  - *The Anglo Guide to Survival in Quebec* (1983)
  - *Drawing Bones: 15 Years of Cartooning Brian Mulroney* (1991)
  - *Hysterically Historical Rhymes* (1996)
  - *One Oar in the Water* (1997)
  - *The Wrecking Ball* (2014)
  - *Was It Good For You?* (his 45th book)
  - A biography of legendary Canadian political cartoonist **Duncan Macpherson** (described as his latest)

## NOTABLE CONTROVERSIES

- **1975:** International outrage for depicting Queen Elizabeth with "little pig feet" propping up a puppet-like Prince Philip
- **1993:** MP Robert Layton denounced an Aislin cartoon depicting outgoing PM Brian Mulroney lying face down in the snow, tripped by a whistling Pierre Trudeau, as "a crime against fundamental Canadian values of decency and mutual respect." Mosher became the **first political cartoonist censured in the House of Commons**. He received the Order of Canada ten years later.

## COMMUNITY AND OTHER ACTIVITIES

- Long association with the **Old Brewery Mission** (Montreal's largest homeless shelter); appointed to its board of directors in 2001
- Member of the **Baseball Writers' Association of America**
- Jury member of the **Baseball Hall of Fame** in Cooperstown, New York
- Subject (with Serge Chapleau) of the 2003 documentary film *Nothing is Sacred*
- 2017: Major exhibition at the **McCord Museum** — *Aislin: 50 Years of Cartoons*. The McCord holds 6,000 of his original cartoons in its permanent collection.

## CAMP KANAWANA CONNECTION

- Terry Mosher is a **Camp Kanawana alumnus**
- On **3 August 2015**, he received the **YMCA Kanawana "Pip" Alumni Award** in Saint-Sauveur, Quebec — he was the **eighth recipient**
- In 2015 he "returned to Camp Kanawana in the Laurentians for the first time in **more than 60 years**," which would place his time at camp in approximately the **early 1950s** (consistent with his birth year of 1942 — he would have been roughly 8-12 years old)
- The "Pip" Award is named for Philip "Pip" Caddell (1913-2004) and his grandson James Caddell (1973-2005). It recognizes former campers, counsellors, or supervisors who exemplify "selflessness and contribution to the community"
