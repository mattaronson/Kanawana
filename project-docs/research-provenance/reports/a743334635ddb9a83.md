**2. Mary Susanne Edgar -- Complete Biographical Timeline**

- Born May 23, 1889, Sundridge, Ontario (daughter of Joseph Edgar and Mary Little)
- Educated: Sundridge Public School, Barrie High School, Havergal Ladies College (Toronto), extension courses at University of Chicago, lectures at Teachers' College Columbia University
- 1915: Graduated from National Training School of the YWCA, New York City
- 1912-1914: First National Girls' Work, YWCA, in Canada
- **1915-1919: Girls' Work Secretary, YWCA Montreal; Director, Camp Oolahwan in the Laurentians** (this is the direct Montreal connection)
- **1917: Founded Camp Oolahwan on Lake Walfred, Sainte-Marguerite-du-Lac-Masson** (YWCA girls' camp)
- 1920: Purchased property on Lake Bernard near Sundridge
- 1922: Opened Glen Bernard Camp (38 campers first summer)
- 1933: Founding member, Ontario Camping Association (alongside Statten, Cochrane, Hamilton, Halliday, Ethel Statten)
- 1936: First Vice-President, Canadian Camping Association (founded May 20, 1936 at Central YMCA Toronto)
- 1945: Published "Wood-fire and Candlelight"
- 1956: Retired as Glen Bernard Camp Director; published "Under Open Skies"
- 1967: Published "The Christmas Wreath of Verse"; donated High Rock Lookout to Sundridge
- 1968: Received centennial award from CCA
- 1970: Published "Once there was a Camper"
- Died September 17, 1973
- Best known hymn: "God Who Touchest Earth with Beauty" (translated into several languages including Cree)

**3. Correction: Camp Oconto vs. Camp Oolahwan**

The original task stated Mary Edgar "founded Camp Oconto." This is incorrect. Edgar founded **Camp Oolahwan** (1917, YWCA) and **Glen Bernard Camp** (1922). **Camp Oconto** was founded by **Ferna Halliday** in 1924 in Tichborne, Ontario. The project's existing articles already have this correct.

**4. Archival Holdings of Edgar Papers**

- **Internet Archive:** "Legacy to a Camper: The Story of Mary S. Edgar" by Jocelyn Palm (1982, CCA publisher)

**5. Edgar-Kanawana Connection**

No direct connection between Mary Edgar and Camp Kanawana was found in any source. The connection is **institutional and indirect**: Edgar worked for YWCA Montreal (1915-1919) during the same period Kanawana was operating under YMCA Montreal. The YMCA and YWCA of Montreal shared the institutional umbrella, and Camp Oolahwan campers visited Kanawana (documented 1946 visit already in KB). The 109-page paper, if accessed, could potentially contain references to the YMCA/YWCA Montreal network and possibly Kanawana, but this remains speculative.
