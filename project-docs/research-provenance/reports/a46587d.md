### Analysis and Conclusions

**The genealogy does NOT identify who led the 1892 YMCA trip to Lake Saint-Joseph.** The document contains no mention of Lake Saint-Joseph, camping, or any 1890s YMCA activity.

**Critical finding: Lemuel Jr. had NO son named Lemuel.** His four children were Walter Edgar (b. 1870), Harold Beveridge (b. 1873), Florence Ethelwyn (b. 1874), and James Stevenson (b. 1878). This definitively rules out a "son of Lemuel Jr." hypothesis.

**There are only two Lemuels who could plausibly be "Lemuel Cushing" in Montreal in 1892:**

1. **Lemuel Cushing (b. 1875)**, son of James Brock Cushing (878), nephew of Lemuel Jr. -- He was in Montreal (family moved there in 1890) and bore the Cushing surname, but he was only 16 years old in 1892. Too young to lead a YMCA camping trip, though old enough to participate.

2. **The name "Lemuel Cushing" could refer to a family member using a familiar/informal name.** Walter Edgar Cushing (b. 1870), Lemuel Jr.'s eldest son, would have been 22 in 1892 -- the right age. He may have been known informally by his father's name or title ("young Lemuel Cushing" or simply "Cushing"). However, there is no evidence for this.

**The most likely candidate based on this genealogy alone is that the person referred to as "Lemuel Cushing" in 1892 was using the family name in a way that doesn't precisely match any living individual named Lemuel.** The research should continue with Montreal YMCA records from the 1890s to identify which Cushing was actually involved.
