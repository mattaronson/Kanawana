# Research Report: People and Documents Connected to Kamp Kanawana

## 1. HEDLEY S. DIMOCK -- Pioneering Camp Researcher

### Biographical Summary

There are **two distinct individuals** named Hedley Dimock, likely father and son, whose records are frequently conflated in library catalogs:

**Hedley Seldon Dimock (1891-1958)** -- the pioneering camp researcher:
- Born February 11, 1891, South Rawdon, Hants, Nova Scotia, to Charles Turner Torry Dimock and Catherine E. Dimock
- Married Elizabeth Marguerite Gardiner; had 2 children
- WWI veteran: enlisted with the Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (University of Saskatchewan group) in Saskatoon, 1917. In August 1918, severely wounded after throwing himself on a German grenade to protect his unit. Months of recovery before returning to Canada.
- WWII: served as Director of Training for the United Service Organization (USO)
- Associated with Columbia University (New York) at the time of his camp research
- Taught at the YMCA College in Chicago (later George Williams College), alongside Charles E. Hendry, Everett Du Vall, and Harleigh B. Trecker -- all key developers of "social group work" theory
- A "dean of YMCA College in Chicago" who in 1930 launched institutes on the role of camps in "character education" of children
- Obituary published in The Oakland Tribune, October 5, 1958
- The ACA hosts a biographical PDF: [Hedley S. Dimock Biography (ACA)](https://www.acacamps.org/sites/default/files/page_documents/about/Dimock-Biography.pdf)
- A longer biographical article exists: [Hedley S. Dimock: His Life and Its Meaning (ACA)](http://www.acacamp.org/anniversary/collection/hedley_dimock/Dimock_Life_and_Meaning.pdf)

**Hedley G. Dimock (born c. 1928?)** -- the later group-work scholar:
- Full middle name possibly "Gardner" (Open Library catalogs him as "Hedley Gardner Dimock, 1928-")
- Coordinator of Staff Development and Training for the Montreal YMCA
- Lecturer in Group Development at Sir George Williams University
- In 1963, became first Chairman of the new Department of Applied Social Science and Director of the Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies at Sir George Williams University, under Principal Robert C. Rae
- Co-initiated a Community Development Study of the Cree of Mistassini-Chibougamau, Quebec (1962)
- Later: Director, Centre for Human Resource Development, Puslinch, Ontario; adjunct professor at University of Guelph; served on faculties of McGill, Columbia, Toronto, Concordia, and others
- Retired from Concordia around 1980 (succeeded by Richard D. McDonald as Centre Director)
- Published extensively through Captus Press, co-authored with Raye Kass (Concordia University)
- Worked with over 300 organizations across Canada; 5 of his books published in Japanese

**Note:** There is also a **Hedley Gilbert Dimock** (RCAF Flight Sergeant) who died June 26, 1944 in India -- a different person entirely.

### The Hedley S. Dimock Award (ACA)

Established 1965 by the ACA Board of Directors. Given to "one who contributes to camping through related fields such as outdoor education, conservation, recreation, medicine, education, architecture, or the social sciences." It is described as recognizing "outstanding service to camping and continuing contributions in creative thought and dedication to the highest values in camping." The 2026 recipient is John Hamilton (Alliance for Camp Health).

### Raye Kass (Collaborator)

Dr. Raye Kass was hired as Assistant Professor of Applied Human Sciences at Concordia University in 1972; promoted to Associate Professor (1976), then Professor (2012). She co-authored multiple books with Hedley G. Dimock. She also presented "Leadership on a Hot Tin Roof: Applications for Camp Professionals" at the 8th International Camping Congress (Quebec City, 2008). Her own publications include *Theories of Small Group Development* (4th edition, 17th printing). See: [Concordia faculty page](https://www.concordia.ca/faculty/raye-kass.html) | [Publications](https://www.rayekass.com/index.php/publications)

### Dimock Quote on Camp Communities

"The camp community possesses most of the elements of a normal community, but in a simplified form." -- Hedley Dimock, co-author of *Camping and Character*

## 2. HAROLD C. CROSS -- YMCA Montreal Historian

### Book: *One Hundred Years of Service to Youth* (1951)

Also referenced as *One Hundred Years: The Story of the Montreal Y.M.C.A.* Harold C. Cross authored this centennial history covering the Montreal YMCA from 1851 to 1951.

### Archival Materials at Concordia University

The YMCA of Montreal fonds at Concordia (Sub-series P0145/1B) contains extensive materials related to Cross's work:
- Manuscript copy of "100 Years of Service to Youth" (1951)
- Draft copies of **Chapters II, IV, V, and VI** of the *History of the Montreal YMCA 1851-1951*
- First draft of **Chapter II** (1950)
- **Chapter 7** titled "Dominion Square Building"
- Printers' quotations for the Harold C. Cross history (1951)
- Harold Cross history book orders (1951)
- A 1949 document: "Suggested organization of materials for the history of the Montreal YMCA"
- Cross-Ross correspondence: "Correspondence between Harold Cross and Murray Ross regarding their respective histories" (~1950)
- "YMCA Events" for a YMCA News Centennial issue covering timeline 1851-1901
- Historical data accumulated by Cross "perhaps useful to some future historian" (1950-1951)
- A photograph of Harold Cross dated 1971
- Harold Cross-National Council Boys' Work file 1940-1945; Nelson McEwen Boys' Work Secretary correspondence 1941-1945

Cross and Murray Ross were working on their respective histories (Montreal Association and national Canadian YMCA) simultaneously, and corresponded about their work.

### Availability

No digitized full text found online. Not on Internet Archive, Google Books, or HathiTrust as of this search. The manuscript and drafts are held at the **Concordia University Archives** (P0145/1B).

A continuation volume was later produced: *First Quarter of Second Century of Service to Youth of Montreal, 1951-1976* by F.G. Hubbard (1976).

## 4. R.L. CHARLTON -- "Notes re Early Days" (1943)

### What Was Found

The Concordia University Archives (P0145/12A) contain **two overlapping references** with variant initials:

1. **"Notes re Early Days of YMCA Camps at Lake St. Joseph and Kanawana, by R.L. Charlton, 1943"** -- listed alongside Ralph Dawson's 1933 history and W.E. Cushing's 1943 historical sketches
2. **"Early Days of YMCA Camps, by R.H. Charlton"** (1943) -- listed alongside "Early Days at Lake St. Joseph, by W.E. Cushing" (1943)

The initials alternate between R.L. and R.H., which may be a transcription error in the finding aids, or could represent two different documents by the same person.

### Biographical Information

From the Canadian Board of Marine Underwriters (CBMU) history: **R.L. Charlton** was a Montreal-based independent marine surveyor who "maintained his own office." He also had "a loose tie with the Western" (presumably an insurance company). Crucially: "He was an ardent YMCA worker and shortly after the formation of the Board [CBMU, founded 1917], he went to France with the YMCA to render service to the Canadian troops serving in the Great War." He served as Treasurer and Executive Committee member of the CBMU.

This profile is consistent with a Montreal business professional who was deeply involved with the YMCA and would have first-hand knowledge of the early camping program dating from the 1890s. By 1943, when he wrote his "Notes re Early Days," he would have been recalling events from roughly 50 years prior.

Photographs of R.L. Charlton are held in the Concordia archives.

### Availability

No digitized text found online. The document exists only in the Concordia University Archives (P0145/12A). Not on Internet Archive, Google Books, or any digitized repository as of this search.

### W.E. Cushing (Related Author)

W.E. Cushing authored "Historical sketches--Lake St. Joseph" (1943) and "Early Days at Lake St. Joseph" (1943), held in the same archival sub-series. The Cushing name also appears in the early camp history: "Lemuel Cushing brought a group of boys up to Lake Saint-Joseph in Saint-Adolphe-d'Howard" in 1892 -- possibly a relative of W.E. Cushing.

## 5. MURRAY ROSS -- YMCA Historian

### Biography

**Murray George Ross, OC, O.Ont** (April 12, 1910 -- July 20, 2000):
- Born in Sydney, Nova Scotia; son of James Alway Ross and Sarah Agnes Kay
- BA in economics and sociology, Acadia University (1936)
- MA in sociology, University of Toronto (1938)
- Post-graduate work: sociology at University of Chicago (1939), social psychology at Columbia University (1949)
- Had "a long association with the Canadian YMCA as a staff member"
- 1951: appointed Associate Professor of Social Work, University of Toronto; Professor by 1955
- 1956-1957: Executive Assistant to the President, University of Toronto
- **1959-1970: Founding President of York University, Toronto**
- Order of Ontario (1988); Canadian Centennial Medal; 125th Anniversary of Confederation Medal
- Murray Ross Parkway, Ross Building, and Murray G. Ross (Valedictorian) Award at York University are named after him

### Publications

1. **The Y.M.C.A. in Canada: The Chronicle of a Century** (1951). Toronto: Ryerson Press. 517 pages.
   - Covers the first 100 years of the YMCA in Canada (1851-1951)
   - [Amazon UK](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Y-M-C-Canada-chronicle-century/dp/B0007JBVIO)
   - [WorldCat Identities](http://worldcat.org/identities/lccn-n81012103)
   - [Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL298837A/Murray_G._Ross)
   - [LibraryThing](https://www.librarything.com/author/rossmurrayg)
2. *The Toronto Y.M.C.A. in a Changing Community 1864-1940* (MA thesis, University of Toronto, 1947)
3. Other works listed on Wikipedia: *Community Organization: Theory, Principles, and Practice* (1967), etc.

### Connection to Kanawana Research

The Concordia archives document that "materials were amassed by Harold C. Cross and Murray C. Ross when they were developing their histories of the Montreal Association and the YMCA in Canada, respectively." Correspondence between Cross and Ross (~1950) survives in the fonds. Ross's 1951 national history would cover the Montreal YMCA as a major chapter, given Montreal's status as the first YMCA in North America (1851).

The University of Minnesota also holds a collection: [YMCA in Canada records](https://archives.lib.umn.edu/repositories/7/resources/992).

### Availability

No digitized full text found online. The book is held by various libraries. No Google Books preview available. Not on Internet Archive as of this search.

## 6. HARRY BALLANTYNE -- YMCA Historian

### Biography

**Harry M. Ballantyne** served as **General Secretary of the Canadian National Council of YMCAs** (the national body, formed 1912 with headquarters in Toronto). After retirement, he established the **Harry M. Ballantyne Award of Merit**, described as the "highest honour awarded by Y's Men" (Y's Men International). The International President gives out this award during his or her term.
