## New Facts to Add (with sources)

### Fact 1: The EOY Award is formally called the "Dr. Victor Cecilioni Award"
- **Claim**: The Environmentalist of the Year award Smee received in 1983 is formally called the "Dr. Victor Cecilioni Environmentalist of the Year Award." It was initiated by CHOP (the Conserver Society's predecessor) in 1979. A committee of community organizations was convened in 1982 to expand the program. Sponsoring organizations include: Conserver Society of Hamilton & District, Hamilton Naturalists' Club, Hamilton Conservation Authority, Environment Hamilton, and Bay Area Restoration Council.
- **Source**: [Conserver Society - EOY Awards Dinner](https://conserversociety.ca/projects/environmentalist-of-the-year-awards-dinner/) | [EOY Awards - Edgar E. Smee 1983](https://eoyawards.com/recipients/edgar-e-smee-environmentalist-of-the-year-1983/)
- **Confidence**: stated

### Fact 2: The Environmental Trust Fund was initiated in 1986, chaired by Smee
- **Claim**: The Conserver Society's first Environmental Trust Fund was initiated in 1986, chaired by Ed Smee, to support environmental projects. In 1999, it was renamed the "Ed Smee Conserver Society Environmental Fund" in his honour when transferred to the Hamilton Community Foundation. It is a "donor advised fund" where the Conserver Society recommends recipients.
- **Source**: [Conserver Society - Ed Smee Fund](https://conserversociety.ca/funds/ed-smee-fund/) | [Hamilton Community Foundation - Ed Smee Fund](https://www.hamiltoncommunityfoundation.ca/grants/grant/ed-smee-conserver-society-environmental-fund/)
- **Confidence**: stated
- **Note**: The existing fact f_0867 mentions the fund's 1999 renaming, but not the 1986 founding of the trust fund itself or that Smee chaired it.

### Fact 3: Conserver Society activities under Smee's leadership
- **Claim**: Under Smee's leadership and afterward, the Conserver Society helped initiate composting programs, bicycle paths, education on and reduction of pesticide use, limiting urban sprawl and destruction of natural areas, and participated in regional waste-management, air quality, and transportation initiatives.
- **Source**: [CanadaHelps - Conserver Society](https://www.canadahelps.org/en/charities/conserver-society-of-hamilton-and-district/) | [Conserver Society home](https://conserversociety.ca/)
- **Confidence**: stated (attributed to the organization, not solely to Smee personally)

### Fact 4: The specific Red Hill Creek incident detail
- **Claim**: When Smee returned to Hamilton in 1968, he found Red Hill Creek polluted by a storm sewer outlet, and a large sign warned the lake was unsafe for swimming. This was the specific catalytic incident for his environmental career.
- **Source**: [EOY Awards - Edgar E. Smee 1983](https://eoyawards.com/recipients/edgar-e-smee-environmentalist-of-the-year-1983/) | [Hamilton Community Foundation - Ed Smee story](https://www.hamiltoncommunityfoundation.ca/impact/stories/ed-smee-conserver-society-environmental-fundd/)
- **Confidence**: stated
- **Note**: Existing fact f_0865 covers the return and the anger but not the detail about the storm sewer outlet or the warning sign.

### Fact 5: Obituary publication date
- **Claim**: Edgar Smee's obituary was published in The Globe and Mail on February 11, 2002 (death was February 7, 2002).
- **Source**: [Legacy.com - Edgar Smee obituary, Globe and Mail](https://www.legacy.com/ca/obituaries/theglobeandmail/name/edgar-smee-obituary?id=41816751)
- **Confidence**: stated

### Fact 6: Possible identification of daughter Susan Smee
- **Claim**: Susan (Sue) Mary Smee, born November 12, 1947, died February 9, 2017 in North Vancouver, BC, of cancer. She and her husband Barry were known to have been in Ottawa in 1973. This is likely Edgar Smee's daughter Susan.
- **Source**: [Legacy.com - Susan Smee obituary, North Shore News 2017](https://www.legacy.com/ca/obituaries/nsnews/name/susan-smee-obituary?id=39749024&fhid=5840)
