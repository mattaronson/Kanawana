### 1. Camp Motto "Non Nobis Solum" (Not For Ourselves Alone)

**Status:** Currently used; Classical Latin origins

According to the KB facts, the camp motto is "Non Nobis Solum" (Latin: "Not For Ourselves Alone"), derived from Cicero's *De Officiis* 1:22. However, this represents a **shift from an earlier motto**: the 1922 camp brochure documented the motto as "Each for all and all for each."

**Sources in codebase:**
- /home/user/Kanawana/kb/facts.json (fact about current motto)
- /home/user/Kanawana/sources/cache/brochures/1922-brochure.txt (original motto)

**Additional context:** The Latin phrase derives from Cicero's philosophical text where he wrote: "non nobis solum nati sumus ortusque nostri partem patria vindicat, partem amici" ("Not for us alone are we born; our country, our friends, have a share in us").

**Timeline:** Unknown when the transition from "Each for all and all for each" to "Non Nobis Solum" occurred - this is a gap in the records.

### 4. The "Capitaine" Role in L&V Games

**Significance:** Leader of the Voyageur team; considered one of the highest honours a staff member can receive

**Selection Process:** Not explicitly documented in codebase. The selection process appears to be determined by camp leadership, but no formal written criteria are recorded.

**L&V Games Context:**
- **Origin:** Started at Pine Crest camp in Ontario in 1940; adopted at Kanawana in 1947
- **Format:** Last week of camp season; entire camp divided into two teams
- **Teams:** Voyageurs (symbol: paddle) vs. Lumbermen (symbol: axe)
- **Structure (by 1958):** Teams decided Friday, captains picked that evening, competition through the week
- **Symbols:** Voyageurs and Lumbermen made model paddles/axes to wear around their necks
- **78th edition:** 2025 (meaning tradition has been continuous since 1947)

**Sources:**
- Codebase: /home/user/Kanawana/kb/facts.json (fact about Capitaine role and L&V Games history)

### 6. Traditions Surviving from 1920s-1930s to Modern Era

**Confirmed continuous traditions:**
- **L&V Games** — originated Pine Crest 1940, adopted Kanawana 1947, 78th edition in 2025
- **Council Ring** — documented 1923, still used in closing ceremonies
- **Camp publications** — Green Triangle (1932-1940), revived as Ka-News (1976, 1978-1982)
- **Honour/badge system** — 1922: 32 subjects; 1923: 42 badges (Large K as highest award); modern system exists but evolution not fully documented

**Traditions with gaps in documentation:**
- Firelighting ceremony (1936 documented, current status unknown)
- Fire of friendship (1939 documented, current status unknown)
- Shawbridge Meet/J. Earl Birks Trophy (documented 1922-1935, end date unknown)
- Spiritual traditions (morning devotions, evening vespers, conversion commitments) — documented in 1920s-1930s, current practice unknown
- Pyjama Parade to post office — documented 1935, current status unknown

**Missing research:** Only Council Ring and L&V Games are explicitly documented as continuous into the modern era in the existing wiki content.

### 7. "The Experience That Lasts a Lifetime" — Tagline and Cathy Reeves Film

**Film Details:**
- **Title:** "Kamp Kanawana: The Experience that Lasts a Lifetime"
- **Director/Producer:** Cathy Reeves
- **Dates:** Produced 1993, also released as colour VHS in 1996 (9 minutes)
- **Content:** Voiceover narration, camper testimonials, describes camp as "the oldest camp in Quebec and second oldest in Canada"
- **Music:** Features song "On My Way to Kanawana" by Richard Itch Kerr

**Original Tagline Status:** 
The codebase shows the phrase was in use by 1993 when the film was made, but **when this phrase was first adopted as official camp tagline is not documented.** The existing wiki notes indicate this requires further research.

**Related Discovery:**
- McMorris thesis (2023) used the same phrase in its title: "An Experience That Lasts a Lifetime: Building Modernity, Man, and Nation at the YMCA of Montreal's Kamp Kanawana, 1894-1967" — suggesting the phrase has become deeply embedded in camp identity and historiography.
