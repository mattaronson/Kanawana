# Research Findings: 1967 Centennial Canoe Events

## PART 1: THE CENTENNIAL VOYAGEUR CANOE PAGEANT

### Overview
A competitive canoe race across Canada held during Canada's centennial year, 1967. As of 2012 it still holds the Guinness World Record for the longest canoe race in history.

- **Dates:** May 24, 1967 (start, Rocky Mountain House, Alberta) to September 4, 1967 (finish, Expo 67, Montreal)
- **Distance:** 3,283 miles (5,283 km) paddled, plus 60 miles of portaging across 70 portages
- **Duration:** 104 days
- **Participants:** 10 teams of 10 men each (100 men total), paddling in 6-man shifts
- **Non-participating provinces:** Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland

### Route
Rocky Mountain House, AB down the North Saskatchewan River, past The Pas, through Cedar Lake, Lake Winnipegosis, Lake Manitoba (with a 19-mile portage from Delta to Portage la Prairie -- the longest portage), along the Assiniboine and Red Rivers to Lake Winnipeg, the Winnipeg River to Lake of the Woods, through Ontario via the Great Lakes, the Ottawa River (reaching Ottawa on August 29), and finally the St. Lawrence River to the Expo 67 site in Montreal.

### Organizers and Key Figures
- **Gene Rheaume** of Flin Flon: originally conceived the idea in 1963 as a two-man canoe race from Edmonton to Montreal
- **Norm Tyson** of Flin Flon: drafted the formal proposal sent to the Centennial Commission in 1964
- **Arnie Charbonneau**: Centennial Commission project officer who transformed it into a voyageur canoe pageant with larger canoes; arranged the organizing meeting at the Fort Garry Hotel, Winnipeg, on May 14, 1965
- **Eric W. Morse**: Canadian wilderness canoeist, writer, and historian; central figure in planning the route, drawing on his book "Canoe Routes of the Voyageurs"
- **John Fisher**: Centennial Commissioner; greeted the canoeists in Ottawa on August 29, calling them "the harbingers of the centennial"
- **Judy LaMarsh**: Secretary of State of Canada; saw off the canoes at Rocky Mountain House on May 24; received them at Expo 67 on September 4, where the paddlers presented her with a bale of beaver furs and a buffalo robe fashioned into a cape
- **Bill Mason**: attended the 1965 organizing meeting; later became famous as NFB canoe filmmaker

### The Canoes
- **Prototypes (1966):** 12 canoes built by the **Chestnut Canoe Company** (Fredericton, NB) in wood-and-canvas; 25 feet long, 51 inches wide, 19 inches deep, ~235 lbs dry
- **Race canoes (1967):** 10 canoes built by **Moise Cadorette** of St-Jean-de-Piles (near Shawinigan), Quebec, in **fiberglass**. The switch was because the wooden prototypes did not stand up to race conditions. The fiberglass canoes weighed approximately **400 lbs** -- significantly heavier but far more durable. 25 feet long, ~4 feet wide, 18 inches deep.

### Final Results (Top 3)

1. **Manitoba** -- Winning time: 507 hours, 21 minutes, 51 seconds (another source gives 531 hours, 6 minutes, 16 seconds -- the discrepancy may reflect different timing methods or total vs. racing time)
2. **British Columbia** -- 509 hours, 41 minutes
3. **Alberta** -- 511 hours, 33 minutes

Bottom three (in order): Northwest Territories, Yukon, Nova Scotia.

Manitoba beat British Columbia by more than two hours. Manitoba also won several sprint races, including a 4,500m sprint on the final day.

### Team Manitoba (Winners)
Captain **Norm Crerar** and **Gib McEachern** (both 27) were the most decorated paddlers, having won the Flin Flon Trout Festival Canoe Derby three times as amateurs and six times as professionals. Full roster:
- Norm Crerar (Captain)
- Gib McEachern
- Wayne "Salty" Soltys
- Roger Carriere
- Joe Michelle
- John Norman
- Blair Harvey (from Winnipeg, Flin Flon Canoe Club alumnus)
- Jim Rheaume (Chief Voyageur)
- Don Starkell
- David Wells

Nearly the entire team was from the Flin Flon area. The team was inducted into the **Manitoba Sports Hall of Fame** in 2010.

### Don Starkell
Member of the winning Manitoba team. Later became famous for the longest canoe journey ever recorded: Winnipeg to Belem, Brazil (19,603 km) with his son Dana, entered in the Guinness Book of World Records. Also attempted a kayak traverse of the Northwest Passage in 1990, losing fingertips and toes to frostbite. Inducted individually into the Manitoba Sports Hall of Fame in 2006.

### Notable Facts
- Nova Scotia was the only team not sponsored by its provincial government; they struggled to finance the $5,000 entry fee. None of their paddlers had extensive canoe experience before the pageant.
- At North Bay, Ontario (August 21), the voyageurs were joined by four canoes of 24 young Jesuits retracing 17th-century missionary routes.
- At La Pas, Manitoba, paddlers re-enacted Henry Kelsey's erecting of a cross in 1691.
- The NWT team averaged 10.5 km/hour and 60-65 strokes per minute.

### Prizes
- Every man who completed the trip: $1,000
- First place team: additional $1,500 per man
- Second and third place: additional $500 per man
- Each finisher received an Expo 67 commemorative watch

### Canoe Fates (current locations)
- *Pierre Radisson* (Manitoba): Fort Dauphin Museum, refurbished
- *David Thompson* (Alberta): roadside shelter at Rocky Mountain House (installed on 25th anniversary)
- *Henry Kelsey* (Saskatchewan): Western Development Museum, Saskatoon
- *Samuel de Champlain* (New Brunswick): Woodsmen's Museum, Boisetown, Miramichi River
- *William McGillivray* (Ontario): with a paddler's family near Thunder Bay, needs restoration
- *Robert Campbell* and *Alexander Mackenzie* (Yukon/NWT): in service at a BC middle school's big canoe program
- Three canoes have been lost/destroyed

### Film and Documentary
- **Glenn Fallis** (Ontario team member) created an hour-long documentary using still photos and short videos, narrated for posterity; he has given public presentations (e.g., Millbrook, ON, December 2024)
- CBC Archives has video of both the launch at Rocky Mountain House and the finish at Expo 67
- Bill Mason attended the 1965 planning meeting but did not make a film of the Pageant itself
- Academic treatment: **Misao Dean**, "The Centennial Voyageur Canoe Pageant as Historical Re-enactment," *Journal of Canadian Studies* 40:3 (Fall 2006), pp. 43-67. Later incorporated into her book *Inheriting a Canoe Paddle* (U of T Press, 2013).

## PART 2: THE CCA CENTENARY JOURNEY

### Overview
A separate, youth/camping-focused canoe expedition organized by the **Canadian Camping Association (CCA)** and the **Ontario Camping Association (OCA)** as their centennial project in 1967. This was NOT a competitive race but an educational canoe trip along historic routes.

### Key Organizer
- **Bruce Hodgins** (1931-2019): Trent University professor, Canadian history expert, and leader of Camp Wanapitei. He was an "extremely active member of the committee that organized the Centenary Journey." His expertise in Canadian history and canoe routes was described as "a key to the success of the project." Later received the Dorothy Walter Award of Excellence from OCA and the Jack Pearse Award of Honour from CCA (2013).

### Structure
- A **cross-Canada canoe route** was laid out from coast to coast by the organizing committee
- **Campers in every province** paddled a section of the route -- it was a relay-style project, not one group going the whole way
- The committee began planning as early as **April 23, 1964** (first committee minutes on record)
- Progress reports were issued (e.g., November 1966)
- A **National Campfire** was held on **August 9, 1967** as part of the program
- A book, **"A Log of Canada's Centenary Journey,"** was published in **1971**

### Participants
- Participants came from organized camps across Canada, including YMCA camps
- Confirmed participants include a group from the **Regina YMCA Camp** who paddled a Saskatchewan section (photographed on Mirond Lake and the Sturgeon Weir River)
- **Camp Deka** (BC) participated; a 2007 *Kanawa* magazine article by Greg Brown titled "Memories of the Centenary Journey" describes their involvement
- One group set off from **Mahood Lake, British Columbia** (photos in archives)

### Requirements
- Participants had to know how to handle a canoe in white water
- Had to be experienced in camping skills: building fireplaces/fires, cooking, pot holders
- Needed proficiency in map reading and compass work
- Exploration and research were "an important part of the responsibilities" -- participants documented old cabins, met local inhabitants on side trips

### Archival Records (Trent University)
The primary records are held in three collections at **Trent University Archives**, Peterborough, Ontario:
- **OCA fonds (72-007):** Personnel data sheets, trip logs, area histories, trip reports, photographs, historical essays on canoe routes, correspondence (1965-68), original CCA proposal and estimated expenses (1964), Ontario Committee minutes (1964-67), application forms, press releases
- **CCA fonds (78-004):** National committee minutes (1964-67), chairman's reports, health and safety criteria, progress reports, maps, instructions for writing trip reports, safety recommendations
- **OCA 1978 additions (78-006):** Additional materials
- A **rolled map of the Centenary route** exists in the Trent University Map Collection

### Connection to Quebec / Camp Kanawana
- The CCA fonds include materials on **"agency liaison with the Quebec section"** of the Canadian Camping Association, suggesting Quebec camps did participate
- **No direct mention of Camp Kanawana** was found in any search results for the Centenary Journey
- However, given that the journey involved "campers in every province" and YMCA camps specifically participated (confirmed for Saskatchewan), and that the CCA had a Quebec section, it is plausible but unconfirmed that Quebec YMCA camps including Kanawana may have been involved
- The detailed participant lists are in the archival records at Trent University and are not digitized online

### Later Echo
In 2006-2009, Bruce Hodgins chaired a similar project: the **Ontario Journey** celebrating OCA's 75th anniversary, involving historical canoe, hiking, sailing, and kayaking trips by campers from many Ontario camps. A book documenting those trips was published in 2009.
