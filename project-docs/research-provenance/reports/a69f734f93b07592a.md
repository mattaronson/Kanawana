### NEW FACTS DISCOVERED (not already in the article/KB)

**1. Founding date discrepancy: 1915 vs 1917**
- The YWCA Montreal 150-year timeline (2025) states: "In 1915, the first summer camp for girls in Canada opened its doors: Camp Oolahwan."
- The YWCA Camp Oolahwan dedicated page and all other sources consistently say "founded in 1917."
- The 140th anniversary press release (2015, Newswire) also references 1915.
- Edgar was YWCA Girls' Work Secretary from 1915-1919 and "Director of Camp Oolahwan in the Laurentians" during this period per the Trent University archival finding aid.
- **Possible explanation:** The camp may have had a precursor or trial season in 1915 before formal establishment on Lake Walfred in 1917. The centennial was celebrated in 2017 (not 2015), confirming 1917 as the official founding year.
- **Source:** YWCA Montreal 150-year timeline (ydesfemmesmtl.org/en/150-years/); Newswire press release (2015).

**2. Closure date discrepancy: 2004 vs 2005**
- The "Blue Skies Today" blog (May 2007) states the camp "closed in 2004, a victim of rising costs and changing times."
- Most other sources (YWCA, West Island Blog, etc.) say the camp operated "from 1917 to 2005."
- The property was sold in 2005 per the Journal Acces article.
- **Possible explanation:** The last camp season may have been summer 2004, with the property sale completing in 2005. The YWCA may count 2005 as the end because that's when the property was disposed of.
- **Source:** Blue Skies Today blog (blueskiestoday.blogspot.com/2007/05/day-is-done-gone-sun.html)

**3. Reasons for closure: "rising costs and changing times"**
- The blog post explicitly names "rising costs and changing times" as the reason for closure, which is the first explicit reason found (the article currently notes no public YWCA statement of reasons).
- **Source:** Blue Skies Today blog (2007)

**7. Mary S. Edgar's full biographical arc (expanded)**
- Born May 23, 1889 in Sundridge, Ontario to Joseph Edgar and Mary Little
- Educated at Sundridge Public School, Barrie High School, Havergal Ladies College (Toronto), extension courses at University of Chicago
- Graduated from YWCA National Training School, New York City, 1915
- 1912-1914: First National Girls' Work, YWCA, in Canada
- 1915-1919: Girls' Work Secretary, YWCA Montreal, and Director of Camp Oolahwan
- 1920: Purchased property on Lake Bernard
- 1922: Opened Glen Bernard Camp with 38 campers
- Directed Glen Bernard until retirement in 1956
- 1925: Wrote "God Who Touchest Earth with Beauty" -- won first prize in ACA hymn competition, published 1926
- Author of "Wood-Fire and Candle-Light" (1945), "The Christmas Wreath of Verse" (1967), and other books
- Died September 17, 1973 in Toronto
- Archival collections at Trent University (82-010) and Queen's University
- **Sources:** Wikipedia, Trent University Archives, search snippets from hymn sites
