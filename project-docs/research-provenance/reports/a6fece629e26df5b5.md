### Findings

#### 1. Did Kanawana send a delegation to Expo 67?

**Answer: Unknown -- no evidence found online, but archival evidence suggests some connection exists.**

There is no publicly accessible evidence that Camp Kanawana sent a delegation to visit Expo 67. However, Concordia University Archives hold a dedicated sub-series P0145/13E titled "Expo 1967/Canadian Centennial" within the YMCA of Montreal fonds. The existence of this sub-series strongly suggests the YMCA of Montreal had organized engagement with Expo 67, but the contents are only accessible through physical archive visit.

#### 2. Was there a YMCA pavilion at Expo 67?

**Answer: No.** There was no YMCA pavilion at Expo 67. The exposition featured:
- A **Youth Pavilion** (built by 35 unnamed youth associations over 3 years; whether YMCA was one of the 35 is unknown)
- An **International Scout Centre** (sponsored by H.J. Heinz Co. of Canada, with 100-member Scout Service Corps)
- A **Sermons from Science Pavilion** (Moody Institute of Science; 840,000 visitors)
- A **Christian Pavilion** (1.3 million visitors)

None of these were YMCA-operated. The YMCA's involvement with Expo 67 appears to have been institutional/organizational rather than through a physical pavilion.

#### 3. What was the "Canadian Centennial family camping project"?

**Answer: Details remain locked in archives.** The Concordia University Archives reference a "Canadian Centennial family camping project, 1966-1967" within the YMCA camping records (sub-series 12A). This was likely a national YMCA initiative to promote family camping as a centennial celebration activity. No published description of this project was found online despite 8+ searches with varied terms. The project may have been connected to the broader movement to encourage outdoor recreation during the centennial year, but specifics (scope, participants, locations, outcomes) require physical archive access.

#### 4. The YMCA of Montreal's Centennial/Expo Project Committee

**Key finding:** The YMCA of Montreal had a dedicated **Centennial/Expo Project Committee** that produced a "Summary Historical Statement / resume historique" in 1966. This document is held in sub-series 1B of the fonds. The existence of a formal committee proves the YMCA of Montreal was actively planning centennial and Expo-related activities, but the details of what they planned and executed are only in the archives.

#### 5. The Centennial Voyageur Canoe Pageant and Expo 67

The Pageant concluded at the Expo 67 site on September 4, 1967. This is already documented in the existing centennial-1967 article. There is no evidence that Kanawana was directly involved in the competitive Pageant (Kanawana participated in the separate CCA Centenary Journey).

#### 6. Context: What the YMCA of Montreal was doing in 1967

In 1967, the YMCA of Montreal was undergoing a significant institutional shift:
- The Park Avenue (Mile End) YMCA launched the **Mile End West Project** -- community action and immigrant support, funded federally
- 1967 was the first year individual Canadian YMCAs sponsored specific overseas "World Service" projects
- Kanawana was in its **final year as a boys-only camp** (1894-1967)
- A **Young Adult Centennial Conference** was held at Camp Otoreke, July 8-15, 1967
