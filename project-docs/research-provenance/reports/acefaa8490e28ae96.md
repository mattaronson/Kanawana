## Research Report: Terry Mosher (Aislin) and Camp Kanawana

### 1. Biography -- Early Life, Family, Education

- **Full name:** Christopher Terry Mosher, OC
- **Born:** November 11, 1942, in Ottawa, Ontario -- "within sight of the Parliament Buildings on Remembrance Day 1942"
- **Childhood:** Attended 14 different schools across Montreal, Toronto, and Quebec City. Lived near the Montreal Forum as a child. At age 12 (1955), he witnessed the Maurice Richard Riot, which he later described as a formative experience: "I realized, wait a minute, some of these French people are pretty angry. And it's not just hockey."
- **Education:** Quit high school to hitchhike across North America for two years. Landed in Quebec City, where he drew portraits and caricatures of tourists on Tresor Street (1963-1967). Gained entry to the Ecole des Beaux-arts (now part of UQAM) by forging his high school diploma -- which he calls "his most successful work." Graduated in 1967.
- **Family:** Two daughters -- Aislinn and Jessica, both McGill graduates. The pen name "Aislin" is a rendering of his elder daughter's name (minus the second 'n'). He said it "started out as a bit of a joke" when signing street caricatures. He has stated the legacy he wants most is to be remembered as "a good dad, good grandfather and pretty good husband."

### 2. Connection to Camp Kanawana

### 3. The Pip Award -- Details

- **Named for:** Philip "Pip" Caddell (1913-2004), WWII officer, Montreal executive and community volunteer, and his grandson James Caddell (1973-2005), UN Peacekeeper and public servant. Pip was a camper in 1928; James was a camper and counsellor in the 1980s-90s.
- **Established:** 2007
- **Purpose:** "To recognize people who have taken the values they learned at Kanawana and applied them to the larger world." Sponsored by Andrew Caddell.
- **Committee:** Composed of Kanawana alumni.
- **Other known recipients:** Richard "Itche" Kerr (volunteer for physically challenged); Richard Patten (former Ontario cabinet minister); Stuart McLean (broadcaster/author); Bruce Netherwood (YMCA leader/author); John Cleghorn (former CEO, Royal Bank); the late Sam Lazarus and the Lazarus family; Jeniene Philips Birks (broadcaster/community volunteer); Carol Skinner (2016, ALS advocate); Chris Adam (2017, environmentalist/teacher); Marina Sharpe (2018, refugee advocate); Dr. James Orbinski (2024, physician/humanitarian).

### 4. Career Highlights

- **First cartoon published:** September 1967 (Charles de Gaulle caricature in Saturday Night magazine)
- **Montreal Star:** Staff cartoonist 1969
- **Montreal Gazette:** Joined 1972; still active (two cartoons per week as of recent reports)
- **1972 Canada-Russia Summit Series:** Traveled to Moscow to illustrate the series for the Gazette, Maclean's, and McClelland & Stewart
- **1993 House of Commons censure:** First political cartoonist censured in the House of Commons (cartoon of Mulroney tripped by Trudeau)
- **Total output:** Over 14,000 editorial cartoons; 50+ books published
