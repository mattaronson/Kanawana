### 1. "YMCA camp songs" history tradition 1920s 1930s

**Confirmations only.** The ACA article "Camp Songs -- History and Traditions" is already cited as `src_aca_camp_songs_history`. Key points confirmed:
- YMCA camping began 1885 with Camp Baldhead/Dudley
- Camp singing likely predated organized camps at city YMCAs
- "Kum Bah Yah" written by Norman Frey in 1930s NYC, introduced at YMCA conference at Green Lake, Wisconsin in early 1950s by Larry Eisenberg
- Pete Seeger at 1987 International Camping Congress attributed camp singing origins to Camp Gospel Revival Meetings

**Minor new detail:** Rev. Larry Eisenberg is described as "one of camp history's most influential songleaders." The ACA article author (unnamed in search results) claims 84 years of personal history with ACA.

### 4. YMCA camp cheers "section cheer" tradition

**Confirmations.** Modern YMCA camps confirm the tradition of group/section cheers:
- YMCA of Greater Brandywine: campers create songs, skits, or cheers to perform on Fridays; winners receive the "spirit stick"
- Riverbrook Regional YMCA: Color War features five teams competing in entrance, mascot, cheer, and dance categories during "Big Show"
- Camp Frank A. Day (YMCA, MA): Olympics events end with "skit and chant battle at the Great Council Ring"

This supports the article's inference that L&V Games at Kanawana "almost certainly" featured section cheers during a culminating "Sing" event.

Sources: [YMCA Greater Brandywine traditions](https://ymcagbw.org/blog/camp/10-best-summer-camp-traditions-y), [Riverbrook YMCA](https://wiltonymca.org/camp-song/)

### 8. "camp song" "YMCA" collection Cooperative Recreation Service

**Significant new contextual finding.** The Cooperative Recreation Service (CRS), founded by Lynn Rohrbough and Katherine Ferris Rohrbough in the late 1920s in Delaware, Ohio, was the major publisher of camp songbooks used across YMCA camps, Scout camps, and churches from the 1930s through 1970s. Key details:

- Started as "Church Recreation Service" while Rohrboughs were at Boston University School of Theology
- Moved to a farm near Delaware, Ohio in 1929; converted barn into print shop
- Published pocket-sized songbook pamphlets used extensively by camps
- Notable titles: *Handy play party book* (1940), *Work and sing* (1944), *Songs of all time* (1946), *Songs of the wigwam* (1955), *African songs* (1958)
- Now operates as **World Around Songs** (transferred to Jalopy Records in 2020)
- **Full archival collection at Library of Congress**: 29,950 items, 107 containers, 42.5 linear feet

The "Canadian YMCA song book" held in Concordia's P0145/12B07 (1941-1945) may well be a CRS publication or a Canadian equivalent. This is worth noting as context.

Sources: [Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/3/resources/289), [World Around Songs history](https://www.worldaroundsongs.com/our-history), [SFDH encyclopedia entry](https://sfdh.us/encyclopedia/cooperative_recreation_service.html)

### 9. YMCA camp musical traditions 1940s 1950s council ring

**Confirmations and minor new details:**
- Council Ring at Silver Bay YMCA (NY): campfire ring rebuilt 1947, rededicated recently with Order of the Arrow
- Camp Frank A. Day (YMCA): council ring used for cheer and song battles
- Camp Woodstock YMCA: 100-year celebration featured council ring dedication and time capsule
- The council ring concept is traced to Jens Jensen (landscape architect) and to Ernest Thompson Seton's Woodcraft Indians (1910 Silver Bay camp)

The article already covers the Kanawana Council Ring thoroughly via McMorris thesis.
