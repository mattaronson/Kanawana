### New Facts Discovered (beyond what's in the existing stub)

**Successor Program:**
- When Camp Oolahwan closed in 2005, YWCA Montreal created "Summer Space Day Camp" -- an urban day camp for teenagers aged 10-13 sharing the same objectives. This is the first concrete detail about age range: the successor served ages 10-13, suggesting Oolahwan likely served a similar or broader age range.

**YWCA Pre-History (1915):**
- The YWCA Montreal website states that in 1915 -- two years before founding Camp Oolahwan -- it was "firmly believed that for a young woman to be successful at her job, she must gain self-confidence." The YWCA claims Camp Oolahwan was "the first summer camp for girls in Canada" and that the idea preceded the 1917 founding. The YWCA also extended services to older women in 1917, "allowing female workers to benefit from a summer vacation at a modest price" -- this may refer to an adult program connected to Oolahwan.

**Programming Philosophy:**
- Activities were designed to "encourage self-esteem and build friendly, healthy and lasting relationships" at "low cost out in nature."
- The camp's central focus was "the emancipation of young women and the development of their self-confidence" and the ability to "laugh and have fun."
- While no specific activity list was found for Oolahwan, Camp Ouareau (co-founded by Oolahwan's Ferna Halliday in 1922 with an identical mission) offered: swimming, canoeing, kayaking, windsurfing, sailing, survival skills, archery, climbing, tennis, basketball, arts, pottery, dance, theater, and language courses. Camp Ouareau's 1922 mission explicitly included "land and water sports, horse riding, the study of nature, crafts, and canoeing expeditions." Given the direct personnel link (Halliday directed girls at Oolahwan 1918-1921, then co-founded Ouareau in 1922), Oolahwan's programming was very likely the template.

**Mary Edgar -- Fuller Biography:**
- Born May 23, 1889 in Sundridge, Ontario (daughter of Joseph Edgar and Mary Little)
- Educated at Sundridge Public School, Barrie High School, Havergal Ladies College (Toronto), extension courses at University of Chicago and University of Toronto, lectures at Teachers' College Columbia University
- Graduated from the National Training School of the YWCA, New York City, 1915
- 1912-1914: First National Girls' Work, YWCA Canada
- 1915-1919: Girls' Work Secretary in Montreal AND Director of Camp Oolahwan
- 1920: Four months volunteer work for YWCA in Japan; purchased property on Lake Bernard near Sundridge
- 1922: Opened Glen Bernard Camp with 38 campers
- Directed Glen Bernard until retirement in 1956
- Worked with YWCA, Canadian Girls in Training (C.G.I.T.), Girl Guides of Canada, Canadian Camping Association, Ontario Camping Association
- Author of *Wood-fire and Candlelight* (1945), *Under Open Skies* (1956), *The Christmas Wreath of Verse* (1967), and *Once there was a Camper*
- Best known hymn: "God Who Touchest Earth with Beauty" (translated into several languages including Cree)
- Died September 17, 1973 in Toronto
- Her papers are at the Trent University Archives (Mary S. Edgar fonds, 82-010)

**Ferna Halliday -- Additional Detail:**
- Graduate of Queen's University
- At the time she co-founded Camp Ouareau (1922), "Quebec law made it so that women could not vote, nor buy or own property without their father or husband's permission." She and Mabel Jamieson had to "fight against the odds to get their fathers' permission" to buy the land.
- Described as one of the "early pioneers of camping for girls"

**Name Origin:**
- The closest linguistic match is the Lakota Sioux word "Olowan" (olowan) meaning "song" or "to sing." Not confirmed but plausible given the era's practice of using Indigenous-sounding names for camps.

**Former Site Today:**
- Lake Walfred in Sainte-Marguerite-du-Lac-Masson now hosts "Club Lac Walfred" -- luxury cottage rentals with lake access. This appears to be on or near the former camp property, suggesting the land was sold and developed after the 2005 closure.

**Centennial Reunion -- Additional Details:**

### Answers to the Open Questions

1. **Programming:** No specific activity list found. Best inference is from Camp Ouareau (co-founded by Oolahwan staff member Halliday): water sports, nature study, crafts, riding, canoeing trips, campfire programs. Philosophy centered on self-esteem, self-confidence, fun, and healthy relationships.

2. **Why the camp closed:** Not answered in any source. The YWCA simply says it "ceased to exist physically in 2005." The appearance of Club Lac Walfred (cottage development) on Lake Walfred suggests the property was sold, possibly for financial reasons. The replacement with an urban day camp (Summer Space) for ages 10-13 may indicate the residential camp model had become financially unsustainable.

3. **Capacity and age range:** Not directly stated. The successor Summer Space served ages 10-13. Glen Bernard Camp (Edgar's next camp) started with 38 campers in 1922 on 225 acres -- this may approximate Oolahwan's early scale.

4. **Additional inter-camp visits with Kanawana:** Not found beyond the 1946 visit already documented.

5. **OurOntario 109-page paper:** Inaccessible via web fetch (403). Remains a high-priority unextracted source.

6. **Surviving photographs/ephemera:** eBay listing confirms at least one item existed. The YWCA website states they "will add more photos to the archive as finances permit." The CBC audio segment may contain verbal descriptions.

7. **Programming influence on Kanawana:** Not directly documented, but the YMCA/YWCA institutional connection and the 1946 inter-camp visit suggest at least awareness of each other's programs.
