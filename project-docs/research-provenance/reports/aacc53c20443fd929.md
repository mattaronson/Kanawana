## Key Findings

### 1. Was she secretary general of the YMCA or the YWCA?

**The claim she was "secretary general of the Y.M.C.A. of Montreal" almost certainly contains an error, and the correct organization was likely the YWCA.** The evidence strongly favors YWCA:

- Her 1915 role was explicitly at the "Christian Association of Young Women of Canada" (YWCA) as National Student Secretary
- Her co-founder Ferna Halliday was Secretary of the YWCA of Montreal (1918-1921)
- Their mentor/colleague Mary Susanna Edgar was Girls' Work Secretary of YWCA Montreal
- All three women worked in the same YWCA network and were involved with YWCA Camp Oolahwan
- No independent YMCA record names Jamieson in any capacity
- The YMCA "secretary general" (or "general secretary") was the organization's chief executive -- it would be extraordinary and historically unprecedented for a woman to hold that title at any YMCA branch in the 1910s-1920s
- Every source making the YMCA claim traces back to a single origin: the Camp Ouareau official website (which Wikipedia cites)

**However, this has NOT been independently verified.** The only way to resolve this definitively is to check the Concordia University Archives, P0145 (YMCA of Montreal fonds), which contains year books and personnel records from 1915-1922.

### 2. Biographical Details

All known facts (every source traces back to the Camp Ouareau website):
- **Full name:** Mabel C. Jamieson (middle name unknown; "C." is the only initial given)
- **Birth/death dates:** Unknown from web sources
- **Education:** Completed studies at "Pupils College in Richmond" in 1918 (institution unidentifiable)
- **Career:** School teacher; National Student Secretary of YWCA Canada (1915); described as "secretary general of the YMCA [likely YWCA] of Montreal" c.1915-1920; member of the Committee of the Sixteen (Montreal anti-vice committee, 1919); co-founded Camp Ouareau (1922); sole director 1924-1934; succeeded by Dorothy Percival
- **Described as:** "school teacher," "university woman," had "considerable experience of camping and summer camp activities"

### 3. History of Camp Ouareau's Founding

- **1917:** Mary Susanna Edgar founded Camp Oolahwan (YWCA Montreal's girls' camp) at Lac Walfred, Sainte-Marguerite-du-Lac-Masson
- **1918-1921:** Ferna Halliday served as YWCA Montreal secretary, responsible for Camp Oolahwan girls
- **1921:** Jamieson and Halliday acquired land from Mr. Leguerrier on Lac Ouareau, requiring their fathers' permission under Quebec property law
- **1922:** Camp Ouareau opened (same year as Glen Bernard Camp in Ontario -- both among the oldest girls-only camps in Canada)
- **1922-1923:** Co-directed by Jamieson and Halliday
- **1924:** Halliday departed to found Camp Oconto (Tichborne, Ontario); Jamieson became sole director
- **1934:** Dorothy Percival succeeded Jamieson as director

Camp Ouareau was NOT a YWCA camp -- it was independently founded. But both founders came from the YWCA network and brought their YWCA camping experience to the new venture.
