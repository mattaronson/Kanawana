### Key Chronological Inconsistency Confirmed

The Wikipedia Camp Ouareau article contains an internal chronological contradiction:
- It states she was "National Student Secretary of the YWCA of Canada in 1915"
- It states she completed her studies "in 1918 at Pupils College in Richmond"
- It then says she "became secretary general of the Y.M.C.A. of Montreal at least from 1915 to 1920"

The phrase "at least from 1915 to 1920" contradicts "after completing her studies in 1918" -- she cannot have become secretary general from 1915 if she only completed her studies in 1918. The "from 1915" date likely refers to her YWCA National Student Secretary role, and the Wikipedia text appears to have garbled the timeline.

### YMCA vs. YWCA Confusion -- Analysis

Based on all evidence gathered:

1. **Ferna Halliday** was definitively "Secretary of the Christian Association of Young Women of Montreal" (YWCA Montreal) from 1918-1921 -- this is confirmed by multiple independent sources including the YWCA Montreal history page and Camp Oolahwan centennial materials.

2. **Mary Susanna Edgar** was "Girls' Work Secretary of the Montreal YWCA" from 1915-1919 and founded Camp Oolahwan in 1917.

3. **Mabel Jamieson** is described as "National Student Secretary of the Christian Association of Young Women of Canada" (YWCA Canada) in 1915, then as "secretary general of the Y.M.C.A. of Montreal" -- note the claim is YMCA (not YWCA).

4. There is no independent verification of Jamieson holding ANY role at YMCA Montreal. The claim appears ONLY in the Camp Ouareau Wikipedia article and the Camp Ouareau official history page (which may be the Wikipedia source or vice versa). The Concordia University Archives (YMCA Montreal fonds P0145) would be the place to verify this.

5. The claim remains plausibly YWCA, not YMCA, given that all three women (Edgar, Halliday, Jamieson) were in the YWCA network. However, it is also possible that the "Y.M.C.A." claim is accurate -- women did hold staff positions at the YMCA in this era.
