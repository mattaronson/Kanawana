### Findings

**The transition date remains unknown.** After 21 distinct searches across multiple agents, no source provides a specific date for when Kanawana switched from "Each for all and all for each" to "Non Nobis Solum."

**The transition window is 1927-1993:**
- The English motto "Each for all and all for each" is documented in the 1922 brochure (`/home/user/Kanawana/sources/cache/brochures/1922-brochure.txt`, line 65) and in a 1927 source (per McMorris thesis).
- The 1935 History of Kamp Kanawana does NOT mention either motto (confirmed by grep of the cached file).
- The Latin motto "Non Nobis Solum" is first attested in the 1993 YMCA video *Kamp Kanawana: The Experience that Lasts a Lifetime*.
- A 2026 Postmedia article quotes a camp alumnus saying the motto "goes beyond camp" (cached at `/home/user/Kanawana/sources/cache/postmedia_kanawana_nature_deficit_2026.txt`).

**The LCC connection is the strongest hypothesis but remains unproven:**
- Lower Canada College adopted "Non Nobis Solum" in 1909 from founder Charles Sanderson Fosbery's family crest.
- The LCC school song "Non Nobis Solum" was first sung at the 1936 return-to-school assembly, making the phrase recognizable in anglophone Montreal.
- Stuart McLean attended LCC, then worked at Kanawana from 1969 -- the only documented personal bridge.
- No institutional link between LCC and YMCA Montreal has been documented.

**The English motto was not unique to Kanawana:**
- Camp Becket (MA, est. 1903): one of 8 mottos linked to director Henry Gibson (1904-1927).
- Camp Hayo-Went-Ha (MI, est. 1904): primary rallying cry, still in use today.
- The phrase echoes the Swiss national motto "Unus pro omnibus, omnes pro uno" and Dumas's "All for one and one for all" but the YMCA variant appears to be an independent adaptation.

**The Penton book *Non Nobis Solum: The History of Lower Canada College* (1972, D. Stephen Penton, 336 pp.)** is on Internet Archive but could not be fetched due to proxy restrictions. This book may contain references to YMCA/camp connections and is the highest-priority unread source for this question.

### Findings

**New facts discovered:**
- **TELUS three-season educational pavilion (2010):** TELUS donated $50,000 to Camp Y Kanawana in 2010 for construction of a "three-season educational pavilion" on the camp site. The pavilion includes a classroom for 50 young people and two accommodations for adults. Built according to sustainable development principles.
- **Green shift expansion impact:** The virage vert and three-season programming allowed Kanawana to welcome "four times more young people than before."
- **Green sanitary installations detail:** Two new green sanitary buildings featured showers with heat exchangers to recover heat from wastewater, composting toilets, skylights, and windows promoting natural ventilation and lighting. The conventional septic system was replaced with an artificial marsh (marais artificiel) treating grey and black water.
- **Installation timeline confirmed:** Green sanitary installations were tested during summers 2007 and 2008.

**Status of historical buildings (summary of what is/is not known):**
- **Lakeside Pavilion (1922):** Built alongside Dining Pavilion. Whether it survives in some form (as Activity Lodge? Beach Lodge?) is unknown. No web source addresses this.
- **Hospital (1922):** A new hospital was built in 1922. The current infirmary has 5 beds. Whether it occupies the same structure is unknown.
- **Icehouse (1923):** "Kept well filled" per 1923 brochure. Its fate is undocumented. No web source mentions it.
- **Outdoor Chapel (1922+):** In use by 1922, documented through 1938 (choir by Benny Leshley). Whether it remains in active use is unknown. Not listed on current YMCA facilities page.
- **Clivus Multrum installation:** 16 commercial composters, 32 dry toilets, 2 central buildings. Installed as part of 2006 green shift; tested 2007-2008. Grand Portage cabin demolished c. 2006 for the new washroom buildings. The system saves 576,000 gallons of water annually.

**No web source addresses whether the 1922 Lakeside Pavilion, Hospital, Icehouse, or Chapel still exist.** The Concordia Archives sub-series P0145/12B03 ("Land, facilities, equipment, supplies") is the most promising unexamined source for building histories.
