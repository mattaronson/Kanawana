## Research Findings: Edgar E. Smee (c.1912-2002)

### Obituary (Globe and Mail, February 11, 2002)

The obituary confirms:
- Died peacefully at the **Glebe Centre** in Ottawa on **February 7, 2002**, aged **89**
- Survived by wife **Frieda**, daughters **Linda, Susan, Sydney (spelled "Syndey" in one version), and Sonia**, and brothers **Norman and Leonard**
- Memorial gathering held **Saturday, February 16, 2:00 p.m.** at the **Workers Arts and Heritage Centre, 51 Stuart Street, Hamilton**
- Donations requested to the **Conserver Society of Hamilton and District**, Fiddlers Green Postal Station, PO Box 81225, Ancaster, ON L9G 4X2

### Biographical Narrative (from EOY Awards and Hamilton Community Foundation pages)

The key narrative, repeated across multiple sources:

> "When Ed Smee returned, after a 30-year absence, to his home town of Hamilton in 1968, he was anxious to show off the beauty spots he'd remembered to his wife Frieda. The first stop was the Red Hill Creek where as a boy he used to swim. There, the outlet for a storm sewer had dumped a foul-smelling mess. The incident sparked a 'deep anger' in Ed, and he resolved to do something about Hamilton's pollution."

Additional detail from one source: he "had been raised to treasure, not squander, the earth's resources."

### Environmentalist of the Year (1983)

- Smee received the **1983 Environmentalist of the Year** award in Hamilton
- The award is formally the **Dr. Victor Cecilioni Award for Environmentalist of the Year**, established by the Conserver Society
- The annual awards dinner has been running **since 1979**, initiated by CHOP (the predecessor to the Conserver Society)
- A dedicated page exists at `eoyawards.com/recipients/edgar-e-smee-environmentalist-of-the-year-1983/` (returned 403 on direct fetch)

### CHOP and the Conserver Society

Timeline confirmed by multiple sources:
- **1969**: CHOP (Clear Hamilton of Pollution) founded. Ed Smee was the **founding Chair**
- **1971**: Save Our Bay (SOB) formed as a parallel citizen group
- **1974**: IJC first listed Hamilton Harbour as "area not meeting water quality objectives"
- **1982**: A committee of community organizations convened to expand and continue the Environmentalist of the Year Award program
- **1983**: The Conserver Society was "established by individuals wanting to improve the quality of life of their community" (this may refer to the formalization process)
- **1984**: CHOP incorporated as **Conserver Society of Hamilton and District Inc.**
- **1985**: Hamilton Harbour designated an Area of Concern
- **1986**: First Environmental Trust Fund initiated, **chaired by Ed Smee**
- **1992**: Hamilton Harbour Remedial Action Plan published
- **1999**: Environmental Trust Fund **renamed the Ed Smee Conserver Society Environmental Fund** in his honour and transferred to management by the Hamilton Community Foundation

### Ed Smee's Credited Accomplishments

Per the Conserver Society and Hamilton Community Foundation:
- **Founding Chair** of the Conserver Society
- Credited with **helping to create the first action plan for Hamilton Harbour/Burlington Bay**
- Led by example through "his personal life work on environmental issues"
- The Conserver Society under his leadership helped initiate: composting programs, bicycle paths, pesticide use reduction education, limiting urban sprawl and destruction of natural areas, participation in regional waste-management, air quality and transportation initiatives

### The Ed Smee Conserver Society Environmental Fund

- A **donor advised fund** managed by the Hamilton Community Foundation
- The Conserver Society acts as advisor, recommending grant recipients
- Supports **small projects focused on education, research, or advocacy** aimed at protecting the natural environment in Hamilton and Burlington
- Funding cycles every two years (2021, 2023, 2025...)
- Dr. Ken Ingham's will included a bequest to this fund

### Red Hill Creek Context

Contextual findings supporting Smee's story:
- Hamilton Harbour pollution was recognized as far back as the 1850s
- Swimming in the bay was banned by Hamilton Harbour Commissioners in the **1930s** due to unchecked industrial pollution and sewage
- Municipal swimming pools arose as a "technological fix" for the recreation crisis
- Red Hill Creek has remained heavily contaminated; a 2002-2003 study found the Red Hill Cliff outfall had 160,000 cfu/100 mL (53x above provincial water quality objectives)
- As of 2020, sewage-related trash was still found near the Windermere Basin at the mouth of Red Hill Creek
