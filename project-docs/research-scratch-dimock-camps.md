### NEW MAJOR FINDING: Nelson McEwen — Key Figure Connecting Bannerman to Kanawana

**Nelson McEwen** (born c.1897, Charlottetown, PEI):
- 1919: Hired as Assistant Boys Work Secretary, Winnipeg YMCA (age 22)
- 1924-1927: Personally directed Camp Stephens, Winnipeg
- c.1927+: Recruited to Montreal YMCA as Boys Work Secretary
- c.1927-1947: **Directed Camp Kanawana** during his Montreal YMCA tenure
- Developed Hi-Y program to "massive size" in Montreal
- Conducted two National Assemblies of Hi-Y at Geneva Park (1940, 1946)
- Co-founded Canadian Camping Association with Taylor Statten (served as Secretary; Statten was President)
  - Note: CCA was formally founded in 1936 at Central YMCA Toronto with Statten as president. McEwen may have been involved in the 1946 reorganization/national assemblies rather than the original 1936 founding.
- 1947: Offered and accepted position of General Secretary, Saint John YMCA (NB)
- At Saint John, recruited Ross Bannerman, Lloyd and Niles Ring, Doug Allen
- Source: Camp Stephens Alumni blog (campstephensalumni.blogspot.com, 2013 posts)

**This is a previously undocumented Kanawana director.** McEwen fills part of the 1927-1947 gap in the director timeline. His dates at Kanawana are approximate: he arrived in Montreal c.1927 and left for Saint John in 1947, so he likely directed Kanawana for part or all of that 20-year period.

1. Recruited by Nelson McEwen at Saint John YMCA (post-1947)

### New Facts Discovered

**Camp Becsies:**
1. **"Camp Becsies" = Camp on Lac des Becs-scie.** The lake is located in Saint-Sauveur (83%) and Mille-Isles (17%), in the same municipality as Camp Kanawana. Area: 0.5 km², length ~2.2 km, width 155-340m. This is almost certainly one of the "3 private lakes" on or adjacent to Kanawana property.
2. Camp Becsies served campers from the **Montreal Protestant Orphans' Home** in 1934 and 1936 — a charitable/social service use of the camp.
3. In the 1960s-1971 period, Camp Becsies was repurposed for **family camping** (family camping brochure c.1962, admin records 1960-1971).
4. **Photographs of Camp Becsies** exist in the Concordia archives.
5. The two record date ranges (1929-1936 and 1960-1971) suggest the site may have been dormant or used informally between 1936 and 1960.

**Camp Dorval:**
6. Camp Dorval (P0145/12E, 1926-1928, 0.5cm records) — likely a short-lived YMCA day camp in Dorval, Quebec (West Island of Montreal, on Lake Saint-Louis). Only 0.5cm of records for 3 years suggests a small operation. Located in the town of Dorval, which is an island/waterfront community.
7. No online content found beyond the archival listing. Completely undocumented outside Concordia.

**Camp Thunderbird:**
8. No Montreal-specific Camp Thunderbird information found online. The name "Camp Thunderbird" is used by YMCA camps in British Columbia (est. 1935, Sooke Hills), Virginia (est. 1966), and Charlotte, NC — none connected to Montreal.
9. The 1974 "Report of the Staff Task Group on Outdoor Education in the Montreal YMCA" and 1975 "Proposed Hike and Trip Camp" may relate to the origin of Camp Thunderbird and/or the Wilderness Survival Camp.
10. Camp Thunderbird may have been a program name rather than a separate physical site — possibly an adventure/outdoor program run at Kanawana or elsewhere.

**Wilderness Survival Camp:**
11. Same archival-only status as Camp Thunderbird. No online content found.
12. The 1974 Outdoor Education task group report and 1975 Proposed Hike and Trip Camp are likely precursor documents. The Wilderness Survival Camp may have been the resulting program.
13. Given the 1970s date range implied by the archival context, both Camp Thunderbird and the Wilderness Survival Camp likely date from the YMCA's expansion of outdoor education programming in the mid-1970s.

**Camp Perrot (bonus finding):**
14. Camp Perrot (P0145/12G) was a boys' camp operated by Montreal YMCA. A document "Comparison of Two Boys Camps operated by Montreal YMCA" compares Camp Perrot and Kamp Kanawana from c.1945-1946.

**Contextual finding — YMCA Montreal outdoor education timeline:**
15. A 1974 "Report of the Staff Task Group on Outdoor Education in the Montreal YMCA" exists in Concordia archives (P0145/12A). This may document the rationale for creating Camp Thunderbird and/or the Wilderness Survival Camp.
16. A 1975 "Proposed Hike and Trip Camp" document exists in the same series.
