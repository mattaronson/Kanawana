# YMCA of Montreal annual reports: which summer is inside which volume

Generated by `scripts/reread/annual_report_year_ends.py`, regenerated 2026-09-06 after the scanner was fixed. Regenerate rather than hand-edit.

The association changed its financial year-end three times over this run, so there is no single rule
for turning a report into a camping season. **30 April** through the 1925 report, **31 March** from
1926 through the 99th report (year ending 31 March 1950), **31 December** from the 100th report
(1951) through the 111th (1962), **31 May** from the 112th report (year ended 31 May 1964) onward
and still in force in 1989. April, March and May years all contain the *previous* summer; a December
year contains its own.

**Both changeovers are dated from the report ordinals, and no volume is missing.** 97th (31 March
1948) through 100th (31 December 1951) is three volumes for three years, so the 100th spans
**twenty-one months**, 1 April 1950 to 31 December 1951. 109th, 110th and 111th are the December
years 1960, 1961 and 1962; the 112th is the year ended 31 May 1964, so it spans **seventeen
months**, 1 January 1963 to 31 May 1964, and contains one summer.

**The summer of 1950 falls inside the 100th report's span, and the report describes only one
summer** — "in spite of an unusually wet summer, aggregate attendance at Kamp Kanawana was above the
previous year," which reads as 1951 against 1950. So the 1950 season is not described anywhere in
the series, and the hole in the attendance table is real rather than an artefact of which volumes
were digitized. One loose end: the 100th report's balance sheet carries a 1950 comparative column
and a line reading "Balance December 31, 1950," so accounts were struck at that date even though no
report covers a year ending then.

The cache filenames are not on one convention. `sgw-ymca-annual-report-1963.txt` is the report for
the year ended 31 May 1964, named for its season; `sgw-ymca-annual-report-1984.txt` is the report for
the year ended 31 May 1984, named for its year-end. Read the title page.

UNREAD is a fact about the regex and the OCR, not about the volume. Those title pages need an eye.

**The scanner was wrong about most of what it called unreadable (2026-09-06).** The first run read
63 of 179 files and reported 116 as UNREAD. Nearly all of that was the tool, not the corpus:

- **Nineteen were the French-language editions**, which state the year-end as plainly as the English
  ones -- "Bilan au 31 mai 1983", "L'exercice financier terminé le 31 mai 1987" -- against an
  English-only regex.
- **Thirteen were camp-level reports** -- Camp Perrot, Kamp Kanawana, Point Saint Charles, Les
  Voyageurs -- which are a different document class covering ONE SEASON and named for it. The
  association's year-end question does not arise for them. They are now excluded rather than
  reported as unreadable volumes.
- **Several state their year-end only in the auditors' report**, past the 20,000-character window.
- **Several were defeated by one OCR character**: "MARCH 3lst, 1946" with an ell for the one, and
  "Bilan au \n Ÿ 31 mai 1984" with a stray glyph that is a word character to Python, so neither
  `\s` nor `\W` matched it. The day is no longer matched at all, since only the month and year are
  reported anyway.
- **Some volumes state no year-end phrase at all**, only a span: "JUNE I, 196k = MAY 31, 1965",
  where the opening year is OCR'd to nonsense and the closing one is clean. These are the 1960s
  volumes, which are exactly where the association changed its year-end and where getting the
  season wrong matters most.

**One real regression was caught and fixed while doing this.** Widening the window changed the 1989
report from May 1989 to May 1988, because the function returned the first PATTERN that matched
anywhere rather than the match nearest the start of the document. Position carries the authority
here: the title page and the auditors' report state the volume's own year-end, and a date deep
inside it is as likely to be a comparative column. Every change was checked against the previous
run; after the fix, no volume that had a reading before has a different one now.

**140 of 166 association volumes now state a year-end.** The 26 that resist are mostly pre-1900,
before Kanawana existed.

## The four transition volumes, read by eye (2026-09-06)

These are the ones that matter, because they sit either side of the change from a 31 December
year-end to a 31 May one, where a misreading moves a camping season by a year.

**1959 and 1960 are now read by the scanner.** Both say "for the year ending Dec-\nember", broken
across a line with a hyphen; joining those back up before matching was a one-line fix. **December
1959** and **December 1960**, containing the summers of 1959 and 1960. The 1960 volume also calls
itself the **one hundred and ninth year**, which matches the ordinal series exactly.

**1961 the scanner still cannot read, and the null is honest.** The volume says "of Montreal for the
year ending Dec-\nember 4lst, I96I1" — the day is OCR'd from 31 to "4lst" and the year to "I96I1",
which is not four digits. A pattern loose enough to accept "I96I1" as 1961 would be loose enough to
misread a year elsewhere, and a wrong year is worse than a null. **By eye: year ending 31 December
1961, containing the summer of 1961.** The volume calls itself the **one hundred and tenth year**,
which agrees.

**1966-67 states no year-end anywhere in its text**, only "**one hundred and fifteenth**". So this
reading is an inference from the ordinal series and is flagged as one. The anchor is firm: the
volume named 1963 says "**ONE HUNDRED AND TWELFTH YEAR**" and states a year ending May 1964, which
is the changeover the rest of this document is built on. The scanner then reads the 1964 volume as
May 1965 and the 1965 volume as May 1966 — three consecutive May year-ends. On one report a year,
the 115th is the **year ended 31 May 1967, containing the summer of 1966**, which is what the
file's own name says. **This rests on there being no skipped or doubled report between the 112th and
the 115th, which has not been checked against anything but the count.**
