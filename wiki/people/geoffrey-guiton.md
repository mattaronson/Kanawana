# Geoffrey Guiton

*Status: draft | Sources: 12*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-07*

## Overview

Geoffrey Guiton was Boys' Secretary of the YMCA of Montreal's **North Branch** from 1920 to at
least 1927, which made him the man North Branch families wrote to when they wanted to send a boy
to Kanawana. The 1922 *Kamp Kanawana* brochure lists him among the four branch secretaries
receiving camp applications, and a counsellor named Guiton turns up in a raided tent with the
North Branch tribe in the 1923 *Gas Bag*.^1

That much this project already knew, from its own cached brochures, under the initial "G." What
it did not know was his given name, when he started, when he stopped, or what he did afterwards.
The Year Book's alphabetical list of employed officers supplies the first three in a single line —
"**Guiton, Geoffrey**, Montreal, Que, North, Boys', **20**" — and a sweep of the Internet Archive
book corpus supplies a candidate for the fourth that is much larger than a camp: a Montreal
Anglican priest of the same name who founded a western missionary movement in 1929 and died in
1947.^2 ^3

**Whether those are one man is not established here**, and the section below says so at length
rather than burying it. The article is written in two halves for that reason.

## The YMCA years, which are documented

He entered Association work in **1920**, at twenty-eight if the priest's birth year belongs to
him. The Year Book prints a man's year of entry beside his name and never revises it, so the
figure follows one person across a run and separates him from others of his surname; Guiton's
reads **20** in every volume he appears in.^2

He comes in as an **assistant**. The 1919-20 volume gives the branch as "Montreal, North Br.
**Asst., Geoffrey Guiton** — Physical, Reginald P. Thomas — Boys, **Charles G. Hewett**," so the
Boys' post was Hewett's and Guiton worked under him. From the 1920-21 volume onward the order
changes and stays changed: "Physical, Reginald P. Thomas — **Boys, Geoffrey Guiton** — Asst.
Boys, Robert E. Blake — Asst. Boys, G. S. Mooney."^2

The 1923-24 volume puts the branch at **2300 Park Avenue** with **W. J. Holliday** as its
Secretary and Guiton as Boys' Secretary under him — the same address the 1922 Kanawana brochure
gives for "G. Guiton, North," and the same pairing the 1922-23 North Branch brochure gives.^1 ^2
Two documents in this project's own cache and a roster in Minnesota agree, which is more
corroboration than most names here get.

He appears in **eight consecutive volumes, 1919-20 through 1926-27, and in none after**. In the
last of them the branch has moved and been renamed — "East Montreal, North Br., **5550 Park
Ave.**" — and he is still its Boys' Secretary.^2

### He ran the branch for a while, and then he left it for a theological college

**Two paragraphs in the association's own annual reports say why the roster stops, and neither had been
read.** Both reports are already in this project — the 1928 volume is cited above for Geo. S. Mooney at
Otoreke — but they were read for their camps sections, and these sentences are in the *staff changes*
section several pages away.

**First, he was acting head of the branch.** W. J. Holliday, North Branch's Executive Secretary, was ill
through 1926, and the report for the year ending 31 March 1927 records that "Mr. T. F. Best, one of the
veteran retired Secretaries of the Association … **relieved Mr. Guiton who had been Acting Secretary in
Mr. Holliday's absence, on February 1st**."^10 So the branch Boys' Secretary had been running the whole
branch, for some months up to **1 February 1927**, in the year the roster last carries him.

**Then he resigned, and the report says where he went.** The report for the year ending 31 March 1928:
"**Mr. Geoffrey Guiton resigned as Boys' Work Secretary in September in order to complete his
theological course at the Diocesan College**, and was succeeded by **Mr. George Porteous, B.A., of
Saskatoon**."^11 On that report's fiscal year the September is **September 1927**, which is exactly where
the *Year Book* run stops.

**This is what Open Question 2 was asking for**, and it comes from the YMCA's side of the record rather
than the church's. The association states, in its own annual report, that its North Branch Boys' Work
Secretary left the post to finish a theological course **at the Diocesan College** — the Montreal
Diocesan Theological College that Open Question 1 names as the register to check. *It does not prove the
two Guitons are one man*, and the section below stands: a man completing a theological course in
1927-28 and a priest appearing in the diocese in 1929 is a fit, not an identification, and the two
records still never name each other. What has changed is the shape of the gap. It is no longer two or
three unaccounted years at the end of a YMCA career; it is a documented move from youth work into
training for ordination, in the right city, at the right college, at the right time. **The next document
to find is the college's own register for 1927-29**, and it is now being asked a much narrower question.

*And his successor is a camp director.* **George Porteous, B.A., of Saskatoon** takes the North Branch
boys' desk in September 1927 and is Kanawana's Chief Director in **1929** — see
[[people/directors-index|the directors index]]. The 1937 report adds his arrival and his departure: he
"came to us from Western Canada some ten years ago", which puts him in Montreal about 1927, and on **1
September 1937** he "resigned the Executive Secretaryship of the North Branch to accept the position of
General Secretary" of the **Brantford** association, being succeeded there by **C. J. McGerrigle**, "a
graduate of McGill University and the United Theological College", who "first served the Montreal
Association at Notre Dame de Grace, later transferring to North Branch" — and who ran Camp Otoreke.^12
**So the North Branch boys' desk passed from Guiton to Porteous to, in time, the branch itself**, and two
of the three men in that line ran a Y camp.

## The priest, who has the same name and a much larger life

Four church histories carry a **Rev. Geoffrey Guiton** of Montreal, and not one of them mentions
the YMCA.

Philip Carrington's national history has the **Fellowship of the West** formed "largely through
the enthusiasm of the Rev. Geoffrey Guiton (who later on devoted his life to missionary
[work])."^4 John Irwin Cooper's history of the Diocese of Montreal gives the Fellowship a chapter
of its own — "The Fellowship of the West: The Colonization Movement" — and names the three
priests "immediately responsible, Geoffrey Guiton, Elton Scott, and R. K. Naylor," describing
Guiton as "later a missionary."^5 Mary Elizabeth Reisner's annals of the diocese date the
founding to **1929** and record that "in 1930 three teams were sent out: Geoffrey Guiton and
Richard Haviland, W. C. Eccleston and Henry [...]."^6

The movement sent clergy from Montreal to the **Peace River** country, and there the record stops
being institutional and becomes personal. Monica Storrs, an Englishwoman running a mission in
northern British Columbia, kept diaries that have been published in two edited volumes, and
Guiton walks through both. "Our first visitors arrived — the Rev. Geoffrey Guiton and another
student called Norton Deane." He and a colleague are "the twin pillars of the Fellowship of the
West." On money: "Guiton says he can raise practically all the money through his Fellowship of
the West in Montreal." And then he goes "back to Montreal."^7 A footnote in the later volume
gives his dates: **Geoffrey Guiton (1892-1947)**.^7

The end of it is in a book about India, on the only hit in the entire corpus for "Reverend
Geoffrey Guiton": "[mis]sionary who had built up the school, the Reverend Geoffrey Guiton."^8
Neither the school nor a date is in the highlight.

## Why the two halves are not joined

The case for one man is real: an uncommon surname with an uncommon given name, in one city, in
adjacent decades, and a shape that fits — a branch Boys' Secretary of thirty-four leaves the
roster after 1926-27, and a priest of the same name appears in the diocese in 1929, which is
about how long theological college takes.

The case against treating that as settled is that **the two records never cover the same year**,
so they cannot corroborate or contradict each other. Nothing read here places one man in both
lives. That is the whole of it, and it is not enough.^9

What would settle it is small and findable: the **Montreal Diocesan Theological College**
register for the later 1920s, a **1947 obituary**, or any church source describing what Guiton
did before 1929. The last is the likeliest, because the Storrs volumes are annotated by editors
who already troubled to look up his birth and death years — a man who supplies a footnote that
precise often gets a sentence about his earlier work somewhere else in the apparatus. Both Storrs
volumes are borrow-restricted on the Internet Archive and were **not read here**; every quotation
above is a search highlight.^7

## Open Questions

1. [Critical, **narrowed 2026-09-08**] Is the North Branch Boys' Secretary the same man as the Rev.
   Geoffrey Guiton (1892-1947)? The YMCA's own 1928 annual report now says its Guiton left in
   September 1927 to complete a theological course **at the Diocesan College**, so the register to
   check is no longer a guess: **the Montreal Diocesan Theological College, admissions and graduations
   1927-1929**. A Montreal newspaper death notice for 1947 remains the other route.
2. ~~[Critical] What did he do between the 1926-27 Year Book volume and the founding of the
   Fellowship of the West in 1929?~~ **[Answered 2026-09-08, from the YMCA side.]** He resigned the
   North Branch boys' secretaryship in **September 1927** "in order to complete his theological
   course at the Diocesan College," having acted as head of the branch during Holliday's illness up to
   1 February that year. See the section above. What is still open is the church side: whether the man
   who entered that college is the man who founded the Fellowship of the West in 1929.
3. [Important] Did Guiton go to Kanawana himself in seasons other than 1923? The *Gas Bag*
   places a Guiton in camp with the North Branch tribe that year; the brochures make him the
   branch's camp contact but say nothing about attendance.
4. [Important] Which school in India, and when? The single highlight in *Journey to India* names
   neither.
5. [Nice-to-have] **Charles G. Hewett**, whom Guiton served under in 1919-20, and **Robert E.
   Blake**, who served under him — ~~none~~ neither appears anywhere else in this
   project. Each was a Montreal YMCA boys' worker in the years the camp was running.
   *(Partially resolved 2026-09-07: **G. S. Mooney** was on this list and is now off it. The annual
   report for the year ending 30 April 1928 names "Mr. Geo. S. Mooney" among the secretaries who had
   charge of Camp Otoreke's programme in the 1927 season — see
   [[site/camp-otoreke|Camp Otoreke]]. Hewett and Blake are still nowhere else.)*
