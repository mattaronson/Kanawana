## Overview

Ralph H. Dawson is one of the earliest documented participants in the history of Camp Kanawana, appearing in an 1898 Camp Jubilee photograph at Lake Saint-Joseph and later authoring "History of Kamp Kanawana" in 1933.^1 ^2 His involvement spans at least 35 years of the camp's existence, making him a uniquely long-serving figure in its history, though his formal role title has never been confirmed.

## Camp Jubilee Connection

Dawson appears in an 1898 photograph at Camp Jubilee (the original name of Camp Kanawana) alongside W. N. Cunningham, Jim Brown, and Frank L. Benedict.^1 The photograph is held in Concordia University Archives, sub-series 12L (Lac St-Joseph / Camp Jubilee).^1 His presence in 1898 — just four years after the camp's founding in 1894 — places him among the earliest generation of Kanawana participants.

## "History of Kamp Kanawana" (1933)

In 1933, Dawson wrote a document titled "History of Kamp Kanawana," which is held in Concordia University Archives sub-series 12A (Committees).^2 ^3 This history is distinct from the anonymous 1935 season chronicle "A History of Kamp Kanawana" that was later digitized on Internet Archive.^4 According to Dawson's history, the meaning of the name "Kanawana" was not learned until years after the name was adopted.^5

The fact that Dawson authored the camp's first known history in 1933 suggests he held a position of authority or esteem within the camp community. Whether he served as camp director in 1933 is unconfirmed; the directors-index lists him with a question mark for that year.
