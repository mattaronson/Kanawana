# Leo J. Robitaille

*Status: stub | Sources: 3*
*Last Updated: 2026-08-14*

## Overview

**Leo J. Robitaille** signed the Kamp Kanawana Director's Report for 1973. He came to it as executive secretary of the Montreal YMCA's **Camping and Outdoor Education Branch** rather than as a career camp man, after a decade of branch postings across the association.^1 ^2

## The 1973 season

The report is signed "Leo J. Robitaille" and dated **17 August 1973**.^1 **No year other than 1973 is documented for him at Kanawana.**

Two details in it are worth keeping. It records a pre-camp training session by an outside consultant as a failure — "totally irrelevant to our pre-camp training and only served to disrupt the unity which was forming amongst the staff" — and then singles out the alternative: "The brief talk given by **Ross Bannerman** was remembered as a high point of training camp, touching people's feelings and relating to the values of the camping experience."^1 Bannerman was the association's chief executive at the time; this is one of the few documents showing him in direct contact with camp staff, and it sits oddly alongside the wiki's former, mistaken belief that he had once *run* the camp. See [[people/ross-bannerman|Ross Bannerman]].

The report also names **Jane Bannerman** on the waterfront and **Cathy Bannerman** in the business office, the latter co-ordinating the first year of the CIT program.^1 The recurrence of the surname across the 1970s reports is the likeliest origin of the long-standing confusion that put Ross Bannerman in this wiki's Camp Directors table.

## Open Questions

1. [Important] Did Robitaille run Kanawana **on site** in 1973, or sign its report as the branch executive above an unnamed on-site director? The 1973 report's first-person voice suggests the former, but the camp's structure in 1972–73 was still the two-tier arrangement of the Turner years, and the branch title is the same one Derek Walsh later held while demonstrably running the camp himself. The documents read so far do not settle it.
2. [Important] Who directed the 1972 season? The only candidate is **Tom Potts**, on a 1993 alumni recollection and one ambiguous branch-table cell. Robitaille held the branch in 1972–73, so if Potts ran the site in 1972 that is another two-tier year.
3. [Nice-to-have] What became of him after 1976?
4. [Nice-to-have] The record gives both "Leo" and "Léo" — the French-language news releases use the accent. No preference is documented.
