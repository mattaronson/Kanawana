# R.H. Hanagan (Ronald Hanagan)

*Status: E1-reviewed | Sources: 10*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-07 (the whole career: Galt, Toronto Central, Montreal Central, Eastern Community)*

## Overview

**His given name is Ronald.** R.H. Hanagan served as Montreal YMCA camp director in 1942 and 1946, a period that included managing the camp during wartime conditions and handling a polio outbreak in which six cases were reported.^1 His title — "Montreal YMCA camp director" rather than simply "camp director" — suggests he had broader responsibilities across YMCA camping operations, not limited to Kanawana alone.^1 He is one of the best-documented directors of the 1940s era thanks to his detailed season reports preserved in the Concordia Archives and a 1942 Montreal Gazette article.^1 ^3

## Appointment and Directorship Structure

No director is documented between Howie Langille, who served as Camp Chief/Director in 1941 (the 48th season, with 68 staff and over 30 British wartime evacuees), and Hanagan's confirmed presence in 1942, suggesting direct succession.^1 ^5

The 1942 season documented a two-tier directorship structure: Hanagan held the title "Camp Director" while Edgar E. Smee held the title "Resident Director."^3 Smee had previously served as Juvenile section director in 1939.^1

## 1942 Season

A Montreal Gazette article from August 5, 1942, titled "Y.M.C.A. Boys Stage Parade and Circus," describes a camp event with Hanagan "in Indian garb on horseback."^3 The article documents camp sections organized as Bantam, Junior, Intermediate, and Senior — the pre-1959 section naming scheme.^3

## 1946 Season and Polio Outbreak

In 1946, six polio cases were reported at Camp Kanawana.^1 Dr. Cushing, identified as a pediatrician (distinct from W.E. Cushing, who wrote "Historical sketches — Lake St. Joseph" in 1943), visited the camp and recommended cancelling canoe trips but keeping the camp open.^1 Three of the six cases were discharged by the end of summer.^1 Hanagan managed this crisis in his capacity as camp director.^1

The outbreak coincided with the onset of Canada's fourth and most serious polio epidemic wave, which ran from 1946 through 1953 and ultimately involved all provinces.^6 Camps, pools, and playgrounds were commonly closed outright during such outbreaks, which makes the decision to keep Kanawana operating — on medical advice, with canoe trips cancelled — a notable piece of crisis management for its era.^6

His detailed report on the outbreak is held in the Concordia Archives sub-series P0145/12A.^2

## Broader YMCA Role

Hanagan authored a report to the Camp Board of Management on the American Camping Association Conference (1946) and a Boys' Camping Committee — Survey Committee report (1950), both held in Concordia's YMCA fonds (P0145/12A).^2 ^4 These sit alongside system-wide documents including a "Comparison of Two Boys Camps operated by Montreal YMCA" (comparing Kanawana and Camp Perrot, c.1945–46) and a "Report of the Montreal YMCA Camps for Boys and Girls" (1946). The 1950 Survey Committee report extends his documented activity to at least that year. This archival grouping confirms that Hanagan's portfolio extended beyond Kanawana to encompass the broader Montreal YMCA camping system.

## The career before Kanawana, which is no longer a guess

The YMCA of Montreal's *News Bulletin* for May 1936 names the executive of the **Ontario and Quebec Boys' Work Secretaries Association**, which that spring planned the conference at which the committee to organise the Canadian Camping Association was struck. One of the four is "**Ron Hanagan, Toronto Central Y.M.C.A.**"^7

**[RESOLVED 2026-09-07: he is RONALD HANAGAN, and the *Year Book* settles it without any inference across sources.** The 1941 and 1943 volumes print the same post **twice**: in the Montreal branch directory, under Central Branch, "Physical, Oscar L. Pearson … **Boys, Ronald Hanagan**"; and in the alphabetical list of employed officers in those same volumes, "**Hanagan, R. H.**, Montreal, Que., Central, Boys, **29**." Same association, same branch, same post, same volume — one form spelled out, one abbreviated. His year of entry into Association work is **1929**, and the career starts in Ontario: the 1929-30 volume gives "**Galt.** … Physical, J. W. McVicar — **Boys, Ronald Hanagan**." The middle initial is still unaccounted for; the rosters give "Ronald" and "R. H." and never both in full together.^8]**

**And the Toronto posting is documented now, five times over.** The paragraph kept immediately below was written when the only evidence was that single line in a Montreal newsletter, and it declined to identify the man. It is superseded and kept, not removed, because the reasoning in it was right for what it had: the *Year Book* volumes for **1936, 1937, 1938, 1939 and 1940** all carry "**Boys, Ronald Hanagan**" in the Central Branch block printed under the heading "**Toronto, 36 College St., Metro. Bd.**" — the block that also lists the Broadview, Danforth, St. Clair, West End and West Toronto branches, so there is no doubt which city it is. The entry-year figure of **29** runs unchanged from Galt through Toronto to Montreal. **A Ron Hanagan at Toronto Central in 1936 was not a coincidence of names; it was him, in the fourth of his eleven documented Toronto years.**^9

*[SUPERSEDED 2026-09-07, kept as written:]* **This article's subject is initials.** A Ron Hanagan at Toronto Central in 1936 is a plausible earlier post for a man who was the Montreal association's camp director by 1942, and it would supply the given name this article does not have. *It is not asserted to be the same man.* The surname is uncommon but not unique, no source here connects the two posts, and a move from Toronto Central to Montreal is an inference from nothing but a name and a six-year gap. What makes it worth writing down rather than dropping is that it is **testable**: the association's own staff-change columns, which this project has now found on the Internet Archive for other years, would record such a move if it happened. See [[connections/institutional-lineage/association-founding-dates|Founding Dates of the Camping Associations]] for what that 1936 meeting did.

*What actually tested it was not the staff-change columns but a national roster in Minnesota, which is worth noticing: the paragraph named the right kind of evidence and the wrong document.*

**The whole shape of it, then.** He entered Association work in **1929** at **Galt, Ontario**, as Boys' Secretary, and the 1931 volume still has him there. He is **absent from the volumes for 1932, 1933, 1934 and 1935** — and that absence is controlled, not assumed: all four volumes are in the digitized run and full-text searchable, and a search on **J. J. Halcrow**, a Toronto West End secretary of the same era, returns him in every one of them. What Hanagan was doing in those four years is not recorded here. He reappears at **Toronto Central** in the 1936 volume and stays through 1940; he is at **Montreal Central** as Boys' Secretary in the volumes for 1941, 1942, 1943 and 1944; and in the **1945** volume the Central Branch Boys' post has passed to **Cameron Gorrie** while Hanagan has become "**Executive, Eastern Community Br., 1441 Drummond St.**", the alphabetical list reading "Montreal, Que., **Met. Bd., Eastern Community**, 29." The 1946 volume repeats it.^9

That last move matters for how this article's own account reads. **The polio season of 1946 was run by a man whose YMCA post was no longer the Central Branch boys' job.** He was directing camp from a different chair — which fits the archival evidence, already noted below, that his portfolio covered the Montreal system's camps rather than one branch's boys. The Eastern Community Branch carries the same street address as Central Branch, 1441 Drummond, so it was not a separate building; what kind of unit it was is not established here.

**He was also on the National Council.** The 1939 and 1940 volumes print the members of the National Council of YMCAs of Canada by city, and **R. H. Hanagan** is under Toronto in both, in a list that also holds **Taylor Statten**, **Harry Ballantyne**, W. J. Hastie, Geo. S. Patterson and John A. Tory. It is a broad body rather than an executive, drawn from every city with an association — but it is the national council, and he sat on it while running Toronto Central's boys' work. Three years later the 1942 volume's conference programme index has him speaking: "**Hanagan, R. H. — Co-operation with Other Agencies, with Special Reference to Schools and Churches — Central, Montreal.**"^9

**And then he stops, in the record if not in life.** The 1946 volume is his last appearance in the series, which goes on listing Canadian associations into the 1980s. Against that stands the Concordia fonds, which holds a Boys' Camping Committee Survey Committee report of his from **1950**. The *Year Book* lists **employed officers**; a man can leave that list without leaving the association's committee work. Which of those happened is not established.

*A Toronto lead for the years after, offered as a lead only.* The **Canadian Almanac and Directory for 1951** prints the staff of the **University of Toronto School of Social Work** — professors, then a long roll of **Field Supervisors**, the practitioners in agencies who took student placements — and "**Ron Hanagan**" is among them.^10 A Toronto man in social work in 1951 is the right shape for a YMCA boys' worker who leaves the roster after 1946 and had spent 1936-1940 in Toronto. It is the wrong shape in one respect: he was writing a Montreal camping report in 1950, and a move in the year between is fast. Two men of the name would explain it as easily as one. **It is written down because it is testable and because leaving it out would look like an absence of evidence.**

2. [Critical] Did Hanagan serve as director in years beyond 1942 and 1946? The gap suggests possible continuous service 1942–1946 or longer. **[Narrowed 2026-09-07: he was on the Montreal association's staff continuously from the 1941 volume to the 1946 one, so continuous camp direction across 1942-46 is at least possible on employment grounds. The *Year Book* records posts, not camp assignments, and says nothing about who ran the camp in any year.]**
3. ~~[Important] What was Hanagan's role in the broader Montreal YMCA organization outside of camping?~~ **[Largely answered 2026-09-07.]** Boys' Secretary of Central Branch from the 1941 volume through the 1944, then **Executive of the Eastern Community Branch** at the same 1441 Drummond Street address in the 1945 and 1946 volumes — so he was running a branch, not a boys' department, in the polio year. Earlier he had sat on the **National Council of the YMCA of Canada** (1939 and 1940 volumes, listed under Toronto) and spoken at a conference on "Co-operation with Other Agencies, with Special Reference to Schools and Churches".^9 **Still open: what the Eastern Community Branch was**, given that it shares Central Branch's street address, and **what he did after the 1946 volume**, his last in the series.
4. [Important] What are the details of the 1946 polio outbreak response? His season report in Concordia Archives (P0145/12A) is the primary source.
5. [Nice-to-have] The Camp Executive Committee and Board of Management minutes (P0145/12B01, 1946) may document the extent of his crisis management authority.
