# Maureen McBride

*Status: draft | Sources: 3*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-06*

## Overview

Maureen McBride (died 8 May 1993) sat on the Kamp Kanawana Committee from at least 1988 to 1992
and on the YMCA of Montreal's own Board of Directors in 1991 and 1992.^2 She came to the camp
from a career in outdoor education that ran outside the association as well as inside it: a
teacher at Dawson College, a director of the YWCA's Camp Oolahwan on Lac Walfred, and, in the
alumni newsletter's phrase, someone with "long associations with both the YWCA and YMCA."^1

What she is remembered for in the one obituary this project has is singing. *The Lookout*, the
Kanawana Alumni Committee's newsletter, put it this way in the autumn of 1993:

> "Any of us who knew Maureen McBride sang with her many times. That was her greatest gift and
> how we will remember her… Maureen could perhaps best be described as the **Johnny Appleseed of
> singing**. She knew how to awaken a joy of singing. The magic she worked through her song
> sessions on the many hundreds of us who were her students, camp counselors and campers changed
> all of us forever."^1

She died after a long illness at a cottage she shared with friends at Newaygo Junction.^1 The
**Maureen McBride Endowment Fund** was established in her memory "to provide camperships for
children whose parents could not otherwise afford to send them to camp," with Richard "Itche"
Kerr — the author of the camp song — named as its contact alongside the Kamp office.^1

## What the Association's Own Records Show

The annual reports name her four years running, and they show two offices rather than one:

| Report | Kamp Kanawana Committee | YMCA of Montreal Board of Directors |
|---|---|---|
| 1988-89 | yes | not listed |
| 1990 | yes | not listed |
| 1991 | yes | yes |
| 1992 | yes | yes |
| 1993 and after | absent | absent |

*The Lookout* calls her "a member of the Board of Directors of Kamp Kanawana," which is a
compression of the two: the camp had a committee, the association had a board, and she was on
both by 1991.^1 ^2 Her disappearance from the 1993 report and every later one is consistent with
her death that May.

**One thing the reports do not corroborate.** In the 1991 and 1992 board lists nearly every
member is printed with an employer in parentheses — "Capelli, Paul (Dawson College)" among them
— and McBride's entry carries none.^2 Her Dawson College teaching rests on the obituary alone.

A **Peter McBride** appears in the same reports from 1992 to 1995.^2 No source found joins them
and no relation is claimed here.

## Camp Oolahwan

The obituary's "a Camp Director at Camp Oolahwan" makes her the **fourth named director** this
project holds for the YWCA of Montreal's girls' camp, after Mary S. Edgar (1915-19), May Brown
(1945-47) and Marjorie A. Booth (1977-79).^1 It gives no dates, so which of that article's two
blank spans — 1919-1945, or after 1979 — she belongs in is unknown. Her Kanawana service in the
late 1980s and the "long career" the obituary describes point at the later end, but that is
inference and nothing more.

## Open Questions

1. [Critical] When did Maureen McBride direct Camp Oolahwan? The YWCA of Montreal's own records
   and the Library and Archives Canada series "Camp Oolahwan committee minutes, reports and
   training programmes, 1916–1974" are the places to look; if her tenure falls after 1974 the LAC
   series will not hold it.
2. [Critical] What are her birth date and full name? A 1993 Montreal death makes a *Gazette*
   death notice likely, and the *Gazette* of that year is not freely searchable from here.
3. [Important] Was she in fact a teacher at Dawson College, and in what department? Dawson's
   Community, Recreation and Leadership Training programme is the obvious fit for a career in
   outdoor education, and the college's own records would settle it.
4. [Important] What became of the Maureen McBride Endowment Fund, and is it still making
   camperships? The YMCA of Quebec's foundation would know.
5. [Nice-to-have] Her song sessions are the strongest lead this project has on where Kanawana's
   later repertory came from. Did she leave song sheets, and are they in the camp's song material
   at Concordia?
