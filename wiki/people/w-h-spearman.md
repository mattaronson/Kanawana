# W. H. Spearman

*Status: draft | Sources: 6*
*Last Updated: 2026-08-14*

## Overview

**W. H. Spearman** was Camp Chief of Kamp Kanawana for at least three consecutive seasons, 1932 through 1934, and a career YMCA of Montreal secretary for thirty-four years, from roughly 1920 until his retirement on May 31, 1954.^1 He occupies the directorship slot between Harold C. Cross and Nelson McEwen's administrative oversight in the late 1920s and [[people/greig-macdiarmid|Greig Macdiarmid]]'s Camp Chieftaincy from 1935 — a slot this wiki previously left blank apart from a single passing mention.^6

Outside camp, his career ran through three Montreal branches and across the Second World War: Young Men's Secretary at Central Branch from 1929, Executive Secretary of Westmount Branch from about 1934, YMCA War Services Secretary for Military District No. 4 in 1940, manager of the Beaver Club in London during the war, and finally Executive Secretary of Central Branch from 1949 to his retirement.^1 ^5

**A caution that governs this whole article: two different men named W. H. Spearman appear in the Montreal YMCA's records, and they must not be merged.** See [Two Spearmans](#two-spearmans) below. The Kanawana director should not be recorded as "William H."

## Kanawana Camp Chief (1932–1934)

Three independent documents establish three consecutive seasons.

The earliest is the camp's own newsletter. The *Green Triangle* of July 30, 1932 carries a joke column in the voice of the advice columnist "Dorothy Dix," in which one of the questions is signed **"Chief. Spearman."**^2 The camp is addressing its own director by title, in-season, in 1932. The following summer he signs a staff notice in the *Green Triangle* of July 29, 1933 as "W. H. Spearman."^3

The YMCA of Montreal's Annual Report for 1934, covering the 1933 season, then states: "Mr. W. H. Spearman was **again** in charge of the camp."^1 The report's own "again" points backwards to at least 1932, which the 1932 newsletter independently confirms — the inference and the primary source agree.

The 1935 Annual Report, covering the 1934 season, records "a fully qualified staff, with Mr. W. H. Spearman as director," alongside a camper figure up from 1,078 the previous year.^1

**The tenure ends there.** Macdiarmid is documented as Camp Chief from 1935.^6 A passage in the *Green Triangle* of July 27, 1935 that might look like a fourth Spearman season is not one: he appears in it twice, both times as a **visitor** — delivering the address at the Sunday chapel service, and in a whimsical narrative voiced by a camp flea who spent the winter "with Chief Spearman" in the city and heard "Mr. Spearman talking about coming up here this weekend."^4 The honorific "Chief" is retained for a former director; the man himself is travelling up from Montreal for a weekend. Read the other way, this passage would manufacture a conflict with Macdiarmid's documented 1935 Chieftaincy where none exists.

Whether he directed before 1932 is open. The 1934 report's "again" is satisfied by 1932 and cannot be stretched further, and no 1930 or 1931 camp newsletter naming a Chief has been located.

## YMCA Career

Reconstructed from the continuous 1929–1955 run of Montreal YMCA Annual Reports:^1

| Date | Position |
|------|----------|
| c. 1920 | Entered YMCA service (established by the "34 years" at his 1954 retirement) |
| pre-1929 | London, Ontario Association, "after several years' service in that city" |
| September 1, 1929 | Young Men's Secretary, Central Branch, Montreal |
| 1932–1934 | Camp Chief, Kamp Kanawana |
| c. 1933–1934 | Executive Secretary, Westmount Branch, succeeding Mr. Beall |
| 1940 | YMCA War Services Secretary, Military District No. 4, succeeding C. J. McGerrigle |
| 1941 | Continued in War Services in Canada |
| 1942–1945 | Manager, the Beaver Club, London, England |
| September 1945 | Returned to Westmount Branch after two years overseas |
| March 31, 1949 | Executive Secretary, Central Branch, succeeding W. H. White; succeeded at Westmount by James Carnie |
| May 31, 1954 | Retired after 34 years of service; succeeded at Central Branch by A. W. Kemball |

Two details are worth drawing out. His wartime posting was not a desk job in Montreal: the **Beaver Club** in London was the large services club for Canadian forces personnel in Britain, and the 1942 report calls it "famous" without further explanation, assuming Montreal readers knew it.^1 And his 1929 arrival placed him on a Central Branch staff roster that also listed **Harold C. Cross** as Executive Secretary, along with W. H. White and W. J. Holliday.^1 Cross was a Kanawana director around 1925–1928 and later the YMCA's own historian; Spearman took over the camp within about three years of joining the branch Cross ran. The two men were direct colleagues, which is the likeliest route by which Spearman came to the camp at all — though no document states this.

## Two Spearmans

The Montreal YMCA records contain a second, younger W. H. Spearman, and conflating the two would produce a false career.

The decisive evidence is a single year. The 1949 Annual Report lists **"North Branch — William H. Spearman"** among fellowship students in practical training — students at Sir George Williams College and McGill being trained to enter YMCA service.^1 In that same 1949 report, the elder Spearman becomes **Executive Secretary of Central Branch**, a senior post held after twenty-nine years of service.^1 One man cannot simultaneously be a trainee and a branch executive secretary.

A 1953 entry belongs to the younger man for the same reason: "Mr. W. H. Spearman, Physical Director at Notre Dame de Grace Branch, accepted a position with the Brantford, Ont. Y.M.C.A."^1 The elder Spearman was Executive Secretary of Central Branch throughout 1953 and retired from that post in 1954.

**Consequence for this wiki:** the Kanawana Camp Chief should be cited as "W. H. Spearman" and never expanded to "William H. Spearman." That given name is documented only for the trainee. The elder Spearman's first name is not established by any source located so far — the reports use initials throughout, as they do for most secretaries of the period.

An unrelated near-miss also appears: the 1935 report's donor list includes an "F. H. Spearman," a different set of initials again and not connected to either man by any source.^1

## Open Questions

1. [Important] What was W. H. Spearman's full given name? The Montreal reports use initials only. Likely routes: the London, Ontario YMCA's own records for the 1920s; Sir George Williams College staff listings; a Montreal death notice after 1954.
2. [Important] Did he direct Kanawana before 1932? The 1934 report's "again" reaches back to 1932 and no further. *Green Triangle* issues for 1930 and 1931, if they survive, would settle it — the 1932 issue is in the digitized run, so the gap may be a digitization gap rather than a publication one.
3. [Nice-to-have] What were his birth and death dates, and did he remain in Montreal after his 1954 retirement?
4. [Nice-to-have] Is the younger William H. Spearman a relative — a son or nephew following him into YMCA service? The pattern is common in the period, but nothing in the reports links them, and the shared initials alone are not evidence.
5. [Nice-to-have] What did the Beaver Club posting involve, and did he write anything about it? Canadian YMCA War Services records are held nationally and were not searched for this article.
