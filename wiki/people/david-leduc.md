# David Leduc

*Status: draft | Sources: 3*
*Last Updated: 2026-07-11*

## Overview

David "Dave" Leduc served as on-site Director of Camp Kanawana for a single summer, 2004, succeeding Morgan Carter. **No Executive Director is documented for 2004** — Gary White's confirmed Kanawana tenure ended in FY2001–2002 (conflict c_015, resolved), and Sean Day's documented Executive Directorship begins in 2005. He went on to a long career in international development and philanthropic administration.

## Directorship (2004)

Leduc managed all of Kanawana's summer and winter programs — recruitment, training, and supervision of 75 employees, all operational budgets, and the safety and security of 800 participants — and developed an intensive youth leadership program, reporting to the camp's Board of Directors.^1 That self-described remit — year-round programming and all operational budgets — reads broader than the three-season on-site Director role the two-tier structure defines, a tension the sources do not resolve. A 2002 dining-hall plaque names him as one of two Directors of that year's LIT program, alongside Avigail Aronoff, placing him at the camp in a staff capacity at least two years before his 2004 directorship.^2

## Career Beyond Kanawana

Leduc holds an undergraduate degree in International Development from Dalhousie University and an MBA in Management from McGill University (2006–2011).^1 **Before** his Kanawana directorship he served as Oxfam-Québec's Lebanon Coordinator for the "Across Borders Project" (March 2000 – June 2002), managing programs in Palestinian refugee camps and securing $340,000 in funding — a role that ended two years before the 2004 summer, and which sits alongside his documented 2002 presence at the camp as an LIT program director.^1 ^2 He was Director of Operations at McGill University's International Community Action Network (ICAN) for approximately 11 years, and was appointed Executive Director of Development and Peace (the Canadian Catholic Organization for Development and Peace) on July 28, 2015, replacing Michael Casey, heading a team of 80 employees.^1 ^3 He later held a role as Director of Philanthropy (Quebec) at Pathways to Education Canada.^1
