# Ross Bannerman

*Status: draft | Sources: 3*
*Last Updated: 2026-07-11*

## Overview

Ross Bannerman connects Kanawana to the wider Winnipeg-Montreal YMCA camping network of the mid-20th century. Recruited into YMCA work by Nelson McEwen at the Saint John YMCA (1947–1951), Bannerman directed Camp Pascobac and Camp Stephens in Manitoba before authoring a 1969 report on Kamp Kanawana and later rising to CEO of the Montreal YMCA. His own role at Kanawana — director, evaluator, or organizational overseer — is not stated directly by any source, though his career trajectory and the act of authoring a camp report both suggest senior standing.

## Recruitment and Early Career (1947–1956)

Nelson McEwen, who had directed Kanawana in an administrative capacity through 1947 before leaving for the Saint John YMCA in New Brunswick, recruited several men into YMCA careers there between 1947 and 1951, including Bannerman, Lloyd and Niles Ring, and Doug Allen.^1 Bannerman went on to direct Camp Pascobac in 1951, then Camp Stephens in Winnipeg in 1956, and at some point between those two postings established the St. James YMCA branch in Winnipeg.^1

## The 1969 Kanawana Report and Later Career

Bannerman authored a "Report on Kamp Kanawana" in 1969, held at Concordia Archives sub-sub-series P0145/12B01, alongside a YMCA International Branch file dated to roughly 1968–1970 placing him at the YMCA du Parc (International) branch in Montreal during the same period.^2 ^3 Neither record states his formal role at Kanawana; report authorship on its own is only suggestive of administrative involvement, not proof of a directorship. Bannerman later became CEO of the Montreal YMCA in the late 1970s, a senior organizational position that would have placed him overseeing Kanawana during the same general period as Richard Patten's tenure as Executive Director (1976–1979).^2
