# Ross Bannerman

*Status: draft | Sources: 7*
*Last Updated: 2026-07-11*

## Overview

Ross Bannerman connects Kanawana to the wider Winnipeg-Montreal YMCA camping network of the mid-20th century. Recruited into YMCA work by Nelson McEwen at the Saint John YMCA (1947–1951), **D. Ross Bannerman** directed Camp Pascobac in New Brunswick (1951)^7 and then Camp Stephens in Winnipeg (1956), before becoming General Secretary and Chief Executive Officer of the YMCA of Montreal from **December 1970 to 1978**.^4 ^5 He authored a 1969 report on Kamp Kanawana. His role at Kanawana was almost certainly organizational rather than a camp directorship: he was Assistant General Secretary of the association in 1969 and became its chief executive the following year, so the report reads as oversight from headquarters.^4

## Recruitment and Early Career (1947–1956)

Nelson McEwen, who had directed Kanawana in an administrative capacity through 1947 before leaving for the Saint John YMCA in New Brunswick, recruited several men into YMCA careers there between 1947 and 1951, including Bannerman, Lloyd and Niles Ring, and Doug Allen.^1 Bannerman went on to direct Camp Pascobac in 1951, then Camp Stephens in Winnipeg in 1956, and at some point between those two postings established the St. James YMCA branch in Winnipeg.^1

## The 1969 Kanawana Report and Later Career

Bannerman authored a "Report on Kamp Kanawana" in 1969, held at Concordia Archives sub-sub-series P0145/12B01, alongside a YMCA International Branch file dated to roughly 1968–1970 placing him at the YMCA du Parc (International) branch in Montreal during the same period.^2 ^3 Neither record states his formal role at Kanawana; report authorship on its own is only suggestive of administrative involvement, not proof of a directorship. Bannerman became the association's General Secretary and Chief Executive Officer effective **December 1, 1970**, succeeding Rix G. Rogers; he was 43 at the time, implying a birth year of about 1927.^4 He held the office until 1978, when Kerry W. Johnston succeeded him.^5 **This corrects a long-standing error in this wiki.** The Camp Stephens alumni blog says he "became CEO of the Montreal YMCA in the late 1970s", which this article previously repeated — the appointment was late **1970**, and a single year appears to have been read as a decade. By the late 1970s his tenure was in fact ending.^4

The correction also dissolves an apparent contradiction with [[people/notable-alumni/richard-patten|Richard Patten]], whose 1976–1979 YMCA role was Executive Director of the **Centre-ville/Downtown branch** — a different office entirely. The 1977–78 annual report lists both men in separate sections: Bannerman under Metropolitan Staff as General Secretary/CEO, Patten under "Branch Chairmen and Executive Directors". Camping and Outdoor Education was its own parallel branch under D. Walsh and then D. Twynam, so neither man's title implies direct authority over Kanawana in the way this wiki previously suggested.^5

## Open Questions

1. ~~[Important] What was Bannerman's actual role at Kanawana circa 1969?~~ **[Substantially answered 2026-08-14]** Organizational oversight, not a camp role: he was the association's Assistant General Secretary in 1969 and became General Secretary/CEO in December 1970. No source states it in those words, but the 1969 report is best read as an HQ assessment.
2. [Important, **premise corrected 2026-08-14**] Concordia sub-sub-series P0145/12B01 holds unattributed "Director's Annual Reports" for 1970–1975. This question previously stated that "physical archive consultation is the only path" — **that is no longer true.** Kamp Kanawana director's and annual reports for 1964–1966, 1969–1971, 1974–1979 and 1987 are digitized and full-text searchable on Internet Archive in the `rma-concordia-publications` collection, along with the Ka-News staff newsletter (1977–1981). Bannerman does not appear as author in the digitized reports checked; the 1976 and 1977 reports are signed "Derek V. Walsh, Director of Camping" and the 1979 report "G. David Twynam / Director". Reading the remaining digitized reports is now a desk task.^6
3. ~~[Nice-to-have] What years did Bannerman serve as CEO of the Montreal YMCA?~~ **[Resolved 2026-08-14]** December 1970 to 1978. Whether that involved direct oversight of Kanawana specifically remains unstated — camping was a separate branch reporting up to him, not a unit he ran.
4. [Nice-to-have] What became of Bannerman after 1978? No obituary or subsequent posting has been found, and neither his birth/death dates nor what the "D." stands for are documented.
