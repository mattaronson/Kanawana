# Taylor Statten and the Canadian Camping Movement

*Status: stub | Sources: src_mcmorris_thesis, src_pine_crest_encyclopedia, src_oca_archives, src_wikipedia_cca*
*Last Updated: 2026-03-15*

## Overview

Taylor Statten (1882–?) was a foundational figure in Canadian organized camping whose influence shaped programs at Camp Kanawana and across the country. A Boer War veteran, YMCA Boys' Work Secretary, and founder of Camp Ahmek, Statten pioneered the integration of Indigenous lore and woodcraft into Canadian camp programming, co-founded the Canadian Camping Association, and established the model of wilderness canoe tripping that Kanawana and dozens of other camps adopted.

## YMCA Career and Camp Ahmek

Statten served as Boys' Work Secretary for the national YMCA from 1906.^1 He created the Canadian Standards Efficiency Training (CSET) program, which standardized youth development across YMCA camps.^1 In 1921, he founded Camp Ahmek at Canoe Lake in Algonquin Park, though some sources date the founding to 1916.^2 In 1924, he established Camp Wapomeo as a companion girls' camp.^2

Statten styled himself "Chief" and took the Ojibwa name Gitchi-Ahmek.^1 In the summer of 1922, he invited Ernest Thompson Seton to Canoe Lake to create a formal Indian Lore program, which became a template for camps across Canada.^3 His "Indian Lore" programming influenced camps nationwide, including Kanawana.^1

## Canadian Camping Association

The Canadian Camping Association was founded on May 20, 1936, at the Central YMCA in Toronto, with Taylor Statten as its first president and Mary Edgar (founder of Camp Oolahwan) as vice-president.^4 Concordia University Archives contain Taylor Statten weekly letters from 1915, suggesting an early connection to the Montreal YMCA network.^5

Statten co-authored *Talks to Counselors* (1939) with Hedley S. Dimock, establishing a direct intellectual link between the Statten camping tradition and the Kanawana-connected Dimock research program.^6

## Connection to Kanawana

Statten's influence on Kanawana was primarily indirect: through the CSET program adopted by YMCA camps, the Indian Lore programming model, the CCA organizational framework, and the Dimock collaboration. Taylor Statten Camps first sent groups to Quetico in 1965 as part of the OCA Centennial Program, which became a permanent tradition — a parallel to Kanawana's own canoe tripping evolution.^7
