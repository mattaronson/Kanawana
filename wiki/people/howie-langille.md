# Howie Langille

*Status: draft | Sources: 6*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-08 (his first name, his Halifax and McGill years, and where he went)*

## Overview

Howie Langille served on the leadership staff of Camp Kanawana through the second half of the 1930s and into the war years, rising from Senior Director in 1935 to Camp Chief by 1941. He is the best-documented wartime director, thanks to a surviving CFCF radio broadcast script in which he personally described that season's operations. No source gives his full name, birth or death dates, or any biographical detail beyond his years at Kanawana.

## 1935: Senior Director

The 1935 season chronicle names Langille as Senior Director, one of three section directors under Camp Chief Greig Macdiarmid — alongside Junior Director Ernie Taylor and Juvenile Director Lorne Hamilton.^1 ^2 "Senior" here refers to the Seniors age section (boys 14 to 17), one of the camp's three age-based divisions at the time.^2

## 1941: Camp Chief and the CFCF Broadcast

By 1941, Langille had become overall Camp Chief, having succeeded Macdiarmid (last documented as Chief in a July 1938 newsletter) at some point in the intervening two or three years.^1 ^3 On June 26, 1941, Langille went on CFCF radio to promote summer registrations, describing the camp's "48th season" — counting from the 1894 founding rather than the 1910 permanent-site acquisition.^1 ^3 The broadcast is the single richest surviving account of any wartime Kanawana season: total staff of 68, with 45 counsellors selected from 58 trained applicants; more than 30 evacuee children from Britain and continental Europe in attendance; registrations running 30 percent above the previous year; and J.W. Perks, Assistant Superintendent of the Protestant School Board, chairing the Personnel Committee.^1 ^3

No director is documented at Kanawana between Langille's 1941 season and R.H. Hanagan's confirmed presence in 1942, suggesting Langille was directly succeeded by Hanagan, though no source states this explicitly.^1

**And he was at camp by 1933**, two years before the Senior directorship this article starts from. The
*Green Triangle*'s account of the Kanawana Circus of 3 August 1933 has him among the staff attractions:
"**Mr. Langille** received over forty Sun kisses; but rumour has it that he paid just about as many
dollars for them, so he wasn't so hot after all."^4 It is a joke in a camp newspaper and it establishes
one thing only, but that one thing is a date: he is "Mr. Langille," an adult member of staff, at
Kanawana in the summer of 1933. His documented run therefore reaches from 1933 to the 48th season of
1941, and he was on staff alongside [[people/greig-macdiarmid|Greig Macdiarmid]] years before serving
as his Senior Director.

## Howard Langille, and where he went

**His first name is Howard, his previous post was Halifax, and he left Montreal for London, Ontario.**
Three sentences in two Montreal annual reports settle what this article's first open question has been
asking, and both reports were in this project's cache the whole time — passed over because a plain search
for "Langille" does not match the way the OCR sets it, broken across a line as "Lan- gille."^5 ^6

The report for the year ending 31 March 1939, on the year's staff changes: "**Mr. John P. Kidd**, Acting
Boys' Work Secretary of Central Branch, resigned to accept a similar position with the **Brantford**
Association, and **Mr. Howard Langille, B.A.**, for several years on the staff of the **Halifax
Y.M.C.A.**, was appointed to succeed him. **Mr. Langille secured his university education at McGill while
one of our Fellowship Secretaries.**"^5

Four things at once. **"Howie" is Howard.** He came to Montreal from **Halifax**, after several years
there. He took his degree at **McGill** while working for the Montreal association as a **Fellowship
Secretary**, which is the association's own training scheme — so he was a Montreal YMCA man before Halifax
as well as after. And the post he was appointed to is the one that ran the camp's own department:
**Boys' Work Secretary of Central Branch**, the desk traced at [[people/boys-work-secretaries|Montreal's
Boys' Work Secretaries]]. *That is also why he was at camp in 1933 as a "Mr." and on the leadership staff
by 1935*: he was a Fellowship Secretary of the association then, not a visitor.

**He is also the man who replaced a Kidd.** John P. Kidd, of the two brothers at
[[people/notable-alumni/kidd-brothers|the Kidd brothers]], left the same acting secretaryship for
Brantford in that year — so the Central boys' desk passed **Kidd to Langille** in 1938-39.

**And the departure.** The report for the year ending 31 March 1942: "**J. H. Langille, Boys' Work
Secretary of Central Branch, resigned to take a position with the London Y.M.C.A.**"^6 That is the year
after the CFCF broadcast and the 48th season, and it fits the succession this article infers: he leaves
the Montreal association, and Hanagan is at camp in 1942. *A caution on the initials.* The 1939 report
writes **Howard** and the 1942 report writes **J. H.** — plausibly J. Howard Langille, a man going by his
second given name, which is common enough and is what "Howie" would follow. **It is not asserted here**;
two Montreal reports three years apart give two forms of one Central Branch boys' secretary's name, and
nothing read here writes it out in full.

## Open Questions

   2026-09-08, both halves, and see the section above.]** His first name is **Howard**, per the
   Montreal annual report that appointed him; he left for the **London Y.M.C.A.** in the year ending
   March 1942. *What is still open is narrower*: the 1942 report calls him **J. H.**, so his full name
   is probably **J. Howard Langille** and this project has not seen it written out. Birth and death
   dates remain unknown, and the London, Ontario association's own records are now the obvious place
