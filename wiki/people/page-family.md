## Overview

The Pagé family were pioneer settlers of Saint-Sauveur-des-Monts, Quebec, whose landholdings became the site of Camp Kanawana. The YMCA of Montreal acquired property from the Pagé family circa 1910 for its new camp, and purchased the "Pagé farm" in 1960 to expand the camp property.^1 The family's four-generation history in Saint-Sauveur — spanning from the 1850s to the present day — is documented through municipal records, the local historical society, and Quebec heritage registries. The Pagé family's settlement of côte Saint-Elmire was part of a broader wave: Jean-Baptiste Desjardins was among the first settlers alongside Magloire Pagé, arriving circa 1837 during the Lower Canada Rebellion period.^10

The family's genealogy may also explain the origin of the name "Lac Desjardins," the original name of what is now Lake Wilson at Camp Kanawana: Magloire Pagé fils married **Olivine Desjardins** in 1875, and if the lake sat on Pagé-Desjardins family land, its name likely referred to Olivine's maiden family — a common Quebec toponymic pattern.^2 ^3

## Genealogy

### First Generation: Magloire Pagé (1826–1901)

Magloire Pagé was a 10th-generation descendant of the original Pagé family who came to New France.^4 He married Adéline Beauchamp (1830–1898) on July 28, 1851, in Saint-Jérôme.^4 By the 1885 census, the couple owned **lot 43 in the concession of côte Saint-Elmire**, Saint-Sauveur.^4

### Second Generation: Magloire Pagé fils (b. 1853)

Born in Saint-Sauveur, Magloire fils acquired the paternal land in 1874 and married **Olivine Desjardins** on August 3, 1875.^2 Olivine died at age 54 on June 20, 1904, and was buried in the Saint-Sauveur cemetery.^2 Their known children include:

- **Léonie Pagé** (1882–1941), married Joseph-Vital Léonard in 1902^2
- **Édouard Pagé** (b. March 30, 1893)^5
- **Télesphore Pagé** (youngest son, married May 27, 1925)^4

### Third Generation: Édouard Pagé (b. 1893)

Édouard began an apprenticeship in baking in 1910 under Ubald Gauthier on Drolet Street, Montreal.^5 In 1912, baker Camille Gauvreau ceded his Saint-Sauveur bakery to his son Ovila, who rented it to Édouard. Pagé acquired the business in 1914.^5 The bakery — Boulangerie Pagé at 7 avenue de l'Église — has operated continuously since then.^5 ^6

Édouard served as president of the Saint-Sauveur School Commission. His efforts led to the construction of a boys' school blessed on January 9, 1949, named **École Saint-Édouard** in his honor (the name was retained until 1995).^5 In 1963, he sold the bakery to his sons **Bernard and Philippe Pagé**.^5

The original bakery building was established in 1897 by Camille Gauvreau (not 1903 as sometimes cited), who purchased the land from notary Malo. The building is listed in the Répertoire du patrimoine culturel du Québec as "Ancienne boulangerie Gauvreau" (ID 206587).^6

### Third Generation: Télesphore Pagé

Télesphore, the youngest son of Magloire fils, inherited the family land and settled there with his wife Clarilda Dagenais (1901–1995). The old Pagé farmhouse "faced the mountains" near Saint-Sauveur.^4

A second Télesphore Pagé (b. April 1908), likely a nephew or cousin, operated a grocery store on rue de la Gare in Saint-Sauveur.^10

## Connection to Camp Kanawana

The YMCA of Montreal purchased the Saint-Sauveur property from the Page family circa 1910, establishing Camp Kanawana at its current location.^1 The 1923 *Gas Bag* lists "Pages" as a hike destination from camp, suggesting the family still had a visible presence in the area.^7

In 1960, the YMCA purchased additional land described as the "Pagé farm" to expand the camp property, though the acreage and terms of this purchase are not specified in available sources.^1

## The Desjardins Connection

The marriage of Magloire Pagé fils to Olivine Desjardins in 1875 provides a genealogical explanation for the name "Lac Desjardins" — the original name of what is now Lake Wilson at Camp Kanawana.^2 ^3 In Quebec, lakes are commonly named for the families on whose land they sit (see the Commission de toponymie's documentation of the Sainte-Lucie-des-Laurentides "Lac Wilson," named for the local Wilson landowner family). If the lake was on Pagé-Desjardins family land, "Lac Desjardins" would refer to the wife's maiden family — a plausible and common naming pattern. See [[people/j-w-mcconnell|J.W. McConnell]] for the lake's subsequent renaming history.

## Domaine-Pagé

"Domaine-Pagé" is a named locality in Saint-Sauveur, with streets including "Chemin du Domaine-Pagé" and "1re rue du Domaine-Pagé." The Commission de toponymie du Québec states that the origin of this name has not been formally determined, though it almost certainly refers to the Pagé family's landholdings.^8

## Mont Habitant

In 1957, Mickey Stein was staying in the old Pagé family farmhouse when he observed the mountain opposite and conceived the idea for the Mont Habitant ski resort. Stein married Sheila Joan Vineberg in 1955 and, with her brothers Stephen and Stanley Vineberg, hired designer Sel Hannah; Mont Habitant opened for the 1958–59 winter season.^9 Mont-Habitant Development Ltd was incorporated on July 27, 1959.^11 Stein died on May 16, 1985.^11
