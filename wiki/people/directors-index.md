# Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana

*Status: E1-reviewed | Sources: 33*
*Last Updated: 2026-08-14*

## Summary

This index compiles all known directors of Camp Kanawana (originally Camp Jubilee) from its founding in 1894 to the present, along with documented staff from seasons where source records survive. The directorship has been documented through camp brochures, season reports, YMCA correspondence, oral history, and secondary sources. Detailed staff rosters survive for 1921-1923 (brochures and the *Gas Bag*), 1935 (season chronicle), 1938 (*Green Triangle*), 1939-1941 (McMorris thesis and CFCF broadcast), and 1980 (*Ka-News*). Significant gaps remain, particularly for the 1894-1920 and 1947-2003 periods. **The 1929-1955 Montreal YMCA annual reports are now known to survive as a complete, digitized, text-searchable run** -- the source that added W. H. Spearman (1932-1934) to this table in August 2026, and the most promising unworked seam for the earlier part of the 1947-2003 gap.

The title "Director" was applied at more than one level: the **Camp Director** (also styled "Camp Chief" in earlier eras) was in charge of the whole camp, while a **section or program director** — Juvenile/Junior/Senior section, Educational, CIT/leadership, etc. — was subordinate to the Camp Director and responsible for one part of the operation. The two roles are tabulated separately below; assistant/deputy camp directors (a camp-wide, not section-specific, subordinate role) remain in the Camp Directors table with an "(Asst.)" qualifier.

### Note: how the 1947–2003 gap was closed

Everything from Roy Locke (1947) to Morgan Carter (2003) was established or corrected on 2026-08-14 from one seam: the Concordia-digitized YMCA of Montreal fonds on Internet Archive, read as bulk-downloaded OCR text. Two document types carry the answer, and **neither is the same one across the whole period** — which is why earlier passes found nothing.

**1947–1965: nobody is named.** The annual reports of these years list only *volunteer* Branch Chairmen and Camp Chairmen. Kanawana's paid director appears in none of them. Locke (1947) and Shantz (1955) are known only because they happen to be named in passing elsewhere — Locke in the 1951 in-house camp history, Shantz on the agenda of the 1956 annual meeting. Their intervening years are inference from continuity, not statement.

**1966–1979: the branch officers table.** Kanawana appears as a *branch* from the 1966-67 report, in a "BRANCHES / CHAIRMEN / EXECUTIVE SECRETARY" table. **But read it carefully.** From 1969 the row is renamed "Camping Branch" and from 1972 "Camping and Outdoor Education Branch" — an association-level department covering Kanawana, Otoreke, Les Voyageurs and Perrot. Its Executive Secretary is the head of *all* YMCA Montreal camping, who in some years ran Kanawana and in others did not. A November 1969 French news release makes the distinction explicit, calling Seaman "**ancien** Directeur de Kanawana" while giving him the new branch. The table is a lead, not an answer; it must be read against the camp's own reports.

**1980–1983: a genuine blank.** No Kanawana season report survives for 1980–1986, and the annual reports of 1980-81 through 1985 name no Kanawana director at all (1982-83 has no staff roster whatsoever). Twynam's 1979–82 span rests on a 1993 alumni newsletter; Goddard is documented in November 1982 and again in FY1986, with 1983–85 bracketed between them — and, from outside the YMCA's own paper, at Kanawana again in early 1983 in the Canadian Camping Association's annual report.^31

**1984–2003: the Metropolitan Staff / Senior Management roster**, which names the Kanawana director directly year by year. This is where Goddard, both Netherwoods, Hoad, White and Carter come from.

**A structural correction.** This wiki dates the two-tier Executive/on-site split to 1995. **A two-tier structure also existed in 1969–1972**, and then closed. The 1970 season report prints an organisation chart with "Camp Executive Director / Director of Camping" above "Camp Director" and "Asst. Camp Director," and a January 1970 news release introduces Turner as Kanawana's "**on-site director and head of program**" while Roy Locke held the branch. By 1974 the split had closed again: Walsh simultaneously heads the branch and signs the camp's own report. The 1995 arrangement is a *return* to a structure the camp had tried twenty-five years earlier, not an invention.

**Ross Bannerman is not in this table**, though he was listed for 1969 until 2026-08-14. Geoff Anderson states in the 1969 report "I was appointed Director on February 1st, 1969" and signs it as Director; a May 1969 news release independently calls Anderson "Director of Kanawana." Bannerman was Assistant General Secretary of the association from 1 July 1968 and General Secretary/CEO from 1 December 1970. His 1969 "Report on Kamp Kanawana" is an executive reporting *on* a branch. See conflict c_023 (resolved). The likely origin of the confusion is worth noting: **Jane, John and Cathy Bannerman** appear in the 1970s reports as Kanawana campers and staff.

**Still open.** Summer **1968** has no named on-site director anywhere — not in the annual report, not in any 1968 news release or newsletter (none of which mention Kanawana at all). **1972** rests on a single 1993 alumni recollection plus one ambiguous branch-table cell. **1948–1954** and **1956–1958** are continuity inferences, not statements. And the post-2003 record thins again: the 2003-04 and 2004-05 Senior Management lists carry no Kanawana director.


## The Two-Tier Era (1995–present): Executive Director and On-Site Director

Beginning in 1995, the single Camp Director role was split into two: a year-round **Executive Director**, headquartered in Montreal and responsible for the organization overall, and a three-season **on-site Director** who ran the camp itself each summer. The on-site role was colloquially called "**Chief**" — a title dating to the camp's "playing Indian" era — until it was retired in the early 2020s as part of Kanawana's reconciliation efforts, after which the role was simply called the "on-site Director" (oral history, M. Aronson; f_1728).

## Staff by Era

### The 1920s: Brochure and Gas Bag Staff (1921-1923)

The 1921, 1922, and 1923 camp brochures and the 1923 *Gas Bag* reunion newsletter provide the most detailed early staffing records. Several individuals served across multiple seasons, indicating a stable core staff during this period.

**Long-serving staff.** Harry Smith served as camp chef from approximately 1913, documented through his 9th year (1921) and 10th year (1922).^3 Johnston W. Abraham served as Business Manager from approximately 1914, documented through his 9th year (1922).^3 F.H. Spinney served as Educational Director from approximately 1917, documented in both 1921 (his 5th year) and 1922 (teaching basketry).^3 Allan B. Cornell served as Educational Director from approximately 1918 through at least 1923 (his 6th year), and held the Large K badge, the camp's highest honour award.^3 ^7 Reg Cowan served as Business Manager from approximately 1915 through at least 1923 (8 years), and also held the Large K badge.^3 ^7

**1921 season.** Under director G.D. Brandon, the documented staff included H.H. Hart (Camp Doctor), F.H. Spinney (Educational Director), and Harry Smith (Chef).^3

**1922 season.** Under director Lemuel P. Ereaux, the documented staff included Hay Finlay (Senior Section Director; ex-Central YMCA Boys' Physical Director, then at McGill Physical Education), Edwin M. Crawford (Junior Section Director; ex-Westmount YMCA Boys' Work Secretary), F.H. Spinney (Educational Director), Harry Smith (Chef), and Johnston W. Abraham (Business Manager).^3

**1923 season.** The 1923 season under Camp Chief Philip G. Paterson is the best-documented early year. The 1923 brochure names Howard Ellis (Junior Director; Boys' Physical Director, Central YMCA), L.P. Little ("Bones"; Camp Doctor, 6th year McGill Medical, and separately titled **Assistant Chief of the Junior Division** -- the only use of "Chief" terminology in the 1921-1923 brochures for a subordinate, section-specific role rather than the overall camp head), Allan B. Cornell (Educational Director), H.K. Ashdown (Senior Division; McGill Theology), and Reg Cowan (Business Manager).^3 The *Gas Bag* adds R.E.G. Davis ("Chief Davis") as Camp Chief, Doc Ross as Camp Doctor, and counsellors Hardie, Sunny (kitchen), Bill Steeves, Dave Hart, Wilf Eagle, and Guiton (assigned to North Branch).^7 The apparent overlap between Paterson (brochure) and Davis (Gas Bag) as "Camp Chief" in 1923 may reflect a seasonal handoff or different titles for different periods -- Little's section-specific "Chief" title shows the word wasn't used exclusively for the top job that year, though Davis's camp-wide activities in the Gas Bag (dining hall, staff-vs-world game, camp auction, regatta) read more like an overall head than a Junior-only role, so this doesn't cleanly resolve the question either way.

### The Mid-1930s (1935-1938)

**1935 season.** The season chronicle names Camp Chief Greig Macdiarmid, Senior Director Howie Langille, Junior Director Ernie Taylor, Juvenile Director Lorne Hamilton, and camp doctors Dr. Weavers (later replaced by Dr. Eardley).^8 There were 35 counsellors on staff.^8 A "Mr. Buckley" was also associated with Kanawana during this period: the *Westmount Examiner* of March 29, 1935, described him as having "many years experience in camping, both at Kanawana and at other camps in Canada and the United States."^17 His specific role and exact dates of service are not documented; the characterization suggests he may have been a director or senior staff member, but this remains unconfirmed.

**1938 season.** The *Green Triangle* newsletter of July 29, 1938 confirms Chief Macdiarmid still in charge, extending his documented tenure to at least four seasons (1935-1938).^9 Staff included Doc Cooper (Camp Doctor, shared between Kanawana and the Junior League camp), Dox Wims (tent leader), "Mr. Brute" (section director, Mount Ritz), Benny Lashley (choir organizer), Morrey Cross (Tent 3 Juvenile), Shrivell (telegraph/radio operator), Clark Merritt, Brock Brace (Junior section), and a staff member named Smith.^9

### The War Years (1939-1941)

**1939 season.** Edgar E. Smee (b. c.1912-13 Hamilton, Ontario; d. Feb 7, 2002 Ottawa, age 89) served as Juvenile section director.^1 Originally from Hamilton, Smee left his hometown around 1938 — a timeline consistent with his appearance at Kanawana in 1939. By 1942 he had risen to "Resident Director," a distinct role from Camp Director R.H. Hanagan, documenting a two-tier directorship structure during wartime.^10 After his YMCA career (the details of which remain untraced), Smee returned to Hamilton in 1968, where he became a prominent environmentalist. He founded the Conserver Society of Hamilton (originally "Clear Hamilton of Pollution" / CHOP, 1969), helped create the first action plan for Hamilton Harbour, and was named Environmentalist of the Year in 1983. The Ed Smee Conserver Society Environmental Fund was established in his honour in 1999. He was survived by his wife Frieda, daughters Linda, Susan, Sydney, and Sonia, and brothers Norman and Leonard.

Miss M. Rayner served as camp nurse and filled a "camp mother" role — one of the earliest documented women in a Kanawana staff position.^1

**1941 season.** The 48th season under Camp Chief Howie Langille was the best-documented wartime year, thanks to a CFCF radio broadcast on June 26, 1941.^4 Total staff numbered 68.^4 Of 58 trained counsellor applicants, 45 were selected.^4 Fourteen counsellors formed an advance party to prepare camp before the season opened on June 28 with approximately 100 campers.^4 J.W. Perks, Assistant Superintendent of the Protestant School Board, chaired the Personnel Committee overseeing staff selection.^4 Over 30 evacuees from England, Scotland, and continental Europe attended camp that year.^4 Registrations were 30% higher than the previous year.^4 A farewell letter from junior counsellors Dave and Don from the 1940 season survives in the broadcast transcript.^4

### Pre-War Staff: The SGW-Kanawana Bridge (1930s)

**Harold H. Potter (1914-2004)** attended Sir George Williams College (B.A. 1935-1939) and worked summers as camp counsellor at Kamp Kanawana during those years.^20 In 1947, he became the first Canadian-born Black sociologist hired by a Canadian post-secondary institution when appointed lecturer in Sociology at SGW College. He set up the Department of Sociology and Anthropology at SGW in 1960 and maintained YMCA ties throughout his career. Potter's trajectory from Kanawana counsellor to academic pioneer exemplifies the deep institutional bridge between the YMCA camp and Sir George Williams University.

### Post-War Staff (1943-1961)

**Camp historians.** [[people/rl-charlton|R.L. Charlton]] authored "Notes re Early Days of YMCA Camps at Lake St. Joseph and Kanawana" in 1943 — see his standalone article for full biography, including a 1951 source crediting him as the person who proposed the camp's name. W.E. Cushing wrote "Historical sketches — Lake St. Joseph" in the same year. Both manuscripts are held in Concordia Archives sub-series 12A.^2

**Staff development.** Hedley G. Dimock, Coordinator of Staff Development and Training for the Montreal YMCA and lecturer at Sir George Williams University, conducted research at Kamp Kanawana on camp counsellor effectiveness in 1960-1961.^2 In 1963, Dimock became the first Chairman of the Department of Applied Social Science and Director of the Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies at Sir George Williams University.^14 He collaborated extensively with Dr. Raye Kass on group development publications at Concordia University from 1972 onward.^14

**Ross Bannerman.** Before authoring his 1969 report on Kamp Kanawana, Ross Bannerman had a long career in YMCA camping. In 1951, Nelson McEwen recruited Bannerman to direct Camp Pascobac. **This article carries two incompatible titles for McEwen in that year** and does not resolve them: the directors table above has him becoming "first Metropolitan General Secretary, Winnipeg YMCA" in 1951, while the source for the Bannerman recruitment describes him as National Council Boys' Work Secretary. They are different posts and he cannot have held both. Recorded as conflict **c_028** rather than silently picking one. Bannerman went on to direct Camp Stephens in Winnipeg in 1956.^2 His YMCA International Branch file (c. 1968-1970) and his "Report on Kamp Kanawana" (1969) are both held at Concordia Archives (sub-series 12B01).^2 Bannerman later became CEO of the Montreal YMCA in the late 1970s, placing him in a senior organizational role overseeing Kanawana during the same period as Richard Patten's tenure as Executive Director (1976-1979).^2

## Gap Periods

Rewritten 2026-08-14. **The 1947–2003 gap, which framed this project's research agenda for six months, is essentially closed** — see the note under the Camp Directors table. What follows is what genuinely remains.

**No confirmed director:**

- **1895-1900**: Between the 1894 founding and John Roy's 1901 letters
- **1902-1907, 1909, 1911-1912, 1914**: What remains of the former "1902-1920" gap after the 2026-08-14 annual-report sweep, which named someone in charge for 1908, 1910, 1913, 1915, 1916, 1917, 1918 and 1919. The surviving holes are single or paired years, not a two-decade blank
- **1924**: Between Paterson and Cross
- **1929-1931**: Between Cross and Spearman's documented 1932 start. McEwen's extended tenure (c.1927-1947 as administrative overseer) may cover it; McMorris documents him on-site only in 1930-1931
- **1940**: Between Macdiarmid's last documented season (1939) and Langille's 1941. Macdiarmid went overseas in December 1939, so 1940 had someone
- **1943-1945**: Between Hanagan's documented years
- **1947-1958**: **Named at both ends but thin in between.** Roy Locke is stated for 1947 and held the camping portfolio to September 1954; Murray Shantz is stated for 1955. The intervening seasons rest on continuity and on the *absence* of any recorded staff change, not on statement. The annual reports of these years name no paid camp director at all — they list only volunteer chairmen
- **1968**: **A single genuinely blank summer.** No document in the digitized corpus names an on-site director. Seaman appears in the branch officers table, but Geoff Anderson dates his own appointment to 1 February 1969, so 1968's on-site arrangement is unaccounted for. Checked: the 1968 annual report, and every 1968 news release and newsletter — none of which mentions Kanawana at all
- **1972**: Tom Potts holds "part of" the year on a 1993 alumni recollection and one ambiguous branch-table cell. Nothing contemporaneous
- **1983-1985**: Bracketed between Goddard's documented November 1982 and FY1986 appearances, not stated — though he is independently documented at Kanawana in early 1983 by the CCA's 1982-83 Publicity report, which thanks "Peter Goddard and the staff of Kamp Kanawana in Montreal" for mailing the National Camping Day posters to every camp in Canada, without naming his title.^31 — and again at the turn of 1983-84, as "Peter Goddard (Qué.)" on the CCA's International Year of Youth Task Force.^33 No Kanawana season report survives for 1980-1986, and the annual reports of 1980-81 through 1985 name no Kanawana director — the 1982-83 report has no staff roster at all
- **2004-present**: The 2003-04 and 2004-05 Senior Management lists carry **no** Kanawana director; the post appears to have been folded elsewhere after Carter's interim year. The modern record picks up again from other sources

**Three claims removed from this section on 2026-08-14, because the documents closed or refuted them:**

*1963-1973* is closed but for **1972** — Seaman signs 1964-66 and holds the branch row through 1968; Anderson 1969; Turner 1970-71; Robitaille 1973. (This line previously also listed 1968 as open, contradicting the table above it, which gives Seaman as director **1959-1968**. Corrected 2026-08-28.)

*1976-1978* is closed outright. Walsh ran the camp on site throughout, and the "Director of Camping" title that made this look uncertain turns out to be the ordinary title of the man who also ran Kanawana — Seaman used it a decade earlier.

*The Richard Patten claim.* This section formerly ended: "Richard Patten was Executive Director of YMCA Montreal 1976-1979, so while not the camp director himself, he would have been the organizational head overseeing Kanawana during this transition." **False.** Patten ran the Centre-ville/Downtown branch; the association's chief executive was Ross Bannerman, and camping was a separate parallel branch under Walsh and then Twynam. It is recorded here rather than quietly deleted because of how it survived: a VERIFY pass flagged the sentence as *uncited* and fixed it by *adding a citation*. Checking that every claim carries a marker is not the same as checking that the claim is true.
