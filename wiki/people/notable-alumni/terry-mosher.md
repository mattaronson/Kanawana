# Terry Mosher (Aislin)

*Status: E1-reviewed | Sources: 12*
*Last Updated: 2026-07-09 (Batch 5 research pass: parents and schools added from 2015 interview coverage; Pip Award ceremony date corrected to August 8, 2015 (day-of-week check: Aug 3, 2015 was a Monday, Aug 8 a Saturday; two independent sources confirm Aug 8))*

## Overview

Christopher Terry Mosher, OC (born November 11, 1942, Ottawa), known professionally as **Aislin**, is a Canadian editorial cartoonist who has drawn for the *Montreal Gazette* since 1972.^1 ^2 One of Canada's most recognized political cartoonists, he has produced more than 14,000 editorial cartoons and published approximately 52 books over a career spanning more than five decades.^2 ^3

Mosher is a Kanawana alumnus who attended camp as a camper in 1952–1953.^4 ^5 In 2015, he received the Pip Alumni Award and returned to Camp Kanawana for the first time in more than 60 years.^6

## Early Life and Education

Mosher was born in Ottawa "within sight of the Parliament Buildings on Remembrance Day 1942," to Jack and Norma Mosher.^2 ^11 He attended fourteen different schools in Montreal, Toronto, and Quebec City, a childhood mobility he discussed in 2015 interviews around his Pip Award visit.^11 He studied art at Toronto's Central Technical School and the Ontario College of Art before dropping out to hitchhike across North America for two years, supporting himself by sketching people in bars and taverns.^2

He graduated from the École des Beaux-arts in 1967 (now part of UQAM), having famously forged his high school graduation certificate to gain entrance — a feat he later called "his most successful work."^2

## Kanawana Connection

Mosher was a camper at Kanawana during the 1952 and 1953 seasons, when the pre-1959 section naming scheme (Bantam, Junior, Intermediate, Senior) would have been in effect.^4 ^5 His return to camp in 2015 for the Pip Award ceremony marked his first visit in more than 60 years.^6 On Saturday, August 8, 2015, he was the eighth recipient of the award, presented at Saint-Sauveur, Quebec — the ceremony date is corrected here from the previously cited "August 3, 2015" (which was in fact a Monday); two independent 2015 sources give August 8.^6 ^11 ^12

## Career

### Early Work (1963–1972)

From 1963 to 1967, Mosher drew portraits and caricatures of tourists on the streets of Quebec City, where he co-founded Artists' Alley, an open-air gallery on Rue du Trésor that remains a popular attraction.^2 His first published cartoon — a drawing of Charles de Gaulle — appeared in *Saturday Night* magazine in September 1967.^2 His first newspaper cartoon, about a police raid on an African entertainment group, was published in the *Montreal Star* on December 10, 1967.^2

In 1969, he was appointed official cartoonist of the *Montreal Star*.^2

### Montreal Gazette (1972–present)

Mosher moved to the *Montreal Gazette* in 1972, where he has remained for more than 54 years — one of the longest tenures of any editorial cartoonist at a single newspaper in Canadian history.^1 ^2 His pen name "Aislin" is a rendition of the name of his eldest daughter Aislinn (without the second "n").^2

He has freelanced for major international publications including *Punch*, *The Atlantic Monthly*, *Harper's*, *National Lampoon*, *Time*, *The New York Times*, and *Reader's Digest* (Canadian edition), and has illustrated multiple covers for *TIME* magazine.^2

### Publications

Mosher is the author of approximately 52 books, including:^2 ^3

- *The Hecklers: A History of Canadian Political Cartooning* (1978, with Peter Desbarats) — a National Film Board film based on this book was released in 1980
- *The Anglo Guide to Survival in Quebec* (1983)
- *Drawing Bones: 15 Years of Cartooning Brian Mulroney* (1991)
- A biography of legendary Canadian political cartoonist Duncan Macpherson

Mosher also holds a Gold National Magazine Award, five prizes from the International Salon of Caricature, and served as President Emeritus of the Association of Canadian Cartoonists.^2 ^3

The Concordia honorary doctorate was conferred during the spring 2018 convocation at Salle Wilfrid-Pelletier, Place des Arts, alongside fellow cartoonist Serge Chapleau.^7

## Community Involvement

Mosher has a long association with the Old Brewery Mission, Montreal's largest homeless shelter, having joined its board of directors in 2001.^2 In 2017, the McCord Museum mounted a major exhibition, *Aislin: 50 Years of Cartoons*, and holds 6,000 of his original cartoons in its permanent collection.^8

## Notable Controversies

In 1993, MP Robert Layton denounced an Aislin cartoon depicting outgoing Prime Minister Brian Mulroney lying face down in the snow as "a crime against fundamental Canadian values of decency and mutual respect," making Mosher the first political cartoonist censured in the House of Commons.^2 He received the Order of Canada ten years later.
