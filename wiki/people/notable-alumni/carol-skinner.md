# Carol Skinner

*Status: draft | Sources: 7*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-05*

## Overview

**Carol Sharman Skinner** (died 30 or 31 January 2021) was a Camp Kanawana counsellor and section director from 1990 to 1995, a camp administrator, an art therapist, and — for the last seven years of her life — one of Canada's more visible advocates for people living with ALS. She received Kanawana's [[traditions/pip-alumni-award|Pip Alumni Award]] in 2016, presented before campers on 7 July and again at the September alumni gathering.^1 ^2

Her ALS advocacy took her from a diagnosis in September 2013 to the first meeting of the all-party ALS Caucus on Parliament Hill in November 2016, a national letter-writing campaign for federal research funding, and the advisory council of the International Alliance of ALS/MND Associations.^3 She died at the end of January 2021. **Her own account of all of it — a blog called *Live Love Laugh with Carol* — no longer exists at its address**, and the reason is not age but a lapsed domain.

## Camp Kanawana (1990–1995)

Skinner worked as a counsellor and section director — not overall camp director — across five seasons.^1 Which section or sections she led is not recorded in any source this project holds.

## After camp: Cape Cod, art therapy, Ottawa

She became Director of YMCA Camp Hayward in Cape Cod, Massachusetts, then took a Master's degree in Art Therapy. She worked as a school counsellor and as Art Therapist for the Canadian Mental Health Association in Ottawa, giving that work up when ALS made it impossible.^1 ^4

She was a longtime resident of **Kirkland**, on Montreal's West Island, and a **McGill University art history graduate**, before making her home in the Ottawa area with her husband, **Travis Skinner**.^4 ^5 That Montreal upbringing and a McGill degree are the ordinary background of a Kanawana staff member of her era; the Cape Cod posting is the unusual part, and it is the same movement between Canadian and American YMCA camps that recurs through [[connections/related-camps/points-of-contact|this wiki's inter-camp record]].

## Living with ALS, 2013–2021

Symptoms began in early 2013, when her legs would not hold her as she tried to skate on the Rideau Canal. She was diagnosed with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis on **19 September 2013**.^4 ^5

What followed is documented mostly by the organisation she worked with. ALS Canada's memorial records that she "soon after became an ALS advocate and an ambassador for ALS Canada," that she "attended the first meeting of the all-party ALS Caucus on Parliament Hill in November 2016 and many other government meetings thereafter," and that she worked "with others in the community to spearhead a letter-writing campaign calling on the federal government to provide dedicated ALS research funding."^3 She was a regular speaker at the Ottawa Walk to End ALS, where her teams raised more than **$26,000** across four years, and she sat on the **International Alliance of ALS/MND Associations' pALS and cALS Advisory Council**, carrying a Canadian patient's perspective into an international body.^3 ^4

On receiving the Pip Award she said of the camp motto: *"Never has* Non Nobis Solum *(Not For Ourselves Alone) meant so much to me."*^2 The award is given for exactly what she was doing at the time — taking what was learned at Kanawana and applying it to the larger world — and she received it three years into the diagnosis, while doing it.

## Her death, and how this article dated it

The 2026 Pip Award news release lists her among past recipients as **"the late Carol Skinner"** and gives no date.^6 That placed her death anywhere between June 2018, when the *Ottawa Citizen* reported her alive, and August 2026 — an eight-year window.

ALS Canada's memorial post closes it. Dated **4 February 2021**, a Thursday, it opens: *"With great sadness, we recognize the incredible contributions of Carol Skinner, who passed away last weekend."*^3 That fixes the death to the weekend of **30–31 January 2021**. It is a two-day window rather than a date, and this article states it as one rather than picking a day [f_4823].

## The blog that is gone

ALS Canada called *Live Love Laugh with Carol* "a source of positivity and strength for many."^3 It was her own long-form account of a life with ALS, written by a Kanawana alumna, and it is **the only first-person source of that kind this project has ever identified for any Kanawana person**.

Fetched on 5 September 2026, `livelovelaughwithcarol.com` returns an Indonesian online-gambling site. Search indexes still carry the old navigation — Love Story, ALS, Blog, Tips & Tricks, Gallery, Travel, Media — and both her and Travis Skinner's contact details, so the site stood in that form recently. The Internet Archive is blocked by this environment's egress policy, so no archived copy could be reached from here [f_4825].

**This is an access failure, not an absence**, and the distinction matters more here than usual: a reader who searched that address and found a casino would reasonably conclude the blog never existed. It did, and someone with archive access can still read it.
