# James Orbinski

*Status: draft | Sources: 6*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-07 (Kanawana nominated him for the YMCA peace award in 2000, and dated his counsellor years before 1990)*

## Overview

Dr. James Orbinski is a Canadian physician and humanitarian who served as President of the International Council of Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) and accepted the 1999 Nobel Peace Prize on the organization's behalf. A camper and counsellor at Camp Kanawana in the 1980s and 1990s, he received the camp's Pip Alumni Award in 2024 — the award's 15th recipient.^1 ^2

## Early Life and Education

Orbinski was born in 1960 in England and grew up in Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, Montreal.^1 He attended John XXIII High School in Montreal before Dawson CEGEP, then earned a BA in Psychology from Trent University (1984), an MD from McMaster University (1990), and an MA in International Relations from the University of Toronto (1998).^1 ^3 ^4

## Humanitarian Career

Orbinski co-founded MSF Canada in 1990 and served as President of the International Council of MSF from 1998 to 2001, accepting the 1999 Nobel Peace Prize on the organization's behalf.^5 His MSF field missions included Peru (1992), Somalia (1992–93), Afghanistan (1994), Rwanda during the 1994 genocide, and Zaire during the refugee crisis that followed it (1996–97).^5 For his work during the Rwandan genocide he received the Meritorious Service Cross (1997); in 2017 he received the Concordia University Loyola Medal.^4 He co-founded Dignitas International in 2004, a medical and research NGO focused on HIV/AIDS and tuberculosis, and was made an Officer of the Order of Canada in 2009.^1 He authored *An Imperfect Offering: Humanitarian Action for the 21st Century* (2008), winner of the Shaughnessy Cohen Award for Political Writing.^4 He currently serves as Principal of Massey College at the University of Toronto.^1

## The Camp Nominated Him, in 2000

The Montreal YMCA's staff bulletin *Pour votre information* of December 2000 lists that year's
nominations for the association's peace award, one from each unit that put a name forward. Alongside a
community organiser nominated by YMCA Saint-Laurent and a women's coalition nominated by YMCA
Pointe-Saint-Charles stands:^6

> **Dr. James Orbinsky, mis en candidature par le Kamp Kanawana.** Le Dr. James Orbinsky est
> actuellement président de Médecins sans frontières… Récipiendaire du Prix Nobel de la paix en 1999,
> **il a été moniteur au Kamp Kanawana avant de se joindre à MSF en 1990**.

*("Dr. James Orbinski, nominated by Kamp Kanawana… Recipient of the 1999 Nobel Peace Prize, he was a
counsellor at Kamp Kanawana before joining MSF in 1990." The bulletin spells the surname "Orbinsky"
throughout.)*

**Two things follow.** The nomination is a fact about the camp as much as about him: **Kanawana put him
forward as its peace-maker**, a year after the Nobel, and the entry the association printed leads with
the camp connection rather than with his medicine. And the sentence gives a **firm upper bound where
this article had only a decade**: open question 1 below notes that sources give his Kanawana years as
"1980s–1990s." The camp's own parent organisation says he was a counsellor there **before joining MSF
in 1990**, which closes the range at the top. It still gives no start year.

The bulletin also quotes his Nobel acceptance speech, in French translation, as its statement of what
a peace-maker is: more than offering aid, "**nous visons à permettre à chaque personne de regagner ses
droits et sa dignité en tant qu'être humain**."
