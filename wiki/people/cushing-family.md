## Summary

The Cushing family had a multi-generational connection to the YMCA of Montreal spanning from the 1860s through at least the 1940s. The patriarch Lemuel Cushing Sr. (1806-1875), a businessman of United Empire Loyalist descent, had thirteen children including eight sons who became prominent in Montreal business and civic life.^1 His son Lemuel Cushing Jr. (1842-1881) served as YMCA president in 1869-1870 and published *The Genealogy of the Cushing Family* (1877).^2 A later Lemuel Cushing — whose exact identity remains uncertain — brought the first group of boys to Lake Saint-Joseph in 1892, inaugurating the Montreal YMCA's camping program two years before the formal establishment of Camp Jubilee.^3 W.E. Cushing wrote historical sketches about the Lake Saint-Joseph camps in 1943, and a Dr. Cushing advised keeping Camp Kanawana open during the 1946 polio outbreak.^4 ^5

## The Patriarch: Lemuel Cushing Sr. (1806-1875)

Lemuel Cushing was born April 29, 1806 in Trois-Rivières, Lower Canada, the eighth child of Job Cushing and Sarah (Sally) Rice, a United Empire Loyalist family descended from Matthew Cushing, who arrived in Hingham, Massachusetts in 1638.^1 He married Catherine Hutchins of Lachute on August 25, 1836.^1 Starting a retail business in the area now called Cushing, Quebec (between Carillon and Grenville), he built wealth through the Ottawa valley timber trade. All eight of his sons became "active and powerful businessmen" in Montreal.^1

## Lemuel Cushing Jr. and the YMCA (1842-1881)

Lemuel Jr. was called to the Montreal bar in 1865 and served as president of the Montreal YMCA from 1869 to 1870 — the first documented Cushing-YMCA connection.^2 He married Ellen Myra Macdougall in 1869 and represented Argenteuil as a Member of Parliament from 1874 to 1875. In 1877 he published *The Genealogy of the Cushing Family*, tracing the lineage from 1638.^2 He died March 1, 1881 at age 39.

A second edition of the genealogy was published in 1905 by James S. Cushing (1878-1957), likely a grandson of Lemuel Sr., who expanded the work to 598 pages.^6

## The 1892 Camping Pioneer

In 1892, a "Lemuel Cushing" brought the first group of boys to an island on Lake Saint-Joseph in the Township of Howard (now Saint-Adolphe-d'Howard), inaugurating the Montreal YMCA's summer camping program.^3 Since Lemuel Jr. died in 1881, this must have been a different family member — most likely a grandson of Lemuel Sr. through one of the eight sons. Thomas Cushing's branch is the best documented (through his son Gordon), but any of the sons could have named a child "Lemuel." Identifying this individual requires access to the 1905 Cushing genealogy or the 1893 camper lists in the Concordia Archives.

## Thomas Cushing and Montreal Brewing

Thomas Cushing founded the Montreal Brewing Company in 1876, which produced India Pale Ale, pale ale, stout porter, and "sand porter." In 1909, Montreal Brewing merged with Dawes Brewery and others to form National Breweries Limited. Thomas's son Gordon Cushing served on the National board and later became a stockbroker.^7

## Later Cushings in YMCA Service

**W.E. Cushing** (likely "William E. Cushing") wrote "Historical sketches—Lake St. Joseph" and "Early Days at Lake St. Joseph" in 1943, both preserved in the Concordia Archives.^5 He also had correspondence regarding the history of East End YMCA work (1906-1933). His exact relationship to the family tree is undetermined.

**Charles Cushing** is mentioned in YMCA research reports held in Concordia Archives sub-series 13D, suggesting involvement with YMCA governance or legal affairs alongside his notarial career.^5

**Dr. Cushing** advised keeping Camp Kanawana open during the 1946 polio outbreak — six cases were reported, canoe trips were cancelled, but the camp remained open on his advice.^4
