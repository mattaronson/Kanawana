## Overview

G. David Twynam (PhD) served as camp director of Kamp Kanawana circa 1979–1980.^1 He subsequently built an academic career in outdoor recreation, tourism, and event management, holding faculty and dean-level positions at three Canadian universities.^2 ^3 His research on volunteer motivation at sporting events, conducted at Lakehead University, has been widely cited (723+ citations on ResearchGate).^4

## Kanawana Directorship

Correspondence from "Camp director Dave Twynam" is dated 1979 in the Concordia University Archives (sub-sub-series P0145/12B01).^1 He is also referenced as "Dave" in the Ka-News 1980 publication.^5 His directorship falls in the late 1970s period between the documented tenures of earlier directors.

## Academic Career

Twynam's career trajectory after Kanawana:^2 ^3 ^4

1. **Lakehead University** (Thunder Bay, Ontario) — Faculty, School of Outdoor Recreation, Parks, and Tourism (by late 1990s). Conducted research on volunteer motivation at sporting events, including the 1996 Scott Tournament of Hearts.^6
2. **University College of the Cariboo / Thompson Rivers University** (Kamloops, BC) — Dean, School of Tourism (until c.2009). Launched the inaugural Bachelor of Tourism Management program, subsequently adopted by other BC institutions.^3
3. **Vancouver Island University** (Nanaimo, BC) — Dean, Faculty of Management. Received the BC Council on Admissions and Transfer Community Leadership Award.^7

## Research Contributions

Twynam co-developed the Special Event Volunteer Motivation Scale (SEVMS), a 28-item instrument with four factors: Purposive, Solidary, External Traditions, and Commitments.^6 Key publications include:

- Farrell, Johnston & Twynam, "Volunteer Motivation, Satisfaction, and Management at an Elite Sporting Competition" (*Journal of Sport Management*, 1998)^6
- Twynam, Farrell & Johnston, "Leisure and Volunteer Motivation at a Special Sporting Event" (2002)^6
- Research on sustainable tourism (WWF Arctic Tourism Guidelines), marine tourism (HMS Erebus and Terror National Historic Site), and residents' perceptions of tourism^4

He was also involved in the Heart of Gold Project, an international sustainability collaboration between VIU and Costa Rican institutions.^8
