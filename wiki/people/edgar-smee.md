# Edgar E. Smee

*Status: E1-reviewed | Sources: 8*
*Last Updated: 2026-03-16*

## Overview

Edgar E. "Ed" Smee (c. 1912–2002) served at Camp Kanawana as Juvenile section director in 1939 and as Resident Director in 1942, the latter a distinct role from Camp Director R.H. Hanagan that documents a wartime two-tier directorship structure.^1 ^2 After a 30-year career away from Hamilton, Ontario — a period that likely encompassed his Kanawana years — Smee returned to his hometown in 1968 and became one of Hamilton's most prominent environmental activists, founding Clear Hamilton of Pollution (CHOP) in 1969 and serving as Founding Chair of the Conserver Society of Hamilton & District.^3 ^4

## Early Life

Smee was born circa 1912–1913 in Hamilton, Ontario, where he grew up swimming in Red Hill Creek.^5 He left Hamilton around 1938 at approximately age 25.^3 ^5

## Kanawana Service (1939–1942)

Smee first appears in camp records as Juvenile section director in 1939, as documented in the McMorris thesis.^1 By 1942, he held the title "Resident Director" alongside R.H. Hanagan, who served as "Camp Director." A *Montreal Gazette* article from August 5, 1942, titled "Y.M.C.A. Boys Stage Parade and Circus," names both Hanagan and Smee in connection with a camp event featuring sections organized as Bantam, Junior, Intermediate, and Senior.^2

This two-tier directorship — Camp Director and Resident Director — is one of the few documented instances of a divided leadership structure at Kanawana and may reflect wartime staffing constraints. It is unclear which man bore day-to-day operational responsibility.^2

## The 30-Year Absence (c. 1938–1968)

The Environmentalist of the Year biography states that Smee returned to Hamilton "after a 30-year absence" in 1968.^3 His departure from Hamilton around 1938 aligns precisely with his earliest documented Kanawana service in 1939, strongly suggesting he relocated to Montreal for YMCA work. His activities during the years between 1942 and 1968 are entirely undocumented in publicly available sources. Given his YMCA camp experience, he may have continued in YMCA or youth-service work, but this remains speculative pending archival research.

## Environmental Career (1968–2002)

### Red Hill Creek and CHOP

Upon returning to Hamilton in 1968, Smee visited Red Hill Creek — the stream where he had swum as a boy — and found it polluted by a storm sewer outlet. He discovered that lake beaches were also unsafe for swimming. This "sparked a deep anger" in a man who had been "raised to treasure, not squander, the earth's resources."^3

In 1969, Smee co-founded **Clear Hamilton of Pollution (CHOP)**, a grassroots environmental advocacy group.^3 ^4

### Conserver Society

CHOP was incorporated in 1984 as the **Conserver Society of Hamilton & District Inc.**, with Smee as Founding Chair.^4 Under his leadership, the organization's activities expanded to include composting programs, bicycle paths advocacy, pesticide reduction campaigns, urban sprawl limitation, waste management, air quality monitoring, and transportation initiatives.^4

Smee is credited with helping create the first action plan for Hamilton Harbour/Burlington Bay.^4 In 1986, he initiated the Conserver Society's first Environmental Trust Fund to support local environmental projects.^4

### Environmentalist of the Year (1983)

In 1983, Smee received the **Dr. Victor Cecilioni Environmentalist of the Year Award**, Hamilton's highest environmental honour, given annually since 1979.^3 The award is co-sponsored by the Conserver Society, Hamilton Naturalists' Club, Hamilton Conservation Authority, Environment Hamilton, and the Bay Area Restoration Council.^3

### Legacy: The Ed Smee Fund

In 1999, the Environmental Trust Fund was renamed the **Ed Smee Conserver Society Environmental Fund** in his honour and transferred to the Hamilton Community Foundation, which continues to manage it.^4 ^6 The fund provides grants for environmental education, research, and advocacy in Hamilton and Burlington.

## Death

Smee died peacefully on February 7, 2002, at the Glebe Centre in Ottawa, at age 89. He was survived by his wife Frieda, daughters Linda, Susan, Sydney, and Sonia, and brothers Norman and Leonard.^5 A memorial gathering was held February 16, 2002, at the Workers Arts and Heritage Centre, 51 Stuart Street, Hamilton.^5 His obituary directed donations to the Conserver Society.^5
