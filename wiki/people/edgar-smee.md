# Edgar E. Smee

## Overview

Edgar E. Smee (c.1912–2002) served at Camp Kanawana as Juvenile section director in 1939 and as Resident Director in 1942, the latter a distinct role from Camp Director R.H. Hanagan.^1 ^2 After leaving camp work, Smee became a prominent Hamilton, Ontario, environmentalist, founding the Conserver Society of Hamilton & District and earning recognition as Environmentalist of the Year in 1983.^3

## Kanawana Service

Smee first appears in camp records as Juvenile section director in 1939.^1 By 1942, he held the title "Resident Director" alongside R.H. Hanagan, who served as "Camp Director" — documenting a two-tier directorship structure during wartime.^2 A Montreal Gazette article from August 5, 1942, titled "Y.M.C.A. Boys Stage Parade and Circus," names both Hanagan and Smee in connection with a camp event featuring sections organized as Bantam, Junior, Intermediate, and Senior.^2

## Biographical Details

Smee was born circa 1912–1913 in Hamilton, Ontario, where he grew up swimming in Red Hill Creek.^4 He left Hamilton around 1938, and his documented Kanawana service (1939–1942) suggests he was based in Montreal during this period.^5

## Environmental Career

Smee returned to Hamilton in 1968 after a 30-year absence and was dismayed by pollution at Red Hill Creek and local beaches, which "sparked a deep anger" leading to environmental advocacy.^3 In 1969, he founded "Clear Hamilton of Pollution" (CHOP), which was incorporated as the Conserver Society of Hamilton & District in 1984.^3 He was the organization's founding chair and helped establish 12 recycling depots in the Hamilton area.^3

Smee was named Environmentalist of the Year in 1983.^3 The Ed Smee Conserver Society Environmental Fund was established in his honour in 1999 and is managed by the Hamilton Community Foundation.^6

He died on February 7, 2002, at the Glebe Centre in Ottawa, at age 89, survived by his wife Frieda, daughters Linda, Susan, Sydney, and Sonia, and brothers Norman and Leonard.^4
