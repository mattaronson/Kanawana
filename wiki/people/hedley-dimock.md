# Hedley Dimock and the Science of Camping

*Status: stub | Sources: src_aca_dimock_bio, src_concordia_atom_12B05, src_mcmorris_thesis*
*Last Updated: 2026-03-02*

## Overview

Two individuals named Hedley Dimock made significant contributions to the study and practice of camping, and both are connected to the YMCA of Montreal and Camp Kanawana. Hedley Seldon Dimock (1891–1958) was a pioneering camp researcher who co-authored *Camping and Character* (1929), the foundational study of camp-based character education. Hedley G. Dimock (born c. 1928) conducted camp counsellor effectiveness research at Kamp Kanawana in 1960–1961 and went on to found the Department of Applied Social Science at Sir George Williams University (now Concordia). Their exact familial relationship has not been confirmed, though the shared surname, YMCA involvement, and overlapping fields strongly suggest a father-son connection.

## Hedley Seldon Dimock (1891–1958)

Hedley Seldon Dimock was born February 11, 1891 in South Rawdon, Hants, Nova Scotia.^1 He served in the First World War with the Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry. In August 1918, he was severely wounded after throwing himself on a German grenade to protect his unit.^1

After the war, Dimock entered camp research. In 1923, he and Charles E. Hendry began a pioneering study at Camp Ahmek in Algonquin Park, Ontario, which was published as *Camping and Character: A Camp Experiment in Character Education* (Association Press, 1929/1931).^1 This was among the first systematic studies to measure the effects of summer camp on child development.

Dimock became dean of YMCA College in Chicago (later George Williams College), where in 1930 he launched institutes on the role of camps in "character education" of children.^1 He remained a leading figure in camping education until his death in 1958.

The American Camp Association established the **Hedley S. Dimock Award** in 1965 to recognize contributions to camping through related fields such as outdoor education, conservation, medicine, education, or social sciences.^1

## Hedley G. Dimock (c. 1928–?)

Hedley G. Dimock served as Coordinator of Staff Development and Training for the Montreal YMCA and lecturer at Sir George Williams University.^2 In 1960–1961, he conducted research at Kamp Kanawana on camp counsellor effectiveness.^2

In 1962, Dimock co-initiated a Community Development Study of the Cree of Mistassini-Chibougamau, Quebec.^2 In 1963, he became the first Chairman of the Department of Applied Social Science and Director of the Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies at Sir George Williams University.^2

From 1972 onward, he collaborated extensively with Dr. Raye Kass (hired as Assistant Professor of Applied Human Sciences at Concordia in 1972) on group development publications.^2 Their work on group dynamics and leadership development built on the empirical camping research tradition established by the elder Dimock.

## Connection to Kanawana

The younger Dimock's counsellor effectiveness studies at Kanawana (1960–1961) represent one of the few documented instances of formal academic research conducted at the camp.^2 ^3 The McMorris thesis cites the Concordia Archives sub-series P0145/12B05 (Staff) as containing materials related to these studies, including interpretation scores from the 1960 research project.^3
