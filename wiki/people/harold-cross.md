# Harold C. Cross

*Status: E1-reviewed | Sources: 16*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-07 (four rungs from the annual reports' staff-changes sections, including a second Acting General Secretaryship)*

## Overview

Harold C. Cross was a significant figure in the YMCA of Montreal during the mid-twentieth century, **Executive Secretary of its Westmount branch by 1928**^11 and camp director at Kamp Kanawana around 1925-1928 and later becoming the organization's principal historian.^3 He authored *100 Years of Service to Youth* (1951), the centennial history of the Montreal YMCA covering the period 1851-1951.^1 The book is also referenced as *One Hundred Years: The Story of the Montreal Y.M.C.A.*^1 Cross's historical correspondence spans from 1939 to 1957, and he was involved with the National Council of YMCAs of Canada's Boys' Work program during the 1940s.^2

No birth date, death date or education has been located. **His working life is now bracketed**, though: he entered Association work in **1912** and retired on **1 March 1953**, a forty-one-year career, which implies a birth around 1885-1892 — the first age estimate this article has been able to make.^12 He was still alive in 1969, when the *Retired Secretaries* list last carries him. A photograph of Harold C. Cross dated 1971 exists in the Concordia Archives, suggesting he was alive at that date.^1 His archival correspondence begins in 1938 (a memo to Les Copland) and extends through at least 1957.^1 ^5

In 1966 he was named in print by **Henry F. Hall**, principal of Sir George Williams University — the YMCA's own university, whose Hall Building carries his name — who thanked "my friend, Mr. Harold C. Cross, dedicated professional leader and **historian of the Montreal YMCA**" in the preface to *The Georgian Spirit*.^10 That is the strongest external statement of Cross's standing this article has: not "a historian" or "the author of a history" but the historian of the association, said fifteen years after the centennial book by a man positioned to know. It is also, still, no biography — Hall gives no dates, no title, no address.

**The career began in Montreal, not in Victoria.** Until 2026-09-07 the earliest thing known about Cross was a 1921 roster figure saying he had entered YMCA employed service in 1912, with the whole stretch to 1919 blank. The digitized Year Book run at the University of Minnesota fills five of those years: he was the **boys' worker at the YMCA of Montreal's North Branch** through the 1911-12, 1912-13 and 1913-14 volumes, and **General Secretary of the Charlottetown YMCA** in 1914-15, before turning up at Victoria.^12 So the man who returned to Montreal in the mid-1920s to direct Kanawana was coming back to the association he had started in, which is a different story from the one this article told for two years. **And he had already been to the camp**, which this article had never said although [[people/directors-index|the directors index]] had recorded it for months: an annual report places him at Kanawana in the summer of **1913**, sharing charge of the season with two Boys' Department colleagues.^13 What the *Year Book* adds is why it happened only once — 1913 was his last Montreal summer before Charlottetown.

Phase 2 research (2026) established that Cross was based at the Victoria, British Columbia YMCA from 1919 to 1925 before transferring to Montreal.^6 A document titled "Personal Reminiscences — Victoria, B.C. 1919-25, by Harold C. Cross" (written 1967) survives in the Concordia Archives, sub-series 14H ("Local YMCA Histories Collected by J.M. Duckworth").^6 The career trajectory now runs: Montreal North Branch, boys' work (1911–1914), including part of the **1913 Kanawana season** → Charlottetown, General Secretary (1914–15) → **Overseas Representative, National Council of Canada (1915–1917)** → Victoria BC YMCA, boys' work (1918–1925) → Montreal YMCA / Camp Kanawana director (c. 1925–1928) → Religious Education Council of Quebec, president (1935–36) and later corresponding secretary (1942–45), with no office in between^14 → National Council Boys' Work involvement (1938–1945) → YMCA institutional historian (1949–1951+).

## Known Roles and Timeline

- **1912**: **Entered YMCA employed service.** The *YMCA Year Book and Official Rosters* for 1921 carries an "Alphabetical List of Employed Officers" whose printed legend says "the figures after each name indicate the year of entry into Association work", and its entry reads "**Cross, H. C., Victoria, B. C., Boys', 12**".^8 That is **seven years earlier than any date this article had**, and it opened a question that did not exist before: where was he between 1912 and 1919? **Five of those years are now answered, and the first three of them say Montreal** — see the entries that follow. The figure sits a year later than his first appearance in the run, which is in the 1911-12 volume; either the volume year and the roster year overlap or the printed figure is approximate, and it is left unresolved rather than argued either way.^12
- **1911-12, 1912-13 and 1913-14**: **Boys' worker at the YMCA of Montreal's North Branch**, more than a decade before he directed Kanawana and before this article had him anywhere at all. Three consecutive Year Books print the same branch staff: "North Br. … Physical, W. S. Maguire — **Boys, Harold C. Cross**," the branch given from 1914-15 as 2300 Park Ave.^12 **This is the earliest Montreal connection on record for him**, and it changes the shape of the career: the man who came back from Victoria in the mid-1920s to run the camp was not new to the association, he was returning to it. The branch secretary through all three years is **W. J. Holliday**, the man who opened Kanawana's Lac Wilson site in 1910 — so Cross and Holliday sat on the same small branch staff. What that proximity amounted to is not documented, and nothing here says the two men knew each other well or that Cross had any camp involvement in these years.
- **Summer 1913**: **In charge of part of the Kanawana season** — his earliest camp connection by more than a decade, recorded in [[people/directors-index|the directors index]] since before this article knew of it. The association's *Sixty-Third Annual Report*, for the year ending 30 April 1914 and so reporting the summer of 1913, says: "The Boys' Camp at Lake Kanawana was open for July and August with 162 boys, apart from visitors, **in charge of Messrs. D. J. Evans, Harold Cross, and H. A. Lordly for successive portions of the season**."^13 All three were Boys' Department staff — Evans had just become the association's Boys' Secretary that August and Lordly had joined the department that July — so "successive portions" describes three colleagues splitting a season, not three directors. **It fits the Year Book track exactly**: he is Montreal's North Branch boys' worker through the 1913-14 volume and gone to Charlottetown by 1914-15, which makes 1913 his last Montreal summer. The man who came back in the mid-1920s to direct the camp had already run part of a season at it.
- **1914-15**: **General Secretary of the Charlottetown YMCA, Prince Edward Island** — "Prince Edward Island / Charlottetown … President, J. Kaye Ross — **Gen. Secy., Harold C. Cross**."^12 A general secretaryship, the top paid post of an association, roughly two years after entering employed service. He does not stay: the 1915-16 volume gives Charlottetown's General Secretary as J. Morrison Clark.
- **1915-1917**: **Overseas with the Canadian YMCA.** The Year Book's Canada section prints a table, "Secretaries of the National Council," giving each man a role — General Secretary, Boys' Work Secretary (**Taylor Statten**), Railroad Secretary — and then a long run of names under the role "**Overseas Representative**," carried down by ditto marks. **H. C. Cross is in that run in both the 1915-16 and the 1916-17 volumes.**^12 He is not in the 1914-15 one, where he is still at Charlottetown. **This answers the question this article has been asking since the 1921 roster figure turned up.** He is in company, too: the same run carries **Thomas H. Miller** — the T. Hicks Miller who had charge of the Kanawana season in July 1915 — along with A. W. Forgie and C. T. Sharpe.

The control was sound. **The inference was not** — two lists naming his colleagues does not make two lists exhaustive, and the Overseas Representative run is a third list nobody here knew existed. ## Four rungs from the staff-changes sections

Six of the association's annual reports were held in this repo at read-state *partial* — their camps sections
read, their staff-changes sections not — and Cross is in four of them.^16 None of what follows was in this
article.

**The return to Montreal is dated, and he replaced a named man.** The 1926 report: "In June Mr. T. F. Best,
who had been for eight months in charge of the Westmount Branch… left to resume his evangelistic work
throughout the Dominion. Mr. J. M. Graham substituted for him until **the arrival in September of Mr. Harold
C. Cross, a former member of our staff, who was called to the Executive Secretaryship as successor to Mr.
Cecil L. Brown**." So Westmount, September 1926, after Best and Graham — and the association's own report
calls him "a former member of our staff," which corroborates from the Montreal side the 1911–1914 North
Branch start this article reconstructed out of the *Year Book* run.

**And the 1927 report puts him in one sentence with Holliday and Guiton.** A senior secretary "spent eight
months of his time in Westmount **pending the arrival of Mr. Cross**, relieved Mr. Guiton who had been
**Acting Secretary in Mr. Holliday's absence**, on February 1st."^16 That is the Westmount succession chain
running straight through the years this article dates his Kanawana directorship to. See
[[people/w-j-holliday|W. J. Holliday]].

**A loan-out in 1938.** The association was asked "to loan one of their senior Secretaries to direct the
**English-Speaking section**" of two projects, "and Mr. Harold C. Cross, **Metropolitan Programme
Secretary**, was appointed. Mr. Cross gave a great deal of his time to this work, **over a period of six
months**, the Association making his services available **without cost**."^16

**There were two Acting General Secretaryships, not one, and they are the same man's absences.** This
article has the 1941–1945 spell from a roster, and
[[people/directors-index|the directors index]] separately quotes the 1942 report saying "**John W.
Beaton, our General Secretary, has continued as Senior Secretary for National War Services**." The two
were never put together: Beaton held the top post, was seconded to national war work, and **Cross was
acting in his place**. Then Beaton returned — and in 1951 he died in office. The 1951 report records the
second caretakership, with its cause: "**Following the death of John W. Beaton, General
Secretary, Harold C. Cross, formerly Programme Secretary, was appointed Acting General Secretary.**"^16 The
caretakership ended the next year, and he did not leave: "F. G. Hubbard, formerly of Vancouver, assumed the
position of General Secretary of the Montreal Association in September. **Harold C. Cross and Clarence M.
McCully were subsequently appointed Assistant General Secretaries.**"^16 So through the years he was writing
and publishing the centennial history he was first the association's acting chief executive and then one of
its two deputies.

**The retirement, in the association's own words**, and it settles a scan. The 1953 report: "On **February
28, 1953**, Mr. Harold C. Cross, Assistant General Secretary, **retired after [4]1 years of loyal and
devoted service**."^16 The OCR reads "ll years," which is garbled; the 1912 entry and the 1953 retirement
established elsewhere in this article make it **forty-one**, and the reading rests on that arithmetic rather
than on the page.

## The 1951 Book

Harold C. Cross's principal work, *100 Years of Service to Youth* (also titled *One Hundred Years: The Story of the Montreal Y.M.C.A.*), was written to commemorate the centennial of the Montreal YMCA, which was founded at St. Helen Street Baptist Church in November 1851 — the first YMCA in North America.^1 Chapter 7 of the manuscript is titled "Dominion Square Building (One Hundred Years of Service to Youth)."^1

The McMorris thesis cites the full publication as: Harold C. Cross, *One Hundred Years of Service with Youth: The Story of the Montreal Y.M.C.A* (Montreal: Association Press, 1951).^3 Association Press was the YMCA's own publishing imprint, which explains why the book does not appear in WorldCat, Library and Archives Canada, or BAnQ — it was an institutional publication rather than a commercial release, in contrast to Murray C. Ross's parallel national history, *The Y.M.C.A. in Canada: The Chronicle of a Century* (Ryerson Press, 1951), which was commercially published.^4 The manuscript copy, chapter drafts, and related production materials (printers' quotations, book orders) are held in the Concordia University Archives as part of the YMCA of Montreal fonds (P0145, sub-series 1B).^1

A continuation was later written by F.G. Hubbard: *First Quarter of Second Century of Service to Youth of Montreal, 1951-1976* (1976).^1

**Kanawana's own newspaper calls him a former Chief, and puts him at Marois in 1935.** *The Green
Triangle* of 4 August 1935: "Last Sunday morning **Mr. H. C. Cross, a former Kanawana Chief**,
addressed the camp assembled in chapel. It seemed just like old times to have Mr. Cross back with us;
perhaps we shall see him again, since **he and his family are at present summering at Marois**."^15

The chiefship itself is not new — [[people/directors-index|the directors index]] has him at Kanawana
in 1913 and as Camp Chief about 1926–1928 — but this is the camp's own contemporary description of him
in that role, printed for campers who would have had no reason to be told anything but the plain fact.
What is new is the domestic detail: in the summer of 1935 he was **holidaying with his family at Lake
Marois**, close enough to walk over and preach, and familiar enough that his return read as "just like
old times." For a man this project knows mainly through his later administrative posts and his history
of the association, it is the only glimpse of him off duty.

[Largely resolved 2026-09-07] What was his official title at the Montreal YMCA? **The Year Book answers it across four decades**: Westmount Branch **Secretary** in the 1927-28 volume, Metropolitan **Acting Secretary** in 1945, Metropolitan Board **Program** secretary in 1948, 1950 and 1951, and **retired 1 March 1953**.^12 What remains unanswered is only 1936, where the religious-education yearbook gives his address and not his post. The 2026-09-06 reasoning follows and still holds for that year. **For 1928 the answer is now in hand**: the camp's own brochure calls him **Executive Secretary of the Westmount Y.M.C.A.**^11 That is a branch executive secretaryship, a substantial post, and it is the first title this article has ever held for him. What remains open is the rest of the career — a man can move branches, and nothing yet says what he was in 1936, when the religious-education yearbook has him at the association's Drummond Street address, or in 1951 when he wrote the centenary.
