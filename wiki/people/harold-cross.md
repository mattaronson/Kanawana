# Harold C. Cross

*Status: E1-reviewed | Sources: 13*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-07 (he went overseas with the Canadian YMCA — the war years are no longer a hole)*

## Overview

Harold C. Cross was a significant figure in the YMCA of Montreal during the mid-twentieth century, **Executive Secretary of its Westmount branch by 1928**^11 and camp director at Kamp Kanawana around 1925-1928 and later becoming the organization's principal historian.^3 He authored *100 Years of Service to Youth* (1951), the centennial history of the Montreal YMCA covering the period 1851-1951.^1 The book is also referenced as *One Hundred Years: The Story of the Montreal Y.M.C.A.*^1 Cross's historical correspondence spans from 1939 to 1957, and he was involved with the National Council of YMCAs of Canada's Boys' Work program during the 1940s.^2

No biographical details (birth date, death date, education, or personal background) have been located through web research. A photograph of Harold C. Cross dated 1971 exists in the Concordia Archives, suggesting he was alive at that date.^1 His archival correspondence begins in 1938 (a memo to Les Copland) and extends through at least 1957.^1 ^5

In 1966 he was named in print by **Henry F. Hall**, principal of Sir George Williams University — the YMCA's own university, whose Hall Building carries his name — who thanked "my friend, Mr. Harold C. Cross, dedicated professional leader and **historian of the Montreal YMCA**" in the preface to *The Georgian Spirit*.^10 That is the strongest external statement of Cross's standing this article has: not "a historian" or "the author of a history" but the historian of the association, said fifteen years after the centennial book by a man positioned to know. It is also, still, no biography — Hall gives no dates, no title, no address.

**The career began in Montreal, not in Victoria.** Until 2026-09-07 the earliest thing known about Cross was a 1921 roster figure saying he had entered YMCA employed service in 1912, with the whole stretch to 1919 blank. The digitized Year Book run at the University of Minnesota fills five of those years: he was the **boys' worker at the YMCA of Montreal's North Branch** through the 1911-12, 1912-13 and 1913-14 volumes, and **General Secretary of the Charlottetown YMCA** in 1914-15, before turning up at Victoria.^12 So the man who returned to Montreal in the mid-1920s to direct Kanawana was coming back to the association he had started in, which is a different story from the one this article told for two years. **And he had already been to the camp**, which this article had never said although [[people/directors-index|the directors index]] had recorded it for months: an annual report places him at Kanawana in the summer of **1913**, sharing charge of the season with two Boys' Department colleagues.^13 What the *Year Book* adds is why it happened only once — 1913 was his last Montreal summer before Charlottetown.

Phase 2 research (2026) established that Cross was based at the Victoria, British Columbia YMCA from 1919 to 1925 before transferring to Montreal.^6 A document titled "Personal Reminiscences — Victoria, B.C. 1919-25, by Harold C. Cross" (written 1967) survives in the Concordia Archives, sub-series 14H ("Local YMCA Histories Collected by J.M. Duckworth").^6 The career trajectory now runs: Montreal North Branch, boys' work (1911–1914), including part of the **1913 Kanawana season** → Charlottetown, General Secretary (1914–15) → **Overseas Representative, National Council of Canada (1915–1917)** → Victoria BC YMCA, boys' work (1918–1925) → Montreal YMCA / Camp Kanawana director (c. 1925–1928) → Religious Education Council of Quebec, president then corresponding secretary (1933–1945) → National Council Boys' Work involvement (1938–1945) → YMCA institutional historian (1949–1951+).

## Known Roles and Timeline

- **1912**: **Entered YMCA employed service.** The *YMCA Year Book and Official Rosters* for 1921 carries an "Alphabetical List of Employed Officers" whose printed legend says "the figures after each name indicate the year of entry into Association work", and its entry reads "**Cross, H. C., Victoria, B. C., Boys', 12**".^8 That is **seven years earlier than any date this article had**, and it opened a question that did not exist before: where was he between 1912 and 1919? **Five of those years are now answered, and the first three of them say Montreal** — see the entries that follow. The figure sits a year later than his first appearance in the run, which is in the 1911-12 volume; either the volume year and the roster year overlap or the printed figure is approximate, and it is left unresolved rather than argued either way.^12
- **1911-12, 1912-13 and 1913-14**: **Boys' worker at the YMCA of Montreal's North Branch**, more than a decade before he directed Kanawana and before this article had him anywhere at all. Three consecutive Year Books print the same branch staff: "North Br. … Physical, W. S. Maguire — **Boys, Harold C. Cross**," the branch given from 1914-15 as 2300 Park Ave.^12 **This is the earliest Montreal connection on record for him**, and it changes the shape of the career: the man who came back from Victoria in the mid-1920s to run the camp was not new to the association, he was returning to it. The branch secretary through all three years is **W. J. Holliday**, the man who opened Kanawana's Lac Wilson site in 1910 — so Cross and Holliday sat on the same small branch staff. What that proximity amounted to is not documented, and nothing here says the two men knew each other well or that Cross had any camp involvement in these years.
- **Summer 1913**: **In charge of part of the Kanawana season** — his earliest camp connection by more than a decade, recorded in [[people/directors-index|the directors index]] since before this article knew of it. The association's *Sixty-Third Annual Report*, for the year ending 30 April 1914 and so reporting the summer of 1913, says: "The Boys' Camp at Lake Kanawana was open for July and August with 162 boys, apart from visitors, **in charge of Messrs. D. J. Evans, Harold Cross, and H. A. Lordly for successive portions of the season**."^13 All three were Boys' Department staff — Evans had just become the association's Boys' Secretary that August and Lordly had joined the department that July — so "successive portions" describes three colleagues splitting a season, not three directors. **It fits the Year Book track exactly**: he is Montreal's North Branch boys' worker through the 1913-14 volume and gone to Charlottetown by 1914-15, which makes 1913 his last Montreal summer. The man who came back in the mid-1920s to direct the camp had already run part of a season at it.
- **1914-15**: **General Secretary of the Charlottetown YMCA, Prince Edward Island** — "Prince Edward Island / Charlottetown … President, J. Kaye Ross — **Gen. Secy., Harold C. Cross**."^12 A general secretaryship, the top paid post of an association, roughly two years after entering employed service. He does not stay: the 1915-16 volume gives Charlottetown's General Secretary as J. Morrison Clark.
- **1915-1917**: **Overseas with the Canadian YMCA.** The Year Book's Canada section prints a table, "Secretaries of the National Council," giving each man a role — General Secretary, Boys' Work Secretary (**Taylor Statten**), Railroad Secretary — and then a long run of names under the role "**Overseas Representative**," carried down by ditto marks. **H. C. Cross is in that run in both the 1915-16 and the 1916-17 volumes.**^12 He is not in the 1914-15 one, where he is still at Charlottetown. **This answers the question this article has been asking since the 1921 roster figure turned up.** He is in company, too: the same run carries **Thomas H. Miller** — the T. Hicks Miller who had charge of the Kanawana season in July 1915 — along with A. W. Forgie and C. T. Sharpe.
  - ***How this article got it wrong first, because the shape of the error is worth keeping.*** This section said for several hours that he was simply "not found" in three volumes, and then that a **controlled negative** stood against the war-service guess: the series' two war-service rosters do not name him, and they *do* name his Montreal colleagues Holliday and Hollinshead, so they reach the right institution in the right years. The control was sound. **The inference was not** — two lists naming his colleagues does not make two lists exhaustive, and the Overseas Representative run is a third list nobody here knew existed. What actually hid him was the typesetting: the entry is printed "**H.C.Cross**" with no spaces, so the exact phrases "Cross, H. C.", "Cross, Harold" and "Harold C. Cross" all miss it. Only a loose single-word search for *Cross* across the three volumes found him. **A negative about a person needs a loose single-token search before it is recorded**, however many exact forms have been tried, because every exact form shares the assumption that the source spaces a name the way you do.
- **1918-19**: **Boys' worker at the Victoria, British Columbia YMCA** — "Victoria. … Gen. Secy., J. M. Graham — Asst., Harry H. Hollins — Physical, James Thompson — **Boys, Harold C. Cross**."^12 This puts him in Victoria in a volume covering 1918 as well as 1919, at or just before the front edge of the 1919-1925 span the article already had from his own "Personal Reminiscences" — and it corroborates that span from outside Concordia's holdings for the first time. (The Victoria pairing with W. S. Maguire recorded under 1919-1925 below comes from the 1921 volume of this same series, already cited at source 8; it is not a separate finding.)
- **1919-1925**: Based at the Victoria, British Columbia YMCA. Wrote "Personal Reminiscences" about this period in 1967.^6 **The role is no longer an inference.** The same 1921 Year Book prints, under British Columbia / Victoria: "Physical, W. S. Maguire — **Boys, Harold C. Cross**".^8 He was the Victoria association's **Boys' Work Secretary**, which is what this article had guessed and can now state.
- **c. 1925-1928**: Camp Director, Kamp Kanawana, YMCA of Montreal. A totem pole was added to the Council Ring in 1927 under his directorship.^3 **And the 1928 season's brochure gives his Montreal job**: "The camp will again be under the personal direction of Harold C. Cross, **Executive Secretary of Westmount Y.M.C.A.**, a camper of many years experience."^11 The word *again* confirms the directorship was already running.
- **1933**: **President of the Religious Education Council of the Province of Quebec**, in the same body's *Yearbook 1933* — "Officers. President — **Harold C. Cross, Y. M. C. A., Drummond St., Montreal**", with James J. Reid of Valois as vice-president and Milly Brisbane of Westmount as secretary.^9 So the presidency recorded below for 1936 was not a single year: he held it across at least three.
- **1936**: **President of the Religious Education Council of the Province of Quebec**, listed in the International
  Council of Religious Education's *Yearbook 1936* at **1441 Drummond Street, Montreal** — the YMCA of Montreal's own
  address. The council sat at 212 Coronation Building with a paid staff of five, and its other officers were Rev. A. M.
  Hill of Verdun, James A. Reid of Valois and J. P. Copland of Montreal.^7 **This is the first thing this article has
  found in the ten-year hole between the Kanawana directorship and the 1938 memo.** It is an office of standing in
  Protestant Montreal — the province's interdenominational religious-education body, presided over by a man working
  out of the YMCA building. It does **not** give his YMCA title, and none should be inferred from it.
- **1942, 1943 and 1945**: **Corresponding Secretary of the same council** — so he moved from its presidency to its secretaryship and stayed.^9 The 1943 entry is the fullest of the run: under President **Dr. E. Leslie Pidgeon** of Erskine and American Church and Recording Secretary **Miss Jean Ferguson**, "Corresponding Secretary … **Mr. Harold C. Cross, Central Y. M. C. A., 1441 Drummond St.**", with J. P. Copland still Honorary Treasurer. By 1945 the president is **Rev. Roland Bodger** of St. Cuthbert's Anglican Church and Cross is still in the post. The council's own address moves from 212 to 106-07 Coronation Building over the period; **his does not** — in every listing from 1933 to 1945 he is at the YMCA.

  *So the "ten-year hole" is a twelve-year office.* This article had one data point, 1936, and treated it as a glimpse into an undocumented decade. Cross was in that body's leadership from at least 1933 to at least 1945, through the Kanawana directorship's aftermath, the 1938 memo and the war, in two different roles.

  *Two things not claimed.* One volume of the series carries no year this read could find, and no year is assigned to it. And the item whose title page says 1933 binds at least two years — its second Quebec listing is identifiable as 1936 by its other officers, so it is not a third year.

- **1938**: Authored memo to Les Copland regarding historical YMCA reminiscences (Rev. Ernest Taylor materials, 1868-1874).^1
- **1939-1957**: Period covered by "History-correspondence of Harold C. Cross" in the Concordia Archives.^5
- **1940-1945**: Maintained a "National Council Boys' Work file" (Concordia Archives, sub-series 14D10), indicating involvement with the YMCA's national boys' work program.^2
- **1949**: A "Special committee on history of Montreal YMCA" was convened, preceding his book.^1
- **1950**: Corresponded with Murray C. Ross, who was simultaneously writing *The Y.M.C.A. in Canada: The Chronicle of a Century* (Ryerson Press, 1951).^4 Both drew on the Montreal YMCA's archival materials.^1
- **1950-1951**: Accumulated "Historical data (perhaps useful to some future historian)" in research for the Montreal history.^1
- **1951**: Published *100 Years of Service to Youth*, the centennial history of the Montreal YMCA (1851-1951). Manuscript, chapter drafts (II, IV, V, VI), printers' quotations, and book orders all survive in the Concordia Archives.^1
- **1966**: Thanked by name in the preface to Henry F. Hall's *The Georgian Spirit: The Story of Sir George Williams University* (preface dated 14 October 1966) as "dedicated professional leader and historian of the Montreal YMCA", his research and records called "invaluable".^10 The book itself does not mention Kanawana.
- **1950s**: Compiled "YMCA Events" timeline (1851-1901) for the YMCA News Centennial issue.^1
- **1971**: A photograph of Harold C. Cross from this year is held in the Concordia Archives.^1


## The 1951 Book

Harold C. Cross's principal work, *100 Years of Service to Youth* (also titled *One Hundred Years: The Story of the Montreal Y.M.C.A.*), was written to commemorate the centennial of the Montreal YMCA, which was founded at St. Helen Street Baptist Church in November 1851 — the first YMCA in North America.^1 Chapter 7 of the manuscript is titled "Dominion Square Building (One Hundred Years of Service to Youth)."^1

The McMorris thesis cites the full publication as: Harold C. Cross, *One Hundred Years of Service with Youth: The Story of the Montreal Y.M.C.A* (Montreal: Association Press, 1951).^3 Association Press was the YMCA's own publishing imprint, which explains why the book does not appear in WorldCat, Library and Archives Canada, or BAnQ — it was an institutional publication rather than a commercial release, in contrast to Murray C. Ross's parallel national history, *The Y.M.C.A. in Canada: The Chronicle of a Century* (Ryerson Press, 1951), which was commercially published.^4 The manuscript copy, chapter drafts, and related production materials (printers' quotations, book orders) are held in the Concordia University Archives as part of the YMCA of Montreal fonds (P0145, sub-series 1B).^1

A continuation was later written by F.G. Hubbard: *First Quarter of Second Century of Service to Youth of Montreal, 1951-1976* (1976).^1

## Open Questions

1. [Critical] What are Harold C. Cross's birth and death dates? The 1971 photograph and 1938-1957 correspondence bracket his active years but biographical details remain unknown.
2. [Partially resolved 2026-09-06; still Important] What was his official title at the Montreal YMCA? **For 1928 the answer is now in hand**: the camp's own brochure calls him **Executive Secretary of the Westmount Y.M.C.A.**^11 That is a branch executive secretaryship, a substantial post, and it is the first title this article has ever held for him. What remains open is the rest of the career — a man can move branches, and nothing yet says what he was in 1936, when the religious-education yearbook has him at the association's Drummond Street address, or in 1951 when he wrote the centenary. [SUPERSEDED 2026-09-06: this entry previously continued "**What is now fixed is his address rather than his title**: the 1936 religious-education yearbook has him at 1441 Drummond Street, the YMCA's own building, presiding over the Religious Education Council of the Province of Quebec.^7 A man given as president of a provincial council, at his employer's address, in a directory that lists paid staff separately from officers, was a volunteer officer of that council and something else at the YMCA. The something else is still the question."] That reasoning stands for 1936; it is the 1928 title that is new.
3. ~~[Important] Was *100 Years of Service to Youth* published as a book for public distribution, or was it an internal YMCA document?~~ [Resolved] Published by Association Press, Montreal — the YMCA's own imprint. This explains the absence from public library catalogs.^3
4. [Important, new 2026-09-06] Is the Harold C. Cross of Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue who wrote "North American Rose Gardens of 1851" in the *American Rose Annual 1970* the same man? The coincidences are suggestive — historical research on a nineteenth-century subject, the year 1851, greater Montreal — and they are only coincidences.^10 Three things would settle it: the article's own text, if it mentions Montreal or the association; an obituary; or the American Rose Society's contributor notes for that volume, which are not indexed by Open Library search-inside.
5. [Important, new 2026-09-07] Where was Cross between the 1914-15 and 1918-19 Year Books? He is General Secretary at Charlottetown in one and boys' worker at Victoria in the next, with three OCR'd volumes in between naming him nowhere.^12 The span is 1915 to 1918, he would have been in his twenties, and the obvious hypothesis is war service — testable against the Library and Archives Canada Canadian Expeditionary Force personnel records, which are digitized and searchable by surname, though "Cross" without a birth date or regimental number may not narrow. A Charlottetown or Victoria newspaper notice of his arrival or departure would do it too.
6. [Important] What is the full content of the Harold C. Cross-National Council Boys' Work file (1940-1945)? This could clarify his role beyond the Montreal Association.
7. ~~[Nice-to-have] Is there a connection between his camp directorship (1920s) and his later Boys' Work involvement (1940s)?~~ [Resolved] Victoria BC YMCA (1919-1925) → Camp Kanawana director (c. 1925-1928) → National Boys' Work (1940s) — a continuous YMCA career spanning at least three decades.^6
8. ~~[Nice-to-have] What role did Harold C. Cross play at Kanawana specifically?~~ [Partially resolved] McMorris confirms Cross was camp director "in the 1920s," introduced the "Kickers and Knockers" council system in 1925, and added the totem pole to the Council Ring in 1927 as part of his expansion of "Indian programming."^3
