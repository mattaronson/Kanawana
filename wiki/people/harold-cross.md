# Harold C. Cross

*Status: draft | Sources: 5*
*Last Updated: 2026-02-14*

## Overview

Harold C. Cross was a significant figure in the YMCA of Montreal during the mid-twentieth century, serving as camp director at Kamp Kanawana around 1925-1928 and later becoming the organization's principal historian. He authored *100 Years of Service to Youth* (1951), the centennial history of the Montreal YMCA covering the period 1851-1951. The book is also referenced as *One Hundred Years: The Story of the Montreal Y.M.C.A.* Cross's historical correspondence spans from 1939 to 1957, and he was involved with the National Council of YMCAs of Canada's Boys' Work program during the 1940s.

No biographical details (birth date, death date, education, or personal background) have been located through web research. A photograph of Cross dated 1971 exists in the Concordia Archives, suggesting he was alive at that date. His archival correspondence begins in 1938 (a memo to Les Copland) and extends through at least 1957.

## Known Roles and Timeline

- **c. 1925-1928**: Camp Director, Kamp Kanawana, YMCA of Montreal. A totem pole was added to the Council Ring in 1927 under his directorship.
- **1938**: Authored memo to Les Copland regarding historical YMCA reminiscences (Rev. Ernest Taylor materials, 1868-1874).
- **1939-1957**: Period covered by "History-correspondence of Harold C. Cross" in the Concordia Archives.
- **1940-1945**: Maintained a "National Council Boys' Work file" (Concordia Archives, sub-series 14D10), indicating involvement with the YMCA's national boys' work program.
- **1949**: A "Special committee on history of Montreal YMCA" was convened, preceding his book.
- **1950**: Corresponded with Murray C. Ross, who was simultaneously writing *The Y.M.C.A. in Canada: The Chronicle of a Century* (Ryerson Press, 1951). Both drew on the Montreal YMCA's archival materials.
- **1950-1951**: Accumulated "Historical data (perhaps useful to some future historian)" in research for the Montreal history.
- **1951**: Published *100 Years of Service to Youth*, the centennial history of the Montreal YMCA (1851-1951). Manuscript, chapter drafts (II, IV, V, VI), printers' quotations, and book orders all survive in the Concordia Archives.
- **1950s**: Compiled "YMCA Events" timeline (1851-1901) for the YMCA News Centennial issue.
- **1971**: A photograph of Harold C. Cross from this year is held in the Concordia Archives.

## The 1951 Book

Harold Cross's principal work, *100 Years of Service to Youth* (also titled *One Hundred Years: The Story of the Montreal Y.M.C.A.*), was written to commemorate the centennial of the Montreal YMCA, which was founded at St. Helen Street Baptist Church in November 1851 -- the first YMCA in North America. Chapter 7 of the manuscript is titled "Dominion Square Building (One Hundred Years of Service to Youth)."

The book has not been located in WorldCat, Library and Archives Canada, or BAnQ. The manuscript copy, chapter drafts, and related production materials (printers' quotations, book orders) are held in the Concordia University Archives as part of the YMCA of Montreal fonds (P0145, sub-series 1B). The existence of printers' quotations and book orders suggests a printed run was produced, but the absence from all major library catalogs indicates it was likely a limited internal YMCA publication rather than a commercial release — in contrast to Murray Ross's parallel national history, *The Y.M.C.A. in Canada: The Chronicle of a Century* (Ryerson Press, 1951), which was commercially published.

A continuation was later written by F.G. Hubbard: *First Quarter of Second Century of Service to Youth of Montreal, 1951-1976* (1976).

## Open Questions

1. What are Harold Cross's birth and death dates? The 1971 photograph and 1938-1957 correspondence bracket his active years but biographical details remain unknown.
2. What was his official title at the Montreal YMCA? "Camp director" at Kanawana is documented, but his later role (historian, staff member, volunteer?) is unclear.
3. Was *100 Years of Service to Youth* published as a book for public distribution, or was it an internal YMCA document? The existence of printers' quotations and book orders suggests publication, but no library catalog record has been found.
4. What is the full content of the Harold Cross-National Council Boys' Work file (1940-1945)? This could clarify his role beyond the Montreal Association.
5. Is there a connection between his camp directorship (1920s) and his later Boys' Work involvement (1940s)?
6. What role did Cross play at Kanawana specifically? Was he responsible for the "playing Indian" programming documented by McMorris, including the Council Ring and totem pole?
