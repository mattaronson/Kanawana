# Sean Day

*Status: E1-reviewed | Sources: 12*
*Last Updated: 2026-03-16*

## Overview

Sean Day served as Director of Camp YMCA Kanawana and Regional Director of Camps for the Quebec YMCAs from approximately 2004 to April 2023, a tenure of 19 years.^1 ^2 He grew up at Camp Kanawana and rose from camper to counsellor to director, holding a dual institutional role overseeing both the individual camp and the broader YMCA Quebec camping system.^2 ^3 His directorship spanned several defining periods, including the Stuart McLean partnership, a camp revitalization effort, the COVID-19 pandemic, and post-pandemic staffing challenges. He also served as spokesperson for the Quebec Camping Association (Association des camps du Québec).^4

## Early Connection to Kanawana

Day grew up at Camp Kanawana, as he stated in a 2017 CBC interview: "Having lived with him for that week up at camp and having myself grown up at the camp and having lived the impact myself, I guess for Stuart it was just a natural thing."^3 He was Matt Aronson's counsellor during the latter's camper years.^1 Day attended Concordia University, though his specific degree and graduation year are not publicly documented.^7

## Directorship (c. 2004–2023)

### Titles and Scope

Day held the title "Director of Camp YMCA Kanawana" from at least 2005.^3 A 2009 press release describes him as "directeur des programmes et du camp Y Kanawana" (Director of Programs and Camp Y Kanawana).^8 From 2017 onward, CBC coverage uses the broader title "Regional Director of Camps for YMCA Quebec" alongside his camp director role.^2 ^3 Both titles appear to have been held concurrently, reflecting a dual role overseeing Kanawana specifically and the YMCA Quebec camping portfolio generally.

### Stuart McLean Partnership

Day was director when Stuart McLean returned to Camp Kanawana in 2005 for a week, during which McLean hosted his CBC Radio program *The Vinyl Cafe* from the camp.^3 This visit led to an ongoing relationship between McLean and the camp. Before McLean's death from cancer on February 15, 2017, the McLean family established the Stuart McLean Camp YMCA Kanawana Fund to provide financial support for children who cannot afford to attend camp.^3

Day spoke about the fund: "That was Stuart's experience, and he's so kindly asked people to support making sure that any kid who wants to experience that magic can."^3 He also articulated the camp's mission: "Helping kids to understand their potential in the context of community and nature is what the camp is all about."^3

In 2016, YMCA Quebec provided over $150,000 in financial support for children to attend Camp Kanawana, with a goal of at least one in five campers coming from financially disadvantaged backgrounds.^3

### Revitalization (2009)

A 2009 Mediaterre article references Camp Y Kanawana pursuing its revitalization under Day's leadership, though the specific scope and outcomes of this effort are not documented in publicly available sources.^8

### Anti-Bullying Initiative

Day featured in a video titled "Sean Day against Bullying" posted by Camp YMCA Kanawana's official Facebook page (circa 2013–2014), indicating an active role in camp culture and community messaging.^9

## COVID-19 Pandemic Response (2020–2022)

The COVID-19 pandemic posed an existential challenge to overnight camping, and Day emerged as a prominent public voice for Quebec summer camps during the crisis.

In 2020, Day noted that new public health rules requiring more staff and space meant some camps could not open at all, calling the guidelines "excellent" but acknowledging they "make it a very tall order to be able to deliver safe camps."^4

For the summer of 2021, Day announced that YMCA Quebec would keep Kanawana's overnight camp closed for a second consecutive season, stating: "More than ever, kids need to be able to play with one another, to get outside, to have a connection with one another and I think camp provides that opportunity."^4 YMCA Quebec pivoted to offering 10 different day camps across Montreal, the Laurentians, and the Eastern Townships — up from 3 the previous summer.^4 Even after Quebec gave sleepaway camps the green light in May 2021, YMCA Quebec maintained its closure decision, as Day said offering overnight services to more than 250 campers at once was not feasible with Kanawana's facilities under pandemic conditions.^5

### Post-Pandemic Staffing Crisis

In May 2022, Day reported significant staffing shortages: "I'm usually fully staffed by the end of April," but the camp had only hired 75% of its counsellors by that date.^6 YMCA Quebec made "huge increases" to salaries to recruit staff, while waiting lists for campers exceeded 100.^6

## Departure and Subsequent Career

Day left YMCA Quebec around April 2023.^1 The Association des camps du Québec subsequently described him as a "former director of Camp YMCA Kanawana."^10 Kate Taylor and Kevin Slezak (Assistant Director) assumed camp leadership.^11

Day became Director of Fund Development at **Tyndale St-Georges Community Centre**, a nonprofit organization in the Little Burgundy neighbourhood of Montreal's South-West borough.^7
