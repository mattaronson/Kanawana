## Overview

Harold Herbert Potter (March 27, 1914 -- May 8, 2004) was a Canadian sociologist, the first Canadian-born Black sociologist hired by a Canadian post-secondary institution, and a former camp counsellor at YMCA Kamp Kanawana. Born in Little Burgundy, Montreal's working-class English-speaking Black community, to parents who had emigrated from British Guiana, Potter earned his B.A. from Sir George Williams College (1935--1939), working summers at Kanawana during those years.^1 ^2

In 1947, on the recommendation of Carl Dawson, Dean Henry F. Hall hired Potter as lecturer in Sociology at SGW College.^2 He went on to found the Department of Sociology and Anthropology at SGW in 1960, building it by hiring Kurt Jonassohn (1961, whom he "knew from the Y") and Hubert Guindon (1962).^2 His 1949 MA thesis on the occupational adjustments of Montreal's Black population became the standard sociological citation on the subject for decades.^1 ^2

Potter's trajectory -- from Kanawana summer counsellor to pioneering academic at the institution that had grown from the YMCA's educational mission -- exemplifies the SGW-Kanawana institutional bridge that also produced Hedley G. Dimock and A. Ross Seaman's parallel move from camp roles into SGW faculty positions in 1963.

## Early Life and Family

Potter grew up in Little Burgundy, known as the "Harlem of the North," Montreal's English-speaking Black community.^1 His father, Alfred Moore Westerby Potter (1885--1947), emigrated from British Guiana in 1907 and worked as a redcap porter at Windsor Station; he and other CNR porters did not receive official union recognition until 1943.^1 His mother, Gertrude Miller Potter (1890--1979), emigrated in 1910 and was a volunteer fundraiser and worker for Charles Este's Negro Community Centre from its opening in 1927. She also rented rooms in the family apartment to Black American tourists who would have been denied accommodation at Montreal hotels.^1

Potter was active at the YMCA on Drummond Street from at least age 14 (c. 1928), winning piano awards and speaking contests.^1

## Education

Potter attended Sir George Williams College from 1935 to 1939, earning a B.A. During these years he worked summers as a camp counsellor at YMCA Kamp Kanawana.^1 ^2 In 1938--39 he served as editor of *The Georgian*, the SGW student newspaper -- a position later held by Mordecai Richler. Potter later described it as "a dull, straight, serious and earnest newspaper."^1

He enrolled in the M.A. program in Sociology at McGill University in 1947, supervised by Oswald Hall, a Chicago School alumnus.^2 His thesis, *The Occupational Adjustments of Montreal Negroes, 1941--48* (defended 1949), documented that nearly 50% of all Black male wage earners in early-1940s Montreal were sleeping car porters, and almost 80% of Black women were employed as domestic servants.^1 ^2 The thesis became a standard citation for decades.

In 1950, Potter went to the University of Chicago to pursue a PhD in Sociology but returned to SGW without completing the degree because he had a teaching position waiting.^2

| 1969 | On sabbatical at Southeastern Massachusetts University during the SGW Computer Centre Affair |
| 1977 | Retired from Concordia University |

Potter maintained he had never experienced discrimination at Sir George Williams. He was on sabbatical during the February 1969 Computer Centre occupation, the largest student riot in Canadian history ($2M+ damage, 97 arrests), which centred on allegations of racial discrimination by a biology professor.^1 ^2

## YMCA Connection

Potter's engagement with the YMCA spanned his entire adult life. From age 14 he was active at the Drummond Street YMCA; as an undergraduate he worked summers at Kamp Kanawana; in 1949 he spoke at an Ottawa YMCA event arguing that "racial superiority causes war"; his 1953 Camp Laquemac publication shows continued camp and adult education involvement; and he hired Jonassohn through YMCA connections in 1961.^1 ^2 ^3

## Personal Life

Potter married Theresia (Theresa) Hebein, an Austrian emigre, in 1970. Their son, Evan Harold Potter, was born in 1964.^1 After Potter's death in 2004, Jean-Philippe Warren (professor in Concordia's Department of Sociology and Anthropology) obtained Potter's papers from Evan and donated them to Concordia University Archives in 2005.^6
