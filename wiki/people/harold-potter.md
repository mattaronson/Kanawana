# Harold H. Potter

*Status: stub | Sources: 2*
*Last Updated: 2026-06-22*

## Overview

Harold Herbert Potter (1914--2004) was a Canadian sociologist and academic who spent his formative summers as a camp counsellor at YMCA Kamp Kanawana while studying at Sir George Williams College. Born in Little Burgundy, Montreal's working-class English-speaking Black community, to parents who had emigrated from British Guiana, Potter earned his B.A. from SGW College between 1935 and 1939, serving as editor of the student newspaper *The Georgian* in 1938--39.^1 ^2

In 1947, Potter became the first Canadian-born Black sociologist hired by a Canadian post-secondary institution, appointed as lecturer in Sociology at Sir George Williams College.^1 In 1960, he established the Department of Sociology and Anthropology at SGW, building one of the university's foundational social science programs.^1 He maintained ties with the YMCA throughout his career.^1

Potter's trajectory -- from Kanawana summer counsellor to pioneering academic at the institution that had grown from the YMCA's educational mission -- exemplifies the SGW-Kanawana institutional bridge that also produced Hedley G. Dimock and A. Ross Seaman's parallel move from camp roles into SGW faculty positions in 1963.

## Early Life and Education

Potter grew up in Little Burgundy, Montreal. His parents were emigrants from British Guiana.^1 He attended Sir George Williams College from 1935 to 1939, earning a B.A. During these years he worked summers as a camp counsellor at YMCA Kamp Kanawana.^1 ^2 In 1938--39 he served as editor of *The Georgian*, the SGW student newspaper -- a position later held by Mordecai Richler.^1

## Academic Career

In 1947, Potter was appointed lecturer in Sociology at Sir George Williams College, making him the first Canadian-born Black sociologist hired by a Canadian post-secondary institution.^1 In 1960, he set up the Department of Sociology and Anthropology at SGW.^1 ^2
