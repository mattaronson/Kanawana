# Hay Finlay

*Status: draft | Sources: 4*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-06 (spun out of [[connections/institutional-lineage/canadian-camping-movement|The Canadian Camping Movement]] and [[people/directors-index|the directors index]])*

## Overview

Hay Finlay ran Kanawana's senior section for the season of 1922 and, two or three years later,
co-founded Camp Nominingue with F. M. Van Wagner. By February 1949 he was Secretary-Treasurer of
the Canadian Camping Association. That is the fullest career of any Kanawana staff member of the
camp's early years, and it is the only one that runs from a Kanawana section, through the founding
of a private Laurentian camp, to national office in the movement.

It also answers a question two articles in this wiki had been carrying as open. Both asked whether
any named individual could be placed on both sides of the Kanawana-to-Nominingue connection, and
both recorded that Camp Nominingue's counsellor Marc Beique had been checked specifically and
produced nothing. Every piece of the answer was already in this project's knowledge base before
2026-09-06. What was missing was anybody putting the pieces beside each other.

**"Hay" is his given name, and he entered YMCA service in 1914.** Every narrative source — the
camp's own brochure, the national magazine across three decades, a Montreal university memoir —
gives him only as "Hay Finlay", which left open whether Hay was a first name, a nickname or a
surname used as one. The 1921 *Year Book*'s continent-wide index of employed officers settles it,
because that index is ordered surname-first: "**Finlay, Hay, Montreal, Que., Central, Asst.
Physical, 14**".^4 The trailing number is not a guess: that list carries a printed legend saying
"the figures after each name indicate the year of entry into Association work".^4 So **Finlay
entered YMCA work in 1914**, and had been in it seven years when that volume went to press and
eight when he ran Kanawana's senior section.

**His line had been passed over three times.** Three other people in this wiki — W. H. Ball,
Harold C. Cross and Nelson McEwen — already have their year of entry read off this same
alphabetical list, from this same cached file. Finlay's line sits four rows above MacKinnon's on
the page that gives Cross his 1912, and nobody had taken it. A document read once for one name is
not a document read.

## Kanawana, 1922

**Kanawana, 1922.** Finlay appears in [[people/directors-index|the directors index]] as the camp's
**Senior Section Director in 1922**, described in Kanawana's own 1922 brochure as "ex-Central YMCA
Boys' Physical Director, then at McGill Physical Education".^3 The overall director that season was
Lemuel P. Ereaux, and the documented staff around Finlay were Edwin M. Crawford as Junior Section
Director, F. H. Spinney as Educational Director, Harry Smith as chef and Johnston W. Abraham as
business manager.^3

The move the brochure describes is datable from the YMCA's own yearbooks. The 1921 volume still has
him among Central Branch's assistant physical directors; the 1922 volume does not. So his move from
the Montreal YMCA to McGill falls between those two rosters — in the very season he was running
Kanawana's senior section, which is what the brochure's "ex-Central … then at McGill" is recording.

## Camp Nominingue, and McGill

**Hay Finlay** is in [[people/directors-index|the directors index]] as Kanawana's **Senior Section
Director in 1922**, "ex-Central YMCA Boys' Physical Director, then at McGill Physical Education",
from the camp's own 1922 brochure. He is also, in *Canadian Camping*, the man who founded Camp
Nominingue with F. M. Van Wagner. G. H. Merrill's February 1963 profile of Van Wagner puts it
plainly: "**In the fall of 1924, Van and a McGill associate of his, Hay Finlay, decided that they
would operate their own camp.**" Anne Vail's 1954 profile says the same from the other end: "He was
co-founder of this Camp with **Hay Finlay** in 1925."^1 The same Hay Finlay is the Canadian Camping
Association's **Secretary-Treasurer in February 1949**, at 475 Pine Avenue West, Montreal.^1

So the Kanawana section director of 1922 co-founded, two or three years later, the Laurentian private
camp that dominates the Quebec content of the national magazine for the next forty years — and then
became an officer of the national association. That is not a thin connection between Kanawana and the
movement. It is the same man.

**And a Montreal memoir adds what he did in between.** *The McGill You Knew: An Anthology of
Memories, 1920-1960* (1975) names him among the university's coaches — "Shag Shaughnessy, Bert Light,
Van Wagner, George Vickerson, Frank Saxton, **Hay Finlay** and our chief sport administrator, Major
Stuart…" — and describes the camp's character:^2

> "In the off season, Van and Hay Finlay, the gymnastics and soccer coach, developed Camp Nominingue
> in County Labelle, where many students worked as counsellors in the summer. Though a private
> venture, the camp was closely connected with the university by personal associations, and was known
> as '**Camp McGill**' to all the residents of the area."

**Camp McGill** is the phrase worth keeping. It is what a private Laurentian camp founded by two
university coaches and staffed by their students was called by the people who lived around it — and
it is the same channel, personal rather than institutional, by which Kanawana's own staff moved
through the McGill Physical Education department in the same years.

## The national association, 1949

By **February 1949** Finlay was **Secretary-Treasurer of the Canadian Camping Association**, at 475
Pine Avenue West, Montreal.^1 The association was then thirteen years old. His co-founder Van Wagner
would be its president from 1958 to 1961, with the national office in Montreal for the length of
that presidency, and would be made President Emeritus in 1978; see
[[connections/institutional-lineage/quebec-camping-association|The Quebec Camping Association]],
which Van Wagner also founded, in 1937.

## Open Questions

1. **Where was he between 1914 and 1921?** The Year Book index dates his entry into Association
   service to 1914 but the 1921 volume is the earliest roster read here that places him. Seven
   years of YMCA service, possibly not in Montreal, are unaccounted for, and the intervening
   Year Book volumes would close it.
2. **When did he leave Camp Nominingue, and when did he die?** The camp is described in the national
   magazine for forty years after its founding, and Van Wagner's several tributes name him only as
   the co-founder. Nothing found here follows Finlay past 1949.
3. **How long was he Secretary-Treasurer?** February 1949 is one masthead. The digitized run of
   *Canadian Camping* would give the span, and the association's own executive lists at Trent would
   give it exactly.
4. **Did he have any further contact with Kanawana after 1922?** Nothing found says so. The question
   matters because his camp and Kanawana drew on the same McGill Physical Education department in
   the same years.
