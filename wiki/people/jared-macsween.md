# Jared MacSween

*Status: stub | Sources: 2*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-05*

## Overview

Jared MacSween was a Kanawana camper from **1982 to 1986** and on staff from **1987 to 1991**, and in
2026 became the seventeenth recipient of the camp's [[traditions/pip-alumni-award|"Pip" Alumni
Award]].^1 He has spent his working life in YMCA camping and outdoor education: from 2012 to 2024 he was
Manager, Training at the YMCA Ontario Regional Development Centre, and he is now General Manager of the
**YMCA Cedar Glen Outdoor Centre**. He holds a B.A. in Applied Human Science and a Diploma in Social
Studies from Concordia University — the institution whose archives hold Kanawana's own records — and
lives in Toronto, where he volunteers as a house-league hockey coach.^1

This is a stub, and the reason is worth stating plainly: **one source carries almost everything on this
page.** The YMCA Quebec news release of 31 August 2026 is the origin of the camper years, the staff
years, the two YMCA posts, the degrees and the hockey coaching. A search of the public web on
2026-09-05 for his name against the Cedar Glen centre returned nothing further — not a staff page, not a
news item, not a directory entry [f_4806].

## Why he clears this wiki's threshold on the camp record alone

Nine seasons at Kanawana — five as a camper, five on staff, 1982 through 1991 — put him inside the
article-spawning rule this project uses for anyone who held a camp role, before the later career is
considered at all. What the later career adds is a pattern this wiki has now seen four times: a Kanawana
camper who becomes Kanawana staff and then spends a working life running someone else's camp. **Bruce
Netherwood** went from the 1984 CIT cohort to directing Kanawana to a vice-presidency at the South Shore
YMCA in Massachusetts. **Carol Skinner** went from counsellor and section director to directing YMCA
Camp Hayward on Cape Cod. MacSween went from the Kanawana staff of 1987–91 to training the staff of an
entire YMCA region, and then to running an outdoor centre north of Toronto.

## The award

The Pip Award has been given since 2007 "to recognize the contributions of distinguished Kanawana
alumni."^1 MacSween received it at camp on **31 July 2026** and again at the annual Kanawana alumni
gathering on **12 September 2026**. Andrew Caddell, who sponsors the award, is quoted in the release:
"The 'Pip' Committee recognizes Jared MacSween's lifelong dedication to work in the community… The
purpose of this award is to recognize people who have taken the values they learned at Kanawana and
applied them to the larger world."^1

The same release fixed the award's ordinals for this project — it calls him the **seventeenth**
recipient, which with James Orbinski as the fifteenth in 2024 places the sixteenth in 2025 — and named
two recipients this wiki had never heard of. See [[traditions/pip-alumni-award|the Pip Award article]]
for the count and what remains unaccounted for.

## Open Questions

1. **[Important]** What did he do at Kanawana in 1987–91? The release gives the span and nothing else.
   Section, role, and whether he was ever a director or a tripper are all unknown. The dining-hall
   plaque corpus covers 1979–1990 and names people by season; **he does not appear in the person index
   built from it**, which is a fact about which boards survived and were photographed, not about
   whether he was there.
2. **[Important]** What is his connection to Concordia's Applied Human Science department? That
   department is the descendant of the Sir George Williams programme Hedley Dimock built out of YMCA
   work, and Kanawana was Dimock's field site — see [[people/hedley-dimock|Hedley Dimock and the Science
   of Camping]]. A Kanawana alumnus taking that specific degree may be coincidence or may be the same
   pipeline still running forty years on. Nothing in the record says which.
3. **[Nice-to-have]** When did he move from the Ontario Regional Development Centre to Cedar Glen? The
   release gives 2012–2024 for the first post and the present tense for the second.
4. **[Nice-to-have]** Is there any documented contact between Cedar Glen and Kanawana — shared staff,
   exchanges, or programme borrowing? Cedar Glen belongs to the YMCA of Greater Toronto, the same
   association as [[connections/related-camps/camp-pine-crest|Camp Pine Crest]], whose Lumbermen and
   Voyageurs Games are the open question in
   [[connections/related-camps/points-of-contact|Points of Contact]].
