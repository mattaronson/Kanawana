# Captain William J. Holliday

*Status: draft | Sources: 5*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-07*

## Overview

**William J. Holliday opened Kamp Kanawana's Lac Wilson site in 1910, and the YMCA of Montreal's report of 1931 calls him the camp's first director.** He is also, on the association's own accounting at his retirement, one of its longest-serving officers: he joined the Montreal staff in **1906** and retired in **January 1948**, "after over 41 years of devoted service," with two overseas absences and two years in Cape Breton the only breaks in it.^4

For most of this project's life he was four initials and a year. He is now a man with a first name, a rank, a war record in each of the two wars, a Nova Scotia posting, a Depression-era portfolio, and a career whose ends are thirty-six years apart in one published series. Almost all of it came out of the *YMCA Year Book and Official Rosters* in the University of Minnesota Libraries' digitized run, read in September 2026, and out of Montreal annual reports that had been in this project's own cache the whole time and that nobody had a reason to interrogate until the rosters narrowed the question.^3 ^2

This article was separated from [[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana]] on 2026-09-07, where the material had accumulated under a heading describing the research rather than the man.

## The Boys' Work question, answered in the negative

**No, Holliday did not get it back.** He is a membership assistant in the 1909-10 volume — "Montreal, Central Dept. … Asst., (Memb.,) W. J. Holliday" — and then a branch secretary for seven straight years: Fairmount Avenue in 1910-11, and the North Branch, 2300 Park Ave., in 1911-12, 1912-13, 1913-14, 1914-15, 1915-16 and 1916-17. He never appears in a boys' work line in any of them.

**And then he vanishes from the roster.** The 1917-18 volume gives the North Branch secretaryship to **Thomas S. Smith**, and so does 1918-19; Holliday is in the Montreal block of neither. The 1922 volume has him back as secretary of the same North Branch.^1 That is a gap of roughly five years in the middle of the career of the man the association's 1931 report calls the camp's first director, and it opens in the last year of the war. **It is a null in one publication, and that is not enough to turn it into a war record.**

## The first war

**It was a war record, and the association says so itself.** The report for the year ending 30 April 1918: "North Branch. — **Mr. W. J. Holliday, Secretary, resigned for overseas service** and was succeeded by **Mr. T. S. Smith, of Quebec, on February 12th**."^2 The refusal above was right to stand — the hypothesis was obvious and the evidence for it was not in the volume — and the answer was in this project's own cache the whole time, in a sentence nobody had reason to look for until the *Year Book* narrowed the question to a two-year window.

**And the going is bracketed to under four months.** The *previous* volume carries its own Canadian list — "Overseas Secretaries, Canadian National Council, **June 1, 1918**" — and Holliday is on that one too. Montreal has him succeeded at the North Branch on **12 February 1918**. So he left between those two dates and is on the Canadian overseas lists of both 1918 and 1919. *(A correction to the sentence above, made the same session: he is not "absent from the 1917-18 and 1918-19 volumes" — he is absent from their **alphabetical list of paid officers and the Montreal directory**, and present in both volumes' war-service sections. An exact-phrase search on the roster form "Holliday, W. J." misses him because those lists print "Holliday, Capt. William J." A man can be missing from one list in a volume and named in another, and saying "absent from the volume" papers over exactly that.)*

**And the roster says where he went next.** Followed on his entry-year figure of 06 through the alphabetical list of employed officers, he is "**Holliday, W. J., Sydney, N. S., Sec, 06**" in *both* the 1919-20 and 1920-21 volumes — running the association at Sydney, Nova Scotia — before returning as "Montreal, Que., North, Sec" in 1922-23 and 1923-24.^3 **So the five-year hole narrows to about two**, and the man who opened Kanawana's Lac Wilson site spent the turn of the 1920s in Cape Breton.

**And the same series gives him a first name.** The 1918-19 volume prints, at page 434, "**Overseas Secretaries, Canadian National Council, June 1, 1919** — Released from regular Y. M. C. A. work for this special service," and on that list: "**Holliday, Capt. William J.**"^3 Every roster this project has ever seen gives him as "W. J." and nothing more. He is **Captain William J. Holliday**, and the overseas service Montreal reported is confirmed by a second publication that had no reason to mention Montreal at all. *Read the heading before reading the rank:* these men were **released from regular YMCA work for special service**, so the ranks are those of YMCA officers attached to the forces, which is exactly the distinction that decides whether a Canadian Expeditionary Force personnel file exists for him.

**And he was still there on the eve of the second war.** The 1941 volume's Montreal block reads "Metro. Bd. … Acting General Secretary, Harold C. Cross — Business, Clarence M. McCulley — **Unemployment, William J. Holliday**†."^3 Three things come off that line. **His Montreal career runs at least 1905-06 to 1941, thirty-six years**, where the latest sighting in [[people/directors-index|the directors index]] had been the 1923-24 volume. His post is **"Unemployment,"** a Depression-era portfolio nothing else here has attached to anyone. And **his given name is printed in full a second time, independently** — "William J. Holliday" here, "Holliday, Capt. William J." on the 1919 overseas list, two publications twenty-two years apart agreeing. **He did go to the second war, and the association wrote his whole career out when he retired.** The 1948 report: "In January, **W. J. Holliday retired after over 41 years** of devoted service in the Association. He joined the staff of the Montreal Y.M.C.A. in **1906** and, **excluding periods of service overseas in World War I and again in World War II, and two years on the Sydney, N.S. staff**, has worked throughout his career in this Association."^4 **Every piece assembled from the rosters is in that sentence, in the same order** — 1906, the first war, the two Sydney years, the second war. The 1946 report dates the second: "absent on leave with Y.M.C.A. War Services, and who **went overseas early in 1940, returned to Canada late in 1945**. He was appointed **Secretary for Christian Emphasis**."

**He broke down in the winter of 1926-27, and the association would not let him go.** The report for
the year ending 30 April 1927 is the only account this project holds of the years between the 1923-24
*Year Book* and 1941, and it is a bad one: "Mr. W. J. Holliday, Executive Secretary of the North Branch,
who first entered Association work in this city in 1906, after **two months in the Laurentians in charge
of Camp Otoreke** and **three months' leave of absence in Georgia**, resumed his duties on **January
1st** but, **finding his work too heavy for his depleted strength, was forced to tender his
resignation**. The Metropolitan Board gave him **one year's leave of absence on full salary**."^5

Dated on the report's fiscal convention, that is the summer of 1926 at Otoreke, the autumn in Georgia,
and 1 January 1927 back at the North Branch — a warm-climate convalescence that did not work, and a
resignation the board refused by paying him to stop for a year instead. The report does not name the
illness and neither does this article.

*It also firms up something recorded the same day.* [[site/camp-otoreke|Camp Otoreke]] has a "Mr.
Holliday" in charge of the senior camp at Lac St. Joseph for the last two weeks of the **1916** season,
listed there as a candidate for this man because the report gave no initials. Here is W. J. Holliday
running that same camp ten years later. It is still not proof — no document carries both the initials
and the 1916 season — but a man documented in charge of Otoreke in 1926 is a much better candidate for
a Mr. Holliday in charge of it in 1916 than a stranger.

## Between the wars, and the second

*[A short-lived correction is recorded here rather than deleted, because its shape is worth more than its content. The sentence above was **first written** from the dagger, then **withdrawn** an hour later when the National Y.M.C.A. War Services Executive Committee turned up with Beaton in it and Holliday not — and is now **restored**, because Montreal's own reports say he served. The withdrawal was the error: that committee is a **governing body of about a dozen men**, not a roster of everyone serving. Beaton is on it because he ran it; Holliday is absent because he was in the field. **Absence from a committee is not absence from service**, and a null in a list that could not have held the man refutes nothing while feeling like diligence.]*

## What is not his

**One thing to keep apart.** The report for the year ending 30 April 1917 has Holliday "in charge" of a camp for a fortnight in the summer of 1916 — but it is the **Men's Camp**, at Lake Saint-Joseph, open 14 July to 19 August with 131 men, where "Mr. Henderson had charge during the two weeks in July, followed by Mr. MacLaren for one week, and Mr. Holliday for the last two weeks."^2 The **Boys'** Camp is described separately in the next paragraph, 24 June to 18 August, 175 boys, "Mr. MacKinnon acted as Leader throughout." Two camps, one season. Holliday's fortnight is not a Kanawana season. The 1931 report's "first camp director" opened the new site in 1910 while running a branch, not while running boys' work.

## Open Questions

1. [Important] **Is there a Canadian Expeditionary Force personnel file for him?** The 1919 overseas list heads its men "released from regular Y.M.C.A. work for this special service," so the rank of Captain is a YMCA officer's rank attached to the forces rather than a combatant commission, which is exactly the distinction that decides whether Library and Archives Canada holds a CEF service file. Library and Archives Canada has refused this execution environment at the gateway (see `p_428`), so this needs the operator or a different environment.
2. [Important] **What did he do overseas in either war?** Montreal's reports say he served and when; neither says where or in what capacity. The second is bracketed tightly — overseas early in 1940, back in Canada late in 1945 — which is a long war for a man who had already served in the first.
3. [Important] **What was the "Unemployment" post?** The 1941 Year Book gives him that portfolio on the Metropolitan Board, a Depression-era brief this project has attached to nobody else. What the job involved, when it was created and whether it survived the war are all unknown here.
4. [Nice-to-have] **Where was he born, and when did he die?** Neither is documented here. He entered Association work in 1906 and retired in 1948; a man with a 41-year career and a captaincy should be traceable in Montreal vital records, in the Gazette, or in the association's own obituary notices.
5. [Nice-to-have] **What did he do at Sydney, Nova Scotia?** The rosters put him there running the association across the 1919-20 and 1920-21 volumes and the 1948 retirement notice calls it "two years on the Sydney, N.S. staff." Nothing else about those two years is held.
