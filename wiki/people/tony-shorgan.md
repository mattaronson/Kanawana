# Tony Shorgan

*Status: draft | Sources: 6*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-05*

## Overview

Tony Shorgan organised the French-speaking camp directors of Quebec into an association, and the body
that accredits Camp Kanawana today carries the name that association gave itself. He was **Executive
Secretary of the Province of Quebec Society for Crippled Children** and **Camp Director of the Crippled
Children's Camp at St-Alphonse-de-Joliette**, a member of the Quebec Camping Association from about
**1943**, first president of its French section from **1958**, **Vice-President of the QCA by 1959**, and
by 1975 one of its Past Presidents and Honorary Members.^1 ^2 ^5

He has no article in this wiki until now, and no connection to Kanawana that this project has
documented. He is here because **the institution that inspects and accredits Kanawana exists in its
present form largely because of what he did between 1957 and 1960**, and because he wrote down how,
which almost nobody in this record did.

## The founding, in his own dates

In April 1965 Shorgan published "Birth of the French Camping Association (l'Association des Camps du
Québec)" in *Canadian Camping*.^1 It is the founding narrative from the man who did the organising:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **1957** | At a committee meeting preparing a joint CCA/QCA conference he asks whether a few sessions might be held in French. Some present doubt French camp directors would be interested. He canvasses directors he knows and calls a meeting at his own office **on twenty-four hours' notice**; "despite one of the worst blizzards we have ever had," some eighteen French-speaking camp directors attend, with **Nan Vail**, past president of both associations |
| **12 April 1958** | At the Windsor Hotel, a special French-language session of the joint CCA/QCA meeting studies forming a French-Canadian section. A committee is struck for a year's trial under his presidency and takes the name **Association des Camps Canadiens Français** |
| **17 July 1958** | At his own Crippled Children's Camp at St. Alphonse, under the honorary presidency of **Monsignor J. A. Papineau, Bishop of Joliette**, the group resolves to apply to become part of the QCA |
| **15 November 1958** | Permanent officers elected after a full day's study in the **Mont St-Louis gymnasium**; Shorgan president, with representatives for the Ottawa, Quebec and Montreal regions |
| **1959** | A newsletter every two months; inter-camp visits inaugurated, French directors visiting English and Jewish camps |
| **1960** | The group changes its own name to **"Association des Camps du Québec (Section Française)"** |

That 1960 renaming is why he matters to this wiki's institutional record. The association Kanawana is
accredited by carries **the French section's name**, adopted by that section for itself before the merger
with the QCA Inc. (English Section) — a different descent from "the Quebec Camping Association renamed"
[f_4863, f_4864]. See [[connections/institutional-lineage/canadian-camping-movement|The Canadian Camping
Movement]].

**He was frank about why the merger waited.** Reporting the first year in June 1959: "The feeling was
that some of the Camp Directors might be handicapped because they did not understand the English
language."^2 The strategy agreed was eventual merger, but for the first year or two the section would
"concentrate its efforts towards properly organizing their own groups on solid ground."^2

**And frank about the resistance.** One director he canvassed early "replied that he knew how to operate
his camp and did not need anyone to dictate to him. My reply was that we did not need persons like him
in the Association." Shorgan then prints the sequel himself: that same man later became president of the
French section, "an example of how our minds can be broadened."^1

## The camp he ran

Writing in December 1960, bylined "Camp Director, The Province of Quebec Society For Crippled Children,
Inc.," Shorgan described a camp of **280 boys in July, aged two to twenty-one**, and claimed it was "the
largest camp of its kind in North America."^3 His argument there is worth keeping beside the camping
literature of the period: "For the crippled child, as for any child, it is often much less serious to get
hurt physically than to be hurt emotionally by over-protection."^4

The camp is also where the French section resolved to join the QCA, and where its first directors'
meeting was held — invitations going to "representatives from all the camps in the Province of Quebec,
approximately one hundred in number."^2

## Where he appears afterwards

- **1959** — Vice-President of the Quebec Camping Association.^2
- **1975** — named among the joint association's **Past Presidents** (with Lionel Perras, Sophia
  Demetelin, Alta R. Kahn and Abbé Raoul Cloutier), and among the English Section's **Honorary Members**
  (with the Powters, A. R. Seaman, Nan Vail and F. M. Van Wagner).^5
- **1987** — Ken Willis, the CCA's Quebec vice-president, reports that "the organized camping movement in
  Quebec was born in 1938. **Tony Shorgun**, who has been involved in camping during many of the
  intervening years, is already at work laying plans" for a fiftieth anniversary in 1988.^6 Thirty years
  after the blizzard meeting he was still the person handed the province's own history.

**On the spelling.** His byline and the 1959 report give **Shorgan**; the 1987 Quebec column gives
*Shorgun*. The byline is the better authority, and both spellings are kept here so a search on either
finds him.

## Open Questions

1. **[Important]** Did the 1988 fiftieth-anniversary observance happen, and did it produce a programme,
   a press notice or a commemorative publication? It would be a history of Quebec camping assembled by a
   participant, and Kanawana would be in it. A 1988 Shorgan anniversary document is the strongest single
   lead attached to his name [f_4620].
2. **[Important]** What are his dates, and what was his first name in full? "Tony" is what every source
   in this project calls him. No obituary has been searched for.
3. **[Nice-to-have]** Is there any documented contact between Shorgan and Kanawana? He and Kanawana's
   directors sat in the same provincial association for two decades and appear in the same officer
   lists, but no source here puts them in a room together.
4. **[Nice-to-have]** Which director was it who told him he needed nobody to dictate to him, and then
   became president of the French section? Shorgan declines to name him; the section's presidential
   succession would identify him.
