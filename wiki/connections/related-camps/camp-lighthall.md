# Camp Lighthall

*Status: R3-verified | Sources: 12*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-06 (VERIFY pass, p_284)*

## Overview

Camp Lighthall was a subsidized residential summer camp for girls, operating in the Saint-Sauveur /
Sainte-Marguerite area of the Laurentians — within walking distance of Camp Kanawana — from **1921**
until at least **1976**. For most of its life it was known simply as **the Junior League Camp**, after
the Junior League of Montreal, which sponsored it; it appears under the name "Camp Lighthall" only
from some point between 1967 and 1972. It served girls from low-income Montreal families at nominal
fees, and had no institutional connection to the YMCA.

This wiki has documented the Junior League Camp for some time, as one of the camps whose girls
visited Kanawana during the boys-only era.^1 What is new (2026-08-25) is the **identification of that
camp with "Camp Lighthall"**, the 1921 founding by a Miss Lighthall on family property, and — most
substantially — eleven mentions across Kanawana's own camp newspaper, *The Green Triangle*, between
1932 and 1940, which turn a footnote about occasional visits into a documented decade-long
neighbourly relationship.

## The Identification, and Its Limits

**No single document says "Camp Lighthall, formerly the Junior League Camp."** The identification
rests on a convergence, and is recorded here as an inference rather than a stated fact:

- The Junior League Camp was founded by **Miss Lighthall**, on Lighthall family property.^2
- It was a subsidized organizational girls' camp in the Saint-Sauveur / Sainte-Marguerite area.
- It appears under the name "Junior League Camp" in the Canadian Camping Association's own journal
  through **June 1967**.^3
- In the **January 1975** and **January 1976** Quebec camp directories, the Junior League Camp has
  vanished and **"Camp Lighthall"** — organizational, girls, St-Sauveur-des-Monts, the same profile
  and scale — stands in its place.^4

There is no Lighthall toponym anywhere in Quebec; the Canadian Geographical Names Service returns
only Lighthall Lake in Ontario and Lighthall Creek in British Columbia.^5 The camp is named for the
family, not for a place.

**Which Miss Lighthall is not established.** McGill's Lighthall family records give four candidates:
Alice M. S. Lighthall and her sister Cybel, daughters of W. D. Lighthall; their aunt Katherine; and
their cousin Anne Dean Lighthall. Alice fits best on age and profile — a young unmarried Montreal
woman in 1921, a First World War V.A.D. in France, later a Member of the Order of Canada (1973) for
"her many years of dedicated service to social and cultural programmes in Montreal" — but **no source
connects any named Lighthall to the camp.** The McGill Lighthall Family Papers scope note was read in
full and mentions no camp.^6 Alice should not be asserted.

## Founding and Operation

The camp's own director set out its origins in 1949, in the Canadian Camping Association's journal:

> "The Junior League Camp was started more than twenty-five years ago through the pluck and
> determination of Miss L'ght-hall of Montreal, on family property near St. Marguerites."
> — Agnes Knox Mutchler, Director, Junior League Camp, Montreal^2

("L'ght-hall" is OCR of *Lighthall* hyphenated across a line break.) The Junior League of Montreal's
own institutional record puts the founding at **1921** and records that the camp "welcomed over 300
girls from low-income families in the early 1960s," with "nearly 12,000 girls" attending in total.^7

The sponsorship was explicit. A 1961 contributor note records that Mutchler, director from 1948 and
year-round executive director from 1956, ran a camp "**subsidized by the Junior League of Montreal
Inc.**"^8 A 1958 note gives the same arrangement and scale: "over one hundred girls for three weeks,
three times during the summer."^9

By the mid-1970s the operation had changed shape. The 1976 Quebec directory lists **150 girls aged
6–14** across four consecutive twelve-day sessions, at **$75.00 for 12 days** — against, for
comparison, $1,150 for eight weeks at a private camp in the same table.^4 It is filed under
*Organizational Residential – Girls*, alongside the Montreal YWCA, Girl Guides and Anglican camps —
sponsored camps, not fee camps.

**A loose end:** in both 1975 and 1976 Camp Lighthall shares its director and winter office (Doug
Anakin, 1390 Sherbrooke St. W., Suite 36) with **"Lewis (1918)"**, a boys' camp at
Saint-Adolphe-d'Howard with an identical programme and an identical $75.00/12-day fee. The two camps
were administered together by then. Whether the Junior League still controlled Lighthall at that date
is **not established**.

## Relationship to Camp Kanawana

This is the substantial find. *The Green Triangle*, Kanawana's own campers' newspaper, mentions the
Junior League Camp in **at least eleven issues between 1932 and 1940**.^10 The two camps were social
neighbours, and the tone is consistently that of boys' camp humour about the girls' camp along the
trail:

- **23 July 1932** — "Who were the boys that hiked to the Junior League Camp on Sunday Evening?"
- **6 August 1932** — of a junior hike to Shawbridge: "The leader in charge had great difficulty in
  persuading Jack Van Every to say good-bye to those specimens of the human race representing the
  Junior League Camp."
- **13 August 1932** — "Why did Jim Carnegie … loose the trail around Junior League Camp on Friday?"
  The same issue runs a spoof advice column signed "**A Junior League Girl**."
- **8 July 1933** — "We finally camped about half a mile [on] the other side of the Junior League
  Camp"; and, separately, "Why the Juveniles stopped so near to the Junior League Camp."
- **13 August 1936** — camp verse: "A young maid called Baikie from **Junior League land**."
- **30 March 1937** — a season retrospective recalling "Gordie Appleton's evening strolls to the
  Junior League Camp."

**The best single entry is one this article had cited and never used.** The issue of **17 August 1935**
records an actual visit:

> "It was a very beautiful day, in fact almost perfect… but best of all, **some twenty girls from Junior
> League were our welcome visitors.** We certainly were glad to have them here — especially the
> directors and certain counselors. In fact, we have it on good authority that **they have been asked
> back for to-morrow.**"^10

The same issue records an evening entertainment at which "there were present besides our own campers
several visitors from Junior League, who declared that it was a rare treat."^10 Twenty girls, a return
visit the next day, and a second occasion in one issue: that is a programme of visits, not boys walking
over the hill.

**A correction to this article's own reading of the 1938 "camp doctor" item.**^10 It was presented here
as the paper recording, over the signature "Chief" — the camp director — that "Our camp doctor is
dividing his time between Kanawana and the Junior League," and read as documentary evidence of a shared
medical officer. **The source does not support that weight.** The item in full reads:

> "Dearest Do: / Our camp doctor is dividing his time between Kanawana and the Junior League. It worries
> me, and I want to know if something can be done about it. / **'Chief.'**"

That is the paper's spoof advice column — the same format noted above for 13 August 1932, where a letter
is signed "A Junior League Girl" — with "Chief" writing *in* as a pseudonymous correspondent, not
signing off as director. The underlying arrangement may well be real, since the joke only works if the
doctor was in fact splitting his time; but it is **a joke told about an arrangement, not an
administrative record of one**, and this article overstated it. *(Corrected in the VERIFY pass,
2026-09-06.)*

And on **1 July 1939**, the fullest account of the relationship:

> "**Three carloads of Staff members drove to the Junior League Camp** last Tuesday night, to visit
> the christening of two beautiful new buildings with Ginger Ale. They were welcomed by **Mrs.
> Perrault, Chairman of the Building Committee**, and **Mrs. Bert Hough, the Camp Director**, with
> their staff of a dozen or so comely maidens. The Kanawanaians thoroughly enjoyed themselves with
> singing, square-dancing, and refreshments, returning home at a late hour."

That single paragraph names two Junior League Camp officers for 1939 and dates **two new buildings to
June 1939**. The item continues past the paragraph usually quoted, and the continuation dates the
neighbours' season: "**Steve Gannon**, guide for one of the cars, lost his way. One development of the
visit was **a second trip made next day** by certain staff men for the alleged purpose of aiding the
Junior League Girls' preparations for **their first batch of campers that evening**. This reason has
been accepted pro tem."^10 So the Junior League Camp opened its 1939 season in the last days of June,
the day after the christening.

The relationship is corroborated from Kanawana's side in the scholarly literature: McMorris's thesis
cites an "Intermediate Section Report, 5 July 1943" recording Kanawana counsellors slipping off to
the Junior League camp to meet the women counsellors there, and describes visits from the Junior
League Camp and [[connections/related-camps/camp-oolahwan|Camp Oolahwan]] as recurring through the
1940s rather than one-off.^1

## Margaret Seaman

A 1967 profile of the Montreal YMCA's Director of Camping in *Canadian Camping* records of his wife:

> "Mrs. Seaman, also a keen camper, has directed Camp Perrot (girls' section), Camp Chapleau of the
> Old Brewery Mission and **the Junior League Camp of Montreal**."^11

This resolves an open question elsewhere in this wiki. [[people/a-ross-seaman|A. Ross Seaman]]'s
article records that a "Margaret Seaman" directed Camp Perrot's girls' section in 1952 and notes she
was "plausibly the same Margaret who was Seaman's wife, though this is not independently confirmed."
The 1967 profile is that independent confirmation — it attributes the Camp Perrot girls'-section
directorship to Mrs. Seaman directly — and adds two further camps to her record, including this one.
It also means the Kanawana–Junior League connection ran through a person, not only through
geography.

## Location

Two data points that do not quite agree. Mutchler in 1949 places the camp "on family property **near
St. Marguerites**."^2 The 1975 and 1976 directories give the camp's address as **St-Sauveur-des-
Monts**.^4 The 1930s *Green Triangle* material places it on the trail between Kanawana and
Shawbridge, past Lac Marois. All three localities are adjacent. Whether the camp moved, or whether
Saint-Sauveur was simply the postal address of a site in the Sainte-Marguerite vicinity, is
unresolved. No lot, lake, or civic address has been found.

## Open Questions

1. **[Critical]** When did the camp close? The last evidence of operation is the January 1976
   directory. No later Quebec directory appears in the *Canadian Camping* run.
2. **[Critical]** Which Miss Lighthall founded it? Four candidates; none documented. See above — do
   not assert Alice M. S. Lighthall without evidence.
3. **[Important]** Where exactly was the site? Sainte-Marguerite (1949) versus
   Saint-Sauveur-des-Monts (1975–76), unreconciled.
4. **[Important]** When did "Junior League Camp" become "Camp Lighthall"? Bounded to between June
   1967 and c. 1972, no closer.
5. **[Important]** Who ran Camp Lewis, and did the Junior League still control Lighthall in 1975–76?
6. **[Nice-to-have]** Is there any photograph, map, or artefact of the camp? The one known image is
   the Canadian Camping Association portrait of the camp's representative, Denise Lamer, c. 1972
   (Trent University Archives, CCA fonds 78-004, Appendix 1, Box 5, photo 577).^12
