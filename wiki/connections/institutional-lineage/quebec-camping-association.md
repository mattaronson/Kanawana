# The Quebec Camping Association / Association des Camps du Québec

*Status: draft | Sources: 26*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-06 (spun out of [[connections/institutional-lineage/canadian-camping-movement|The Canadian Camping Movement]]; the 1982-84 Quebec material followed on 2026-09-06)*

## Overview

Kanawana was an accredited camp of the Quebec Camping Association, and the association is
therefore the body that inspected the camp, wrote the standards it was measured against, set
the minimum age and experience for its director's post, trained its staff, and made it
automatically a member camp of the Canadian Camping Association. Almost none of that appears in
Kanawana's own surviving papers. It appears in the association's.

The institution has a longer and stranger history than a provincial trade body might suggest.
It was founded in **1937** by **F. M. Van Wagner** of Camp Nominingue as the English Section of
a body that would eventually have two of them; it was incorporated **twice**, by Federal Charter
in 1949 and under Quebec law on 23 January 1961; its first *Manual of Standards for children's
summer camps in the Province of Quebec* was written by Van Wagner in **1955**, four years before
inspection and accreditation were, in Alta Kahn's phrase, still "in the pioneer stage"; and by
1975 it was re-accrediting **every camp in the province every year**, which no other Canadian
province did. Then, between 1978 and 1982, its English Section disappeared from the national
association's printed lists, and the province's national representation passed entirely to the
French-language **Association des Camps du Québec**.

That last movement is the part of this article to read carefully, because the evidence for it is
mostly **absence** — a body that stops being printed in a list. Absence from a directory is weak
evidence and is treated as weak evidence here. What gives it weight is that the national
association itself began, in 1982 and 1983, describing the English Section in the past tense
while handing its officers certificates of appreciation.

**Where this material came from.** Nearly all of it is from the digitized run of *Canadian
Camping* magazine, read word for word, and it was written first inside
[[connections/institutional-lineage/canadian-camping-movement|The Canadian Camping Movement]],
where it had grown to some four and a half thousand words inside a single subsection of an
article about the national movement. The Quebec association has its own arc, and at least four
other articles in this wiki need to point at it, so it is here instead. The parent keeps the
national story; this article keeps the provincial one.

## Founding: F. M. Van Wagner, 1937, and the first Manual of Standards, 1955

**Who founded the Quebec Camping Association, and who wrote its first standards.** The QCA's 1937 founding is recorded in [[connections/institutional-lineage/canadian-camping-movement|The Canadian Camping Movement]] from the Concordia holdings, without a name attached to it. The name is **F.M. Van Wagner**, and the fullest account of him is the tribute the English Section's executive secretary wrote when the CCA made him an honorary member in 1975:^1

> "As early as **1937**, as promoter and charter member of the C.C.A., Mr. 'Van' **organized the Quebec Camping Association, English Section**, to improve the camping methods and to raise the standard of the camps throughout the Province of Quebec. **He was also the first President of the Q.C.A.** … **In 1955 he formulated and first introduced a Manual of Standards for children's summer camps in the Province of Quebec.** He was **President of the Canadian Camping Association with Office in Montreal from 1958 to 1961.**"^1

Three of those facts are new here and one of them matters a great deal to Kanawana. **Quebec camp standards begin with a named document by a named author in 1955** — a *Manual of Standards for children's summer camps in the Province of Quebec*. The sequence is now continuous: the manual in 1955; accreditation practice "in the pioneer stage" under Alta Kahn in 1959-60; provincial incorporation on 23 January 1961; and by 1975 every Quebec camp re-accredited annually.^2 ^3 ^1 Kanawana, as an accredited Quebec camp, was measured against a document whose origin is now datable, and finding that manual is a distinct and tractable search.

The rest of the tribute: born in **Hyde Park, New York**, a farm childhood and a career in physical education; **founded Camp Nominingue in 1925** on Petit Lac Nominingue, which "celebrated its 50th season" in 1974; the **Centennial Medal in 1967**; an **Award of Merit from the QCA's joint Provincial Board in 1968**; **eighty years old and still ski-touring in 1975**, having "probably travelled more ski-miles in the Laurentians than anyone" except Jackrabbit Johannsen.^1 He is the "F. M. Van Wagner, Montreal" unasterisked in the CCA's 1972 past-presidents list, and an honorary member of the QCA English Section in 1975.^3 Whether he was related to **Peter Van Wagner**, who was president of that section in 1972 and chaired its Standards Committee in 1975, is not stated in any source seen here and is not assumed.

## Alta R. Kahn and the association's own chronology

**Alta R. Kahn, and the QCA's own chronology.** The fullest account the run gives of the Quebec association's internal history is the tribute read when the QCA English Section made **Mrs. Alta R. Kahn its Honorary President on 23 May 1973 — the first in the Association's history**.^2 It supplies dates the Concordia holdings do not.

Kahn "began her camping career with the Quebec Camping Association in **1946**." In **1958** she chaired the joint QCA and Canadian Camping Association conference and "courageously suggested that the Quebec Camping Association celebrate its **'coming-of-age' party** by holding, for the first time, its Conference in a hotel" — a twenty-first birthday in 1958, which independently corroborates the **1937** founding recorded above from the archival holdings. **From 1959 to 1960 she was Co-Chairman of the QCA Standards Committee, "when inspection of camps and accreditation were in the pioneer stage"** — pushing Quebec camp accreditation back several years before the 1965 round this project previously treated as the earliest documented. She was **President of the English Section from 1960 to 1962** and simultaneously an executive member of the CCA; "a major contributor to the realization of the founding of the **Provincial Board** (co-ordinating body of the English and French Sections), and served as the Board's **second President from 1963 to 1965**." And a corporate fact that should be checkable in Quebec's own registers: "**It was through Mrs. Kahn's efforts that the Quebec Camping Association finally secured its Provincial Charter in 1961.**" By 1973 she was still Chairman of the English Section's Standards Committee, Co-Chairman of the joint QCA-ACQ Provincial Board Standards Committee, and a member of the CCA's National Standards Committee — and the tribute credits her with having "assisted greatly in the establishing of Standards in the French-speaking Camps."

This matters for Kanawana directly. Kanawana was an accredited Quebec camp, and Kahn co-chaired the committee that wrote and applied the standards it was measured against, from the pioneer years of 1959-60 through to 1973. Her career before camping is also given: **the first Jewish teacher to graduate from Macdonald College, in 1908**; a founder of Montreal Hadassah in 1918 and organiser of the first Junior Hadassah chapter in Canada in 1920; Executive Director of the **Young Women's Hebrew Association from 1936 to 1945**; and **Executive Secretary of the Jewish Community Camps (B'nai B'rith, Jewish Laurentian Fresh Air, and Wooden Acres) from 1946 to 1949**, during which she "instituted many new projects, including **the first Pre-Camp Training Programs for Staff**." A Montreal figure whose working life ran from Jewish community camping in the Laurentians into the province's camp accreditation machinery. **She died on Thursday, 7 March 1985**, and the obituary printed then — signed by **Frances Kelly** and **Robert Lazanik** — reworks the 1973 tribute and adds two offices: she was **President of the English Section of the Quebec Camping Association from 1960 to 1962**, and, having been "a major contributor to the realization of the founding of the Provincial Board," she served as **that Board's second President from 1963 to 1965**.^4 The two texts disagree on one point: the 1973 tribute gives her Jewish Community Camps service as **1946 to 1949**, the 1985 obituary as **1946 to 1969**. A single-digit slip is the most economical explanation, but they are two documented sources in disagreement and the question is left open as conflict `c_030` rather than resolved by preference.

## Two incorporations: a Federal Charter in 1949, a Provincial Charter in 1961

**The association's own corporate history, and two incorporations.** The Canadian Camping Association's January 1975 national Directory Issue prints the Quebec association's self-description in both languages: "The QUEBEC CAMPING ASSOCIATION INC. — L'ASSOCIATION DES CAMPS DU QUEBEC INC. is a bilingual non-profit organization **incorporated under Quebec law, January 23rd, 1961**. **The English Section was started in 1937, and was incorporated by Federal Charter in 1949.**"^3

That gives the exact date of the provincial incorporation Alta Kahn was credited with securing, and adds a step this project had not recorded at all: **a Federal Charter for the English Section in 1949**, twelve years before the provincial one. Two separate incorporations, and the 1937 founding date corroborated a third independent time.

## Abbé Raoul Cloutier and the 1975 officer list

**Abbé Raoul Cloutier, past president.** The Quebec association's 1975 officer list names its past presidents as **M. Lionel Perras, M. Tony Shorgan, Mme N.D. Demetelin, Mme Alta R. Kahn** and **H. Capt. Abbé Raoul Cloutier**, who is also listed as an Honorary Member.^3 Cloutier is the co-founder of Camp-École Trois-Saumons and the man credited with bringing Georges Hébert's *méthode naturelle* to Quebec; the retained military rank of Honorary Captain matches the account of him as a serving officer in France during the war. The founder of hébertisme in Quebec had presided over the province's camping association, and was still an honorary member of the body that accredited Kanawana.

## Accreditation: what a Quebec camp was measured against

**Quebec re-accredited every camp every year.** In the same issue, John Hasell, chairman of the CCA's National Standards Committee, sets out how often camps are revisited: "with some provinces requiring a visit every two or three years, but **Quebec has developed its programme to the point where every camp is re-accredited annually**."^3

For Kanawana this changes the shape of a known gap. Kanawana was an accredited Quebec camp; Alta Kahn dates Quebec accreditation to the pioneer years of 1959-60; Madelene Allen of Camp Ouareau describes what a visitation actually covered, down to inspecting septic tank outlets after heavy rain and working from a written marking scheme.^2 ^5 So what is missing is not a report but **a series** — an annual, itemised, externally written assessment of the camp's administration, personnel practices, site, programme, health and safety, running from about 1960. Hasell lists what each visitation examined: written objectives, budgets and financial statements, "properly maintained accounts, records and inventories", insurance, transportation, diet, and staff competence assessed by "at least one experienced camp director".^3 Where those reports were kept is the open question — and the same directory shows the QCA English Section had **an Archives committee chairman, Mr. Joseph A. Hood**, in 1975.^3

**Accredited by the province, a member of the nation.** The association stated the distinction plainly in October 1980, printing cut-out logos for camps to paste into their own brochures with a warning beside them: "**Your camp can only be accredited by your Provincial Camping Association and not by the C.C.A. Therefore your camp is a member camp of the C.C.A. and not an accredited member.**"^6 That is the precise form of words this wiki should use about Kanawana: **accredited by the Quebec association, and a member camp of the Canadian one** — the accreditation being provincial and the membership flowing automatically from it. The same page shows how the national logo actually reached camp stationery: as a sheet of three sizes to be cut out and pasted up. The association had to say it twice: in May 1981 a memo repeated the rule and added that "**it has come to our attention that some camps are still using the wording 'accredited by C.C.A.' on their literature. This is incorrect and should be changed.**"^7 Any Kanawana printed material of this period claiming accreditation by the national body is therefore using wording the association itself was trying to stamp out, and should not be read as evidence of a second accreditation.

**The Quebec association set age and experience requirements for camp directors.** A December 1980 advertisement for an executive director at **Camp Amy Molson**, a Montreal charity running a residential camp for five- to eight-year-olds, lists among its requirements "formal training in a child related profession; demonstrated competence in working with young children and adolescents; **working knowledge of French and fluency in English; meet age and experience requirements of A.C.Q.**"^8 So by 1980 the Association des Camps du Québec set minimum age and experience standards **for the director's post itself**, not only for camp facilities and programme — a requirement a Montreal camp advertised as a condition of employment. That is one more component of the accreditation regime Kanawana operated under, and one more thing the province's standards documents (`p_314`) would specify.

## Training the directors: the association's own curriculum, February 1976

**The Quebec association's own staff-training curriculum, dated February 1976.** From February 1980 the national newsletter began serialising extracts from "the **Quebec Camping Association's Director Education Program**" — in French, as "**Formateurs-multiplicateurs**", and dated: "Ce programme… **était préparé par l'Association des Camps du Québec en février 1976**."^9 It is the only Quebec camp-training document this project has seen in full, and it covers exactly the period of Kanawana's own thinnest records.

The first instalment is on recruitment and selection. Recruitment draws on "**le personnel actuel, les anciens du camp, le milieu scolaire, collégial, universitaire**", specialised faculties — "**récréologie, éducation physique, psychologie**" — the **centre de main-d'œuvre** (the Manpower centre through which Camp Ouareau found its chef), and "**le service offert par l'A.C.Q.**", so the association ran a placement service of its own. Selection is by interview, prepared with a form: "**un formulaire est nécessaire pour obtenir les renseignements utiles… (cf. feuille A.C.Q.)**" — **the Quebec association issued a standard interview sheet to its member camps**, which is a specific document to look for.

The advice itself is more thoughtful than the equivalent English-language material of the period. On the setting: "**le style de l'entrevue détermine le style de leadership d'un camp**." On the questions: they must draw out ideas and feelings rather than yes-or-no answers, and must not be confined to the camp but be "une occasion de se connaître mutuellement" — with four samples given, of which two are "**Qu'est-ce que tu respectes chez les autres?**" and "**Quel genre de musique préfères-tu écouter lorsque tu es joyeux?**" And it closes by turning the assessment on the interviewer: "Avez-vous écouté ou avez-vous trop parlé? Avez-vous laissé le temps au candidat de finir son histoire, ou si vous l'avez interrompu? **Avez-vous vraiment écouté ou si vous étiez impatient d'entendre la réponse que vous désiriez?**"

Kanawana was an accredited member of this association throughout, so this is the training its directors were offered and the form its interviews were expected to follow. (Compare the CCA's own English-language interview rating chart of December 1977, which scores candidates one to ten on appearance, composure, maturity, self-confidence, frankness, leadership potential and experience with children.^10)

## The province arrives as a second regulator, 1976-77

**The government arrives alongside the association, 1976.** The association's own newsletter shows a second regulator appearing in Quebec in the last months of 1976, on both sides of the ledger. In October the Quebec news column reports that "**The High Commission of Leisure and Sports has provided funds to ACQ-QCA so that a full time person may be hired to set up provincial training programmes in camping**" — a provincially funded, full-time camp-training post inside the accrediting body itself.^11 Two months later the same column reports the other side: "**A somewhat scary proposal is now being made by the Department of Labour…Waterfronts in camps may not be deeper than six feet!!!**", together with the formation of "**a very active Day Camp Standards Committee**" in the province.^11 The exclamation marks are the association's, and they register how a body that had written and applied camp standards in Quebec since 1955 received a government department proposing to write one of its own. Quebec was not alone in this — in the same December column New Brunswick, Saskatchewan and Manitoba report government grants conditional on association membership or accreditation, and British Columbia reports being asked to run the in-service training for the Department of Health's own camp inspectors.^11 Whether a six-foot depth rule was ever enacted, and what it would have meant for a camp whose waterfront is a natural lake, is not stated; but by the winter of 1976-77 a Quebec camp such as Kanawana was answerable to a provincial ministry as well as to the association that accredited it.

**February 1977: the scale of the Quebec government's involvement, and a fortieth birthday.** Three months after the Department of Labour proposal, the association's Quebec column reports the other side of the same relationship in figures: "**Support from the Provincial Government is still very evident in this Province, and a close liaison exists when it comes to new legislation. $300,000 is being given to the Association for their spring Training Programme.**"^12 That is a provincial grant an order of magnitude larger than any other reported in the run — Nova Scotia's recreation grant the same winter was $3,600 — and it was for staff training, the function the association had performed on its own since 1937. **Rosaire Corbin** is named as the ACQ-QCA officer responsible for the training programmes. The province also mounted a promotional campaign, **"Camp Week," 5-11 March 1977**, with "promotional spots on radio and television; advertisements and public service announcements in every newspaper; buttons, posters, badges" — a marketing push on behalf of every accredited camp in Quebec, Kanawana included.^12

The same column supplies a fourth independent corroboration of the QCA English Section's founding date: "**Highlight of the spring will no doubt be the 40th Anniversary Banquet of the English section on March 26th.**"^12 Forty years before March 1977 is 1937 — the year given by the Concordia holdings, by Alta Kahn's 1958 "coming-of-age" conference, and by Van Wagner's tribute. Four sources of three different kinds now agree.

## The association at work, 1978

**Where the Quebec association actually met.** The association's calendar for 1978 records its English-section executive meeting **at the Montreal YMCA** on 13 February, 13 March, 10 April, 8 May and 12 June.^13 The provincial body that accredited Kanawana, then presided over by Kanawana's own director, held its monthly executive meetings in the building of the institution that owned the camp. The same calendar gives the section's public programme for the year — a general meeting on 28 February titled "**Looking at Camp Thru the Eyes of a Camper**," built around a panel of four campers; the QCA Conference on 17-18 March; a general meeting on 18 April, "**Let's Get Ready for the Summer**"; a Counsellor Conference and a Standards Meeting on 27 May — alongside the French section's own calendar of stages de plein air, soupers-causeries and a séminaire des directeurs de camps on 7-9 April.^13 Minutes or attendance lists from any of these would place Kanawana's director by name and date.

**The Quebec section in the autumn of 1978, at work.** Jack Pearse's account of a presidential visit to Montreal that October records a working afternoon of the kind the QCA's calendar had been advertising: "a panel concerning **staff recruitment, training and supervision** with **Barb Patterson, Ron Braverman and Bob Lazanik**, organized by **Ken Willis, Herb Finkelberg and the English Section of Q.C.A.**", followed by "a superb dinner meeting of A.C.Q.-Q.C.A. **en deux langues**" chaired by Abbé Robert Maisonneuve.^10 Braverman is of Pripstein's Camp (see [[connections/related-camps/quebec-camp-landscape|The Quebec Camping Landscape]]) and Lazanik a QCA past president; Pearse also visited **Tony Shorgan and Yves Beauregard at the Society for Crippled Children** and lunched with Maisonneuve, **Raymond Hade** and **Lucille Cardinal**. The subject of the panel — recruiting, training and supervising staff — is the same subject the Quebec association had scheduled a souper-causerie on in February 1978, and the same one every Kanawana director's report of the period is preoccupied with.

**Van Wagner made President Emeritus, 1978.** The association's last honour to the man who founded the Quebec Camping Association came in December 1978, when the CCA executive appointed **F. M. Van Wagner President Emeritus** and printed his handwritten reply.^10 The accompanying notice restates his record in the association's own words and adds one thing this project had only by inference: "**He shared in the founding of the C.C.A. in 1936 and is one of its Charter Members. The next year he organized the Quebec Camping Association, English Section, and became its first President.**" It also dates his honorary membership to **1974** and gives the Award of Merit as coming from the ACQ-QCA Provincial Board, with the Centennial Medal in 1967.^10 Taken with Charles Plewman's own testimony — the Kilcoo founder, speaking at 87 on being made an honorary member, said "when the Canadian Camping Association was formed back in 1936, it was an act of faith, pure and simple" — that is a second charter member dating the founding to 1936 in the association's own pages, which bears on the competing 1946 date recorded in the sources below.^10 ^14

## Receding from the national record, 1979-1985

**The English Section drops out of the printed list, December 1979.** The association's list of provincial associations in October 1978 carried three Quebec entries — the **Quebec Camping Association, Inc.** under Dr Ken Willis at 2233 Belgrave Avenue, the **Association des Camps du Québec** under René Rousseau, and the joint **ACQ-QCA Provincial Board** under Abbé Robert Maisonneuve.^12 By December 1979 the same list carries **one**: "**Association des Camps du Québec — M. Raymond Bonneau, President — 1415 est, rue Jarry, Montréal**."^15 The English Section and the joint Provincial Board are both absent, and the calendar in the same issue lists only ACQ events ("A.C.Q. Semaine des Camps", "A.C.Q. Assemblée Annuelle").

**A Quebec annual meeting announced, then moved.** Ken Willis invited the CCA to hold its 1980 annual meeting in Quebec, and the invitation was minuted at Wolfville in May 1979 and printed in the calendar that October as "**Annual Meeting — P.Q. — May**."^16 By December the calendar reads "**C.C.A. Board Meeting/Toronto, May 8-9**" and "**C.C.A. Annual Meeting/Toronto, May 9**."^15 Whatever the reason, the national meeting did not go to Quebec that spring.

**And no Quebec name on the national executive after May 1981.** The 1981-83 executive installed with President **Jocelyn Palm** at the annual meeting in **Moncton, New Brunswick, 21-24 May 1981** — held with the Spring Board Meeting and the New Brunswick Camping Association conference — was Cliff Labbett as Past President, George Matthews (Eastern), **Jane McCutcheon (Central)** and Gary Luthy (Western) as Vice-Presidents, Jan James as Secretary and George Ross as Treasurer.^17 Derek Walsh, whose vice-presidency had covered Quebec and Ontario together and who had agreed the previous December "to stay on as the representative of Quebec and Ontario until May, 1981", is succeeded by McCutcheon, who is of London, Ontario (see [[people/derek-walsh|Derek Walsh]]). **From that meeting the region containing Quebec is represented nationally by an Ontario officer, and no Quebec-resident name appears on the executive.** Taken with the disappearance of the English Section from the printed list of provincial associations, that is the second indication in three years of Quebec's English-language camping organisation receding from the national body's visible structure. The new executive's brief was to "implement the **Action Plan adopted by the C.C.A./A.C.C. Board last January**" — a January 1981 national planning document worth asking the association's archive for by that name. It was the association's real operating framework: at the semi-annual Board meeting at **Halifax, 20-22 November 1981**, reports were received from **twenty committee and task force chairmen** on their responsibilities "as outlined in the Action Plan", budgets were approved for 1982-83 and for the 1983 Congress, and **a submission to Fitness Canada requesting $109,617** was authorised, with the answer expected in late March 1982; the 1982 Annual Meeting was set for **Camp Rayner, Saskatchewan, 30 April – 2 May**, and minutes were available to members through the provincial presidents.^18 In the spring of 1982 the president reported that "**the CCA/ACC has recently been advised that our application for status as a registered Canadian Charitable Institution has been accepted**", a corporate fact checkable against the federal charities register and a second dating point beside the 1949 Letters Patent.^19

By Winter 1982 the association's printed list of national and provincial offices gives Quebec a single line — "**Association des Camps du Québec, 1415 est rue Jarry, Montréal, Québec H2E 2Z7, Téléphone: (514) 374-4700 ex 447**" — and no English-language body at all.^18 That is the third such list in four years to carry one Quebec entry where October 1978 carried three.

The national promotion portfolio, meanwhile, stayed in Quebec hands. The Central Region report of the same issue names "**Marnie Ellis, our Public Relations and Promotion Chairman, and an active A.C.Q. member**", chairing a National Workshop on Promotion for the 1983 Congress — the office Derek Walsh had held from 1977, now held by another member of the Quebec association.^18 So Quebec's institutional presence at the national table survived the disappearance of its English section from the directory; what changed was which Quebec body it came through.

**Quebec held three national posts at once in the spring of 1982.** Jocelyn Palm's President's Perspective lists every committee and task force chairman with their province, and Quebec appears three times: **Marnie Ellis (Qué.)** on Public Relations and Promotions; "**Rosaire Corbin (Qué.) chairs the French Language Committee, and we are constantly seeking his input in order to develop an adequate picture of the French Language members of CCA/ACC**"; and "**Derek Walsh (Qué.) is investigating the realities of publishing a Canadian Camping Magazine**".^20 Corbin was simultaneously the Association des Camps du Québec's *directeur général*, so the same man held the province's chief executive post and the national French-language chairmanship at once — and the national body was, forty-six years after its founding and eleven years after Belanger's constitutional change, still trying to form "an adequate picture" of its own French-language membership.

**Frances Kelly honoured, and the English Section called "former" by the national body.** At the ACQ Conference at **Laval in March 1982**, where the national executive also met, "**Jocelyn Palm presented Miss Frances Kelly, former secretary of the English Section of the Quebec Camping Association, with a CCA/ACC Certificate of Appreciation. Although Miss Kelly was honoured in Quebec, her long service to the camping movement was recognized by the CCA/ACC Board.**"^20 That is the second national Certificate of Appreciation this project has found going to an officer of the Quebec English Section, both described in the same past tense — Derek Walsh received his in January 1983 as "a Past President of the **former** English Section" (see [[people/derek-walsh|Derek Walsh]]). Two honours to a body the national association was, by then, describing as finished.

Absence from a printed list is weak evidence and is recorded here as that, not as proof of dissolution. But it is consistent with something the project has from another direction: the citation for Derek Walsh's 1983 CCA award thanks him "as a Past President of the **former** English Section – Quebec Camping Association" (see [[people/derek-walsh|Derek Walsh]]). The English Section still appeared with its own president and address in October 1978 and had gone from the list by December 1979 — which brackets the change, if not the date of it, and gives a place to start looking for what happened.

**The ACQ's own annual report for 1981-82, in French.** Printed among the nine provincial reports and signed **Claire Menard**, it names the year's two priorities and one document this project should have: "Les activités de l'ACQ ont gravité autour de deux pôles en 1981-82 soit le **Congrès 82** et la promotion. Sous le thème: **Les Vacances, Facteur de Croissance**, le Congrès qui s'est déroulé au mois de mars a rencontré un franc succès sur la qualité de l'organisation et des sessions offertes aux participants. La promotion de l'ACQ a voulu au cours de l'année à affermir sa représentation et l'image des camps du Québec par la publication de **l'Annuaire 82**, par une **Semaine des camps** intensive, par une présence de plus en plus incitante auprès des media et du publique. **Il est à espérer que la fréquentation des camps de vacances se maintiendra et s'accroîtra.**"^19

Three things follow. **Claire Menard** is a new name for the Quebec association's officers — and she was working outside the province: at the Saskatchewan association's spring conference in Saskatoon on 1 May 1982, "'Parachute Play to Music', a very popular session, was given by **Claire Menard and Michel Charron**, **proving that there is no language division where fun and laughter are concerned**".^21 A second new Quebec name, and evidence that the ACQ's people were teaching at other provinces' conferences rather than only at their own. **The *Annuaire 82* is a dated, published directory of Quebec camps** — Kanawana was an accredited Quebec camp and would be listed in it, which makes it the most directly useful Quebec document this run has named (queued as `p_326`). And the closing sentence is a hope rather than a report: the ACQ was worried about attendance in exactly the years this project's own record of Kanawana's enrolment is thinnest.

**Quebec sent nobody to the association's national leadership workshop.** Twenty-one provincial and national leaders met at Bolton Camp and Conference Centre on **13-15 October 1983** for a Volunteer Executive Development Workshop, chaired by Terry Burkhalter and facilitated by Arnie Cohen, with Fitness Canada paying the delegates' airfares. The full delegate list is printed: Newfoundland, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta and British Columbia all sent people. **Quebec sent none** — six months after Louis Jean's election to the national executive and around the time he left it for the ACQ's staff.^2 That absence is a fact; it should not be read, as it might be alone, as Quebec drifting out of national affairs, since the vice-presidency Louis Jean vacated went to another Quebecer.

## The accreditation regime rebuilt, and the association's own numbers, 1982-84



The national association printed its provincial statistics with every heading legible for the first time in 1982, which is what makes the two years below readable at all. The tables are the Canadian association's; the finding is Quebec's, and it settles something this wiki had been stating loosely. See [[connections/institutional-lineage/canadian-camping-movement|The Canadian Camping Movement]] for the national series these two years sit at the end of.

**The 1982 statistics, fully labelled at last — and every Quebec member camp was accredited.** The 1982 Annual Report, bound into the Summer 1983 issue, prints "CCA/ACC STATISTICS 1982 - SUMMER" broken down by province with every heading legible, which is the first time in this run that the association's provincial tables can be read without guesswork. Quebec returned **129 member camps, of which 129 were accredited and none were not** — the only province in the table where those two numbers are identical (nationally, 500 of 618 were accredited). It reported 117 resident camps and 10 day camps, **15,791 camper-beds**, **52,977 individual campers**, **820,812 camper-days**, **4,618 paid staff against 790 volunteers**, a wage bill of **$5.371 million** and a food bill of **$3.208 million**.^22

Three things follow, and all three bear directly on how this wiki should describe Kanawana. **First, in Quebec there was no such thing as an unaccredited member camp**, which confirms in the association's own arithmetic what Marjorie Booth had said in interview about the Ontario and Quebec entry gate: "member of the ACQ" and "accredited by the ACQ" name one status, not two, so Kanawana's membership and its accreditation are the same fact. **Second, the table prints its own response rate** — a "% Return/Province" row giving **Quebec 48%**, against Alberta 75%, Saskatchewan 68%, Nova Scotia 56%, Manitoba 54%, Ontario 51.5%, British Columbia 49%, Newfoundland 24% and New Brunswick 16%. Quebec's figures therefore rest on returns from about half its member camps and are extrapolated from there, which is the caution Rosaire Corbin raised in October 1981 now quantified province by province and printed beside the numbers. **Third, Quebec camping ran on wages.** Nearly six paid staff for every volunteer, where Alberta reported 1,232 paid against 5,000 volunteers and British Columbia 726 against 2,884 — about **$101 of wages and $61 of food per individual camper**. The long-session, higher-fee, non-denominational character Booth ascribed to Quebec and Ontario is visible here as a labour fact.^22

**Quebec was 100% accredited a second year running.** The 1983 provincial table gives Quebec **117 member camps, all 117 accredited and none non-accredited**, after 129 of 129 in 1982 — which puts beyond a single year's accident the finding that in Quebec "member of the ACQ" and "accredited by the ACQ" named one status. Its other 1983 figures: 103 resident and 5 day camps plus 9 "other"; 16,568 camper-beds; 75,768 individual campers; 790,595 camper-days; 1,395 volunteer against 3,788 paid staff; $4.89M wages and $3.29M food; and a **return rate of 41%**, down from 48%. **Two cautions belong with those numbers.** Quebec's membership fell by twelve camps in the same year its response rate fell seven points, so year-on-year movement in this column mixes real change with sampling change. And **the printed 1983 table does not add up** — its national paid-staff total is roughly double the sum of its provincial columns, and Alberta's camper-days are given as 5,403 against neighbours in the hundreds of thousands — so the provincial figures are usable but that table's national totals are not.^23

**The Quebec accreditation programme Kanawana was subject to was rebuilt in 1982-83.** The ACQ's annual report, again signed **Claire Menard**, states that "le programme d'accréditation de l'Association des camps du Québec a fait l'objet d'un **renouvellement important**. Ce programme est orienté vers une **auto-évaluation des membres**, le respect des **normes universelles** et du **code d'éthique**." So an accreditation file from before that change and one from after are different documents, assessed on different terms. The same report records that **an ACQ market study of camp clientele was carried out that year** and showed a high rate of client satisfaction; that **a *guide d'évaluation des services* was in preparation**; that **la Semaine des camps had by then been running "depuis plus de cinq ans"** — i.e. from about 1977 — through exhibition halls, shopping centres, radio and television and newspaper and magazine articles; and that a colloquium for directors, senior staff and counsellors was set for **13-15 May 1983**. Two named Quebec documents about camp customers and camp service quality, and a five-year run of Quebec press coverage of camping with Kanawana a member camp throughout, are all new leads.^22

**The Quebec association in 1983-84, in its own words.** The ACQ's report for the year, again signed **Claire Menard**, records that its activities were "ajustées aux besoins des membres" after **a questionnaire sent to member camps in the autumn of 1983** asking what they needed and what services they expected; thematic days on computerised management, new games, and the place of parents at camp; and two things new to this project. The first is a precisely dated promotional week: "**La Semaine des Camps du 23 au 28 avril sera la principale activité de promotion au printemps. Un projet spécial, en collaboration avec le Ministère du Loisir, de la Chasse et de la Pêche, nous permettra d'avoir un 'message radio' durant cette semaine**" — so **23-28 April 1984** carried a Quebec-wide camp promotion with government-funded radio. The second is regulatory: "**Au Québec, la Loi de la protection du consommateur a fait l'objet de plusieurs rencontres avec différents intervenants afin d'obtenir une exemption pour les camps de vacances**." **The ACQ spent the year seeking to have summer camps exempted from Quebec's Consumer Protection Act** — a question of how camp fees, contracts and refunds with parents were governed in law, applying directly to Kanawana.^23

## The association in 1987: full camps and a growing membership

The last full report the ACQ gave the national magazine is the most positive in the run, and it comes from a president new to this project. **Henri Chalifoux** — also the Quebec provincial representative from the autumn of 1987, replacing Colette Pouliot-Marier — reports on the summer:^24

- **The camps were full.** "Attendance at our camps was excellent, with the **no-vacancy sign up throughout the summer for most of the province's camps** and many had a long waiting list."
- **"Over eighty (80) camps were visited this past summer by five consultants. Out of this number ten (10) were trying to gain provincial recognition and become part of the provincial association for the first time."** Against 76 camps and three consultants in 1986, that is a larger visitation programme and **ten first-time membership applications in a single year**.
- A **three-part triennial development plan** — an orientation plan accepted in May 1987, a revision of each programme's specific objectives due before spring, and a finance-committee plan for self-financing.
- **Civil liability insurance**, with the executive "closely working with the provincial government" on a problem "devenu complexe pour les directeurs de camps, depuis quelques années."
- **A study to identify who actually attended Quebec camps**, funded by the **ministère des Loisirs, de la Chasse et de la Pêche**, renewing user research first done in **1982**.

The last is a specific and findable document, and the closest thing to a demographic survey of Quebec camp clientele this project has encountered; if it and its 1982 predecessor survive, they describe the population Kanawana drew from. *(The magazine's own English translation garbles the funder into two ministries; the French names one, the M.L.C.P., and is the text to trust.)*

**The picture this gives is worth stating against the national one.** In the year the CCA was passing the hat among a hundred camp directors and had become a delivery agent for a federal fitness programme, **the Quebec association's camps were full, its accreditation programme had grown from three consultants to five, and ten camps were applying to join it.** Whatever was failing in Canadian camping in the late 1980s was not failing in Quebec.

**The language policy was adopted.** Pouliot-Marier, re-accepting the French Language Committee chair in May 1987, credits Gérard Bachand's two years with the INCEF '87 needs analysis and with "l'élaboration des politiques linguistiques, **dont les recommendations faites au Conseil des présidents furent acceptées**."^24 So the policy first drafted in January 1987 was formally adopted by the President's Council that year — the end of a thread this article has followed from the founding of the French Language Committee in the autumn of 1974. An **INCEF 1988** was planned, to carry a review of the three inventories made since 1985. The committee for 1987-88 was **Carmelle Deveau (New Brunswick), Michel Gratton (Ontario), Eileen Mayotte (Saskatchewan) and Ken Willis (Quebec)** — Willis staying on as the committee's Quebec member after retiring from the vice-presidency, and **Eileen Mayotte**, the Quebec association's secretary of the 1940s, still serving nationally.

## The *Annuaire*: the province's own camp directory, and where a volume survives

The 1981-82 annual report quoted above names **l'*Annuaire 82*** as one of the year's two
instruments of promotion.^19 Kanawana was an accredited member camp throughout, so the *Annuaire 82*
should carry an entry for it — which makes finding one of these volumes worth the trouble.

**The word names a genre, not one publication.** In January 1975 a French article on choosing a camp for
a child gives as its principal source "la section de l'**Annuaire des Membres et Associés de Camps de
l'Association des Camps de l'Ontario**, intitulée *In Choosing your Child's Camp Be Sure*" — the Ontario
association's own annual members-and-associates directory, with a parents' guidance section inside
it.^25 The following January the national magazine's directory issue carries the title page **"Annuaire
National des Camps 1976"**.^25 So each association published one, and the ACQ was doing in 1982 what the
movement around it had been doing for years.

**One volume of the ACQ's series is already located, three years short of the one wanted.** Concordia's
YMCA of Montreal fonds, sub-sub-series **P145/12N04, box HA1888**, holds "**Annuaire 1979, liste des
membres**" among the association's 1978-1980 papers — material the YMCA kept because Kanawana was a
member camp.^25 That box has not been consulted, and the request for it has been added to the standing
Concordia letter.

**A caution about what these volumes contain.** The 1979 title calls the thing a *liste des membres*. It
may be a directory in the CCA's fuller sense — address, director, capacity, ages served, session
structure — or it may be a list of names and addresses and nothing more. Nobody here has seen one, and
this article does not know.

## Quebec at the very end, 1988

The national association's last two issues carry the Quebec association's last reports to it. They are the final entries in the record this project holds, and neither knows it is the last.

**Quebec at the start of 1988.** Gérard Bachand's first report as vice-president records a new leadership programme for administrators, an advanced directors' course ("Directeur 2") begun in December, a "Formateur-plus" course for camps' own trainers, **two studies with the M.L.C.P. — one on insurance and one a market study of summer-camp clientele** (confirming from a second source the study Henri Chalifoux described in the autumn), the development plan going to the Conseil d'administration in mid-January, and "**une Foire des Camps se prépare pour le début de 88, où l'ACQ est le maître d'oeuvre. Le tout se déroulera au Complexe Desjardins de Montréal**."^26 He also records a handover on the provincial board: "I also want to extend a warm thank you to **Mr. Ken Willis** for all the years he spent working for the QCA… By the same token, I wish to welcome **Mrs. Madelene Allen** who will be taking Ken's place" — so **the director of Camp Ouareau joined the ACQ's board of directors at the start of 1988**.

**The last of the language question.** Colette Pouliot-Marier reports that under the new policy Quebec francophones had for some months been doing the magazine's translation in both directions, "**une amélioration importante**" that francophone readers appreciated — and then appeals, twice, for French copy, because "**les derniers numéros témoignent toujours de la rareté des textes en français**."^26 Thirteen years of committee work produced a policy, a translation service and an inventory; what it could not produce was contributors.

## Open Questions

1. **What happened to the English Section between October 1978 and December 1979?** It appears
   in the national list of provincial associations with its own president and address in the
   first, and is gone from the second. No source seen here states a dissolution, a merger or a
   renaming. Minutes of the ACQ-QCA Provincial Board, or of the English Section's own executive
   at the Montreal YMCA, would settle it.
2. **Where is Van Wagner's 1955 *Manual of Standards for children's summer camps in the Province
   of Quebec*?** It is a named document by a named author with a firm date, and it is the origin
   of the regime Kanawana was assessed under. Ask the ACQ, BAnQ, and the Concordia P0145 fonds.
3. **Where were the annual accreditation reports kept?** Quebec re-accredited every camp every
   year from some point before 1975, which means an itemised external assessment of Kanawana's
   site, programme, personnel and health practice exists — or existed — for each year. The QCA
   English Section had an Archives committee chairman, **Joseph A. Hood**, in 1975.
4. **Get box HA1888 at Concordia.** The *Annuaire* section below locates one volume of the ACQ's
   series — "Annuaire 1979, liste des membres" in the YMCA of Montreal fonds, sub-sub-series
   P145/12N04, box HA1888 — three years short of the *Annuaire 82* named in the association's own
   annual report. The box has not been consulted and nobody here has seen any volume, so whether
   these are full directories or bare name-and-address lists is unknown. Queued as `p_326`.
5. **Did the ACQ get camps exempted from Quebec's Consumer Protection Act?** Its 1983-84 report
   records a year of meetings sought "afin d'obtenir une exemption pour les camps de vacances". The
   answer governs how Kanawana's fees, contracts and refunds with parents stood in law, and it
   should be findable in the Act's own history and in the Ministère du Loisir, de la Chasse et de
   la Pêche's files.
6. **Where are the two ACQ documents of 1982-84?** A market study of camp clientele, carried out
   that year and reporting high client satisfaction, and a *guide d'évaluation des services* then
   in preparation. Both are named in the association's own annual report and neither has been seen.
7. **What did the 1982-83 accreditation rebuild change?** The programme was reoriented to
   self-evaluation by members against "normes universelles" and a code of ethics, so a Kanawana
   accreditation file from before that change and one from after are different documents assessed
   on different terms. The two versions of the standards would say how different.
8. **Who were Claire Menard, Raymond Bonneau, Rosaire Corbin and Marnie Ellis?** Four officers
   of the Quebec association named in the national record between 1976 and 1983, none of whom
   this project can yet place.
