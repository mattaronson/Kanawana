# The YMCA of Montreal / YMCAs of Quebec

*Status: E1-reviewed | Sources: 15*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-08 (the chief-executive succession 1980-2009, and Michael Weil's marriage to the chair of Kanawana's advisory committee)*

## Overview

The YMCA of Montreal, founded November 25, 1851, was the first YMCA in North America, predating the Boston YMCA by approximately one month.^1 ^2 The founding meeting was held at St. Helen Street Baptist Church in Old Montreal.^1 J.H. Winn was elected first president.^1 T. James Claxton's father was directly involved in the founding; Claxton chronicled the history in a 1944 letter to G.W. Birks, a document that links the YMCA's institutional memory to Camp Kanawana's founding era.^1

The organization has operated continuously for over 170 years, evolving from a young men's Christian association into a broad-based community organization. It established Camp Kanawana in 1894 as one of the earliest YMCA camps in Canada, and the camp remains its sole residential camp today.^1

YMCA Montreal's national context: Canadian YMCAs formed a "National Federation" in 1912.^12 A body catalogued in a 1972 archival source under the name "National Council YMCAs of Canada" may or may not be the same organization under a later name — this has not been confirmed and is noted here as an open naming discrepancy rather than resolved fact. Concordia's master finding-aid PDF (checked 2026-07-10) confirms "National Council of YMCAs of Canada" is the consistent name Concordia's own archivists use for this body's entire documented span, 1892-1999 — i.e., the "National Council" name is not merely a 1972 snapshot but the term used across the full fonds. This doesn't resolve whether "National Federation" (1912) and "National Council" are the same body under different names at different times or genuinely distinct organizations, since no item-level constitutive document was accessible to check a founding date directly.

## Buildings

The Montreal YMCA built a succession of purpose-built facilities reflecting its growth:^1

| Year | Building | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| 1873 | Victoria Square | First purpose-built YMCA building |
| 1891 | Dominion Square | Reportedly the tallest building in the British Empire at the time |
| 1912 | Drummond Street | Cost $540,206; became YMCA headquarters |
| 1931 | Drummond Street annex | 500-room residential addition, built during the Great Depression |

The Drummond Street address (1435 Drummond Street) served as the organizational headquarters through much of the 20th century and is referenced in Camp Kanawana documents and correspondence. The YMCA moved into the building in 1912, adding the 12-storey residential annex in 1930–31; the building was demolished in 2002.^10 On October 11, 2023, YMCA Quebec publicly announced its intent to sell its (successor) downtown Montreal building — the clearest indication yet that the organization no longer maintains a permanent flagship downtown presence of the scale described above.^11

## Sir George Williams University

In 1926, the YMCA of Montreal organized Sir George Williams College as its educational arm, named after YMCA founder George Williams.^3 The college received a university charter in 1948, becoming Sir George Williams University (SGWU).^3 [[people/notable-alumni/stuart-mclean|Stuart McLean]], who would become one of Kanawana's most prominent alumni, graduated from SGWU with a BA in Applied Social Science in 1971.

On August 24, 1974, SGWU merged with Loyola College to form Concordia University.^3 The Concordia University Archives (YMCA of Montreal fonds, P0145) now hold the most significant collection of Camp Kanawana historical records.

## Renaming and Restructuring

In January 2009, the YMCA du Grand Montréal and its eight regional branches took the name "Ys du Québec"; the organization now operates as Les YMCA du Québec / The YMCAs of Quebec (ymcaquebec.org).^1 ^8 The same period saw a 2008 decision to build a new centre in the Ahuntsic-Cartierville borough and renovations at Camp Kanawana (2007–2009) framed as part of the organization's commitment to sustainable development.^8 As of 2025, the organization operates 9 centres and 36 service points, serving over 120,000 people annually.^1

In November 2019, the YMCAs of Quebec underwent a major strategic restructuring, closing the Guy-Favreau and Hochelaga-Maisonneuve centres and shifting away from large fitness facilities toward community-based programs.^4 Camp Kanawana was closed for two consecutive summers (2020 and 2021) due to COVID-19.^6 In summer 2024, a new "Kanawana Adventure" day camp program was launched at the Saint-Sauveur site — the first day camp to operate at the residential camp location.^7 Day camps were discontinued across YMCA Quebec in fall 2025; Camp YMCA Kanawana's summer residential program continues as the organization's flagship outdoor program.^1 ^4

## Chief Executives

Pascale Audette was named President and CEO of the YMCAs of Quebec effective January 6, 2025 — the first woman to hold this position in the organization's 170-year history.^5

**She had a long line of predecessors, and until 2026-09-08 this article named none of them.** The cached annual reports sign themselves, so the succession can be read straight off the signature blocks:^13

| Chief executive | Documented span | Title as printed |
|---|---|---|
| K. W. Johnston | FY1980–81 | *Directeur général* / Director General and Chief Executive Officer |
| S. (Sol) Kasimer | FY1981–82 to at least 1985 | Director General and Chief Executive Officer |
| Michael Goldbloom | by 1992 to 1 June 1994 | President and Chief Executive Officer |
| Bernard W. Matte | from 1 June 1994, signing through 1996 | President and Chief Executive Officer |
| Michael Weil | April 1997 to FY2000–01 | President and Chief Executive Officer |
| Stéphane Vaillancourt | by FY2002–03 to at least 2009 | President and Chief Executive Officer |
| Pascale Audette | from 6 January 2025 | President and CEO |

Two of the entries carry more than a name. **Goldbloom left for a newspaper**: "On June 1, 1994, Michael
Goldbloom resigned as the President and Chief Executive Officer of the Montreal YMCA to become the President
and Publisher of *The Gazette*." **Matte was an insider**, elected effective the same day, after fifteen
years as a volunteer and a term as Chairman of the Board from 1985 to 1988. **Weil came back**: he had begun
his career at the Montreal YMCA in 1979, ran the Saint-Laurent and Downtown branches among other posts, left
to be CEO of the YM-YWCA of Winnipeg, and returned in April 1997 to the chief executive's office.^13 [f_5764]
[f_5765] *One of those four posts now has a date.* The 1988 report's Branch Boards of Management list gives
the Saint-Laurent board as "Berger, Craig; Bisscheroux, John; Brownrigg, Marlyn; Buteau, Georges; Daly, Henry
A.; Girard, Robert; Manios, Irène; Parent, Roch; **Weil, Michael (YMCA)**" — the "(YMCA)" marker being this
series' convention for the branch's own staff member on its board. So he was running Saint-Laurent in **1988**,
nine years after joining the association and nine before the chief executive's office.^15 [f_5778]

**And one of them came back.** Michael Goldbloom signs the association's community reports for **2010, 2011
and 2012** as **Chair of the Board of Directors**, sixteen years after resigning its chief executive's office
for *The Gazette*; the 2013 board list gives him as "Goldbloom, Michael — Chair of the Board — **Bishop's
University**," the association's own document supplying his day job. He appears again in the 2014, 2015, 2017
and 2018 reports, in a roll of past leaders and major donors that also carries
[[people/notable-alumni/john-cleghorn|John E. Cleghorn]], Paul Dunne and Michael Novak.^14 This wiki has
documented former Kanawana staff returning to the camp's advisory committee after their paid tenures — Morgan
Carter, Arleen Boyer, Roxanne Martel, Matt Hamerman. The same thing happens at the top of the association, and
across two decades. [f_5775]

*Two cautions about the table.* The 1992 report lists **Sol Kasimer as Director General and Michael Goldbloom
as President and Chief Executive Officer in the same governance block**, so these were two coexisting posts
for at least part of the 1980s and 1990s and the table flattens them. And the spans are the years the cached
reports cover: 1986–1991 was not checked for this, and no cached report covers 2010–2024, which is a
fifteen-year hole between Vaillancourt and Audette.

**The Kanawana connection is Weil's, and it is by marriage.** Judy Weil sat on Camp Kanawana's local advisory
committee from its founding year, FY2002–03, and chaired it in FY2004–05, in the years just after Michael
Weil left the chief executive's office. The association's 2009 report names them together: the Gabrielle and
Maurice A. Massé Award went that March "to Judy and Michael Weil, **a couple** who has contributed greatly to
the successes of The YMCAs of Québec through their involvement over many years as directors, employees, and
volunteers."^13 This project has been wrong about name matches before, so it is worth saying that the
relationship here is not an inference from a shared surname: the association printed it. See
[[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana]] for the committee rosters.

## Connection to Kanawana

The YMCA of Montreal established Camp Kanawana in 1894, making it one of the oldest continuously operating YMCA camps in Canada. The camp has remained under YMCA Quebec governance throughout its history, though the specific organizational relationship has evolved:

- In the founding era (1894–1920s), camp was organized directly by YMCA Montreal's Boys' Work department. [**Note**: Earlier versions of this article named "Herbert C. Wilkinson" and "Eldon 'Pop' Evans" as founding-era Boys' Work figures; however, exhaustive web searches (23+ queries for Wilkinson, 26+ for Evans) found zero biographical records, zero KB facts, and zero source references for either name. These names are unverified and may require confirmation from Concordia Archives (YMCA fonds P0145) or YMCA Montreal annual reports.]
- Through the mid-20th century, camp operations were managed under the YMCA Montreal's camping portfolio, with governance passing through a series of bodies documented in the Concordia archives: the Kamp Kanawana Committee (1910–1939), the Kamp Kanawana Board of Management (1939–1946), a Camp Executive Committee (1946–1958), a dedicated Camping Branch with its own Board of Management (by 1969–1970), and the Camping and Outdoor Education Branch (records 1972–1979).^9 The chronology now extends one step further: a Special Intercommittee Task Force on Camping and a French-language "Comité du service des camps" appear in Concordia's finding aid with records into 1981–82, narrowing (but not closing) the governance gap — no body has been identified for 1983–2003.^9
- By the 2000s, the camp director (Sean Day, c. 2004–2023) also held the title "Regional Director of Camps for YMCA Quebec," reflecting the broader provincial structure.
- Camp Kanawana is now the sole remaining residential camp in the YMCA Quebec system, following the closure or transfer of Camp Weredale and other satellite programs.
