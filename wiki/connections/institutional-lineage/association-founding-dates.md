# Founding Dates of the Camping Associations

*Status: draft | Sources: 3*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-06 (spun out of [[connections/institutional-lineage/canadian-camping-movement|The Canadian Camping Movement]])*

## Overview

Neither the Canadian Camping Association nor the Quebec Camping Association has a settled
founding date, and the disagreements are not between outside historians. They are between the
associations' own presidents, their own publications, and the people who were in the room. The CCA
printed two different foundings in a single issue of its own magazine. Its founder's daughter, who
kept the minutes, gave a third date and was talked out of it by her interviewer on the ground that
an anniversary was already being celebrated. The Quebec association ran on two datings and chose to
commemorate the later one.

This article collects those competing datings and the evidence behind each. It does not
choose between them. Every dating here comes from inside the associations, which is what makes the
disagreement worth recording rather than resolving: it shows that the people running these bodies in
the 1980s did not agree about when their own institutions had begun. The open conflict record is
`c_029`.

It matters to this wiki because Kanawana's own age is contested on the same pattern —
see [[history/oldest-camp-question|The Oldest Camp Question]] — and because the Quebec association's
dates decide what "the movement" Kanawana belonged to actually was, and from when.

## The Canadian Camping Association: 1936, c.1947, or 1938

**A sitting president dates the association to about 1947 — in the same issue that dates it to 1936.** In the autumn of 1985 the magazine reprinted the presidential article **Douglas G. McEwen** had published in **Autumn 1967**, and one sentence in it cuts across everything the association said about itself in the 1980s: "**For the first 20 years of its life the Canadian Camping Association's strength has lain in strong provincial associations.**"^1 Twenty years before 1967 is **1947**. That is not a recollection at eighty or a historian's inference; it is the sitting president describing the span of his own association's life, in its own magazine, and placing its beginning two decades back rather than three.

**The same issue carries the opposite dating twice.** Its president's message opens "In 1986 CCA/ACC will be celebrating fifty years of service to children and youth in Canada," and the publications catalogue bound into it repeats in both languages that "the Canadian Camping Association was founded in 1936."^1 The association printed both datings in one issue without apparently noticing, which is the most useful thing about the find: people inside it were operating on both at once. Neither is preferred here. It is the strongest evidence yet for the two-stage reading offered above — a 1936 founding out of the Ontario association's circle, then constitution as a genuinely national body at Ottawa across 1946-49 — because a Manitoba-based president in 1967, with no Toronto institutional memory to defend, naturally dated the association from the second stage. See conflict `c_029`.

**The founder's daughter said 1938, and was talked out of it in print.** In the association's own fiftieth anniversary issue, the magazine interviewed **Adele Ebbs** — Taylor Statten's daughter, secretary to the founding circle, and the person who kept its minutes. Asked whether the received dates sounded right, she said: "**'36 sounds early to me**… '33 sounds right." Told that her father had helped F. M. Van Wagner form the Quebec Camping Association in 1937, she answered: "**Well that is why I thought the Canadian Camping Association was not until…** my impression was that it was **1938**." The interviewer replied, "We may be celebrating 50 years under false pretenses, so let's not upset that apple cart," and she conceded: "No, 50 years makes it '36. Well, I'll buy that."^2

**The best-placed living witness to the founding put it two years later than the association did, reasoning from the sequence of the Quebec association, and was overruled on the ground that the anniversary was already under way.** With Douglas McEwen's c.1947 dating of 1967, the founding-date question now has three answers, all from inside the association: **1936 in its 1980s literature, c.1947 from its president in 1967, and 1938 from the founder's daughter in 1986.** None is preferred here. A 1937 Quebec association followed by a 1938 national one is exactly the sequence a founding-circle secretary would remember, while 1936 is what a later corporate record of reorganisation would show — which is once more consistent with the two-stage reading. See conflict `c_029`.

## The founding circle was the Ontario association's

**Before the association there was a supper club.** Ebbs describes what preceded it: "in the early 30's there were regular meetings, mostly social — **Edgar, Hamilton, Halliday, Chapman, Cochrane and Statten**, those six," meeting in each other's houses "4 or 5 times a year," where "**a great deal of it was social and anything they talked about was sort of trivial in a way because they had secrets. Nobody ever knew what anybody paid their staff.**" The six were Mary S. Edgar of Glen Bernard, Mary G. Hamilton of Camp Tanamakoon, Ferna Halliday of Camp Oconto, Ernie Chapman of Camp Kagawong, A. L. Cochrane of Camp Temagami, and Statten; **Ebbs became their secretary and "finally started to take notes sometime before I got married, and that was 1935"** — so minutes of the pre-founding meetings exist from about 1934-35. Charles Plewman and the Dansons joined soon after. Of Arnold Ward she remarks that the circle was of "private Camp Directors and maybe because Arnold Ward was an **agency** Camp Director" he was not part of it — the private/agency division at the association's very origin. She gives the early presidential sequence as **Taylor Statten, Dais Gass, Anne Vail, Ted Yard, Irv Haladner, F. M. Van Wagner**.^2

**The founding six were the Ontario association's, and a second witness says so.** Interviewed about Mary S. Edgar, **John Gilchrist** listed the circle unprompted — "**Taylor Statten, Ferna Halliday, A. L. Cochrane, Mary Hamilton, Ernie Chapman**… and **Mary S. Edgar**. They got together and decided they wanted to form a Camping Association as a vehicle where they could exchange ideas and learn things from one another" — and the interviewer identified it: "**That was the Ontario Camping Association. Taylor Statten was the first President, in 1933.**"^3 That is the same six Adele Ebbs named in 1986, from an independent witness, and it attaches them to the **provincial** founding. The supper club of private directors this article recorded from Ebbs is therefore the OCA's founding circle; whatever happened nationally in 1936, 1938 or 1947 was a separate act, which removes one recurring source of confusion from `c_029`.

## The Quebec association: 1937, 1938, 1958, or January 1961

**The Quebec association also ran on two datings — and it celebrated the second.** In the summer of 1986 the Quebec column reported that its members would "**again celebrate the 25th anniversary of the Quebec Camping Association**" at their August meeting at Camp Trois-Saumons, explaining: "**Le regroupement des responsables des camps des communautés anglophone et francophone avait lieu notamment en janvier 1961**" — "the reorganization of the directors of the French and English speaking camping community took place primarily in January of 1961."^2 **So the association dated itself from the January 1961 amalgamation rather than from the 1937 founding of the English Section**, which is why different sources answer the question of the QCA's founding differently: 1937 is the English Section, 1961 the bilingual association. It also fixes the amalgamation to January 1961 from the association's own mouth.

**A fourth date for the birth of Quebec camping — from the Quebec seat on the national board.** In the winter of 1987 **Ken Willis**, writing as CCA/ACC Vice-President (Quebec Region), opened his column with a flat statement and a plan: "**The organized camping movement in Quebec was born in 1938.** Tony Shorgun, who has been involved in camping during many of the intervening years, is already at work laying plans for the **50th Anniversary of this birth** which, obviously, will be held next year."^3

This article already carries three datings for Kanawana's own provincial association: **1937** from the archival holdings, corroborated by Alta Kahn's "coming-of-age party" of 1958; the founding of the **French section in 1958**; and **January 1961**, the amalgamation the ACQ itself commemorated as its twenty-fifth anniversary in 1986. Willis adds **1938**, and not in passing — a fiftieth anniversary was being organised around it for 1988, which means the year was the premise of an event rather than a slip in a column.

**It is also the year Adele Ebbs gave for the national association, and for the opposite reason.** Ebbs dated the CCA to 1938 *because* she remembered her father helping F. M. Van Wagner form the Quebec association in 1937. Willis dates Quebec camping itself to 1938. The two recollections cannot both stand as offered, and what they establish between them is that **the 1937/1938 boundary was genuinely unclear to the people who were present at it** — which is a finding about the record, not a reason to prefer either. See conflict `c_029`. If the 1988 anniversary took place it will have produced a programme or commemorative publication compiled by Shorgun, a name new to this project; that document would be a participant's history of Quebec camping, and Kanawana would be in it.

## Open Questions

1. [Important] Did the 1988 fiftieth-anniversary celebration of Quebec camping take place? Ken
   Willis records **Tony Shorgun** already laying plans for it in the winter of 1987. A programme or
   commemorative publication compiled by Shorgun would be a participant's history of Quebec camping,
   and Kanawana would be in it. Shorgun is a name new to this project.
2. [Important] Do the pre-founding minutes survive? Adele Ebbs "finally started to take notes
   sometime before I got married, and that was 1935," so minutes of the supper-club meetings should
   exist from about 1934-35. Where they are held is unknown.
3. [Nice-to-have] Which dating did the CCA's incorporation documents use? The association's January
   1975 national directory refers to two incorporations; the corporate record would fix at least the
   legal dates, though not the felt ones.
