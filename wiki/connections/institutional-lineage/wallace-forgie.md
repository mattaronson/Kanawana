# A. Wallace Forgie

*Status: draft | Sources: 6*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-07*

## Overview

A. Wallace Forgie (1883 – 1 December 1967) was a Canadian YMCA boys' worker whose career runs from
Ottawa in 1907 to Madras in 1967, and who is in this wiki for three reasons that have nothing to do
with each other. He was the man the young leaders of Canadian camping called **"Commodore"**. He was
**Taylor Statten's brother-in-law** and, on one account, the leaders' leader Statten's own daughter
was first taken canoe-tripping by. And — this part was not known here until 2026-09-07 — he was
**Secretary of the C.S.E.T. Committee**, and the programme's own governing body credits him with the
idea the whole thing was built on.^1 ^2

He has no documented role at Camp Kanawana. He is here because CSET shaped what YMCA camps in Canada
did with boys, Kanawana among them, and because he stands at the junction of three or four other
articles in this wiki.

## The name, and why it took this project two tries

The rosters give **"A. W. Forgie"**. The obituary and the profiles give **"Wallace Forgie"**. This
project reasoned that these were one man from the shape of the career — boys' work at Ottawa and
Toronto and nationally, overseas in the first war, western Canada, India from 1927 — and wrote that
the identification "never needed 'A. W.' to resolve to 'A. Wallace'."^3

The C.S.E.T. manual of 1918 prints it: "**Capt. A. Wallace Forgie**, Secretary of the C.S.E.T.
Committee."^1 The reasoning was sound and the document was there all along. **The first initial is
still unaccounted for.**

## Toronto Central, 1907, and Luke 2:52

*The Boy's Own Book*, published in 1929 by the National Boys' Work Board of the Religious Education
Council of Canada — the body that by then ran the programme — tells the story of its own origins:

> The origin, however, goes back another five years, during which period Messrs. **Taylor Statten
> and Wallace Forgie, as Boys' Work Secretaries of the Central Branch of the Y.M.C.A., in Toronto**,
> were carrying on experiments which in 1912 were to blossom out into what were then called the
> Canadian Standard Efficiency Tests. **It was in 1907 that Mr. Forgie got the idea of Luke 2:52**,
> "And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature and in favour with God and man," **as a basis for such
> a programme.** It was about this time also that the idea of charting boys on a four-fold scale was
> first practiced.^2

Luke 2:52 is the verse behind the **fourfold** scheme — wisdom, stature, favour with God, favour with
man; intellectual, physical, spiritual, social — which is the thing CSET is actually remembered for,
and which YMCA camps including Kanawana organised programme and evaluation around for decades. The
same passage calls Statten "the moving spirit in and through it all." **Both men are in it, and this
wiki has been carrying only one of them.**^2

## The programme, in two versions with one acronym

The 1929 account is precise about a sequence this project did not have.

The forerunner was "prepared and published" in **1912**, by a group appointed by the YMCA's National
Council, as the **Canadian Standard Efficiency *Tests*** — "the little red handbook," of which
"**forty-two thousand had been used**" before anyone thought about replacing it. When they did, the
brief was insignia, initiation ceremonies, badge work, "and possibly a division of the programme with
more appealing activities for boys from twelve to fourteen," which is Trail Rangers in embryo.
**"Mr. Forgie of the Y.M.C.A. was asked to direct the work."**^2

He did not finish it. "When Mr. Forgie returned to the front, Gordon Hignell, of Winnipeg, was set
aside by the Association to complete the work of publication."^2 The manual itself, printed that year,
says the same from the other side: "On his going to France in September last the work was continued
by Mr. Gordon K. [H]ignell."^1 And then, in a black-bordered note on the same page, the manual records
that Hignell "contracted pneumonia and passed away in Winnipeg, on **October 21, 1918**, just one week
after he had returned from Toronto and his labors in connection with this book."^1

"Finally in October 1918, the work was complete. The name '**Canadian Standard Efficiency
Training**' became" the name it kept.^2 **So the acronym's expansion changed** — Tests in 1912,
Training from October 1918 — and they are two programmes, the second a deliberate revision of the
first.

**This conflicts with what this wiki says elsewhere**, which is that Statten "established the Canadian
Standards Efficiency Training (CSET) program" in **1914**, a date a verify pass recorded as
independently confirmed in February 2026. Neither 1918 source supports 1914, and neither is
overwritten by it. The disagreement is recorded as **conflict c_069** and left for a human, because
two documented sources disagreeing is what that rule is for. It is quite possible 1914 is true of a
third event nobody here has identified.

## Alberta, and a change of employer nobody noticed

The obituary and the profiles describe "boys' work in Western Canada, during which he conducted many
canoe trips into the north-west," and this project has read those years as YMCA years. The
International Council of Religious Education's yearbook for **1924** has him as "**A. Wallace Forgie,
Boys' Work Secretary, Alberta Religious Education Council**," with an office at 302 I.O.O.F. Building,
Calgary.^4

That is not the YMCA. It is the provincial religious-education council — the same kind of body that
published *The Boy's Own Book* — and it explains something that had looked like a gap: the YMCA's own
roster has him at Calgary in 1919-20 and then stops listing him.^3 He had not left the work. He had
changed sides of it.

## Kanawana's stake in any of this

Indirect, and worth stating plainly rather than overclaiming. Kanawana ran a YMCA boys' programme
through the decades when CSET was the national framework for exactly that, and the fourfold scheme
turns up in camp evaluation and programme language across the movement. **No document read here shows
Forgie at Kanawana, corresponding with it, or naming it.** What the material establishes is that the
framework Kanawana worked inside had two authors rather than one, and that the second of them is the
man already in this wiki as the Commodore.

## Open Questions

1. [Critical] What is the "A."? Capt. A. Wallace Forgie in 1918, A. W. Forgie in the rosters,
   Wallace Forgie everywhere else. A 1967 obituary notice or a war-service record would carry it.
2. [Critical] What happened in **1914**? Both the 1912 and the October 1918 dates come from the
   programme's own board; the 1914 date in this wiki has not been traced back to its source. See
   conflict c_069.
3. [Important] Which camp of the Taylor Statten Camps did Forgie direct? *Algonquin Story* says one
   was "directed by Wallace Forgie" — seen as a search highlight only, in three separate scans, and
   not read.
4. [Important] Was Forgie Ethel Statten's brother or the husband of Statten's sister Bolina? This
   is a specific genealogical question and it decides how the Statten kinship should be written.
5. [Nice-to-have] The 1918 C.S.E.T. manual's **programme content** is unread here — only its
   acknowledgements. It would say directly what CSET asked of a camp, which is the part that bears
   on Kanawana.
6. [Nice-to-have] Seven further books name him and none has been read: a Canadian diplomat's memoir,
   a mission history that has him replaced in South India by a man named Dunderdale, and a life
   shaped "chiefly through the inspiration of Mr. Wallace Forgie." All are listed in the cache.
