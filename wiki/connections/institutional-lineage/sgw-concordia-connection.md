# Sir George Williams / Concordia University and Camp Kanawana

*Status: E1-reviewed | Sources: 9*
*Last Updated: 2026-07-09*

## Overview

The institutional relationship between Sir George Williams University (now Concordia University) and Camp Kanawana is one of the most distinctive threads in the camp's history. SGW grew directly from the YMCA of Montreal's educational mission -- from evening courses begun in 1873, to YMCA Schools, to Sir George Williams College (1926), to SGW University (1948/1960), and finally to Concordia University through the 1974 merger with Loyola College.^1 ^4 For decades, the university and the camp drew from the same institutional well, producing a pipeline of personnel who moved between YMCA camping, camp research, and university faculty positions.

This connection was not merely historical. As late as the 1970s, SGW students used the YMCA chapel, gymnasium, and pool, even after the university severed its formal financial ties with the YMCA in the late 1960s.^1 And beginning in 2019, a new YMCA-Concordia partnership brought the relationship full circle, with Concordia students and Stingers athletes serving as camp counsellors for a Fine Arts camp program that expanded by 2024 to include Science and Engineering and STEAMpunks programming.^5

## The YMCA-to-University Evolution

The institutional lineage runs as follows:

- **1873**: YMCA Montreal begins evening courses^4
- **1926**: SGW College organized as the YMCA's educational arm^1
- **1948/1960**: SGW receives university charter^1
- **Late 1960s**: SGW severs formal financial ties with the YMCA^1
- **1974**: SGW merges with Loyola College to form Concordia University^1
- **1997**: Department of Applied Social Science amalgamates with Leisure Studies to form Department of Applied Human Sciences^3

## Key Figures at the Intersection

### Harold H. Potter (1914--2004)

Potter attended SGW College (B.A. 1935--1939) while working summers as a camp counsellor at YMCA Kamp Kanawana. In 1947, he became the first Canadian-born Black sociologist hired by a Canadian post-secondary institution, appointed lecturer in Sociology at SGW College. In 1960, he established the Department of Sociology and Anthropology at SGW. He maintained YMCA ties throughout his career.^6

### Hedley G. Dimock and A. Ross Seaman (1963)

In 1963, both Dimock and Seaman moved simultaneously from YMCA/camp roles into SGW -- Dimock as the first Chairman of the Department of Applied Social Science and Director of the Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies (CHRCS), Seaman as part-time faculty.^2 ^3 The CHRCS was described as growing "directly out of Sir George Williams University's roots in the community, specifically through the YMCA of Montreal."^3 The Department of Applied Social Science itself evolved from 1940s-era YMCA/SGW courses in counselling, group work, organization development, and supervision.^3

### Raye Kass (1972--present)

Hired as Assistant Professor of Applied Human Sciences in 1972 (B.A. with Distinction from SGW, 1970; MSW, University of Toronto, 1972), Kass became Program Director of the CHRCS and presented on camp leadership at the International Camping Congress.^3 ^7 She collaborated extensively with Dimock on group development publications.

## Research and Scholarship

Several significant pieces of camp-related research were produced through this institutional nexus:

- **1960--1961**: Hedley G. Dimock's camp counsellor effectiveness studies conducted at Kanawana^2
- **1970**: Report of the Seminar on Camp Leadership Research at SGW (April 16--17, 1970)
- **1974**: Report of the Staff Task Group on Outdoor Education in the Montreal YMCA^4
- **2023**: Grace McMorris, "An Experience That Lasts a Lifetime: Building Modernity, Man, and Nation at the YMCA of Montreal's Kamp Kanawana, 1894--1967" (MA thesis, Concordia University)^8

## The Modern Partnership (2019--present)

In 2019, Concordia's Faculty of Fine Arts signed a partnership agreement with the YMCA, launching a Fine Arts camp program. By 2024, this had expanded to include a Science and Engineering Camp and STEAMpunks (ages 13--15). Camp counsellors are primarily Concordia students and Stingers athletes.^5 The partnership represents a renewal -- in a different form -- of the century-old institutional connection.
