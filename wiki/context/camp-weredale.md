# Camp Weredale and Its Relationship to Kanawana

*Status: stub | Sources: 5*
*Last Updated: 2026-03-15*

## Overview

Camp Weredale is a youth residential camp on Lac l'Achigan in Saint-Hippolyte, Quebec, approximately 55 miles north of Montreal. Founded in 1934, it is one of several Montreal-area camps whose history intersects with Camp Kanawana's, though — contrary to some assumptions — Camp Weredale was never a YMCA property. Its relationship to Kanawana centers on a proposed but ultimately abandoned two-site operation in the late 1970s.

## Origins: The Boys' Home of Montreal

In 1870, Charles Alexander (1816–1905), a Scottish immigrant who had made his fortune as a confectioner in Montreal, founded the Boys' Home of Montreal on Mountain Street. The institution housed homeless and at-risk anglophone boys aged nine to eighteen, providing cheap board, evening classes, and manual training. Alexander built the original building at his own expense and served as president from 1870 until his death, during which time the Home sheltered more than 1,000 boys.

By 1930, larger quarters were needed. A campaign conducted by the Rotary Club of Montreal raised $289,000, and a new building was constructed on Weredale Park in Westmount. The institution became known as Weredale House.

## Camp History

Before the formal establishment of Camp Weredale, Weredale House boys attended a shared camp in Sainte-Marguerite from the 1920s. An even earlier boys' camp, "Camp Boy," had been sponsored in 1918. The following year it was renamed "Camp Lewis" in memory of Major John Lewis, a friend of the boys killed in the First World War.

In 1934, the Rotary Club of Westmount purchased 260 acres with a mile of shoreline on Lac l'Achigan and donated the property to Weredale House. Camp Weredale was established as a summer home for the boys and staff. At its peak, the camp featured approximately two dozen buildings, including a dining hall, recreation hall, craft shops, hospital, shower rooms, sleeping cabins, playground, open-air chapel, Indian Council ring, rifle range, tree house, and campsites.

The camp suffered a fire in 1965 and was rebuilt.

## Institutional Transitions

In 1977, Weredale House merged with Summerhill Homes, the Girls' Cottage School, and Allencroft Reception Centres to form Youth Horizons (now part of the Batshaw Youth and Family Centres network). Camp Weredale survived this transition and continues to operate today under the Weredale Foundation as an independent co-ed camp for children aged 6–17, welcoming four cohorts of approximately 85 campers each from late June through mid-August.

## Relationship to Camp Kanawana

In 1977, the YMCA of Montreal produced situation reports profiling Kamp Kanawana, Camp Otoreke, and Camp Weredale together, and by approximately 1980 had developed plans for a proposed two-site operation combining Kanawana and Weredale. The plan was ultimately abandoned; the reasons are not documented in available sources. Concordia University Archives sub-series 12A holds the relevant planning documents (1977–1983).
