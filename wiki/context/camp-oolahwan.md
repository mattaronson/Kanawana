## Overview

Camp Oolahwan was a YWCA summer camp for girls founded in 1917 by Mary Susanna Edgar (1889–1973) on Lake Walfred in Sainte-Marguerite-du-Lac-Masson, in the Laurentian mountains north of Montreal.^1 ^2 It operated for 88 years until 2005, making it the longest sustained initiative in YWCA Montreal history.^1 The YWCA describes it as the first summer camp for girls in Canada, though Camp Northway in Ontario (founded 1906) predates it; the YWCA claim likely refers to the first YWCA-operated girls' camp.^1 ^3

## Founding and Direction

Mary Susanna Edgar served as Director of Camp Oolahwan from 1915 to 1919 while simultaneously serving as YWCA Girls' Work Secretary in Montreal.^4 Edgar later founded Glen Bernard Camp (1922) and served as first vice-president of the Canadian Camping Association (1936).^3

Ferna G. Halliday, a Queen's University graduate, served as Secretary of YWCA Montreal from 1918 to 1921 and was responsible for girls at Camp Oolahwan during that period.^5 Halliday went on to co-found Camp Ouareau (1922) and Camp Oconto (1924), and was a founding member of the Ontario Camping Association in 1933.^5

## Location and Operations

The camp was located on Lake Walfred in Sainte-Marguerite-du-Lac-Masson, Quebec.^1 ^2 It was an English-speaking camp exclusively for young girls.^2 No detailed records of its programming, capacity, or facilities have been located in available sources.

## Connection to Kanawana

Camp Oolahwan's connection to Camp Kanawana runs through the YMCA/YWCA Montreal institutional network. Both camps were operated by branches of the same parent organization. Direct inter-camp contact is documented: YWCA Camp Oolahwan sent visitors to Kanawana, and in 1946, Kanawana campers were covered in cocoa powder to greet Oolahwan visitors as "Indians."^6

## Centennial and Closure

Camp Oolahwan closed in 2005 after 88 years of operation.^1 In 2017, a centennial reunion was held at Camp Ouareau in Notre-Dame-de-la-Merci.^7 The exact dates of the reunion are disputed: the YWCA gives September 8–10, while the West Island Blog gives August 25–27 (see conflicts.json, c_002).^7 ^8
