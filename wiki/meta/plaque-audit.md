# The Plaque Audit

*Status: R3-verified | Sources: 1*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-02*

## Overview

Kanawana's dining hall walls carry about 151 surviving painted and carved boards — section rosters, canoe-trip records, honour rolls, novelty objects — photographed in an album this project has held for years. In August 2026 every one of them was read against the knowledge-base fact that cites it. **Eighty-three of those facts under-recorded their object.** Roughly 1,825 names were recovered, most of which had never been written down anywhere in this project.^1

This article records what the audit found and, more usefully, how the collection failed to be read the first time.

## How it started

Not with a plan. The operator, asked to place a former staff member at camp, named six 1995 Counsellors-in-Training from memory. Three matched the wiki's transcription of the "CITS 95" plaque and three did not — so the plaque image was opened. Two of the three "missing" names were on the board all along, in a portion of the photograph nobody had transcribed. The fact carried eleven names. The plaque carries twenty-nine.

That raised the obvious question: how many of the other 150 were like this? The answer was most of them.

## What the audit found

| Verdict | Count |
|---|---|
| Fact under-records the object | **83** |
| Fact complete | 37 |
| Object genuinely carries no names | 13 |
| Names present but not readable | 8 |
| Read in full, not line-compared | 6 |
| **No fact described the object at all** | **4** |

The worst ratios were on the largest boards, where the loss is greatest: the Staff '97 plaque held three names of roughly one hundred; Staff 1990, five of about 102; Staff 02, six of 94; the Knights of Kanawana honour roll, three of twenty-nine.

## Findings that are not name counts

**A correct fact can still fail.** The Rangers 1956 plaque was transcribed completely and correctly. It names **John Cleghorn** — and [[people/notable-alumni/john-cleghorn|his article]] carried an open question asking which years he attended camp, answered only as "approximately the 1950s." Nothing had ever carried the fact to the article that needed it. An audit that only counted names would have marked that row clean and moved on.

**Things the camp recorded that this wiki did not know existed.** A "Wolf" credit beneath Counsellor and Tripper on two separate 2009 boards — read during the audit as a third *staff* role, and corrected afterwards to a rank on the leadership track, of the same kind as CIT and LIT. See [[site/named-places-and-camp-vocabulary|Named Places and Camp Vocabulary]]. An epitaph — "May the Sanctum R.I.P." — for a camp structure that ended in 2007 and appeared nowhere in [[site/the-kanawana-site|the site article]] until this audit put it there. A place called the Suez Bridge — since identified by the operator as the crossing over the channel between Lake Kanawana and Lake Wilson. A column of six national origins on the Pathfinder paddle — Australia, England, Holland, Cape Breton, France, Virginia — and a 2009 board that calls itself the "International Tent," together the only documentation this project has of international staffing.

**Coeducation in the campers' own handwriting.** "The 1st Ladies of the Barracks" (2007). "To the women that filled the Lookout with love and friendship" (2008). And an all-female Voyageur crew in 2010 whose board does not remark on it at all — thirty-eight years after the first all-female canoe trip was worth painting a plaque about. See [[history/coeducation-gender|Coeducation and Gender at Kanawana]].

**Culture the roster-facts had discarded.** A Robert Frost epigraph on the 1984 CIT board. Thirteen catchphrases from the summer of 1985. A dated 2006 setlist that turns out to be pop covers, not camp songs. Kanawoodstock running across at least three widely separated years. And a bone-shaped board with a paw print painted on it, which is how we know Raku was a dog.

## Errors found, including my own

Three facts pointed at the wrong object, all among the undated files with non-descriptive names — including two facts that describe the front and back of a single birdhouse as though they were separate boards, one of them calling it undated when the other carries the year. Twelve names and roles were corrected.

Six readings made *during* the audit were later overturned by other plaques in the same collection: a nickname read as "Sassim" is "Saddam"; a surname read as "Waff" is "Watt"; a carving dated by style to the 1940s is from 1999; and both boards flagged as uncatalogued turned out to be objects the knowledge base already held, seen obliquely from the side. These are recorded rather than quietly fixed, because an audit that hides its own error rate is not evidence of anything.

## Method notes

Four rules came out of this pass, each of them learned by getting it wrong first:

1. **Alphabetical order on these boards is a tendency, not a rule.** Late additions get appended out of sequence at the foot of a column. Ordering may *suggest* a reading for a faint name; it must never be used to *exclude* one.
2. **The shape of an object is evidence.** A bone, a guitar pick, a birdhouse, a drum head, a moose antler, a route map — several boards carry their meaning in their form rather than their text.
3. **Check whether a central device is a divider or a spine.** One board splits given names to the left and surnames to the right; read as two lists it is nonsense.
4. **"No names" and "names unreadable" are different claims.** The first closes a question. The second keeps it open — which matters most on the 1994 Nature Trip board, recorded as carrying no names, which in fact carries about fifteen effaced lines. 1994 is the one year with no CIT plaque, making those wings the only surviving roster from that season.

## Open Questions

1. [Important] Can the 1994 Nature Trip board be re-photographed under raking light? It is the highest-value object in the collection and its names are the only ones from that season.
2. [Important] Four other boards would give up substantially more to a better photograph: Staff 2000 (60–70 names, half illegible), the second 1999 CIT board, the 2001 Malibu canoe (obscured by electrical conduit rather than by wear), and the effaced Kanawana board.
3. [Nice-to-have] Two facts describe one birdhouse. How many other pairs in this knowledge base are two records of a single object?
