# What the Plaques Say Besides Names

*Status: R3-verified | Sources: 3*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-02*

## Overview

For most of this project's life the dining-hall plaques were treated as rosters: read a board, extract the names, record the fact. The [[meta/plaque-audit|plaque audit]] of August 2026 read all 151 of them properly and found that the names are frequently the *least* interesting thing on the wood.^1 Campers painted mottos, catchphrases, song titles, jokes, epitaphs and epigraphs onto these boards, and almost none of it had been recorded — not because it was illegible, but because nobody was looking for it.

This article collects what the boards say when they are not listing people.

## The one literary quotation

The 1984 CIT board carries, painted in red above a canoeist:

> *We have promises to keep / and miles to go before we sleep* — **R.F.**

Robert Frost, "Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening," initialled rather than attributed — and altered. Frost wrote *"But I have promises to keep, / And miles to go before I sleep"*; the board puts both lines in the first person **plural**, which for a graduating CIT cohort painting its own name-board is either a slip or the whole point. It is the only literary quotation found on any Kanawana plaque, and the fact recording that board — which had twelve of its eighteen names — did not mention it.^1

## Thirteen catchphrases from the summer of 1985

The 1985 CIT board is cut in the shape of the numerals **85**, and its centre is filled not with names but with the things that cohort said all summer:

> *Bronze 1. · Somethin' fierce! · Hey ho hip hop · Freak of evolution · Negatory. · Psyche. · Woah Bess! · Mom Lindsay. · C.I.T. Trips '85. · Aces! · CIT Project? · Where do you find us? Under "C". · Dude Monger.*

This is the richest surviving sample of 1980s camp slang in the collection and none of it was in the knowledge base.^1 Compare the 1975 CIT board, whose centre motto is **"Why Can't We Be Friends?"** — the title of a War single released that same summer — painted around a grinning face, with a separate line naming the cohort's **mascots**.

Nicknaming was the dominant idiom throughout. The 2007 Roadhouse board labels every girl with a catchphrase rather than a description — *"im sorry" Foris, "i can't do it" Tebeka, "jellybean" Kaminski, "keeps talking" Riley* — and the 1985 sailing staff are listed only by nickname: *Gumby, Mau-Mau, Mr. R, Bambi, S. Beauty, Opie, Conductor*.

## A dated setlist

The lower third of the 2006 Senior Girls board carries four song titles in faded red, of which **"Tainted Love"** and **"Proud Mary"** are clearly legible and **"Step by Step"** probably so.^1

This matters more than its size suggests. [[traditions/camp-songs-cheers|Camp Songs and Cheers]] is built almost entirely from oral history and secondary sources. Here is a **documented, dated setlist on a camp object** — and it is pop covers, not traditional camp songs, which is itself a fact about what was actually being sung at Kanawana in 2006.

## Kanawoodstock

Three objects, widely separated, establish a recurring music festival the wiki does not treat as a named event:^1

| Object | Year | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| A wooden **guitar**, body and neck | 1993 or 1995 (digit ambiguous) | title and year only |
| A **guitar pick** | 2000 | four names; *"I want to rock & roll all nite"* (KISS) |
| A real **drum head**, still in its hoop | 2009 | seven signatures; *"Anarchy in the KK"*, the A drawn as an anarchy symbol (Sex Pistols) |

Each generation reached for the music of its own moment to name the same event — and the 2009 object is not a plaque at all but a piece of the festival's equipment, kept.

## Themed days and all-camp games

Four boards record a competition format the wiki has not named as a tradition:^1

- **Western Day, 1968** — a shield naming the winning team, the *Cowboys*.
- **Viking Day, 1972** — an arrow-shaped board with a longship, reading **"Sweden"** in heavy black across the middle. A *national* assignment implies the camp was divided into country teams, not simply staging a Viking pageant.
- **Wizard of Oz Day** — a deeply carved board, badly weathered, reading approximately "Wizard of Oz Day / Kamp Kanawana / Winning Team / of 19[?]0." The year and team are not recoverable.
- An **Arthurian all-camp day**, "Sir Kay, or Arthur's Lost Knight," ran in 1987 and is separately documented in the camp's own director's report, which rated it "slightly less than mediocre."

Alongside these the **all-camp game** was named and commemorated each session: a ship-shaped board for *"Treasure Island KK — 1st Session All Kamp 1996,"* and a bone-shaped board for the third session of 1995.

## Raku

That 1995 board reads **"We saved Raku and the red-day tuck!"** — which on its own is a private joke. But the board is cut in the shape of a **bone**, with a **paw print** painted beside the lettering.^1

Raku was a dog — and specifically he was **Camp Director Joanna A.A. Hoad's** dog.^2 A plaque's shape carried the whole meaning of a sentence that is otherwise a private joke, and the date fits exactly: Hoad's on-site directorship is recorded as running from 1995 to 2000, so an all-camp game built around rescuing Raku falls in her **first season**. The new director's dog, made the object of the whole camp's game.

That is worth one further note. Hoad's Kanawana tenure is the one span in [[people/directors-index|the directors index]] that no document has ever corroborated — two full research passes across nine or more surfaces found nothing, and the 1995–2000 dates rest on oral history alone.^2 A bone-shaped board dated to the third session of 1995, naming a dog the operator independently identifies as hers, is not proof of her appointment; it is a dated physical object consistent with her being at camp that summer, produced by a different route than the memory it agrees with. That is the first material evidence of any kind touching those dates.

He remains the only camp animal recorded anywhere in this project.

## A cabin gets its name

A 2000 board is headed **ZODIAC** and reads: *"This cabin was named 1st Session 2000 by:"*, followed by eight girls.^1 It is the only object in the collection that records an *act* rather than a season — and [[site/the-kanawana-site|the site article]] has no account of how camp buildings acquired their names. ## Two decorative traditions running side by side

The boards draw on two visual vocabularies that do not mix:^1

**Indigenous-derived imagery.** A 1982 Senior Girls Staff wood slice carries a large **thunderbird in a Pacific-Northwest formline style** — ovoids, split-U feathers, a red-black-white palette. The Montreal-to-New-York paddle carries a stylised mask and sunburst in the same idiom, and the 1959 La Vérendrye board is painted directly onto a **moose antler**, naming a section "The Cawasachouanes." [[site/indigenous-names-and-land|Indigenous Names and Land]] exists as an article, but no fact in this knowledge base records the camp's use of such imagery on its own objects — which is dated, material evidence bearing directly on that discussion.

**European folk carving.** Against this, one shield-shaped board carries only chip-carved rosettes, scrolled triangular banding and dotted borders — a Scandinavian or Slavic decorative vocabulary with nothing Indigenous about it. And the 1983 Senior Girls Staff pennant is a hand-drawn **Sierpinski gasket**, three or four levels deep, in alternating pink and blue.

## Open Questions

1. [Nice-to-have, answered 2026-08-28] ~~Who was Raku?~~ Director Joanna A.A. Hoad's dog.^2 Whether there were other camp animals is still open — he is the only one on record.
2. [Important, advanced 2026-09-03] Was Kanawoodstock an annual event with three surviving objects, or an occasional one? **It was running in 1987**, six years before the earliest object — the Director's Report for that year lists it among the special events that "went exceptionally well," beside Glissade d'Eau, Secret Friends, the Green Triangle, The Legend of the Tipi, Kabarets and the Chicago Coffee House.^3 So the objects are a sample of a longer run, not the run itself. The gaps between 1987, 1993/95, 2000 and 2009 are gaps in what survived.
3. [Important] What did the camp's use of Indigenous-derived imagery look like over time, and did it change? Three dated objects exist; nothing in the knowledge base discusses them.
4. [Nice-to-have] The Wizard of Oz board's year and winning team could not be read even after contrast enhancement. Does anyone recall a Wizard of Oz Day?
5. [Nice-to-have] How were cabins normally named? The Zodiac board suggests campers did it themselves, in a recorded act.
