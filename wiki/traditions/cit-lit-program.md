# The CIT/LIT Program

*Status: draft | Sources: 11*
*Last Updated: 2026-07-13*

## Overview

Camp Kanawana's Counsellor-in-Training (CIT) and Leader-in-Training (LIT) program prepares older teenage campers for staff roles, combining structured training with a capstone canoe trip. Introduced in the 1960s, it has run continuously for over six decades — documented on dining-hall plaques from 1960 through the 1990s and, in its present form, branded "Trailblazers" — making it one of Kanawana's longest-running structured programs, though it has never before been synthesized into its own article.

## Origins and Early Decades

The Counsellor-in-Training program was introduced at Kanawana in the 1960s.^1 A. Ross Seaman's directorship (1959–1967) — which also oversaw the 1959 section renaming and the La Vérendrye canoe-tripping expansion — provides the most likely institutional context for the CIT program's creation, though no source directly credits Seaman with introducing it specifically. The earliest documented CIT cohort is captured on a 1960 dining-hall plaque naming Ron McCallum as "Director" of that year's program.^2 Grand Portage served as the CIT director's cabin through the 1980s and 1990s.^3

By the 1980s and 1990s, the program appears regularly on surviving dining-hall and canoe-board plaques, naming successive cohorts and their program directors year over year — a continuity record that, taken together with the 1960 plaque, spans at least three and a half decades before the program's most recent documented form. Individual CITs and program directors named on these plaques are already catalogued collectively in [[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana]]'s Section and Program Directors table, consistent with this wiki's policy of treating undocumented recent staff as collective reference-table entries rather than standalone biographical subjects.

## Kanawana's CIT Program in National Context

No single, well-documented origin event for the term "counsellor-in-training" (or "CIT") turned up in a dedicated search across Wikipedia, the American Camp Association's own history timeline, and the Trent University Archives finding aids for the Ontario Camping Association and Canadian Camping Association fonds — all treat it as a taken-for-granted current practice rather than a dated innovation, and none contain the term at all.^7 ^8 ^9 The underlying concept — using older teen campers as apprentice staff within a structured, character-education framework — traces institutionally rather than terminologically to the 1920s-30s wave of applying child-development theory to camping in North America, led most prominently by Hedley S. Dimock (already documented in this wiki; see [[people/hedley-dimock|Hedley Dimock]]), who ran YMCA College Chicago's Summer Camp Institutes on camp "character education" from 1930 to 1948 and co-authored *Talks to Counselors* (1939) directly with Taylor Statten, founder of Camp Ahmek and founding president of the Canadian Camping Association.^10

The circumstantial link to Kanawana specifically runs through a connection this wiki already documents: Dimock's son, Hedley Gardner Dimock, Coordinator of Staff Development and Training for the Montreal YMCA, conducted counsellor-selection and -effectiveness research *at Kanawana* in 1960-1961 — the same year as Kanawana's earliest documented CIT cohort (the 1960 Ron McCallum plaque) — and returned in 1970 for further research on the selection and training of camp leaders, also based on Kanawana data. Separately, a YMCA of Montreal archival item titled "Camp leaders' training class" is dated to the 1930s, indicating the Montreal YMCA had some form of leadership-training class decades before 1960, though it is unconfirmed whether this item is Kanawana-specific or used CIT-type terminology.^11

On relative timing: a search-engine synthesis of the Taylor Statten Camps history page (direct fetch blocked; flagged as unverified pending confirmation) states that formal leadership/CIT-designated programs at Ahmek and Wapomeo were introduced only "into the 1970 and 80s."^12 If that holds up, Kanawana's 1960 CIT program would be early relative to the flagship camp most associated with the professionalization of Canadian camping — not a case of adopting an established provincial trend, but of running parallel to, or even ahead of, it, within the same small YMCA-Dimock-Statten institutional network. This remains a hypothesis for future archive-level research, not a resolved claim — no source found actually uses the term "CIT" or "counsellor-in-training" in connection with Dimock, Statten, *Talks to Counselors*, or the CCA/OCA fonds.

## The LIT Track and Notable Participants

A parallel or overlapping "Leader-in-Training" (LIT) designation is also documented: [[people/notable-alumni/notable-alumni|notable alumnus]] Leigh Evans attended Kanawana for six summers — one as a camper, one as a Leader-in-Training, and four as staff — before being named Capitaine of the Voyageur team in the 2012 L&V Games.^4

## Current Structure: Trailblazers

As of 2026, the CIT program is branded "Trailblazers": a 48-day program (four sessions of 12 days each) for campers aged 16–17.^5 Participants complete over 200 hours of workshops, training sessions, and activities covering creative programming, risk management, team building, and effective communication, capped by a five-day canoe trip.^5 Certifications earned include Standard First Aid and CPR (a 16-hour course) and a CIT certificate from the Association des camps certifiés du Québec (ACC).^5 A separate "Leaders in Training Information Guide" for Summer 2026 documents the current LIT program structure specifically, published as a PDF on the YMCA Quebec website.^6
