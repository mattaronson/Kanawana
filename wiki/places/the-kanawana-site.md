## Summary

Camp Kanawana occupies a 550-acre site in Saint-Sauveur, in the Laurentian Mountains approximately 90 km north of Montreal.^1 The camp has three private lakes — Lake Kanawana, Lake Wilson, and Round Lake — connected by a dam between Lake Kanawana and Lake Wilson that is ceremonially opened at the end of each season.^2 The site was acquired from the Page family after the camp's move from Lake Saint-Joseph (where it operated as Camp Jubilee from 1894 to approximately 1909).

## Location and Access

The camp is situated 45 miles (72 km) from Montreal, with the nearest railway station historically at Piedmont on the CPR Sainte-Agathe line.^3 In 1922, campers took the 7:30 AM train from Place Viger Station, disembarking at Piedmont and traveling 6 miles to the camp.^4 Hike destinations from camp included Val Morin, Lac Hughes, Sainte-Agathe, Sixteen Island Lake, Lac Wilson, Pages, and Sainte-Adolphe.^5

## Camp Layout

The site is organized into distinct areas:

### Centre Camp
Small cabins without electricity or plumbing, along with platform tents — the traditional camping experience.^6

### Front Camp
Contains the Farmhouse and Blockhouse, both winterized buildings with full kitchens and electricity.^6

### Lake Wilson Area
Private campsites available for rental, providing a more secluded experience.^6

### Outpost Camps
In 1923, outpost camps included Otoreke (the senior camp) and Lake Marois.^5

## Facilities

Historical facilities documented across the decades include:^7 ^8

- Two pavilions (a Dining Pavilion and a lakeside Pavilion, both new as of 1922)
- Hospital (new as of 1922)
- Fenced swimming pool (1922)
- Boats and canoes
- Four diving boards and a zinc slide
- Chapel (outdoor, with an organized choir documented in 1938)
- Council Ring
- Icehouse
- Archery range
- Lacrosse field
- An "Indian Grave" marking on the camp map
- A totem pole (added 1927 under Harold Cross; still visible in 1970s photos)

A 1928 Kanawana map and a 1962 Kanawana map were both analyzed in the McMorris thesis.^9 A 1974 orienteering map (6 photocopies) is held in the Concordia Archives.^10

## Proposed Two-Site Operation

From 1977 to 1980, plans existed for a proposed two-site operation using Kanawana and Weredale.^11 The outcome of this proposal is not documented in current sources.
