# Camp Otoreke

*Status: stub | Sources: 4*
*Last Updated: 2026-02-17*

## Overview

Camp Otoreke was the YMCA of Montreal's original Lake Saint-Joseph camping site, renamed from Camp Jubilee in 1909. The name "Otoreke" is Iroquoian for "north."^1 After the main YMCA camp relocated to Saint-Sauveur (becoming Kamp Kanawana), Camp Otoreke continued operating on the original islands in Lake Saint-Joseph until it closed in 1982.^1

## History

Camp Jubilee was established in 1894 on islands in Lake Saint-Joseph, near Saint-Adolphe-d'Howard, as the YMCA of Montreal's first organized camping venture.^2 The camp was named for the YMCA's 50th anniversary (founded 1844, jubilee year 1894).^3 When the main camp moved to the Saint-Sauveur site around 1909–1910, the Lake Saint-Joseph location was renamed Camp Otoreke and repurposed.

After 1910, Camp Otoreke served a different population from Kanawana: men 18+, women and men 18+, married couples, and low-income families.^1 It evolved from a boys' camp into an adult and family camping operation.

## Relationship to Kanawana

Camp Otoreke remained connected to Kanawana as an outpost and trip destination:

- In 1923, Otoreke served as the senior camp outpost, alongside Lake Marois.^4
- In 1935, a four-day trip to Otoreke was made from Kanawana.^5
- Otoreke was a regular canoe trip destination from Kanawana.^5

The Concordia Archives hold records for Camp Otoreke in the YMCA of Montreal fonds (P145, Series 12), alongside records for Kanawana and other YMCA camping operations.^6
