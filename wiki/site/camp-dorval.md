# Camp Dorval

*Status: stub | Sources: 4*
*Last Updated: 2026-09-07 (created from the annual reports, read as a run)*

## Overview

Camp Dorval was a YMCA of Montreal weekend and overnight camp on the island of Montreal, running from
1925 to about 1928 for working boys from the Côte St. Paul and Verdun branches who could not get to the
association's Laurentian camps. It was never a property: it was a large house leased from the Marcil
Trust Company, furnished with donated cots, and it asked to be discontinued in 1928 unless the
association could find it a better site.^1

Its records at Concordia amount to half a centimetre of paper — a director's report for 1926 and a
season report for 1928.^2 What follows comes almost entirely from the association's own annual reports,
which had never been read for this camp as a run.

## What it was for

The 1926 season, in the report's own words: "This camp, **overlooking the Royal Montreal Golf Club, is
in singular contrast to it!** Carried on for **boys in St. Paul and Verdun who would otherwise have no
vacation**, it has for equipment the use of **a house leased from the Marcil Trust Company at a nominal
rental**; **canvas cots for thirty boys** made from material given by the **Dominion Textile Company**
and steel pipe donated by **Sessenwein Brothers**; four tents, two boats, three canoes; a football; soft
ball; table and chairs. And this equipment served last year **over two hundred working boys**. **The
complete cost per week-end was $1.00 plus twenty cents for car fare.** … This camp has meant so much to
**the boys south of the canal**."^1

Membership rose "during the year from **193 to 248**."^1

**The whole camp is an argument about distance and money, and the report makes it itself.** Kanawana was
forty-five miles north in the Laurentians and cost $5.00 a week in 1916; Dorval was a leased house on
the island at a dollar and twenty cents a weekend. The sentence about the golf club is the association
noticing the contrast and choosing to print it.

## Founding, 1925

"An **over-night camp** was organized and maintained at Dorval during the warm summer months **for the
boys of Côte St. Paul**, and **due entirely to the initiative of Mr. J. G. MacKinnon**."^1 That is the
same **J. G. MacKinnon** who was Boys' Work Secretary at Central and ran Kanawana through the 1917, 1918
and 1919 seasons; see [[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff]].

## 1928: the camp asked to continue and its association proposed to close it

Two documents cover the same season and they do not reach the same conclusion.

**The association's annual report**, on the 1928 season: "Camp Dorval was **again operated jointly by
the Verdun and Côte St. Paul Branches** using the large house leased from the Marcil Trust Company in
1925. **Eighty-one boys spent 214 week-ends** in this camp, which was operated for **two full weeks and
ten week-ends**. We have experimented with the idea of a '**Near-the-city' camp for older boys** and
have found that **there is a very important need for such an enterprise**. The facilities available,
however, are **so inadequate** that **unless some property can be secured** which will better meet the
needs of the situation, **it is proposed to discontinue this service**."^1

**The camp's own season report** for the same summer says the opposite about its future: "**the
near-the-City Camp idea has proven itself**… **strongly recommended that the Camp be continued**."^3
That report also gives the economics the annual report does not: the house leased from the Marcil Trust
Company for **$75** from May to November, a total annual cost of roughly **$500**, and meals at **twenty
cents**. The camp lost its boats to theft or destruction that year, and on one occasion "through the
kindness of **Mr. Dent Harrison**, all the boys were taken for a spin over **Lake St. Louis** in the
Harrison motor launch" — a camp charging twenty cents a meal did not own a launch, and the man who lent
one is part of how it ran.^3

**The two are not quite contradictory, and the difference is worth seeing.** Both say the facilities are
inadequate. Both say the idea works. The camp concludes that it should therefore continue; the
association concludes that it should therefore stop unless somebody buys it a site. **It did not fail
for lack of demand** — neither document claims that — and which body's view prevailed is not established
here. Concordia's records for the camp stop in 1928.

## The Lakeshore Club, 1941: the association answers itself, thirteen years later

*This section replaced a paragraph offering the sequence below as an inference. It is not an inference
any more.*

The report on the summer of **1941**: "**(3) Lakeshore Club: For years the Association has sought a place
in near proximity to the city**, where members and their friends might enjoy the benefits of outdoor
life during the summer months. **Last summer Central Branch was able to lease such a place on the shore
of Lake St. Louis in Dorval municipality.** The property includes **a house which provides living
accommodation for about 20 young men**. There is ample grass space, **fine elms** and other trees and
shrubs, boating and swimming facilities, **tennis court**, space for **archery, horse-shoes, softball**,
etc. **The view of the lake is unexcelled. Transportation is by bus and tram car. It would be a
wonderful thing if the Association might secure the permanent use of such a property.**"^4

Set that against the 1928 sentence above. The association said then that the near-the-city idea met "a
very important need" and that it would stop **unless some property can be secured**. In 1942 it writes
that it has sought exactly such a place **"for years,"** has leased one, **in the same municipality**,
and would like to own it. Same need, same place-name, same word about property. **The continuity is
documented rather than inferred** — though it is emphatically *not the same property*: Camp Dorval's
house overlooked the Royal Montreal Golf Club and this one is on the Lake St. Louis shore, and no
document connects the two leases.

By **1943** it was "open from **May to October** for a limited number of residents, and in addition, was
available for boating, swimming, tennis, archery, horse-shoes, picnics, etc. **Several conferences also
took place on the property**."^4

**And the war took it.** The same 1943 report: "**Camp Otoreke at Lake St. Joseph, our Lakeshore Club at
Dorval, and the Y.M.C.A. Ski Lodge at Ste-Adèle were continuously used, in the[ir] seasons, by men of
the Forces.**"^4 That is the association's whole outdoor plant turned over to servicemen — and
[[site/the-kanawana-site|Kanawana]] is conspicuously not in the list of three. See
[[history/wartime-kanawana|Wartime Kanawana]].

*What still is a reading and not a finding:* that [[site/camp-becsies|Camp Becsies]] in 1929 and
[[site/camp-perrot|Camp Perrot]] in 1945, bought "after many years of search," belong to the same
search. No document joins those to Dorval.

## Open Questions

1. [Critical] **Which view prevailed?** The camp's own 1928 report recommends continuation; the association's
   annual report for the same season proposes discontinuation unless a property is secured. Did the 1929
   season run, and when did the Marcil Trust lease end? The annual reports after 1929 have not been
   swept for Dorval.
2. [Important] Is the fall from "over two hundred working boys" in 1926 to eighty-one in 1928 real, or a
   change in what was counted? The 1926 figure is boys served; the 1928 figure is boys attending
   weekends, and 214 weekend-attendances across 81 boys is not obviously fewer boy-days.
3. [Nice-to-have] Where exactly was the house? "Overlooking the Royal Montreal Golf Club" locates it
   within a few hundred metres on the Dorval shore, and a 1925-1928 fire-insurance plan or lot roll would
   settle it.
4. [Nice-to-have] What do the Concordia director's report (1926) and season report (1928) add? Neither
   has been read; both are in Box HA1894.
5. [Nice-to-have] Who were Sessenwein Brothers and what was the Marcil Trust Company's interest in
   lending a house at a nominal rent? Both are Montreal firms of the period and neither appears elsewhere
   in this project.
