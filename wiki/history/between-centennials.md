# Between Centennials (1968–1993)

*Status: draft | Sources: 12*
*Last Updated: 2026-08-14*

## Overview

The quarter-century between Canada's 1967 Centennial and Kanawana's own 1994 Centennial saw the camp's most consequential social change — coeducation — alongside a directorial record that grows steadily thinner as the period goes on. Girls were admitted starting in 1968–1969, and within three years the camp sent its first all-female Voyageur canoe trip — on its most demanding and symbolically masculine programme. The wider reorganization of the section structure around a gendered parallel system came later and is not precisely dated: it is documented as in place by the early 1980s, and that structure persists today. But the same decades produced two of the camp's most poorly documented directorial gaps, in the 1970s and again in the 1980s.

## Coeducation (1967–1972)

Kanawana's Co-ed Camping Pilot Project is documented in Concordia Archives records spanning 1967–1970.^1 The available sources give two different dates for the transition: the YMCA Quebec's own official history timeline states girls were first welcomed in 1968, while the separate "Kamp Kanawana Facts" institutional sheet states the camp "became coeducational" in 1969.^2 ^3 The most plausible reading treats both as correct about different milestones — a small pilot cohort in 1968, with full integration formalized in 1969 — rather than one source being in error; a 2005 YMCA John Island Alumni Newsletter describing a 1969 visitor "hired as a counsellor in the senior boys section" is consistent with a still-phased transition that year.^9 The Concordia archives also list a "Boys' Camping Committee" persisting through 1968–1969 alongside a broader "Camping Commission" from 1968–1970, likely reflecting the institutional reorganization that accompanied the shift.^1 See [[history/coeducation-gender|Coeducation and Gender at Kanawana]] for the fuller sourcing discussion, including its own Revision History resolving the exact-date question.

The change proved substantive rather than token: in 1972, just three years after formal coeducation, the first all-female Voyageurs de la Vérendrye canoe trip departed — notable because the Voyageurs program, introduced in the 1950s, had been one of the camp's most demanding and symbolically masculine offerings.^4 By the early 1980s, the section structure had been reorganized along gender lines: Pathfinders and Pioneers for girls, Coureurs des Bois and Woodsmen for boys — the parallel-track structure that (with the 2022 addition of the gender-neutral Mountaineers) persists into the present day.

## The 1970s: A Thinning Record

Kanawana's administrative record continues through the 1970s — Concordia's archives show "Camping and Outdoor Education Branch of Management minutes" for 1975–1978 replacing earlier committee structures — but the directorship itself grows harder to trace.^1 Stuart McLean, later the creator and host of CBC's *The Vinyl Cafe*, worked five summers at Kanawana beginning in 1969, rising from counsellor to assistant summer camp director for the 1974 and 1975 seasons; McMaster University's Stuart McLean fonds confirms he served under director Derek Walsh, whom McLean sublet his Montreal apartment to on leaving for Toronto.^5 See [[people/notable-alumni/stuart-mclean|Stuart McLean]] for his fuller biography. Richard Patten's 1976–1979 YMCA role, previously described here as placing him at the organizational head overseeing Kanawana, was in fact Executive Director of the **Centre-ville/Downtown branch** — a different office from the association's, and one with no line authority over the camp. Camping and Outdoor Education was its own parallel branch. See [[people/richard-patten|Richard Patten]].^10 G. David Twynam's directorship is documented from 1979, extending into at least the 1980 season per that year's *Ka-News* newsletter.^6 The 1976–1978 gap is narrower than this article previously stated. Digitized Kamp Kanawana director's reports on Internet Archive show the **1976, 1977 and 1978 reports all signed "Derek V. Walsh, Director of Camping"**, and the 1979 report signed "G. David Twynam / Director" — so Walsh remained in a camping leadership role for four years beyond the 1975 season this article had as his last.^11 ^12 **1978 is no longer unaccounted for**, as this article stated until 2026-08-14; the 1978 report survives, is signed by Walsh, and names Kerry Finucan as Assistant Camp Director. The caveat that does survive is the title: "Director of Camping" is the *branch* title, and whether Walsh was also running the camp on-site in those summers is not stated by any document read so far. See [[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana]]'s Gap Periods section.

## The 1980s: The Second Thin Decade

The 1980s are, after the 1947–1958 postwar gap, the thinnest-documented decade in Kanawana's directorial record. Twynam's tenure is last confirmed in 1980. Peter Gilling Goddard (1953–2016) is understood to have served as director for some or all of the 1981–1985 span, but his Kanawana connection is confirmed only via an obituary condolence mentioning "Kamp Kanawana," not by any camp record giving exact dates.^7 Jay Netherwood directed the camp in 1986 and 1987, succeeded in 1988 by his brother Bruce Netherwood, whose own directorship would carry through to the camp's 1994 Centennial.^8 Concordia's archives record "Situation reports profiling Kamp Kanawana, Camp Otoreke and Camp Weredale" from 1980, suggesting the three camps were being assessed together as a portfolio around this time — a rare institutional record from an otherwise sparse decade.^1 See [[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana]] and [[people/bruce-netherwood|Bruce Netherwood]] for full detail on this transition.

## Open Questions

1. [Important] Who directed Kanawana between 1976 and 1978, between Derek Walsh/Stuart McLean's departure and Dave Twynam's 1979 arrival?
2. [Important] What are Peter Goddard's exact directorship years within the 1981–1985 span? Only his obituary condolence, not a camp record, currently confirms his Kanawana connection at all.
3. [Nice-to-have] What prompted the 1980 "situation reports" assessing Kanawana, Camp Otoreke, and Camp Weredale together — was a consolidation or divestment being considered?
4. [Nice-to-have] Beyond the 1972 all-female Voyageur trip, how quickly did other Kanawana programs (beyond section structure) integrate coeducationally through the 1970s and 1980s?
