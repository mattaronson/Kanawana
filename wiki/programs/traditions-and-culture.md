## Summary

Camp Kanawana developed a rich set of traditions over its first four decades, documented in the 1922 and 1923 brochures, the 1935 season chronicle, and the 1938 Green Triangle newsletter. These traditions encompassed spiritual observances, competitive events, camp publications, ceremonies, and end-of-season rituals. Many reflected the YMCA's "four-fold" development philosophy (physical, intellectual, social, spiritual) while also building camp-specific identity and lore.

## Spiritual Life

The YMCA's Christian character shaped daily camp routines. Morning devotions were held around the flagpole, and evening vespers provided a contemplative close to each day.^1 An open-air chapel held its first service of the 1935 season on June 30.^2 On final nights of the camp season, conversion commitments were sought from campers.^1

## Camp Identity

The camp cheer in 1922 was "Yo Triumphy! Yo Triumphy! Hoben Sloben Rebecca Leeammour..." — a nonsensical chant that served as a rallying cry.^3 The camp motto was "Each for all and all for each."^3

## Annual Events and Competitions

The season was punctuated by recurring events:

- **Shawbridge Meet**: An annual athletic competition involving a hike to Shawbridge, with the J. Earl Birks Trophy awarded. As of 1922, it had been running for ten years (since approximately 1912).^3 ^4
- **Silver Trophies**: Annual inter-branch competition awards; in 1923, Westmount won Athletics and Aquatics for the third consecutive year.^5
- **Boating Carnival**: Documented in 1935.^2
- **All-Camp Regatta**: Documented in 1935.^2
- **Marois Day and Marois Regatta**: Events connected to the nearby Marois lake area.^2
- **Annual Circus**: Raised $5 in proceeds during the 1935 season.^2
- **Fancy Dress Ball**: Documented in the 1938 Green Triangle.^6
- **Pyjama Parade**: An annual procession to the post office.^2
- **Eating-Out Day**: At a "haunted house" location, documented in 1935.^2
- **Torch Ceremony for World Friendship**: Held in 1935.^2

## Camp Publications

- **The Green Triangle**: A camp newsletter published from 1932 to 1940. Only the July 29, 1938 issue has been digitized.^7
- **Ka-News**: Revived from the Green Triangle tradition in 1976 and 1978-1982.^7
- **The Gas Bag**: A 1923 "Re-union Number" documenting the 14th season — the earliest surviving camp publication.^5

## Ceremonial Traditions

The **Council of the Tribes of Kanawana** was newly introduced in 1935, reflecting the camp's engagement with woodcraft and "playing Indian" programming.^2

End-of-season traditions included:
- **Council Ring ceremony**: A closing gathering where a record of the season's activities was buried, followed by a "Leaders Grand Feed."^5
- **Dam release**: The dam was let out at the end of the 1935 season as a ceremonial close.^2
- **Time capsule**: A record of camp activities was buried during the 1935 season.^2
- **Best Camper Shields**: Awards presented at season's end.^2

## Honour System and Badges

The 1922 brochure documented a 32-subject Honour System, with the Large K as the highest award.^3 By 1923, this had expanded to 42 badges organized in a structured hierarchy:^5

- **Triangle Badge**: Required 8 badges across 4 categories (Athletic, Educational, Aquatic, Social Service)
- **Small k**: Required Life-Saving + 2 Aquatic, Bible Study + 2 Educational, Altruism + 2 Social Service, Hike + 2 Athletic
- **Large K**: Required 2nd-year camper, hold the Small k, instruct 3 weeks, complete 2 activities, attend Sunday service
- **Altruism Badge**: The highest badge, awarded by Director/Leaders, could not be "tried for"
- **KLS (Kanawana Life Saver)**: Based on the RLSS Award of Merit
