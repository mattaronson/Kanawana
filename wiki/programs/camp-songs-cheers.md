# Camp Songs and Cheers

## Overview

Music and communal singing have been part of Camp Kanawana's culture since its earliest documented years. The 1923 *Gas Bag* describes "the evenings around the fire with their story-telling and their sing-songs" as among the most treasured camp memories [src_gas_bag_1923]. By 1935, the camp held a "musicale" described as "a fine effort much appreciated by the whole camp" and "without precedent in the annals of Kanawana" [src_history_1935]. Over the decades, Kanawana developed a repertoire of camp-specific songs, cheers, and musical traditions, many of which remain part of camp life today.

## The Camp Song: "On My Way to Kanawana"

The official camp song, **"On My Way to Kanawana,"** was composed and performed by **Richard "Itch" Kerr** [f_0468]. Kerr was a Kanawana alumnus who received the inaugural Pip Alumni Award circa 2007 [f_0154]. The song accompanies the 1993/1996 YMCA film *Kamp Kanawana: The Experience that Lasts a Lifetime*, produced by Cathy Reeves (9 minutes, colour VHS) [f_0469].

The original recording is preserved in the Concordia University Archives as **P145/SR0001** (Sound Recordings series) [f_0468]. It appears to be the only known audio recording in the Kanawana archival collection.

The song held particular significance in camp culture. In 1967, a busload of Kanawana campers singing the Kanawana song greeted the CCA Centenary Journey paddlers upon their arrival in Ottawa [f_0283, f_0388].

## Camp Cheers and Yells

### The 1922 Cheer

The earliest documented camp cheer dates to 1922: **"Yo Triumphy! Yo Triumphy! Hoben Sloben Rebecca Leeammour..."** [f_0299]. The full text and the cheer's meaning or origin have not been identified. The nonsense-syllable structure is characteristic of early twentieth-century camp yells, which often drew on pseudo-Indigenous or deliberately absurd phrasing.

## Sing-Songs and Campfire Music

The **sing-song** was a central campfire tradition by 1923. The *Gas Bag* describes evenings around the fire with "story-telling and their sing-songs" [src_gas_bag_1923]. A "Sing-Sing Service" was held around the Senior raft during the 1923 season [src_gas_bag_1923]. The 1922 brochure lists "musical instruments" and "music" among items campers could bring to camp [src_brochure_1922].

## Song Books

The Concordia University Archives hold **Kanawana song books** and a **Canadian YMCA song book (1941-1945)** in the Program sub-sub-series P0145/12B07 [f_0459]. These archival materials likely contain the camp's historical song repertoire and would be the definitive source for documenting Kanawana's musical heritage.

## Grace at Meals

Grace before meals is a longstanding Kanawana tradition. The McMorris thesis identifies "Grace" as a feature of camp life [src_mcmorris_thesis], and the practice reflects the YMCA's Christian heritage and the camp's founding values. The specific words of Grace as said at Kanawana have not been documented in available written sources.
