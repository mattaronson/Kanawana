# The Modern Era: The Two-Tier Era (1995–Present)

*Status: draft | Sources: 9*
*Last Updated: 2026-07-11*

## Overview

In 1995, Kanawana split its single Camp Director role into two parallel positions: a year-round Executive Director based in Montreal, and a three-season on-site Director who ran the camp itself. That structure still governs the camp three decades later. Within it, the period saw the camp's second Centennial (1994), a major environmental "green shift" that transformed Kanawana into a three-season outdoor education centre, one Executive Director's eighteen-year tenure through two pandemics' worth of disruption, the introduction of gender-expansive programming, and the camp's first-ever day camp. This quarter-century-plus of history has previously been tabulated only director-by-director in [[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana]]'s Two-Tier Era tables, never narrated as continuous history.

## The Two-Tier Structure Begins (1995)

Bruce Netherwood's directorship, which had run since 1988 through the camp's 1994 Centennial (see [[chronology/centennial-1994|The 1994 Centennial]]), ended that year. In its place, the YMCA created a year-round Executive Director role, headquartered in Montreal and responsible for the organization overall, and a three-season on-site Director who ran the camp each summer — colloquially called "Chief," a title from the camp's "playing Indian" era, until it was retired in the early 2020s as part of Kanawana's reconciliation efforts.^1 Arleen Boyer served as the first Executive Director (1995–2000), with [[people/joanna-hoad|Joanna A.A. Hoad]] as on-site Director for the same span.

## Directorial Succession, 2001–2023

The Executive Director role passed from Boyer to [[people/gary-white|Gary White]] (2001–2002, per the Montreal YMCA's own annual reports), then — after an administratively murky mid-2000s stretch involving François Dauphin's concurrent head-office title in 2007 — to Sean Day, whose tenure ran from roughly 2005 until spring 2023.^2 Day's Executive Directorship spanned two pandemic-era closures: Kanawana closed overnight in 2021 and pivoted to day camps, then reopened in 2021 under his continued leadership; he also navigated 2022 staff shortages amid an industry-wide camp counsellor shortage.^3 On the on-site side, the directorship passed from Morgan Carter (2001–2003) to [[people/david-leduc|David Leduc]] (a single summer, 2004) to Nicolas Garcia (documented June 2009 – March 2014) to [[people/kate-taylor|Kate Taylor]] (2013 through the third session of 2023).^4 Taylor led the camp through both the 2020 and 2021 COVID-19 closure decisions and introduced a gender-expansive tent option for transgender, non-binary, and gender-questioning campers.^5 See [[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana]]'s Two-Tier Era tables for the full year-by-year record, including the structural ambiguities the annual reports themselves leave open about how the Executive/on-site split was actually documented in any given year.

## The Green Shift (2006–2012)

Beginning in 2006, Kanawana undertook a "virage vert" (green shift) aimed at reducing its ecological footprint and repositioning itself as an outdoor education centre; the Montreal YMCA's 2007 annual report frames it as a $5 million development and Quebec's first "green camp."^6 The Grand Portage cabin was demolished around 2006 to make way for new washroom buildings housing 16 commercial Clivus Multrum composting units and 32 dry toilets, installed specifically to protect Lake Kanawana from septic pollution; two green sanitary installations were tested during the summers of 2007 and 2008.^7 By December 2008, $1.48 million had been raised toward the $5 million goal, and by 2009 Phase I was nearly complete, with twelve new three-season cabins erected for younger campers and counsellors.^8 The project earned an honourable mention for environmental achievement at Saint-Sauveur's 2010 Gala Méritas, and a new eco-educational pavilion opened in 2012, funded partly by a $50,000 TELUS contribution toward a three-season educational pavilion.^9 The green shift enabled the camp to serve "four times more young people" than before, transforming it from a summer-only operation into a three-season facility — see [[context/environmental-history|Environmental Education and Stewardship at Kanawana]] for the full year-by-year account, including subsequent water-quality monitoring data and school-partnership conservation programming.

## Gender-Expansive Programming and the Mountaineers (2022)

Building on the coeducation-era section structure established in the late 1960s and 1970s (see [[chronology/between-centennials|Between Centennials]]), Kanawana introduced the Mountaineer section in 2022 — a gender-neutral program for campers aged 13–15, alongside Kate Taylor's earlier gender-expansive tent option. Where coeducation had introduced a second gender category to a previously single-gender institution, the Mountaineer program represents a further evolution beyond a strictly binary framework — see [[context/coeducation-gender|Coeducation and Gender at Kanawana]] for the fuller history of this progression.

## Leadership Transition and the First Day Camp (2023–Present)

In spring 2023, [[people/marie-pierre-lacasse|Marie-Pierre Lacasse]] became Executive Director, succeeding Sean Day; she had earlier held a Kanawana role as director of expeditions (2007–2010) before a 13-year break. Kate Taylor departed partway through the 2023 season, succeeded on-site for the remainder of that summer by her Assistant Director, Kevin Slezak, who then served the full 2024 season; Justin Caldwell has held the on-site role since 2025. In 2024, Lacasse oversaw the launch of "Aventure à Kanawana" (Kanawana Adventure) — the first day camp in the camp's 130-year history, an English-immersion program for ages 5 to 12.
