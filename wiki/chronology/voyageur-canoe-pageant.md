# The Centennial Voyageur Canoe Pageant (1967)

*Last Updated: 2026-03-15 (stub created from 14 KB facts)*

## Overview

The Centennial Voyageur Canoe Pageant was a competitive canoe race held from May 24 to September 4, 1967, as part of Canada's centennial celebrations.^1 Ten teams — representing eight provinces plus Yukon and Northwest Territories (Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland did not participate) — paddled 3,283 miles with 60 miles of portaging over 104 days, following the historic fur trade route from Rocky Mountain House, Alberta, to Expo '67 in Montreal.^1 ^2

The event was organized by the federal Centennial Commission and was distinct from the CCA Centenary Journey, a separate youth camping canoe trip that took place the same year.^3

## The Race

Each team consisted of 10 men paddling in 6-man shifts.^1 Each canoe was named after a Canadian explorer (David Thompson, Henry Kelsey, Pierre La Vérendrye, among others).^1 Judy LaMarsh, Secretary of State, started and finished the race.^1 John Fisher, the Centennial Commissioner, greeted paddlers in Ottawa on August 29, 1967.^1

Manitoba won the race with a time of 507 hours, 21 minutes, and 51 seconds.^1 The full Manitoba team was inducted into the Manitoba Sports Hall of Fame in 2010.^4

The route concluded with paddling the Lachine Rapids, where the Yukon team was swamped and had to be rescued.^1

## Notable Participants

Don Starkell, later famous as an adventurer and author (*Paddle to the Sea*, *Paddle to the Amazon*), was a member of the Manitoba winning team.^1

Twenty-four young Jesuits in four canoes retraced 17th-century missionary routes and joined the pageant at North Bay on August 21, 1967.^1

## Connection to Kanawana

The Voyageur Canoe Pageant was part of the same 1967 centennial spirit that shaped programming at Camp Kanawana and other Canadian camps. The CCA Centenary Journey — a separate youth canoe trip organized by the Canadian Camping Association — is the more direct camping connection.^3 Taylor Statten Camps also began sending groups to Quetico in 1965 as part of the OCA Centennial Program.^3

## Academic Study

Misao Dean published "The Centennial Voyageur Canoe Pageant as Historical Re-enactment" in the *Journal of Canadian Studies* 40:3, analyzing the event's nationalist symbolism.^5
