# The Postwar Gap (1947–1966)

*Status: draft | Sources: 8*
*Last Updated: 2026-07-11*

## Overview

The two decades between Nelson McEwen's 1947 departure and Canada's 1967 Centennial are the thinnest-documented stretch in Kanawana's directorial history — CLAUDE.md itself names the "1947–2003 director gap" as a standing research priority. Within that stretch, the years 1947–1958 remain a true blank: no director is documented at all. But the period is not silent institutionally — it includes A. Ross Seaman's eight-year directorship (1959–1967), the founding of Sir George Williams University's Department of Applied Social Science by a Kanawana-linked researcher, and the beginnings of the La Vérendrye canoe-tripping program that still bears his stamp today.

## The True Gap (1947–1958)

Nelson McEwen, who had directed Kanawana in an administrative capacity since roughly 1927, left Montreal in 1947 to become General Secretary of the Saint John YMCA in New Brunswick.^1 No source in this KB names a Kanawana director for the following eleven years. A. Ross Seaman's directorship is confirmed only from 1959, and neither the transition itself nor any interim director has been documented despite extensive search.^2 This is the single largest undocumented span in the camp's post-founding history — see [[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana]]'s Gap Periods section for the full research status.

What is known from this window comes from people who passed through Kanawana on their way elsewhere, rather than from any camp record itself. In 1951, McEwen — by then National Council Boys' Work Secretary — recruited Ross Bannerman into YMCA camp work at Camp Pascobac; Bannerman went on to direct Camp Stephens in Winnipeg in 1956 before eventually authoring his own 1969 report on Kamp Kanawana. See [[people/nelson-mcewen|Nelson McEwen]] and [[people/ross-bannerman|Ross Bannerman]] for both men's fuller Kanawana-adjacent careers. Separately, A. Ross Seaman himself has a documented Camp Perrot directorship "about 1950," predating his own arrival at Kanawana — a 1952 Camp Perrot annual report thanks him as "Past Director of Camp Perrot," and a 1959 internal memo records him personally planting trees there around that date.^3 See [[places/camp-perrot|Camp Perrot]] for the primary-source detail.

## A. Ross Seaman's Directorship (1959–1967)

A. Ross Seaman directed Kamp Kanawana from 1959 until 1967, overseeing the 1959 section renaming and the launch of the La Vérendrye canoe-trip program, which grew from exploratory trips in 1958 into a formal satellite operation with a lease at Lac Landron by the early 1960s.^4 ^5 The Montreal YMCA's 1966–67 Annual Report, read directly from the Internet Archive, documents Seaman's "participatory democracy" philosophy and that season's structure.^6 Seaman went on to a parallel academic career, joining Sir George Williams University's Department of Applied Social Science as part-time faculty in 1963, the same year he transitioned away from full-time camp directing — see [[people/a-ross-seaman|A. Ross Seaman]] for his full biography.

## The SGW–Kanawana Research Bridge (1960–1963)

In 1960–1961, Hedley Gardner Dimock — Coordinator of Staff Development and Training for the Montreal YMCA and a lecturer at Sir George Williams University — conducted research at Kanawana on camp counsellor effectiveness, producing two internal studies on counsellor attitude change and selection prediction, held unpublished in Concordia Archives Box HA2323.^7 A related, previously undocumented third report extended this research program to 1970. In 1963, with the support of SGW Principal Robert C. Rae, Dimock became the first Chairman of the university's Department of Applied Social Science and first Director of its Centre for Human Relations and Community Studies — an institution that grew, in its own words, "directly out of Sir George Williams University's roots in the community, specifically through the YMCA of Montreal."^7 Seaman himself joined the department as part-time faculty that same year, an active collaborator in the Centre's research going forward. See [[people/hedley-dimock|Hedley Dimock]] for the full research history and [[context/sgw-concordia-connection|Sir George Williams / Concordia University and Camp Kanawana]] for the institutional pipeline this represents.

## Open Questions

1. [Critical] Who, if anyone, directed Kanawana between 1947 and 1958? This is the single largest undocumented span in the camp's post-founding directorial history. See [[people/directors-index|Directors and Staff of Camp Kanawana]]'s Gap Periods section for the ongoing research status.
2. [Important] What programming or institutional changes occurred at Kanawana during the 1947–1958 gap? No source addresses this period's day-to-day operations at all.
3. [Nice-to-have] What specific programming changes did Seaman make at Kanawana during his earlier (pre-1966) seasons? Only his final 1966–67 season is documented in detail via the Annual Report; his 1959–1965 seasons remain comparatively thin.
