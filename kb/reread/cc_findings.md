# Canadian Camping — full word-for-word read

Started 2026-09-04. Every issue read in full, not term-searched. One block per
issue: what it is, what it contains that bears on Kanawana, the YMCA of Montreal,
Quebec camping, or the people already in the KB — and an explicit "nothing" when
that is the honest answer, so a later pass can tell a read issue from an unread one.

## 1 — Vol. 1 No. 1, February 1949

The first printed issue; the masthead says it is "the natural evolution of two
years of mimeographed bulletins," so the CCA had been publishing since c. 1947.
Quarterly, 25c a copy, included in CCA membership.

**No mention of Kanawana, and no mention of the YMCA of Montreal.** The camps
named in this issue are Ontario ones (Ahmek, Pine Crest, Adanac, Wabi-Kon,
Kenemogay, Bolton, Powow, De la Salle).

Bears on the KB anyway:

- The CCA executive of 1949 is half Montreal. President **Dais L. Gass**,
  4870 Côte des Neiges Rd, Montreal. Secretary-Treasurer **Hay Finlay**,
  475 Pine Ave West, Montreal. Honorary President A. L. Cochrane (Toronto);
  Past President Taylor Statten; Editor Mary S. Edgar; Publishing agent
  Fred Haiblen. Vice-Presidents are the provincial presidents ex officio —
  which is the structure Seaman, Locke and Walsh later moved through.
- **Hedley S. Dimock** writes the lead article, "The Magic of Camping," and is
  described as *Dean of George Williams College, Chicago* and *Canadian-born,
  in Saskatchewan*. His "thirty years" of camping goes back to the Saskatchewan
  prairie. Reviewed in the same issue: his *Administration of the Modern Camp*
  (Association Press, 1948). His earlier books cited: *Camping and Character*
  (1929, with Hendry) and the standards work *Marks of Good Camping* (1941).
  He and Taylor Statten co-wrote *Talks to Counselors*.
- Cover photograph credited to **Arthur Friedman, Montreal, Quebec**.
- Anne Vail, a former CCA secretary, writes from London.
- The editor's address to readers names "the vast Laurentian playground of
  Quebec" as one of the magazine's five constituencies.
- The 1949 OCA conference (Feb 18-19, Central YWCA, 21 McGill St, Toronto) had
  Dimock and Fritz Redl as key speakers; the 1948 conference drew 280 people
  plus a separate camper assembly of 100+ children.

## 2 — Vol. 1 No. 2, April 1949

**No Kanawana.** But the single most useful page in the issue for us is
"Canadian Camping Items," which minutes the CCA board meeting at the Royal York
Hotel, Toronto, **20 February 1949**, and names the Montreal delegation:

- Miss **Dais L. Gass** — President, CCA
- Miss **Sybil Ross** — Secretary
- Miss **Diana Cumner** — President, **Quebec Camping Association**
- Miss **Dorothy Percival** — Director, **Camp Ouareau**
- Mrs. **Mutchler** — Secretary, Junior Leagues
- Mr. **C. A. Wiley** — Chairman, Finance Committee

Two decisions recorded: the CCA is to take **a part-time office secretary and an
office at 1421 Atwater Avenue, Montreal**; and the CCA Annual Meeting is to be
held **in Montreal in October 1949**, concurrent with the Recreation Congress.
So the national association's office was in Montreal from 1949 — worth holding
against the Kanawana directors' later CCA/QCA offices.

Also here: Ottawa camp leaders met at the Château Laurier on **12 March 1949** to
organize an Ottawa Regional Section of the OCA. The February OCA conference drew
**over 400** enrolled (the previous issue, written before it, had said 280 at the
1948 one). C. T. Sharpe, Assistant Director of Camping, Ontario Department of
Education, outlined provincial financial assistance to agency camps meeting
health and low-cost requirements — the Ontario precedent for the kind of
government support Quebec camps later sought.

A CCA **crest design competition** ran in 1949: India-ink designs, minimum four
inches, due 15 September 1949 to Arthur Buckley, Chairman of the Crest Committee,
YMCA, Geneva Park P.O., Ontario; $15 and an honorary membership to the winner,
plus a scroll to the winner's camp. (Whether a Kanawana camper entered is
unknown; the results should appear in a later issue — watch for them.)

Taylor Statten, writing from Palm Beach, is identified as **first President of
both the OCA and the CCA**. His wife's camp name "Tonakela" appears.

Nothing on the YMCA of Montreal. Camps named: Ahmek, Wapomeo, Bolton, Glen
Bernard, Pine Crest, Ouareau, Wabi-Kon, Parker, Onawaw, Adanac.

## 3 — Vol. 1 No. 3, June 1949  ★ Kanawana named

Contains **"Quebec Camps," by Agnes Knox Mutchler, Director, Junior League Camp,
Montreal** (pp. 19-20) — the single most substantial early survey of the Quebec
field in the run, and the article that names Kanawana. A prior pass mined this
issue for the Kanawana sentence; it did not take the rest of the article, so
three named Quebec camping officers below appear nowhere in the KB or wiki.

Verbatim, the passage that matters most:

> "Certainly a boys' camp sponsored by the Y.M.C.A. of Montreal in 1893 and
> called Otoreke was among the earliest. Kanawana, at present directed by Roy
> Locke, Vice-President of the Quebec Section, had a mysterious birth about this
> time under the same sponsorship."

Two things worth holding onto. First, a 1949 Quebec camping officer writing for
the national magazine dates Otoreke to **1893** and calls Kanawana's own birth
**"mysterious"** and only "about this time" — i.e. the founding date was already
unsettled inside the Quebec camping community forty-five years after the fact,
which is evidence about the state of the record, not just about the date.
Second, it fixes **Roy Locke** as simultaneously Director of Kanawana and
**Vice-President of the Quebec Section** in June 1949.

The rest of the article, none of which is in the KB:

- **Forty-five permanent camps** registered with the Quebec Camping Section in
  1949; **twenty-nine** of them non-profit, split between those funded by public
  contribution and those whose fees cover operating expenses.
- Two weeks is the shortest camping experience generally offered by 1949.
- **Camp Lewis**, under **Mr. Wylie**, takes **five hundred boys** for two weeks
  or longer each year. (Almost certainly the C. A. Wiley who chaired the CCA
  finance committee in the April issue — spelling varies.)
- **Junior League Camp**: one hundred girls, three weeks, three times a summer.
  Started "more than twenty-five years ago" (so c. 1923 or earlier) by **Miss
  Lighthall** of Montreal, on **family property near Ste-Marguerite**.
- **Camp Chapleau** — "which is Quebec's Bolton" — takes mothers and children;
  Director **Miss Nan Vail**. NEW NAME, not in KB. (Probably kin to the Anne
  Vail who was CCA secretary and wrote from London in the February issue.)
- **Camp Oolahwan** (YWCA) "was hewn from the woods in 1919" under **Mary
  Edgar** — the magazine's own editor — has **its own lake and six hundred
  acres**, and is directed in 1949 by **Diana Cumner**, President of the Quebec
  Section. NEW NAME, not in KB.
- The **Quebec Camping Association "had its origin in the thirties"** and is an
  original member of the CCA.
- Registered Quebec camps cluster in two areas, the Laurentians and the Eastern
  Townships; the Section knows almost nothing of camping along the lower St.
  Lawrence.
- The Section **"has backed the appointment of year-round Directors for
  non-profit camps,"** for continuity of programme and winter training — the
  policy context for Kanawana's own year-round directorship.
- The Section was pressing camps to **winterise** for skiing: "In a Province
  almost as famous as the Alps for luxury skiing, it seems ridiculous that any
  camps are closed to ordinary people." Bears directly on `winter-programming.md`.
- **Mr. Al Dodds of the Central Y.M.C.A. is Secretary of the Quebec Section.**
  NEW NAME, not in KB — a Montreal Central Y man in a Quebec camping office in
  1949, alongside Locke as Vice-President.
- Mutchler's opening paragraphs are an oral-history lament: the pioneers'
  stories "are simply amazing … Some day these stories will be written and may
  they appear, at least in part, in Canadian Camping." They largely were not.

Elsewhere in the issue, Quebec notes: a **four-evening counsellor training
course in March 1949 with more than one hundred registered**, representing all
types of camps; a meeting of **ten non-profit camps** convened by **Miss Ross of
the Social Agencies** (the Sybil Ross of the April board list), discussing costs
and relations with the social agencies, with a follow-up planned for autumn; and
a Spring Conference for Counsellors, 13-14 May 1949. A **National Recreation
Forum, "Leisure — a National Issue," was set for on or about 20 October 1949 in
Montreal**, sponsored by eight national organizations, replacing the Canadian
Recreation Congress; the CCA annual meeting was to sit immediately before or
after it.

Also: the first **National Camp Directors' Seminar and Workshop**, Camp Wabi-Kon,
Timagami, 6-11 September 1949, $37.50 all in — director Charles E. Hendry,
resource specialists Hedley S. Dimock and Alan F. Klein. Open to directors in
all provinces.

## 4 — Vol. 1 No. 4, December 1949

**Kanawana not named.** But this is the issue in which the CCA's whole officer
slate moves to Montreal, which matters for reading everything that follows.

At the CCA annual/board meeting, **Château Laurier, Ottawa, 11-13 November
1949**, an interim executive was elected to serve until the February annual
meeting:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Anne Vail** | President | Montreal |
| Irwin Haladner | 1st Vice-President | Toronto |
| W. E. (Ted) Yard | 2nd Vice-President | Toronto (nominated by BC and Sask.) |
| **Agnes Mutchler** | Secretary | Montreal (1445 Crescent St.) |
| **Ernest (E. O.) Freedman** | Treasurer | Montreal (10 Ontario St. W.) |

Past Presidents now listed as Taylor Statten (Toronto) and **Dais L. Gass**
(Montreal). The **CCA National Office address is given as Coursol Street,
Montreal** — the masthead says **2045** Coursol St. and Vail's own message on
p. 12 says **2035** Coursol St., in the same issue. Recording both; do not
"correct" one silently.

**Anne I. Vail is almost certainly the "Miss Nan Vail" named as Director of Camp
Chapleau** in the June issue (Nan is a standard short form of Anne, both are
Montreal camping women, and the February issue has Anne Vail writing from London
as a former CCA secretary while the June issue was written in her absence). Not
asserted — flagged as a candidate identity for `p_298`-style human judgement.

Quebec notes, autumn 1949 — the Section held two meetings before the season's
camps were closed. **"The program arranged by Roy Lock[e] was different and
stimulating"** at the October meeting: short talks by young staff on swimming,
canoe trips, religion in camp programs, counsellor personality problems,
peculiar camper behaviour and nature experiences. **Ruby Smith** was Social
Convener. NEW NAME. The provincial contact list gives **Quebec — Miss Diana
Cumner, Y.W.C.A., 1124 Dorchester St. West, Montreal (President)**.

So for late 1949 the Quebec Section officers are: Cumner President, **Locke
Vice-President**, Al Dodds Secretary, Ruby Smith Social Convener — with Vail,
Mutchler and Freedman of Montreal simultaneously running the national body.

Other content: the **OCA minimum standards adopted in 1949**, printed in full
(licence from the Provincial Department of Health; pasteurized milk; weekly hot
bath in camps over two weeks; doctor available; resident nurse where over two
weeks and over thirty children; staff minimum age **seventeen**; ratios of one
cabin counsellor per **six** campers under ten and per **eight** aged ten to
seventeen; one lifeguard per ten swimmers; a rest period of not less than one
hour daily). Useful as the contemporary yardstick against which Kanawana's own
staffing ratios and waterfront rules can be read. Standards committee chaired by
John Hoyle.

**A. L. Cochrane**, CCA Honorary President and Director of Camp Temagami, was
given a plaque by Ontario's Attorney-General and Minister of Education, the Hon.
Dana Porter, for fifty years directing his camp; he was in his eightieth year,
and still annually let himself be thrown off the dock sewn in a sack with a
ten-pound stone, hands and feet tied, surfacing inside a minute. Motto on the
tablet: *Ad Unguem Factus Homo*.

## 5 — Vol. 2 No. 1, February 1950

**Kanawana not named.** Two leads worth chasing, one of them possibly large.

**★ Alan Mills spoke to the Quebec Camping Association on camp music.** The
Quebec notes list the Section's regular monthly meetings that winter: "Dr. Allan
Ross speaking on Health, **Mr. Allan Mills, the radio singer on Camp Music** and
Dr. Wilson speaking on Camp generally." This is almost certainly Alan Mills, the
Montreal folksinger and CBC broadcaster (born Albert Miller), the best-known
collector and popularizer of Canadian folk song of that generation. A named
occasion on which Quebec camp directors were addressed on camp music by a
professional folk-song collector, in the same city and the same association as
Kanawana's director, is a concrete vector for how songs travelled into Quebec
camps. **Queue as a research thread for `p_048` (songs and cheers).**

**★ Associated Screen News Limited, 2000 Northcliffe Avenue, Montreal**,
advertises "de luxe photographic postcards as sold in leading resorts of Canada
… in lots of 1,000 or more," plus lantern slides, transparencies and murals, to
camps. A Montreal firm selling camp postcards by the thousand in 1950 is the
most likely origin for any Kanawana postcard that survives. **Queue for the
Phase 2 visual-culture campaign** — ASN's records are at Library and Archives
Canada and the McCord.

The **QCA Annual Spring Conference** was set for **31 March - 1 April 1950,
Sir Arthur Currie Gymnasium, McGill University**, jointly with the Eastern
Ontario Camping Association; key speaker **Catherine Hammett**. QCA address
1124 Dorchester St. W. Separately the **McGill Extension Department** gave the
Quebec Section a ten-week lecture course that winter; the camping names on it
were **Anne Vail, Norah Bailey** (new name) **and Dorothy Percival**.

The **CCA was incorporated** — a new constitution accepted by the Secretary of
State for Canada, with the objects printed in full (including the power to
acquire and dispose of real property). Ten Goals for 1950 are listed, among them
a **Camp Directory of all Canadian camps** in two sections, members and
non-members — if that directory was ever printed it would list Kanawana, so
**watch later issues for it**. Also goal 8: to explore having the Dominion
government build camps for children in national parks, rentable by agencies.

Ontario's research committee bulletin "Know Your Camps" gives comparative
figures worth keeping: **114 camps and camp groups in the OCA; 41% private, 49%
organization, 10% "special"; boys' camps outnumber girls' two to one; almost as
many co-educational camps as girls' camps; twenty-eight camps opened since
1945**; and **"The first camp in Ontario opened in 1893 and is the Y.M.C.A. Camp
On-Da-Da-Waks at Golden Lake, Ontario."** That 1893 date sits beside Mutchler's
1893 for Otoreke — the YMCA was starting camps in two provinces in the same
year, which is context for the founding question rather than evidence about it.

## 6 — Vol. 2 No. 2, April 1950

**Kanawana not named.** One identification resolved and one small Quebec name.

**★ The Vail identification is settled by the magazine itself**, on p. 18: "Miss
Anne Vail, President of the Canadian Camping Association, is Executive Secretary
of **Iverley Community Centre, Montreal**, and **Director of Chapleau Camp in the
Laurentians**. Miss Vail is a graduate of the **McGill School of Social Work**."
So the "Miss Nan Vail, the Director" of Camp Chapleau in the June 1949 issue and
Anne I. Vail, CCA President, are one person. The candidate identity flagged under
issue 4 is confirmed on documentary evidence — treat it as established, not a
guess.

CCA board met in **Toronto, 12 March 1950**, Vail presiding. Committee chairs
recorded: **Dorothy Percival** (Membership), **Mary S. Edgar** (Magazine),
**Art Buckley** (Public Relations), **Rev. John Hoyle** (Standards), **Keith
Cleverdon** (Sites Development) — Cleverdon a civil engineer and camp director
who undertook to write a series on sanitation and site development for the
magazine, starting next issue. Alan Klein offered a programme advisory service
answering letters at the CCA, **2035 Coursol Street, Montreal**.

Quebec notes: the 31 March-1 April conference was organised by **Mr. Sam
Freedman** and his committee (distinct from Ernest O. Freedman, the CCA
treasurer). The McGill Extension course is named: **"Organized Camping," ten
lectures.**

Two things to file for later chasing:

- A **Red Cross Water Safety Instructor course was scheduled for Montreal in May
  1950 specifically "for Canadian Camping Association"** — the mechanism by
  which Quebec camp waterfront staff got certified. Relevant to any Kanawana
  waterfront qualification claim of this period.
- **Empire Crockery Company, 139 Peter St., Montreal** — wholesale crockery,
  cutlery, kitchenware and manufacturers of food-serving equipment — is the
  first *Montreal* camp supplier to advertise in the run. Worth noting as
  Kanawana's plausible supply channel; the Toronto houses dominate everywhere
  else.

Obituary: **Ernest A. Chapman**, "Skipper," died **12 March 1950**, having
directed **Camp Kagawong for forty-three years** with more than six thousand
campers; past president of the OCA. The OCA closing banquet stood in silence
that night.

Also: John Fisher devoted a CBC broadcast to camping after speaking at the OCA
conference (standing in for Bernard Mason, who withdrew); **disk recordings of
the fifteen-minute talk were available from the CCA in Montreal**. Ontario's
Department of Education announced that minimum standards would become a
**condition of licence from 1951**.

## 7 — Vol. 2 No. 3, June 1950  ★ Kanawana named

This is the issue with the Caserta hospital reminiscence, already in the KB as
part of `f_2288`. What the earlier extraction took was the passage; what it did
not take is **who wrote it**.

**★ John P. Kidd is identified in the byline as "Executive Director, Canadian
Citizenship Council, Ottawa."** The article is "Campers Are Citizens" (pp. 8-9).
He writes in the first person as the Kanawana leader the wounded soldier
recognised — "the way some of you leaders helped me" — so the 1934 Kanawana
staff member is the man who by 1950 ran the national citizenship body. `f_2288`
records the memory but not the identification, and "Citizenship Council" appears
nowhere in the KB or the wiki. **This meets the article-spawning threshold
(named person who held a camp role) and should get a stub**; it also opens a
documented biography — the Canadian Citizenship Council's records and Kidd's
own later career are findable.

The rest of the article is Kidd's programme for camps as "a laboratory for
democracy": that the CCA survey citizenship programming and democratic practice
in Canadian camps, institute a national **"Know Canada Week"** for camps, and
arrange a **CBC national broadcast originating in a camp with the Prime Minister
participating**; that individual camps invite MPs, mayors and councillors to be
a camper for a day and a night, bring in **"the D.P.'s and other new
immigrants"** as part of programme, use Citizenship Branch filmstrips, and make
1 July more than a sports day. Whether Kanawana acted on any of this is an open
question worth putting to the 1950s annual reports.

Quebec notes for the season, signed **Nora Bailey** — who is identified on p. 7
as **"Psychologist and Educational Assistant at the Mental Hygiene Institute,
Montreal, and co-owner and co-director of Camp Macaza, P. Quebec."** New name,
new camp (Macaza), and a professional psychologist inside the Quebec camping
executive. Her article, "Let's Take the Pressure Off," argues camp is first a
holiday and a mental-health resource for apartment-dwelling city children —
a useful period statement of what Montreal camp people thought camp was *for*.

- **QCA Annual Spring Conference, 31 March-1 April 1950: over three hundred
  attended**, from all over Quebec and Eastern Ontario, with **representatives
  from some French camps** — the first note in the run of French-language Quebec
  camps appearing at the anglophone association's conference. Twenty-five
  exhibits. Chairman Sam Fredman (spelled Freedman in the April issue).
- The Quebec **annual meeting in May 1950** was a supper meeting; guest speaker
  **Miss Anne Silver, Executive Director of the Montreal Y.W.C.A.**

Red Cross in Quebec: a **Montreal and District refresher course 8-10 May 1950**,
and an **Instructors' Course in Swimming and Water Safety in Montreal in May
"specially prepared to train Swimming Instructors and Waterfront Supervisors for
camps and beaches in the Province of Quebec … in co-operation with the Canadian
Camping Association (Quebec Section)."** This is the certification pipeline for
Quebec camp waterfront staff in 1950 and should be cited wherever the wiki
describes waterfront qualifications in this era.

New Montreal supplier in the advertising: **Canadian Sanitation Company, 804
Jean Talon St. W., Montreal**, selling the "Hygeosan" septic toilet needing
neither electricity nor running water, "Installed in many schools, camps and
cottages." With Empire Crockery (Peter St.) this is the second Montreal-based
camp supplier; both are candidates for Kanawana's actual supply chain.

R. K. Cleverdon's first Camp Site Development article launches a **free
mimeographed series mailed from the CCA office in Montreal to paid-up members**,
twelve titles listed (master plan, water supply, latrines and septic tanks,
kitchen waste, low-cost shower unit, kitchen layout, administration centre, camp
hospital, waterfront improvement/canoe racks/diving towers, cabin types, tent
floors, cost estimation). If any of those mimeographs survive in the YMCA fonds
they would document exactly the terms in which Kanawana's own buildings were
planned. He gives camp capital cost as **$300 to $1,000 per camper** in 1950.

## 8 — Vol. 2 No. 4, December 1950

**Kanawana not named.** Two Montreal items of substance.

**★ The CCA opened its first National Office, in Montreal, in October 1950** —
Anne Vail's own account, headed "EXTRA! Exciting News!": "After exploring all
kinds of possibilities … we finally decided it was time to act, not to plan. In
October, we signed the lease… The address is **771 BURNSIDE, Montreal;
telephone Harbour 4350**. (Eaton's parking lot is just across the street.)" The
masthead gives it as **Room 221, 771 Burnside Ave.** The furnishing is described
in detail — a rug, a typewriter desk and chair, a borrowed typewriter, a
standard lamp and a McCaskel photograph "Marine" from a rummage sale, fifty
bricks and boards lent by a camp for a bookcase, tables and chairs on loan from
other camps. **Agnes Mutchler was appointed part-time Executive Secretary**;
Dorothy Percival moved the membership files out of her apartment; **Ruby Smith**
took pamphlets and books; **Dais Gass** convened a committee on office
furnishings. Two counsellors were making a wall map of Canada to pin members to.

That is the national association of Canadian camping, run out of one rented room
on Burnside Avenue in Montreal by four women, at the moment Roy Locke was
directing Kanawana and sitting as a Quebec vice-president.

**★ "International Canoe Trips," submitted by Anne I. Vail for Scotty Brotman**,
reported at a **Quebec Camping Association meeting on 24 November 1950**. For
five seasons (so from 1946) **Scotty Brotman** had led a crew of eight from
**Montreal to New York by canoe — over four hundred miles in a nineteen-foot
canoe**. The route is given in full: push off from **Victoria Pier, Montreal**,
across the St. Lawrence to St. Lambert; portage by truck to St-Jean to save
eighty miles; down the Richelieu to Lake Champlain; Canadian customs at
**Lacolle**, American customs at **Rouses Point**; the length of Champlain,
often in five-foot waves and ship backwash; west from Ticonderoga to **Lake
George** ("the Lake Louise of the east"); trucked around Hudson Falls to the
Hudson; then down through the locks to New York City. Brotman also ran "his own
personal crusade to bring adults in touch with the outdoors."

Not in the KB (grep count above). This is the most ambitious Quebec camp canoe
trip documented anywhere in the run so far and belongs as context in
`traditions/canoe-trips.md` — it sets the scale against which Kanawana's own
trips should be read, and Brotman is a Montreal camping figure worth a search.

Two more Montreal supplier addresses to add to the growing list: **Foodcraft
Laboratories Ltd. now at 1462 Bishop Street, Room 15, Montreal** as well as
Toronto; Simpson's Special Contract Division lists a Montreal branch.

The rest of the issue is national and Ontario: A. L. Cochrane's Christmas
greeting (noting he "began trip-camping with boys in 1900, and since then he has
directed Camp Temagami for fifty seasons"); Mary S. Edgar on story-telling;
Bruno Morawetz of Camp Ponacka; the second Cleverdon article, on sanitary
facilities, which is a full technical survey of open pit, pit privy, "bucket a
day" semi-septic tank and flush-and-septic systems with 1950 costs ($100-125 for
a single semi-septic outfit; $175 for one installed flush toilet; ~$350 for a
1,250-gallon two-compartment septic tank; **one toilet per ten children** and
never fewer than two per living area) — the yardstick for any claim about
Kanawana's sanitation in this period. And "Camps that are Different," on camps
for children with disabilities — Merrywood-on-the-Rideau, Blue Mountain and
Woodeden (Ontario Society for Crippled Children), Illahee Lodge at Cobourg for
children with heart, chest and diabetic conditions (founded 1946, also running
three sessions a year for people over sixty), and five CNIB camps across Canada.
Worth holding beside Kanawana's own 1970 cystic-fibrosis campers: this is the
movement-wide context that decision sat in, twenty years earlier.

## 9 — Vol. 3 No. 1, February 1951  ★ Roy Locke, twice

**Kanawana not named**, but its director is, twice, and the issue is the richest
so far for Quebec.

**★ Roy Locke attended the CCA national board meeting at the YWCA in Montreal on
10 December 1950.** The attendance list is printed in full: "Anne Vail, Agnes
Mutchler, Dorothy Percival, Dais Gass, Nora Bailey, Ruby Smith, **Kenneth
Murray**, **C. A. Wylie**, **Roy Locke**, Sam Freedman, and from Ontario, Mary
Edgar, Joyce Bertram and C. T. Sharpe." Out-of-town members had been shown the
new Burnside Street headquarters the evening before.

**★ Roy D. Locke is named "president of the Quebec Association"** and chaired
the programme committee for the **Biennial National Conference of the CCA, held
jointly with the 1951 Spring Conference of the Quebec and Eastern Ontario
Association, 6-8 April 1951, in Montreal at the Grenadier Guards' Armoury**,
theme **"Outdoor Living"**. Sam Freedman was conference chairman; the CCA annual
meeting sat on the Sunday morning, 8 April. Locke's committee "has already met
several times" and introduced a structural innovation — splitting conference
topics into **three integrated workshops or seminars following each other in
logical sequence** on campcraft, naturelore and handicrafts, rather than
single one-hour sessions.

So Kanawana's director went **Vice-President of the Quebec Section (June 1949)
→ President of the Quebec Association (by February 1951)**, and personally
designed the programme of the national conference the year the CCA met in
Montreal. Both offices are documented here; "Grenadier Guard" and the 1951
national conference appear nowhere in the KB.

The advertised programme names the speakers Locke's committee secured:

- **"Cap'n Bill" Vinal**, Professor of Nature Education, University of
  Massachusetts — nature-lore
- **Dr. Carter Storr**, co-author of *The Canoe and You*, campcraft workshops
- **Dr. Taylor Statten, Jr., Child Psychiatrist, Children's Memorial Hospital,
  MONTREAL** — in the April 1949 issue he was at Johns Hopkins in Baltimore, so
  he had come to Montreal in the interval; a Statten practising child psychiatry
  in Montreal while Kanawana's director ran the Quebec association is a
  connection worth following
- **Miss Betty Jaques**, Art Instructor, **Macdonald College** — handicrafts
- **Mrs. Alice Turnham**, Assistant Curator, **Redpath Museum, McGill** —
  nature-lore

**★ Quebec Section, November 1950: "Mr. Sam Vatcher of the Y.M.C.A. spoke on
Indian camp fire ceremonies."** (OCR gives his name twice, once as "Vateller" —
Vatcher is the correct reading.) A Montreal YMCA man giving the province's camp
directors a talk on Indian campfire ceremonial, five months before the national
conference, is direct evidence about where the Council Ring material in Quebec
YMCA camps came from. **Not in the KB — zero hits. Queue for `council-ring.md`
and for a biographical search on Sam Vatcher.** The October 1950 Quebec meeting
had members from various camps describing their summers — "a red-letter day at
one camp, how another built a summer program around a central theme, religion in
camp, how one camp endeavoured to encourage democratic practises" — unattributed,
but one of those camps may well be Kanawana.

Another Montreal supplier: **Bell, Rinfret & Co. Limited, 366 Notre Dame St.
West** — crockery, cutlery, glassware, kitchen equipment, silverware, soda
fountain supplies, plastic tableware.

The rest is Ontario and technical: Cleverdon's third article, "So You're Going to
Build," a complete primer on frame camp construction with 1951 Toronto unit
costs and, usefully, **minimum floor areas — 40 sq. ft. per camper, 60 per
junior staff member, 90 per senior or married staff**; W. E. Yard on winter
camping, recording the Vancouver YMCA's **first winter camp for boys, December
1943** on Hollyburn Ridge ("The Skyline Camp for Boys," 23 boys and 7 staff,
logging-camp nomenclature — highriggers, sparkchasers, whistlepunks, snag
